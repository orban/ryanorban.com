## Summary

James Clear's guide to intermittent fasting on his personal site covers the main protocols, the research behind them, and practical implementation. James Clear is the author of Atomic Habits, and his writing style is characteristic: clear explanations, actionable frameworks, references to research without being academic.

Intermittent fasting is not a diet in the traditional sense — it doesn't specify what to eat, only when. The major protocols are: **16:8** (fast for 16 hours, eat within an 8-hour window — usually skipping breakfast), **the 5:2 diet** (eat normally 5 days, restrict to ~500 calories on 2 non-consecutive days), and **eat-stop-eat** (24-hour fasts once or twice per week). The 16:8 method is the most popular because it maps easily onto skipping breakfast without significant lifestyle disruption.

The proposed mechanisms include: insulin sensitivity improvements (lower insulin during fasted periods improves fat metabolism), autophagy activation (cellular cleanup processes that are suppressed when insulin is elevated), caloric restriction by default (most people don't fully compensate for the eating window restriction), and human growth hormone elevation during fasting. The evidence quality varies significantly across these claims — caloric restriction effects are most robust, autophagy benefits in humans are more speculative.

The guide is notable for including a section on the history and evolution of human eating patterns — the argument that frequent meals are a modern artifact, and that humans evolved with irregular access to food. Whether this evolutionary argument supports intermittent fasting as optimal is contested, but it provides useful framing.

## Key points

- Main protocols: 16:8 (most common), 5:2, eat-stop-eat (24-hour fasts).
- Proposed mechanisms: insulin sensitivity, autophagy, default caloric restriction, HGH elevation.
- Most robust evidence is for caloric restriction effects — the eating window naturally reduces total intake.
- Autophagy claims are real but most human studies are short-term or indirect; the benefit magnitude is uncertain.
- No specific foods required — it's a timing protocol, not a dietary restriction.
- By James Clear of Atomic Habits — good practical framing even if the research section is simplified.
