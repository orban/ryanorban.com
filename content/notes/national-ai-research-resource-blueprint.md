## Summary

Daniel Ho, Jennifer King, Russell Wald, and Christopher Wan at Stanford HAI propose a National AI Research Resource (NAIRR) — a shared infrastructure of compute, data, and software tools for academic AI researchers who lack access to the GPU clusters and proprietary datasets available inside large tech companies. The white paper (October 2021) became one of the most influential policy documents shaping US AI research policy.

The argument is structural: AI research has bifurcated into a two-tier system. Industry labs (Google, OpenAI, Meta, Microsoft) have essentially unlimited compute and massive proprietary datasets. Academic researchers are restricted to small-scale experiments on public data — which increasingly cannot reproduce or challenge industry results. This threatens the diversity and independence of AI research, concentrating capability in institutions with commercial incentives and creating a talent pipeline that trains primarily for industry.

The NAIRR blueprint proposes a federated system modeled on NSF supercomputing centers: federally funded compute accessible via competitive grants, curated datasets with proper governance, model repositories, and training tools. The goal is not to out-compute industry but to ensure the research community can study, evaluate, and challenge frontier AI systems. The paper shaped subsequent Congressional action; the NAIRR Pilot launched under NSF in 2024, providing limited access to cloud compute for eligible researchers.

## Key points

- NAIRR addresses AI research inequality: industry has unlimited compute; academics can't reproduce frontier results
- Proposed federal compute infrastructure modeled on existing NSF supercomputing and research network programs
- Includes data governance, model sharing, and curated datasets — not just raw compute
- Independent academic research is essential for AI safety, fairness, and policy evaluation — can't be fully industry-led
- Blueprint shaped the NAIRR Pilot launched by NSF/OSTP in 2024 — a smaller version is now operating
- Reflects Stanford HAI's policy mission: bridging AI research capability and democratic oversight
