## Summary

This Dune Analytics dashboard by hagaetc tracks decentralized exchange (DEX) metrics across the major DeFi protocols — primarily on Ethereum but expanding to Layer 2 networks and other chains. Dune Analytics enables anyone to write SQL queries against indexed blockchain data and publish dashboards; this DEX metrics dashboard became one of the most-referenced public resources for tracking the competitive landscape between exchanges.

The metrics covered: daily and weekly trading volume by DEX, market share trends (how much of total DEX volume goes to Uniswap vs SushiSwap vs Curve Finance vs Balancer vs 0x relayers), unique traders, liquidity (TVL — total value locked), and fee revenue. Uniswap v2 and v3 dominated throughout 2021-2022, with Curve taking significant share for stablecoin swaps and SushiSwap occupying the vampire attack storyline from its Uniswap fork origin.

Dune Analytics itself is a significant platform: it democratized blockchain analytics by abstracting raw node data into SQL-queryable tables. Before Dune, getting this data required running your own node and writing custom indexers. The dashboard sharing model created a community of on-chain analysts who built valuable public resources, often for free, to establish expertise and reputation in the web3 data space.

## Key points

- Tracks DEX trading volume, market share, and liquidity across Uniswap, SushiSwap, Curve, Balancer, dYdX
- Dune Analytics: SQL-queryable blockchain data — democratized on-chain analytics without running nodes
- Automated Market Maker (AMM) model dominates: liquidity pools replace order books for most DEX volume
- Uniswap v3 (May 2021) introduced concentrated liquidity — capital-efficient but more complex for LPs
- TVL and volume are distinct metrics: TVL measures parked capital, volume measures actual trading activity
