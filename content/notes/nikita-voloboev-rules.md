## Summary

Nikita Voloboev maintains a public personal wiki at wiki.nikitavoloboev.xyz containing his evolving set of personal rules — explicit principles that govern how he approaches work, learning, creativity, and life decisions. The rules page is notable for its specificity: not vague maxims but concrete behavioral guidelines that can actually inform decisions in the moment.

Nikita Voloboev is known in the productivity and developer community for his obsessive system-building — he maintains one of the most comprehensive public personal knowledge systems, built in Notion and later Obsidian-adjacent tools. His rules page is part of a broader knowledge graph covering his tools, approaches, reading, and goals.

The rules cover several domains: how to approach learning (optimize for understanding over completion), how to manage attention (do one thing at a time, minimize context switching), how to maintain creative output (establish systems over goals, review regularly), how to handle relationships and communication (be direct, be kind), and how to make decisions under uncertainty. The page has been cited in the productivity community as an example of personal operating system design — making implicit values and heuristics explicit.

The link now redirects to an unrelated domain, suggesting the wiki has moved or gone offline — a common fate for personal wikis over time.

## Key points

- Making personal rules explicit forces clarity about values — the act of writing I will always X when Y surfaces whether you actually believe it.
- Public personal wikis create accountability and invite collaboration: others find patterns in your rules that you've missed.
- Nikita's rules reflect the productivity maximizer subculture: deliberate about attention, learning efficiency, and creative output in ways most people leave to habit.
- The wiki-as-second-brain approach (public, linked, evolving) influenced many personal knowledge management practitioners.
- Related: Buster Benson's Codex Vitae, Derek Sivers's public notes — a tradition of public explication of personal philosophy.
