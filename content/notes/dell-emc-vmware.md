## Summary

The $67 billion Dell acquisition of EMC in 2015 was the largest tech acquisition ever at the time, and Andreessen Horowitz's breakdown makes the strategic logic clear. Dell's core problem: revenue was falling across nearly every hardware category as markets shifted toward cloud computing and commodity hardware. Going private in 2013 bought flexibility but not transformation. The EMC acquisition was Dell's move to become a full-stack enterprise infrastructure company.

The most underexamined driver was activist investor pressure. Elliott Management had been pushing EMC to break itself apart — the traditional activist playbook of unlocking hidden value by splitting the federation. By merging with Dell (which had just beaten off Carl Icahn), EMC could go private and execute its long-term strategy without quarterly public market scrutiny. The deal wasn't just about scale; it was about freedom from short-term capital markets pressure.

VMware is the most complex piece. Dell structured the transaction to maintain 97% voting control of VMware while holding only 28% economic interest — via a tracking stock that traded against VMware's financials. Michael Dell publicly committed to VMware's independence, but the arrangement left genuine ambiguity about whether VMware would eventually be fully absorbed or truly spun out. The VMware complexity reflected a real tension: it was the deal's most valuable asset but also the one whose value depended most on independence.

## Key points

- Dell's strategic problem: commodity hardware decline required a move to full-stack enterprise infrastructure.
- Activist pressure (Elliott on EMC, Icahn on Dell) was a significant driver — going private removed that constraint.
- VMware tracking stock structure: 97% voting control, 28% economic interest — unusual financial engineering.
- Combined entity rivaled IBM in enterprise infrastructure revenue.
- Lesson: large enterprise deals often hinge on distribution (salesforce) as much as product — "these products do not sell themselves."
- VMware was eventually spun off fully in 2021; the ambiguity lasted years.
