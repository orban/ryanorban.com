## Summary

The Simple Sabotage Field Manual was written in 1944 by the OSS (Office of Strategic Services, the predecessor to the CIA) as a guide for ordinary citizens in occupied countries to subtly disrupt enemy organizations. The document teaches non-violent sabotage through bureaucratic inefficiency rather than physical destruction.

The section on organizations and conferences has become famous because it reads like a satirical manual for bad management. Recommendations include: insist on doing everything through proper channels; talk as frequently as possible and at great length; raise irrelevant issues whenever possible; refer matters to committees for further study and investigation; make speeches; be pedantic about procedures; demand written confirmation for verbal decisions; never arrive at a decision — bring it up at the next meeting. The document was declassified and a GitHub gist made it widely circulated among management-critical tech workers.

The resonance is uncomfortable: behaviors described as deliberate sabotage are indistinguishable from what happens naturally in large organizations through bad incentives and bureaucratic drift. The field manual is a useful lens for diagnosing institutional dysfunction — if your organization's decision-making looks like an occupied enemy's, something has gone wrong with the incentive structure.

## Key points

- 1944 OSS/CIA manual on non-violent organizational sabotage through deliberate bureaucratic inefficiency.
- The conference/management section is darkly funny: its sabotage tactics are normal dysfunction in large orgs.
- Declassified and widely circulated; the GitHub gist version became a popular management reference.
- Useful diagnostic: if your org's decision process resembles the sabotage instructions, incentives are broken.
- Connects to Goodhart's Law, institutional inertia, and analyses of bureaucratic dysfunction.
- Written to disrupt enemy organizations; accidentally documents how organizations fail by default.
