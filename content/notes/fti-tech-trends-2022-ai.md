## Summary

FTI Consulting's 15th annual tech trends report (Vol. 01) covers the state of artificial intelligence in 2022 across enterprise, consumer, creative, research, geopolitics, and society. It reads as a timestamp of the AI discourse just before large language models and diffusion models broke into mainstream awareness — GPT-3 and DALL-E are already present, but the generative wave hasn't yet peaked.

The enterprise section covers MLOps, low-code/no-code machine learning, serverless computing, AI in the cloud, and AI at the edge. The creative field section features AI-assisted invention, generative algorithms for content production, and neural rendering. The report dedicates substantial space to AI ethics, bias in AI, and emerging regulatory frameworks from both the EU and US — concerns that remain unresolved three years later.

The geopolitics section examines US-China competition in AI development, China's AI ethics frameworks versus its strategic AI deployment, and the emerging mil-tech industrial complex. The society section flags expanding facial recognition use, ambient surveillance, and deepfakes as persistent risks. The research section discusses supersized AI models, textless NLP, and the early work on AI alignment — all of which have since accelerated dramatically.

## Key points

- Theory of mind models and emotion simulation listed as near-term consumer trends in 2022 — still largely unrealized
- MLOps and AI in the cloud identified as key enterprise infrastructure trends; this has since matured into a standard stack
- AI bias and problematic training data flagged as persistent unsolved problems alongside the push for larger models
- The China spotlight section gives a grounded view of geopolitical AI competition: market expansion, ethics lip service, and strategic panopticon
- Protein folding (AlphaFold) and AI-accelerated scientific discovery highlighted in the health section as genuine near-term breakthroughs
