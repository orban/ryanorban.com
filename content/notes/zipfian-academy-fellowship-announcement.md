## Summary

Zipfian Academy announced two new program tracks in June 2014: the Data Fellowship and the Data Engineering Immersive. The original Zipfian Academy program was a single 12-week data science cohort — this expansion acknowledged that the data science umbrella covered genuinely different roles that benefit from different training.

The Data Fellowship targeted students who needed more time to build foundational skills before the full immersive — a part-time or preparatory track. The Data Engineering Immersive addressed the growing demand for engineers who could build the data pipelines, ETL systems, and infrastructure that data scientists depend on. By 2014 it was clear that many companies needed people who could set up Hadoop clusters and build Spark jobs, not just people who could run analyses on clean data.

The expansion reflects a broader maturation in how companies thought about data roles. The early data scientist does everything era was giving way to specialization: data analysts, data scientists, data engineers, and ML engineers as distinct (if overlapping) roles. Zipfian Academy's curriculum split acknowledged this segmentation while it was still forming.

## Key points

- The split from single cohort to multiple tracks reflects the specialization happening in industry: data scientists and data engineers need different toolsets.
- Data Engineering track covered Hadoop, Spark, distributed computing, and data pipeline design — the infrastructure layer that data scientists often can't build themselves.
- Zipfian Academy was competing with General Assembly, Galvanize, and Insight Data Science for students and instructor talent by mid-2014.
- The Fellowship format (lighter-touch preparation) addressed a pipeline problem: many interested candidates lacked the quantitative baseline for the full immersive.
- Historical context: Zipfian Academy was later acquired by Galvanize in 2015, merging the data science and general tech bootcamp ecosystems.
