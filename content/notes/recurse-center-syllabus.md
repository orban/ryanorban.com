## Summary

Ashley Blewer (digital preservation specialist, archivist, and programmer) published her self-directed curriculum for her Recurse Center batch in Spring 2018. Recurse Center (RC) is an unusual educational program: a free, self-directed coding retreat in New York where programmers spend 6 or 12 weeks working on projects they choose themselves, with the structure being primarily peer-driven. There are no teachers, no grades, no curriculum — participants are expected to define their own learning goals. Ashley's syllabus is her approach to that blank-slate situation.

Her areas of focus reflect her professional work in digital preservation and media archiving: audio/video formats and codecs (FFmpeg, codec internals, container formats), networking protocols, compiler construction, and systems programming fundamentals. This is notably different from the standard learn web dev learning path — it's oriented toward the deep internals of the systems that process and transmit media, which most self-taught programmers never study formally. The syllabus includes specific books and resources for each area.

The value of publishing this isn't the specific curriculum (anyone's RC batch differs) — it's the model of intentional self-directed learning. RC is famous for the impossible things framing: bring a list of things that feel too hard and work on those, rather than solidifying already-comfortable skills. Ashley's syllabus embodies this: audio codec internals and compiler construction are exactly the kind of technically intimidating topics that a self-directed retreat is designed to tackle. The document is a useful reference for anyone designing their own learning program in a sabbatical, study period, or structured self-directed phase.

## Key points

- Recurse Center: self-directed coding retreat — no curriculum, no teachers, peer-driven learning in 6/12-week batches.
- Focus areas: audio/video formats, networking, compiler construction, systems programming — chosen based on professional context in digital preservation.
- The "impossible things" principle: tackle technically intimidating topics rather than comfortable ones.
- Useful as a template for structuring intentional self-directed learning periods.
- By Ashley Blewer — digital preservation specialist known for FFmpeg documentation and media archiving work.
- RC alumni community is strong: many notable programmers have attended, and the peer network outlasts the batch.
