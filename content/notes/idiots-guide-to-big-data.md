## Summary

Mediasmiths' explainer targets business audiences trying to understand the big data wave of 2013. The framing around the "3 Vs" — Volume (more data than fits on one machine), Velocity (data arriving faster than traditional systems can process), and Variety (structured tables plus unstructured text, images, logs) — was the standard framework popularized by Gartner analyst Doug Laney in 2001 and widely adopted as big data's defining characteristics.

The explainer covered the basic technology landscape: Hadoop for batch processing, NoSQL databases (MongoDB, Cassandra) for flexible schema storage, and cloud platforms (AWS, Google Cloud) for scalable infrastructure. It positioned data scientists as the professionals who extract value from these systems and noted the emerging talent scarcity.

As a 2013 artifact, the piece reflects the hype peak of the big data cycle. Many of the specific tools it references were already being challenged by faster alternatives (Apache Spark over MapReduce), and the 3 Vs framing would be extended to 5 Vs and beyond by subsequent analysts. But the underlying problem statement — data volumes are growing faster than traditional database architectures can handle — was and remains accurate.

## Key points

- 3 Vs of big data: Volume (scale), Velocity (stream speed), Variety (structured + unstructured)
- Hadoop + HDFS for distributed batch storage and processing — the 2013 default stack
- NoSQL as a response to Variety: schemaless storage for heterogeneous data
- Gartner analyst Doug Laney originated the 3 Vs framework in a 2001 report
- The big data label was already contested in 2013 — some argued it was marketing, not a new discipline
- Business value chain: raw data → processing → insight → decision — the gap at each step is where vendors positioned
