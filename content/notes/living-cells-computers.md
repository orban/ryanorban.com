## Summary

This Nature News piece covered research on implementing Boolean logic gates inside living cells using engineered DNA and protein components — a milestone for biological computing. The content field notes this as "First data storage, now logic gates forged from DNA" and tags it as synthetic biology and the singularity framing suggests it was shared as evidence of accelerating biological engineering capability.

The fundamental insight behind biological logic gates: cells already perform complex conditional computation (gene regulatory networks evaluate multiple signals and produce outputs). Synthetic biologists at MIT, Stanford, and other institutions were engineering artificial versions of this logic using standard biological parts — promoters, transcription factors, and RNA components that could be composed into AND, OR, NOT, and NAND gates operating inside live bacteria or yeast.

The practical applications were significant: cells that execute therapeutic logic (release drug only if cancer biomarkers A AND B are present but NOT normal cell marker C), biosensors that detect environmental conditions through multiple simultaneous inputs, or bioreactors with embedded control logic. The DARPA-funded work on biological computing was motivated by applications in programmable medicine and bio-manufacturing.

## Key points

- Boolean logic gates in cells: engineered genetic circuits implementing AND/OR/NOT operations — cells that compute based on molecular inputs
- Synthetic biology standard parts: the iGEM BioBricks library was building a catalog of characterized, composable biological components for this kind of engineering
- Practical application: therapeutic logic — cells that sense a combination of biomarkers and release a response only when conditions are met
- DNA data storage (also advancing in 2013) and biological computing represented two separate but related tracks — storage and processing at the molecular level
- The Central Dogma of molecular biology (DNA → RNA → protein) provides the physical substrate for information processing in cells
