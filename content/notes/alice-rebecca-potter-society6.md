## Summary

Alice Rebecca Potter is an illustrator whose work draws on natural history illustration — the tradition of precise, beautiful scientific drawings of plants, animals, and geological specimens. Her Society6 shop sold prints and merchandise derived from her illustration work, which typically featured botanical subjects rendered in careful ink with selective color.

Society6 (launched 2009) was part of the early print-on-demand wave for artists — along with Etsy, Redbubble, and Threadless, it created a path for illustrators to build commercial audiences directly, without requiring gallery representation, publisher agreements, or licensing deals. Artists upload work, Society6 handles printing and fulfillment on phone cases, prints, tote bags, and home goods. The economics were thin but the access was transformative for emerging illustrators.

The natural history illustration aesthetic had a cultural moment in 2012-2013: the combination of precision craftsmanship, scientific subject matter, and the sense of rediscovering historical illustration techniques felt like an antidote to digital design's clean geometry. It also connected to the broader maker culture interest in craft and handwork. Illustrators working in this style found audiences on Tumblr and Pinterest that converted to Society6 sales.

## Key points

- Alice Rebecca Potter: illustrator working in the natural history / botanical illustration tradition
- Society6: print-on-demand platform for artists — handles production and fulfillment; artists keep a margin on sales
- Natural history illustration: scientific illustration tradition going back to botanical surveys, field guides, and cabinet of curiosity documentation
- Platform dynamics: Society6 / Etsy / Redbubble created the first generation of illustrators with direct-to-consumer audiences
- The botanical/natural history aesthetic was a 2012-2015 cultural moment, concurrent with the broader craft/handmade revival
