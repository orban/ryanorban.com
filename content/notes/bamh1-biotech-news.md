## Summary

BamH1.com was a biotech and life sciences news aggregator positioned for technically literate readers — the for nerds framing in the tagline distinguished it from mainstream science news sites. The name is a molecular biology pun: BamHI is one of the most widely used restriction enzymes in molecular cloning, derived from the bacterium *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens*. It recognizes the sequence GGATCC and cuts between the two G bases — known to anyone who has done basic cloning work.

The site occupies a niche between mainstream science journalism (too accessible, too slow) and academic journals (too specialized, too slow to cover news). In 2013, this space was inhabited by a handful of blogs and aggregators: GenomeWeb (industry news, paywalled), In the Pipeline (Derek Lowe's drug discovery blog), and various researcher-written blogs. The DIY bio and synthetic biology community had its own informal news culture via Twitter and email lists.

The bookmark was saved alongside BioRxiv precursors, biohacking content, and DIY biology links — the early 2013 version of what's now a larger biotech Twitter/Bluesky/Substack ecosystem.

## Key points

- BamHI restriction enzyme: the name is the joke — ubiquitous in molecular biology labs, instantly recognizable as a signal for "this site is for people who do wet lab work or equivalent"
- Restriction enzyme: bacterial defense protein that cuts DNA at specific recognition sequences; the fundamental tool of recombinant DNA technology since the 1970s
- Aggregator niche: between mainstream science journalism and academic papers — for practitioners who want fast, technically accurate coverage
- 2013 context: biotech news was fragmented; Twitter was becoming the dominant real-time channel for the research community
- The synthetic biology and biohacking communities overlapped heavily in this media space
