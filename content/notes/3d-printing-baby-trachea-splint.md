## Summary

Doctors at the University of Michigan used 3D printing in 2013 to fabricate a custom tracheal splint for an infant named Kaiba Gionfriddo, who suffered from tracheobronchomalacia — a condition where the airway collapses during breathing. The splint was designed using imaging data from the baby's own trachea and printed in polylactic acid (PLA), a biodegradable material that dissolves over approximately three years as the baby's airway grows into a stable shape.

The case was significant because conventional treatment couldn't resolve the underlying problem quickly enough. The FDA granted emergency clearance for the custom device under a humanitarian device exemption. The manufacturing turnaround from design to printed device was about a day — a timeline that would have been impossible with traditional machined implants requiring specialized tooling.

The tweet that originated this bookmark came from MakerBot, connecting the story to the broader consumer 3D printing movement. This connection was partly accurate (the physical technology is related) and partly misleading — the medical-grade device was printed on professional equipment, not a desktop FDM printer. But the story circulated as evidence that consumer-grade additive manufacturing was on a trajectory toward meaningful medical applications.

## Key points

- Tracheobronchomalacia: the airway lacks sufficient cartilage rigidity — the trachea collapses during breathing, starving the infant of oxygen.
- Custom geometry from medical imaging: CT scan data of the baby's airway fed directly into the CAD model — impossible to replicate with off-the-shelf implants.
- Biodegradable implant: polylactic acid scaffold dissolves over ~3 years, by which time the trachea should have grown structurally stable.
- FDA emergency humanitarian device exemption: regulatory pathway that enables fast-tracked use of novel devices when no alternatives exist.
- Validated the mass customization case for medical 3D printing — patient-specific geometry is the application that best justifies additive manufacturing's cost.
