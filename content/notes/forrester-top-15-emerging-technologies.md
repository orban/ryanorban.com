## Summary

Forrester Research published its annual emerging technology list for 2013, covered by VentureBeat. Forrester analyst reports carry significant weight in enterprise technology adoption — IT budgets are partly allocated based on analyst guidance, so inclusion on these lists is commercially valuable for vendors.

The 2013 list reflects the dominant anxieties and opportunities of the moment: big data and Hadoop, cloud computing (particularly hybrid cloud), mobile enterprise applications, natural language interfaces, and what would later be called IoT. Forrester was known for coining the term zero trust (network security), but in 2013 the security framing was more about mobile device management and BYOD (bring your own device).

What's interesting about these annual lists as historical artifacts: they reveal what the enterprise analyst class (who lag the practitioner cutting edge by 2-3 years) considered validated and ready for mainstream adoption. Something appearing on a Forrester list in 2013 was typically already 3-5 years into development in tech-forward companies — the list marks its graduation to enterprise sales cycle.

## Key points

- Forrester lists in 2013 likely included: big data analytics, cloud computing (IaaS/PaaS), mobile applications, social business platforms, and early IoT framing
- Enterprise analyst reports (Forrester, Gartner) function as technology legitimacy signals — inclusion validates enterprise budget allocation for a category
- The lag: technologies appear on Forrester lists when they're safe to recommend, typically 3-5 years after early adopters proved them out
- Gartner Hype Cycle and Forrester's similar lists are useful historical documents — comparing predictions to actual outcomes is informative about analyst accuracy
- 2013 was the year when cloud computing moved from emerging to established category in enterprise buyer thinking
