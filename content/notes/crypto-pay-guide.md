## Summary

The Crypto Pay Guide is a series from C3 Group (a compensation consultancy) covering how full-time employees and contributors in the crypto industry get paid. Published on Mirror, the series addresses the unique challenges of compensation in Web3 — where typical packages mix traditional salary with token grants, and where the volatility, vesting, and tax treatment of token compensation create complexity that doesn't exist in traditional tech.

The core tension in crypto compensation is that token grants can be enormously valuable (if the project succeeds) or worthless (if it doesn't), and unlike equity grants in traditional companies, tokens often have immediate liquidity — which creates different vesting and tax dynamics. An employee who receives tokens with immediate liquidity owes tax when the tokens vest (as ordinary income) even if they don't sell, and then capital gains if they sell later. This catches many crypto employees off-guard.

The guide likely covers: base salary benchmarking in crypto (typically competitive with traditional tech at similar-stage companies), token grant structures (cliff vesting, linear vesting, lockup periods), the distinction between tokens issued to employees vs. tokens purchased at a discount, DAO contributor compensation models (often token-based with no salary equivalent), and the role of token grants in aligning incentives between contributors and protocol success.

The 2022 context matters: this was during the crypto hiring boom, when many traditional tech employees were being recruited with promises of large token grants. Understanding the mechanics of those grants was genuinely important.

## Key points

- Crypto compensation packages mix base salary (cash) with token grants — the token component creates unique vesting and tax complexity.
- Token grant taxation: tokens received as compensation are taxed as ordinary income at vesting, even if illiquid.
- DAO contributor models often substitute token compensation for traditional employment entirely.
- Vesting schedules (cliff + linear) exist for tokens just as for equity, but immediate token liquidity changes the incentive structure.
- Published on Mirror — itself a Web3 publishing platform, signaling alignment with the crypto-native audience.
