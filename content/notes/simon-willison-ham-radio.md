## Summary

Simon Willison is a developer and writer who publishes detailed TIL (Today I Learned) posts on his site. This post documents how he used LLMs — primarily Claude — to prepare for the ham radio General class license exam. The General exam is significantly harder than the Technician entry-level exam, requiring knowledge of propagation, antenna theory, electronic circuits, operating procedures, and FCC regulations.

His method: work through question pools from the exam database, ask Claude to explain any concept he didn't understand, and use the LLM to generate related practice questions and deeper explanations of the underlying physics. Rather than rote memorizing 400+ questions (the traditional approach for ham exams), he built conceptual understanding using the LLM as a patient, always-available tutor. The LLM's breadth of knowledge across RF engineering, electronics, and regulations made it well-suited for this role.

The meta-lesson is about how LLMs change self-directed learning for technical domains. The traditional approach (textbooks, online courses, flashcards) works but is slow. LLMs let you ask "why does the SWR go up when the feedline is an odd multiple of a half-wavelength?" and get an immediate, pitched-to-your-level explanation, then follow up with "give me an analogy using water pipes or what are the practical implications for a portable station?" This tightly-coupled question-answer-follow-up loop is qualitatively different from passive study.

## Key points

- Used Claude to explain ham radio concepts behind exam questions rather than memorizing answers.
- LLM as tutor: immediate, personalized explanations at whatever depth needed.
- Topics covered: RF propagation, antenna theory, electronics, FCC regulations.
- The value isn't passing the exam — it's actually understanding the material.
- Demonstrates LLMs as tools for self-directed learning in technical domains.
