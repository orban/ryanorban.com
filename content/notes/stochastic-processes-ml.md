## Summary

This textbook by Vincent Granville covers stochastic processes and simulation from a machine learning perspective — bridging the gap between classical probability theory (as taught in mathematics or statistics programs) and the computational, simulation-heavy approach prevalent in ML research. Volume 1 focuses on point processes in one and two dimensions, including spatial processes and clustering.

The distinguishing framing is ML perspective: rather than proving theorems, the book emphasizes simulation and empirical exploration. Granville introduces the Poisson-binomial process as a foundational concept, from standard Poisson processes to lattice-based variants, with coverage of GPU-accelerated clustering, nearest neighbor analysis, Weibull distributions, random graphs, and extreme value theory.

The book includes 28 exercises with solutions, Python source code, and Excel spreadsheets — a practitioner-oriented approach more aligned with how data scientists and ML engineers actually encounter probabilistic reasoning (through simulation and testing) than through measure-theoretic proofs. Aimed at college-educated professionals in quantitative fields: physics, finance, machine learning, engineering.

## Key points

- Covers stochastic processes (point processes, spatial processes) from an ML/simulation perspective.
- By Vincent Granville — applied quantitative practitioner background, not pure mathematics.
- Volume 1: point processes, Poisson-binomial process, GPU clustering, extreme value theory.
- Python code + Excel + 28 exercises with solutions — hands-on, simulation-first orientation.
- Bridges classical probability theory and machine learning's computational approach to uncertainty.
- Useful for data scientists who want rigorous probabilistic foundations without measure theory.
