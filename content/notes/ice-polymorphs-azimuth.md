## Summary

John Carlos Baez surveys the remarkable variety of ice polymorphs — distinct crystal structures of solid water — on his Azimuth blog. At the time of writing (2012), at least 15 distinct phases of ice were known, each stable under different pressure and temperature conditions. Ordinary ice (the kind in your freezer) is ice Ih, a hexagonal crystal structure. As you increase pressure, you get different structures: ice III, ice V, ice VI, and so on, each with different densities and properties.

The bookmarking context (the bookmarker noted "one step closer to ice-nine and the end of the world as we know it") references Kurt Vonnegut's Cat's Cradle, where ice-nine is a fictional polymorph of water that is stable at room temperature and spreads on contact with liquid water, eventually freezing all the oceans. Ice IX actually exists in the real phase diagram of water — it's stable at very low temperatures and high pressures — but unlike Vonnegut's version, it's not stable at room temperature and won't trigger any civilizational catastrophes.

What makes this genuinely interesting from a materials perspective is that the polymorphism of water is unusual in its richness: most substances have 2-3 solid phases; water has 15+. This is a consequence of the hydrogen bond network that water molecules form — the directionality of hydrogen bonds allows many different stable geometric arrangements, each corresponding to a different pressure-temperature regime.

## Key points

- At least 15 distinct ice polymorphs are known, each stable under different pressure/temperature conditions.
- Ordinary ice is ice Ih (hexagonal); the Roman numeral progression tracks increasing pressure phases.
- Ice IX is real but bears no resemblance to Vonnegut's fictional version — it's only stable at extreme cold and pressure, not room temperature.
- The richness of water's phase diagram comes from the directionality of hydrogen bonds, which allows many stable geometric arrangements.
- Baez connects this to active research: ice XV was discovered in 2009 and there may be more phases at extreme pressures.
