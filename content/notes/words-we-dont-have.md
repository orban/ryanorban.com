## Summary

Words We Don't Have is a site collecting and explaining words from other languages that lack direct English equivalents — terms that capture emotional states, social situations, or concepts that English speakers experience but don't have precise vocabulary for. Examples: the Portuguese saudade (longing for something absent or lost), the Japanese komorebi (sunlight filtering through leaves), the Danish hygge (cozy contentment), the German Weltschmerz (world-weariness), or the Finnish sisu (stoic resilience under hardship).

The premise rests on a thesis about language and thought: the words a language has shape (or at least make more accessible) the concepts its speakers can express. This is related to linguistic relativity — the idea that language influences cognition, associated with Sapir-Whorf hypothesis. The softer version (which most linguists accept) is that having a word for something makes that concept more cognitively available — easier to notice, communicate, and reason about. Collecting untranslatable words is therefore a kind of concept archeology: discovering things other cultures found important enough to name.

The genre has been popular in books too — Adam Jacot de Boinod's *The Meaning of Tingo*, Howard Rheingold's *They Have a Word for It*, Tim Lomas's Positive Lexicography project cataloguing positive emotional terms across languages. The web format allows for community submissions and expansion. These collections appeal to people interested in linguistics, anthropology, cross-cultural psychology, and language design.

## Key points

- Collects words from other languages that lack English equivalents — concepts English speakers experience but can't precisely name.
- Examples: Portuguese saudade, Japanese komorebi, Danish hygge, German Weltschmerz.
- Touches on linguistic relativity: having a word makes a concept more cognitively accessible.
- Related to positive lexicography (Tim Lomas) and the broader project of mapping human emotional vocabulary cross-culturally.
- The genre appeals to linguistics, anthropology, and anyone interested in how culture shapes perception.
- Community-extensible — users can submit words from languages they know.
