## Summary

Fletcher Richman's post on the Galvanize blog takes a pragmatic, demystifying approach to startup fundraising — aimed at technical founders who understand code but haven't navigated venture capital before. The hacker framing is about approaching fundraising as a system to understand and work efficiently, not about tricks.

The core advice covers: distinguishing seed round from Series A dynamics (very different processes with different investor expectations); how to run a fundraising process efficiently (warm intros, compressed timeline, creating urgency through parallel conversations); understanding term sheets well enough to know which terms actually matter (valuation, dilution, pro-rata rights, control provisions) versus which are noise; and the psychological dimension of rejection — fundraising has a very low hit rate and founders who internalize individual "no"s lose momentum.

Galvanize was positioned at the intersection of tech education and startup support — its campuses served both bootcamp students and early-stage companies. Posts like this reflected the dual audience: people learning to build technical skills and people trying to build companies in those same buildings. The fundraising landscape of 2015 was frothy (the peak of the last unicorn boom before the 2016 correction), which adds context: advice that worked in 2015 had somewhat different base rates than later years.

## Key points

- Seed round and Series A are different processes — different investors, different diligence, different timelines.
- Compressed, parallel fundraising process creates urgency and prevents investors from stringing founders along.
- Term sheet terms that actually matter: valuation, dilution, pro-rata, board control — most other terms are noise.
- Low hit rate is structural, not personal — preserving psychological momentum is a real SKILL.
- Written from within Galvanize's startup-community context — practitioner perspective, not VC perspective.
