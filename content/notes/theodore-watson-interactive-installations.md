## Summary

Theodore Watson (Theo Watson) is a New York-based creative technologist who has been one of the most prolific contributors to the openFrameworks ecosystem. He co-created ofxKinect, one of the first open-source drivers for the Microsoft Kinect sensor in openFrameworks, which was released in November 2010 — within days of the Kinect's retail launch — and became the standard tool for Kinect-based interactive installations.

His own work spans large-scale interactive installations, often using computer vision, motion tracking, and physical computing to create environments that respond to viewer presence and movement. Works include interactive shadow play, generative audio-visual systems, and collaborative pieces with Emily Gobeille (his partner, who works as Design I/O).

Watson's position in the creative coding community was distinctive: technically rigorous enough to write core infrastructure (drivers, addons) while also being a practicing artist who used those tools to make work. The openFrameworks community in 2012-2013 included many practitioners in this dual role — artist-engineers who built the tools and used them simultaneously.

## Key points

- ofxKinect: first open-source openFrameworks addon for Microsoft Kinect; released days after Kinect's launch in 2010; standard tool for Kinect art/installations
- Design I/O: collaborative studio with Emily Gobeille; interactive installations for museums, events, and public spaces
- Technical contribution: Watson wrote and maintained several critical openFrameworks addons beyond Kinect
- openFrameworks ecosystem builder: demonstrates the artist-engineer archetype common in creative coding — makes infrastructure and makes work with it
- The 2010-2013 Kinect moment: low-cost depth sensing democratized computer vision for interactive art; Watson's driver was central to that
