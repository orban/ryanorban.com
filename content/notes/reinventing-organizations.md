## Summary

[Reinventing Organizations](/notes/reinventing-organizations/) is Frédéric Laloux's influential framework (book published 2014, wiki companion) for understanding organizational evolution through the lens of human development stages. The core thesis: every management paradigm rests on unconscious fundamental assumptions about human nature and the nature of work — and these assumptions, not structures or processes, are what actually need to change for organizations to operate differently. The wiki page on fundamental assumptions lays out these hidden premises for each organizational stage.

Laloux uses color coding for developmental stages (drawing on Ken Wilber's Integral Theory): Red organizations (power, fear — gangs, mafia), Amber (conformist hierarchy — traditional institutions, military, churches), Orange (achievement — corporations, management consulting), Green (pluralistic — progressive companies like Patagonia), and Teal (evolutionary self-management — Buurtzorg, Patagonia, FAVI). Each color represents a shift in fundamental assumptions: Red assumes people need fear to perform; Amber assumes people need clearly defined roles; Orange assumes people are motivated by material success and competition; Teal assumes people are intrinsically motivated and capable of self-direction.

The Teal stage is the book's focus: organizations that replace hierarchical management with self-management, bring employees' whole selves (not just professional persona) to work, and orient around evolutionary purpose — letting the organization's purpose evolve rather than being controlled by founders or executives. Examples include Buurtzorg (Dutch nursing organization with 10,000 nurses and no managers) and Morning Star (tomato processing with no job titles). The critique of the model is that it works best for knowledge work and care organizations; applying it to manufacturing or compliance-heavy industries is more contested.

## Key points

- Each organizational paradigm rests on hidden assumptions about human nature — changing structures without changing assumptions produces superficial results.
- Teal organizations: self-management (no bosses, distributed decision authority), wholeness (authentic rather than professional-persona-only), evolutionary purpose (organization as a living system with its own direction).
- Buurtzorg is the canonical Teal case study: 10,000 nurses, no management layer, self-organizing teams, better outcomes than traditional nursing organizations.
- Draws on Ken Wilber's developmental psychology and Integral Theory — controversial in academic management circles.
- Tension: works better for knowledge work / care / creative industries; manufacturing and regulated industries are harder cases.
- The wiki is a free companion to the book — useful for exploring specific concepts without reading the full text.
