## Summary

MIT 6.046 (Introduction to Algorithms, now cross-listed as 6.006 and 6.046) is one of the canonical algorithms courses in computer science education. The Fall 2005 version featured Erik Demaine and Charles Leiserson as instructors. Demaine — who became a full professor at MIT at age 20 and works at the intersection of computational origami, data structures, and computational geometry — is known for making algorithms intuitive and visually engaging.

Lecture 2 of the Fall 2005 course likely covered one of the foundational topics in the early curriculum: asymptotic analysis, recurrence relations, or the master theorem for analyzing divide-and-conquer algorithms. The standard 6.046 arc begins with asymptotic notation (O, Ω, Θ), moves through sorting (merge sort, heap sort, quicksort), then covers divide and conquer, dynamic programming, greedy algorithms, and graph algorithms.

The course textbook is CLRS (Introduction to Algorithms by Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, and Stein) — the definitive algorithms reference, dense enough to use as a weapon. MIT OCW made the course materials freely available, and the video lectures on videolectures.net represented one of the early high-quality open educational resources before platforms like Coursera and edX existed.
