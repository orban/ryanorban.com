## Summary

This Zipfian Academy post became a reference document in the early data science community — widely cited as one of the most honest, practical articulations of what the job actually requires. Where most data science writing in 2014 was either too high-level (data science is about insight!) or too tool-specific, this post tried to map the actual SKILL set: what you need to know, what tools you need to use, and why.

Clare Corthell (herself a prominent figure in open-source data science education, creator of the Open Source Data Science Masters) endorsed it explicitly: "still one of the best posts on the topic." That endorsement signals it held up over time — not just as a list of tools but as a framework for thinking about the discipline.

The post reflects Zipfian Academy's curriculum philosophy: data science is fundamentally applied statistics + engineering, and the practitioner needs both. This contrasts with the "data science is just SQL and dashboards" interpretation on one end and the "data science is just machine learning research" interpretation on the other. The Zipfian view was that data scientists need to get data (engineering), understand it (statistics), model it (machine learning), and communicate findings (data visualization).

## Key points

- Zipfian Academy's curriculum philosophy: data science = applied statistics + engineering + communication, not any single component.
- The post distinguishes between data *engineering* (pipelines, storage, retrieval) and data *science* (modeling, inference, insight) — a distinction many 2014 job descriptions blurred.
- Skill areas covered: Python, SQL, probability, statistics, machine learning, data visualization, data storytelling.
- Clare Corthell's Open Source Data Science Masters drew heavily on this framing as a curriculum scaffold.
- Historical context: 2014 was when the what is a data scientist debate was loudest — this post was a ground-level practitioner answer.
