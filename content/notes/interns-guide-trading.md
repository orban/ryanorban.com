## Summary

This Nasdaq-published primer covers the basics of professional trading for new entrants to financial firms. The audience is interns and junior employees stepping into trading-adjacent roles — operations, sales, research, or the desk itself — who need to understand the mechanics without having a finance background.

The content typically covers: order types (market orders vs. limit orders vs. stop orders), bid-ask spread mechanics, market microstructure (how prices form through order flow), liquidity and what it means practically, the difference between buy-side and sell-side roles, and how market makers vs. agency brokers function differently. This is distinct from retail investing concepts — the professional trading context involves execution quality, VWAP, TWAP, and minimizing market impact, not just picking securities.

The frame of an intern's guide is useful because these concepts are often assumed as implicit knowledge on trading floors but aren't well documented for non-specialists. Understanding order flow, why spreads widen in volatile markets, and what the desk is actually doing when executing a large order is foundational for anyone working near a trading operation.

## Key points

- Market microstructure: how prices form through the interaction of buyers and sellers placing orders.
- Order types: market (execute now), limit (execute at price or better), stop (trigger when price reached).
- Bid-ask spread as the cost of immediacy — tighter in liquid markets, wider in thin ones.
- Buy-side (asset managers, hedge funds) vs. sell-side (banks, brokers) — different roles in the market structure.
- Professional trading focuses on execution quality (VWAP, minimizing market impact) more than retail investing does.
