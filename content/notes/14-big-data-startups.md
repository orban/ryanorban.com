## Summary

Business Insider's 2013 list captured the venture-backed big data startup landscape at its most excited moment — companies building analytics, visualization, infrastructure, and tooling layers for enterprises trying to derive value from large datasets. The list included names like Datameer, Sqrrl, Trifacta, Platfora, and others that were raising significant rounds on the premise that enterprises needed help extracting insights from Hadoop clusters.

The context: Cloudera and Hortonworks had established the Hadoop distribution market, but a second layer of companies was emerging to address the gaps those platforms left — data preparation (Trifacta), security (Sqrrl, with its graph-based access control), visualization on top of Hadoop (Datameer, Platfora), and specialized analytics.

Looking back, many of these companies had difficult outcomes: the market consolidated, Apache Spark made some intermediate analytics tools obsolete, and large platforms (Databricks, Snowflake, BigQuery) absorbed much of the tooling value. Sqrrl was acquired by Amazon for its cybersecurity graph technology (used in Amazon Detective).

## Key points

- 2013 big data startup landscape: infrastructure, analytics, data prep, and security layers above Hadoop
- Datameer: Hadoop analytics and visualization for business users without SQL
- Sqrrl: graph-based Apache Accumulo analytics for security/threat detection (acquired by Amazon → Amazon Detective)
- Trifacta: data wrangling and preparation tool, co-founded by Joe Hellerstein (acquired by Google 2022)
- Platfora: in-memory analytics on Hadoop (acquired by Workday 2016)
- Market consolidation lesson: the tooling layer above Hadoop got squeezed as Spark and cloud data warehouses absorbed value
