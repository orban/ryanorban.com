## Summary

[High Scalability](/notes/high-scalability/) is a technical publication focused on distributed systems architecture and how large-scale systems are built in production. The site has been running since the late 2000s and covers architectural deep dives, post-mortems, case studies from companies like Google, Uber, Meta, LinkedIn, and Amazon, and foundational concepts in system design.

The content falls into a few categories: how companies do X at scale case studies (Kafka at Netflix, S3's internal architecture, Presto query engine), foundational distributed systems concepts (gossip protocols, consistent hashing, quorum reads), and links to notable engineering blog posts from industry. The newsletter digest format makes it possible to follow without visiting the site directly.

The site is particularly useful for engineers preparing for system design interviews and for practitioners building systems who want to see how production decisions were made at companies dealing with orders of magnitude more traffic than typical. The aggregation of engineering blog content from across the industry in one place is the core value proposition — it reduces the need to follow dozens of individual company engineering blogs.

## Key points

- Case studies of how major companies (Google, Netflix, Uber, LinkedIn, Meta) solve scalability problems at production scale.
- Covers foundational distributed systems concepts: consistent hashing, gossip protocols, CAP theorem, quorum reads/writes.
- Newsletter digest aggregates engineering blog content from across the industry — reduces the need to follow dozens of company blogs.
- Popular reference for system design interview preparation alongside resources like Designing Data-Intensive Applications.
- Long-running site (since ~2007) with a deep archive of architectural case studies.
