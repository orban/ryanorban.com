## Summary

Liane Gabora's chapter for the Oxford University Press volume *Secrets of Creativity* draws together findings from cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and complex dynamical systems theory to explain how creative thinking works. The paper asks not just what creativity is, but what gives some individuals the capacity to reshape entire cultural trajectories — and what developmental and personality conditions make that possible.

The core argument is that creativity isn't a single cognitive event but a worldview that takes shape over time. Gabora traces how early-life associative thinking and the ability to hold ideas in contextual superposition — entertaining multiple interpretations simultaneously — distinguish highly creative people from those who default to analytical, convergent reasoning. The dual-process theory of cognition gets reworked here: rather than opposing fast intuition and slow deliberation, Gabora emphasizes the fluidity of moving between diffuse and focused attention modes.

From a complex systems perspective, the paper models creative individuals as agents who explore a fitness landscape in ways that differ structurally from ordinary problem-solving. High creativity correlates with a flatter search — more lateral connections, more tolerance for ambiguity — which occasionally produces not just novel solutions but novel problem framings. This has implications for how we cultural evolution models treat individual variation in a population.

## Key points

- Cognitive psychology and neuroscience evidence converges on creativity as a mode of thought rather than a trait, tied to how attention is allocated
- Highly creative people show stronger associative thinking networks and more fluid transitions between default mode network and executive control network activation
- Complex dynamical systems framing: creative cognition is an exploration of a high-dimensional fitness landscape, with wider search radius than routine problem-solving
- Developmental psychology findings suggest early experiences that encourage divergent thinking — particularly open-ended play — shape lifelong creative capacity
- The paper situates individual creativity within cultural evolution: creative individuals are the agents through whom ideas mutate, recombine, and get selected at the population level
