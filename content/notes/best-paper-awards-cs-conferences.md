## Summary

Jeff Huang's best paper awards page is one of the more useful reference resources in computer science: a single maintained list of best paper (and honorable mention) awards across 30+ major venues — AAAI, ACL, CHI, CIKM, CVPR, FOCS, FSE, ICCV, ICML, ICSE, IJCAI, INFOCOM, KDD, NSDI, OSDI, PLDI, PODS, S&P, SIGCOMM, SIGIR, SIGMOD, SODA, SOSP, STOC, UIST, VLDB, and WWW.

The value is in the curation and cross-venue scope. Following a single conference, you get best papers from that community. This list lets you scan landmark work across systems, theory, ML, HCI, databases, networking, programming languages, and security in one place — a forcing function to see outside your subfield. Best paper selection is imperfect (committees are variable) but it's a decent proxy for work the community found significant enough to highlight.

The page is also a historical document. Scanning best papers from the 1990s and 2000s across OSDI, SOSP, and SIGCOMM gives a condensed view of what systems research considered important decade by decade. Similarly, ICML and NIPS best papers trace the evolution of machine learning research from the kernel-method era through the deep learning transition.

## Key points

- Covers 30+ venues across AI, systems, theory, HCI, databases, networking, NLP, computer vision, and security — the breadth is the point.
- Best paper selection as imperfect signal: worth reading, not definitive — committees miss things and occasionally reward fashionable topics over durable contributions.
- Historical value: tracing best papers at SOSP or OSDI over 20 years shows what systems research considered central.
- Useful for literature entry points: finding the canonical "best" paper in an unfamiliar area before doing a full literature review.
- Jeff Huang maintains it actively — one of those quietly essential researcher-built resources that Google Scholar doesn't replace.
