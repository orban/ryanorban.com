## Summary

Researchers at Tokyo University of Science published findings in April 2022 showing that disulfiram — the drug sold as Antabuse for treating chronic alcoholism — reduced anxiety in rodent models. The mechanism under study: disulfiram inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase 2 (ALDH2), which affects acetaldehyde metabolism. The hypothesis is that ALDH2 inhibition in the amygdala and related circuits influences anxiety signaling, though the precise pathway wasn't fully established in this study.

The drug repurposing angle is the most interesting part. Disulfiram has a decades-long safety record in humans (it's been used since the 1950s to create aversive reactions to alcohol by blocking acetaldehyde metabolism in the liver). If its CNS effects include anxiolytic properties at doses that don't cause the disulfiram-alcohol reaction, it could be a candidate for anxiety treatment with a known side effect profile — far ahead of novel compounds in the safety characterization process.

This kind of research sits in the broader drug repurposing literature, which has become more systematic with tools like large-scale screening against existing drug libraries and retrospective analysis of electronic health records to find unexpected outcomes. Disulfiram itself has been studied for repurposing purposes across several conditions — cancer (copper-chelating effects), HIV, and now anxiety. The rodent findings need replication and clinical translation, which is a long road, but the starting safety profile is the hook.

## Key points

- Disulfiram (Antabuse) reduced anxiety in rodent models in Tokyo University of Science study (April 2022).
- Proposed mechanism: ALDH2 inhibition affects acetaldehyde metabolism; may influence anxiety circuits in the amygdala.
- Drug repurposing: disulfiram's long human safety record accelerates the path to clinical investigation.
- Disulfiram has multiple repurposing threads: cancer (copper chelation), HIV, and now anxiety.
- Current standard-of-care anxiolytics (SSRIs, benzodiazepines) have significant limitations; new mechanisms are valuable.
- Rodent models; long path to human clinical evidence — but starting with known safety profile is a significant advantage.
