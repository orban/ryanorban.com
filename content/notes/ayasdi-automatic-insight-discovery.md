## Summary

Ayasdi was a Stanford spinout commercializing [topological data analysis](/notes/topological-data-analysis/) (TDA) for enterprise use. The company was co-founded by Gunnar Carlsson (Stanford mathematics professor and co-developer of the Mapper algorithm), Gurjeet Singh, and Harlan Sexton. The product was Iris — a platform for finding hidden structure in high-dimensional datasets without requiring analysts to specify what to look for in advance.

The homepage automatic insight discovery tagline captured the core sales proposition: unlike traditional business intelligence tools that required analysts to formulate specific hypotheses and build queries, Ayasdi would surface structure that analysts didn't know to ask about. This was an ambitious claim and partly aspirational — TDA genuinely finds structure that other methods miss, but the "automatic" framing understated how much domain expertise is required to interpret the results.

This bookmark was saved alongside the Wired article about Iris (see 2013-01-21-ayasdi-iris-topological-data-analysis), which covers the technical substance in more detail. The homepage is primarily useful as a record of how the company positioned itself at launch.

## Key points

- Ayasdi targeted enterprise verticals: healthcare/genomics, financial services, defense/intelligence
- The TDA-as-BI pitch was genuinely novel in 2013 — no other enterprise analytics product used algebraic topology
- Later acquired by Prove AI (2019); the TDA methods have since been implemented in open-source tools (Gudhi, scikit-tda)
- Gunnar Carlsson's academic work on TDA predates Ayasdi by a decade; the company was his attempt to make it commercially useful
