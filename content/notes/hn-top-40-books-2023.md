## Summary

HN Reads compiled the most-recommended books from Hacker News discussions in 2023 by analyzing mentions across comment threads. The top titles reveal what the Hacker News community genuinely reaches for when asked what should I read? — not what's being marketed, but what has accumulated recommendation weight over time.

The top cluster: SICP (Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs) with 26 mentions, Designing Data-Intensive Applications by Martin Kleppmann with 18, Gödel, Escher, Bach with 18, The C Programming Language (K&R) with 17, and How to Win Friends and Influence People at 15. The mix is revealing — two CS classics, a math/philosophy crossover, a foundational systems text, and a soft skills book.

Three themes dominate: first, CS fundamentals and timeless engineering texts massively outweigh recent trendy books — DDIA, SICP, and The Mythical Man-Month all appear over books published in the last five years. Second, mathematical depth is valued: Rudin's Principles of Mathematical Analysis, Spivak's Calculus, and Polya's How to Solve It all appear, suggesting HN readers associate mathematical rigor with deep understanding. Third, professional development and team dynamics appear alongside technical titles — Peopleware, The Mythical Man-Month — reflecting that seniority eventually requires thinking beyond code.

## Key points

- Top-recommended: SICP (26 mentions), DDIA (18), GEB (18), K&R C (17).
- Strong bias toward timeless CS fundamentals over recently-published books.
- Mathematical texts appear prominently: Rudin, Spivak, Pólya.
- Professional/management books: Peopleware, The Mythical Man-Month.
- Also notable: Crafting Interpreters, The Elements of Computing Systems (nand2tetris), Code by Petzold.
