## Summary

Rex St. John (former Intel IoT executive) shares observations from Consensus 2022 about [MachineFi](/notes/machinefi/) or the "Machine Economy" — the concept of IoT devices participating directly in crypto economic systems. The premise: physical machines (sensors, vehicles, devices) could earn tokens for providing verifiable data or compute, stake assets, and participate in DeFi protocols autonomously.

The idea combines several threads: IoT device networks that generate valuable data, blockchain for trustless payment rails to those devices, and token incentives to grow device networks organically (like Helium's model for wireless coverage). Projects like IoTeX (focused on MachineFi), IOTA, and Helium represented this space at the time.

In practice, the Machine Economy concept was ahead of the hardware and infrastructure needed to make it work at scale. Lightweight blockchain clients on edge hardware, oracle reliability, and the cost of on-chain transactions vs. the value of individual device data points were all unsolved problems. The concept saw renewed interest later under the DePIN (Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks) framing, which achieved more traction.

## Key points

- MachineFi = IoT devices participating in crypto economic systems — earning tokens, staking, autonomous DeFi.
- Observed at Consensus 2022 — a niche but active area during the 2022 bull market tail.
- Key projects: IoTeX (MachineFi-native), IOTA, Helium (wireless coverage via token incentives).
- Technical challenges: lightweight clients on constrained hardware, oracle reliability, on-chain cost vs. data value.
- Later reframed as DePIN (Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks) with more traction.
- Rex St. John: Intel IoT veteran observing the blockchain × hardware intersection.
