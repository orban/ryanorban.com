## Summary

*Statistical Inference for Everyone* (SIE) is a free statistics textbook by Brian Blais (Bryant University) designed for non-statisticians who need to reason under uncertainty. The book takes a Bayesian approach to inference from the ground up rather than teaching frequentist hypothesis testing as the primary framework — a pedagogical choice that was controversial in 2014 but has since become more mainstream as Bayesian methods have grown in accessibility.

The core argument of Bayesian pedagogy: probability as degree of belief is more intuitive than probability as long-run frequency. When someone asks "what is the probability that this drug works?", they want to update beliefs given data — not run a thought experiment about infinite repetitions of an experiment. Teaching Bayes' theorem and posterior distributions first, before null hypothesis testing, produces students who can reason about real uncertainty rather than students who memorize p-value thresholds.

The book covers probability rules, Bayes' theorem, prior distributions, likelihood functions, posterior distributions, parameter estimation, model comparison, and some linear modeling — all through worked examples rather than derivation-first presentation. This approach was particularly relevant for the Zipfian Academy bootcamp context where students came from diverse backgrounds and needed statistical intuition fast.

## Key points

- Free introductory statistics textbook using Bayesian inference as the primary framework — not frequentist.
- Author: Brian Blais, Bryant University — accessible writing aimed at practitioners, not statisticians.
- Core insight: probability as "degree of belief" is more intuitive than long-run frequency for most practical questions.
- Covers Bayes' theorem, prior, likelihood, posterior, parameter estimation, model comparison.
- Relevant alternative to frequentist texts like OpenIntro Statistics or Freedman/Pisani/Purves.
- The Bayesian pedagogical approach has grown more mainstream since 2014 with tools like Stan, PyMC, and ArviZ.
