## Summary

Sergei Treil (Brown University) wrote this textbook as course notes for Honors Linear Algebra, targeting students who are ready for rigorous mathematics but encountering formal proofs for the first time. The title is intentionally provocative: the subject is done "wrong" relative to standard cookbook-style courses, meaning it prioritizes abstract definitions, formal proofs, and theoretical understanding over mechanical computation.

The scope is deliberately constrained: only vector spaces over real or complex numbers (no abstract field theory), only finite-dimensional spaces (no functional analysis). This keeps the book focused on material that directly feeds analysis, geometry, and probability theory — disciplines where linear algebra is most commonly applied. The emphasis on what's excluded is as informative as what's included.

Unlike algebraist-written texts, Treil organizes content around analytical and geometric applications. The treatment of topics reflects what students will actually need in subsequent courses, making it a useful companion to calculus, differential equations, and statistics.

## Key points

- Restricts to real and complex fields — no abstract field theory, which would dilute focus for the intended audience
- Only covers finite-dimensional spaces, avoiding the need for convergence and completeness (which belong to functional analysis)
- Designed as a first exposure to rigorous proof and formal mathematical definitions, not just computation
- Emphasizes topics important for analysis, geometry, and probability — not the standard algebraist's selection
- Available free under Creative Commons license; originally lecture notes that grew into a full textbook
