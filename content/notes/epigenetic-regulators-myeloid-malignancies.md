## Summary

This Nature Reviews Cancer paper (2012) synthesized discoveries from early next-generation sequencing studies of myeloid cancers — acute myeloid leukemia (AML), myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS), and myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPN). The key finding from this body of work: a substantial fraction of myeloid malignancies carry somatic mutations not in traditional oncogenes or tumor suppressor genes but in genes encoding epigenetic regulators — proteins that control DNA methylation, histone modification, and chromatin state.

The mutated genes include DNMT3A (de novo DNA methyltransferase), TET2 (converts 5-methylcytosine to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine, part of the demethylation pathway), IDH1 and IDH2 (metabolic enzymes whose mutant forms produce the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate, which inhibits TET2 and other alpha-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases), and EZH2 (H3K27 histone methyltransferase, part of the Polycomb repressive complex). Mutations in these genes alter the epigenome globally rather than disrupting a specific growth pathway.

The implication is a new model for leukemogenesis: epigenetic disruption creates an aberrant chromatin landscape that locks cells in an undifferentiated state or blocks apoptosis, without the classical signaling mutations. This also suggested therapeutic targets — if the epigenome is disrupted rather than the coding sequence, epigenetic therapies (DNMT inhibitors, HDAC inhibitors, IDH inhibitors) could reverse the aberrant state.

## Key points

- DNMT3A mutations (~20% of AML): disrupt de novo DNA methylation; one of the most common AML mutations found by large-scale sequencing
- IDH1/IDH2 mutations (~20% combined): produce oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG), which broadly inhibits epigenetic regulators — a metabolic route to epigenetic disruption
- TET2 mutations: disrupt the DNA demethylation pathway; found in AML, MDS, MPN, and in clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP)
- EZH2 mutations: both gain-of-function and loss-of-function occur in different myeloid malignancies; context-dependent oncogene/tumor suppressor
- Therapeutic opportunity: IDH inhibitors (enasidenib, ivosidenib) were later developed specifically targeting mutant IDH enzymes in AML — a direct clinical translation of this biology
