## Summary

Grokit published a structured computer science review curriculum — a topic index covering the fundamentals typically tested in software engineering interviews and expected in graduate CS programs. The site organized content into clear topic areas with resources for reviewing each one.

In late 2013, technical interview preparation was increasingly systematized. LeetCode launched in 2011 but was still early; Cracking the Coding Interview was the dominant book resource. Sites like Grokit and InterviewBit were trying to build structured curriculum around the scattered landscape of CS review materials.

The Grokit approach was topic-indexed rather than problem-indexed: instead of "here are 100 problems to solve," it organized review around the underlying concepts — sorting algorithms, graph traversal, dynamic programming, operating systems fundamentals, networking, database internals — with resources for each.

## Key points

- Structured CS fundamentals review: algorithms, data structures, systems, theory — organized by topic rather than by problem type.
- Target audience: engineers reviewing for technical interviews or covering gaps in self-taught backgrounds.
- 2013 context: the technical interview preparation ecosystem was growing rapidly — LeetCode, HackerRank, and similar platforms were establishing the practice.
- Topic-indexed approach: good for systematic review vs. the problem-grind approach of competitive programming sites.
- Covers: sorting algorithms, graph algorithms, dynamic programming, OS concepts, networking, database internals.
- Grokit.ca appears to have been a Canadian CS education site; the URL is no longer active.
