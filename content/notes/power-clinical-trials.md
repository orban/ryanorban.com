## Summary

Power (withpower.com) is a platform designed to connect patients with clinical trials that match their condition, history, and location — without requiring a specialist physician to make the referral. The founding thesis (from Brandon Li's launch thread): clinical trial access is systematically biased toward patients who happen to have the right doctors, live in major medical centers, or are already embedded in research hospital networks. Most patients who could benefit from trials never hear about them.

The problem is structural: trial recruitment relies on physician networks, academic medical centers, and word-of-mouth within specialties. A patient with a rare disease in a rural area, or without a specialist managing their care, simply doesn't encounter the trial that could help them. Power tries to make trial matching direct-to-patient — a database of open trials with plain-language eligibility criteria, matched against patient profiles and proactively surfaced.

The business model sits at the intersection of patient advocacy and pharmaceutical clinical trial recruitment. Pharma companies pay enormous amounts to recruit trial participants — clinical trial recruitment failures are one of the leading causes of trial delays and cost overruns. A platform that efficiently matches eligible patients to trials has revenue on both sides: helping patients find trials (potentially subscription or insurance-covered) and helping pharma recruit faster. Power is one of several companies in the decentralized clinical trials and trial recruitment space that emerged around 2020-2022.

## Key points

- Matches patients with clinical trials directly, without requiring a specialist referral.
- Addresses systematic access inequality: trial participation skews toward patients in major medical centers with specialists.
- Dual-sided market: patients find trials; pharma companies get better recruitment with fewer delays.
- Clinical trial recruitment failure is a major industry cost — better matching has clear commercial value.
- Part of the decentralized clinical trials movement that accelerated after COVID-19 forced remote trial operations.
- Founded by Brandon Li; launched publicly August 2022.
