## Summary

This Data Science Central post compiled the questions and concepts that kept appearing across data science interview loops circa 2014 — a time when the interview format was still being invented. Unlike software engineering interviews (which by 2014 had converged on LeetCode-style algorithmic questions), data science interviews were more varied: some companies tested statistics, others ML theory, others SQL and coding, and many tested all of the above plus business judgment.

The topical coverage mirrors what a working data scientist actually needed: probability and statistics fundamentals (conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals), core machine learning algorithms and when to use them, SQL proficiency for data access and aggregation, at least one scripting language (Python or R), and the ability to frame ambiguous business problems as tractable analytical ones.

The 2014 era was notable because data science was still a relatively new job title; many interviewers were figuring out what they wanted to test at the same time candidates were figuring out how to prepare. Posts like this served as community knowledge aggregation — building a shared understanding of what the role actually required.

## Key points

- Statistics foundation: central limit theorem, Bayesian vs. frequentist, A/B testing, p-value interpretation, regression assumptions.
- Machine learning: bias-variance tradeoff, model selection, cross-validation, overfitting, regularization techniques.
- SQL is non-negotiable: joins, window functions, aggregation, query optimization for large tables.
- Coding: data manipulation in Python (Pandas, NumPy) or R, plus comfort with scripting for data pipelines.
- Business framing: turning vague questions (how do we grow?) into specific measurable hypotheses is the most-tested soft SKILL.
