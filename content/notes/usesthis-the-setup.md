## Summary

The Setup (usesthis.com) is an interview series run by Daniel Bogan that asks one simple question to interesting people: "What do you use to get stuff done?" The interviews cover hardware (computer, input devices, phone), software (editors, communication tools, specialty apps), and the person's philosophy about their tool choices. The format is short and consistent, making it easy to scan across hundreds of interviews for patterns.

The appeal is specific: it's not a product review site (no benchmarks, no affiliate links, just real usage) and not a productivity advice site (no methodology, just tools). It captures what practitioners actually use, including idiosyncratic choices and the reasoning behind them. Discovering that a respected developer uses a 10-year-old keyboard or a writer does all their drafting in a plain text editor on a five-year-old laptop challenges assumptions about needing the latest tools.

By 2013, The Setup had interviewed hundreds of people: programmers (John Gruber, DHH, Ryan Dahl), artists, scientists, musicians, journalists, writers. The interviews formed a reference for anyone configuring their own environment — you could search programmer or vim or mechanical keyboard and see who used what.

## Key points

- Daniel Bogan: runs The Setup; has maintained the series for 15+ years with minimal format changes
- The four questions: who are you / what hardware do you use / what software do you use / what would be your dream setup?
- Value: real usage patterns from practitioners, not marketing — exposes the gap between marketed tools and what people actually reach for
- Pattern observation: high-output people often use minimal, stable setups; constant tool-switching is more common among lower-output workers
- The series influenced the later uses this format that appeared on many personal developer blogs
