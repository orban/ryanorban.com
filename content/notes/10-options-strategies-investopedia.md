## Summary

Investopedia's overview covers the 10 most common options trading strategies, from simple to complex. Options are derivatives — contracts giving the right (but not obligation) to buy or sell an asset at a set price before expiration. The strategies covered span a spectrum from risk-reduction tools to speculative positions.

**Basic strategies**: The **covered call** (own stock, sell a call against it — collect premium, cap upside) is the most widely used conservative options strategy. The **protective put** (own stock, buy a put — pay premium for downside insurance). The **cash-secured put** (sell a put on a stock you'd be happy to own at the strike price — a way to buy at a discount or earn premium if it doesn't hit).

**Spread strategies**: The **bull call spread** (buy a call, sell a higher-strike call — reduced cost, capped upside) and **bear put spread** (buy a put, sell a lower-strike put) are defined-risk directional bets. **Iron condors** (sell an OTM call spread and an OTM put spread simultaneously) profit from low volatility — you collect premium when the underlying stays within a range.

**Volatility strategies**: **Straddles** (buy both a call and put at the same strike) profit from large moves in either direction. **Strangles** are the cheaper variant (out-of-the-money on both sides).

## Key points

- Covered call: own stock, sell calls against it — income generation with capped upside.
- Cash-secured put: sell puts on stocks you want to own — buy at discount or earn premium.
- Iron condor: defined-risk range trade profiting from low implied volatility.
- Straddle / strangle: volatility plays — profit from large moves regardless of direction.
- Theta (time decay) and implied volatility are the key Greeks for options sellers vs. buyers.
