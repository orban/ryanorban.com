## Summary

gifpop launched to the public in December 2013, offering a service to convert animated GIFs into physical lenticular prints — small cards that display different frames of the animation depending on the viewing angle, creating an illusion of motion you can hold in your hand.

Lenticular printing is an old technique (used for holographic baseball cards and "3D" effect novelties for decades), but applying it to user-submitted animated GIFs was a novel product direction. The timing aligned with the peak of GIF culture in early social media: Tumblr had made GIFs a primary communication medium, and turning digital reaction GIFs into physical objects had obvious appeal.

The product is a small but interesting example of the digital to physical category — bridging internet culture artifacts with tangible objects. The launch tweet's enthusiasm (Holy cow, we're live!) reflected the bootstrapped indie product energy of 2013 SF.

## Key points

- Lenticular printing: shows different images at different angles — creates motion illusion without power or electronics.
- gifpop applied this to user-submitted animated GIFs at a time when GIF culture was at peak mainstream visibility.
- "Digital to physical" product category: internet-native content as physical artifacts (cf. also Canvs, Artifact Uprising, etc.).
- Launched December 2013, peak of GIF-as-internet-language era.
