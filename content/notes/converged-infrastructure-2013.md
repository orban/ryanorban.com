## Summary

This 2013 enterprise IT blog post declared the year a tipping point for converged infrastructure — pre-engineered, pre-validated stacks that combine compute, storage, and networking from a single vendor. The market was crystallizing around offerings like Vblock (VCE, a joint venture between VMware, Cisco, and EMC), FlexPod (NetApp + Cisco), and the newer hyper-converged infrastructure category led by Nutanix and SimpliVity.

The appeal was reducing data center procurement and integration complexity. Traditional three-tier architecture required separate procurement, cabling, and firmware management for servers (Dell, HP), storage (EMC, NetApp), and networking (Cisco, Juniper). Converged infrastructure collapsed this into a single validated SKU — one vendor call, one support contract, tested interoperability.

The distinction between converged and hyper-converged was emerging in 2013: converged infrastructure (Vblock, FlexPod) still had discrete server, storage, and network tiers in a pre-integrated rack; hyper-converged infrastructure (HCI, led by Nutanix) collapsed all three into software running on commodity x86 nodes. HCI was the more disruptive architecture.

## Key points

- Converged infrastructure: pre-integrated server + SAN storage + networking in a validated bundle (Vblock, FlexPod)
- Hyper-converged infrastructure (HCI): software-defined all three tiers on commodity x86 nodes (Nutanix, SimpliVity)
- Operational appeal: single support contract, tested compatibility, faster deployment vs. three-tier custom builds
- VMware vSAN (announced 2012, GA 2014) was the hypervisor platform's response to Nutanix
- 2013 marked the transition from early adopters to mainstream enterprise interest in HCI
- The category consolidated by 2018: Nutanix, VMware vSAN, and cloud services (AWS, Azure) absorbed most of the market
