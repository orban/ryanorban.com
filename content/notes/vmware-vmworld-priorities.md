## Summary

InformationWeek published this pre-VMworld 2013 analysis outlining what VMware needed to demonstrate to maintain its enterprise position. The four priorities broadly came down to: articulating a credible cloud strategy, addressing the OpenStack threat, responding to hyper-converged infrastructure challengers like Nutanix, and transitioning from a hypervisor company to a software-defined data center (SDDC) platform company.

VMware in 2013 was facing existential pressure from multiple directions simultaneously. Amazon Web Services had proven that enterprises would run workloads in public cloud, threatening the on-premises virtualization market that VMware dominated. OpenStack gave enterprises an open-source alternative to vSphere for private cloud. Nutanix and SimpliVity were winning storage accounts by collapsing compute and storage into hyper-converged infrastructure that didn't require the traditional VMware + SAN combination. And Microsoft Hyper-V was improving rapidly and bundled with Windows Server licenses.

VMware's response at VMworld 2013 centered on the vCloud Hybrid Service (later acquired by and merged with OVH) and continued investment in NSX (network virtualization) and VSAN (VMware's own HCI offering). The strategic framing was: everything runs on vSphere, and we virtualize the entire data center — not just compute but also network and storage.

## Key points

- Software-defined data center (SDDC): VMware's strategic framing — virtualizing compute (vSphere), network (NSX), and storage (VSAN) under one management plane.
- OpenStack threat: an open-source private cloud platform that gave enterprises an escape valve from VMware licensing costs — actively courted by Rackspace, HP, and Red Hat.
- Hyper-converged infrastructure pressure: Nutanix, SimpliVity, and emerging VSAN were disrupting the traditional VMware + SAN architecture that drove premium ASPs.
- vCloud Hybrid Service: VMware's answer to AWS — connected on-premises vSphere environments to VMware-managed cloud capacity with consistent APIs.
- VMworld as strategic signal: the annual conference was where VMware made announcements that shaped enterprise data center roadmaps for the following year.
- Historical trajectory: VMware navigated this period successfully in the short term; the long-term challenge from public cloud proved more fundamental.
