## Summary

Steve Roberts is a technologist and adventurer who, starting in 1983, began building fully computerized nomadic vehicles for long-distance travel. His first project, BEHEMOTH (Big Electronic Human-Energized Machine, Only Too Heavy), was a solar-powered recumbent bicycle festooned with computers, radios, and networking equipment. He rode it across the US, writing, networking, and computing while traveling — this was in the early 1980s, when mobile computing meant hauling a 12-pound laptop and finding a phone line.

The microship.com site documents the full arc of this project: BEHEMOTH (eventually in the Computer History Museum), and later Microship — a sail-powered amphibious vessel designed to continue the nomadic computing vision on water. The projects span decades and are remarkable as artifacts of what it meant to be a hacker and networker before the modern internet made any of this easy.

Roberts connected to early networks like Fidonet and ARPANET from the road and was a corresponding member of early hacker culture while physically nomadic — a life of constant motion powered by technical ingenuity that anticipates themes in modern digital nomadism by thirty years.

## Key points

- Steve Roberts built computerized nomadic vehicles starting in 1983 — decades before "mobile computing" was a category.
- BEHEMOTH (computerized recumbent bicycle) is in the Computer History Museum; Microship continued the vision on water.
- Connected to early networks (Fidonet, ARPANET) from the road — a nomadic networker in the literal sense.
- The site is a historical archive of one of the more remarkable long-running hacker projects.
