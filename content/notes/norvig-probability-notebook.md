## Summary

This is one of Peter Norvig's pytudes — his collection of Python programs of considerable difficulty designed to perfect specific skills. The probability notebook is a Jupyter Notebook walkthrough of probability theory using clear, idiomatic Python, covering both analytical computation and Monte Carlo simulation approaches.

Norvig's approach to probability here is characteristically clean: he defines a `Fraction` class for exact rational arithmetic, a `P()` function for computing probability from sample spaces, and demonstrates through concrete problems — dice rolls, card draws, Bayesian updates — how to think about probability computationally. The problems progress from simple counting to conditional probability and Bayes' theorem.

The notebook sits inside the broader pytudes repository, which covers algorithms, puzzles, Advent of Code problems, and mathematical recreations. What makes these notebooks worth studying isn't just solving the problems — it's seeing how a world-class programmer thinks: which abstractions to introduce, how to structure solutions so they generalize, and how to make code readable without sacrificing correctness.
