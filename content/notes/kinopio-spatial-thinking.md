## Summary

Kinopio is a visual thinking and note-taking tool built around a spatial canvas — you create cards, connect them with lines, and group them in boxes. It sits in the mind mapping / whiteboarding category alongside Miro, FigJam, and Obsidian Canvas, but with a distinctly indie aesthetic and a deliberate philosophy against AI automation.

The key design principles: low-fidelity (cards look handmade, not polished), keyboard-accessible, and spatial memory-respecting — where you put things on the canvas matters for how you remember them. You can embed URLs, images, code blocks, GIFs, and PDFs inside cards. Real-time collaboration works. Export to JSON Canvas (the standard format also used by Obsidian). Free tier supports 100 cards with no signup.

Kinopio's creator has explicitly positioned it against the VC-funded productivity tool trend — it's community-funded (subscription), the creator prioritizes user needs over growth metrics, and there's no AI feature roadmap. This is a meaningful product choice in 2024+ where every tool is racing to add LLM features. The use cases it covers: brainstorming, research organization, project planning, mood boards, and personal knowledge management.

## Key points

- Spatial canvas with cards, connections, and boxes — drag to organize, not hierarchy-first.
- JSON Canvas export: interoperable with Obsidian and other tools using the open canvas format.
- Community-funded indie tool — no VC, no AI features, no growth-at-all-costs roadmap.
- Real-time collaboration with granular privacy controls (private, read-only, collaborative).
- Free up to 100 cards, no account required — low friction to try.
