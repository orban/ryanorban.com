## Summary

Pushing Pixels is an archive of interviews with the screen graphics designers and visual effects artists who create fantasy user interfaces (FUIs) — the fictional screens, dashboards, and digital environments built for films, television, and other media. The site has been publishing since 2007, making it one of the most comprehensive records of this specialized motion graphics and UI design discipline.

FUI design sits at an unusual intersection: designers must make interfaces that look believably functional within a fictional world while supporting the narrative and aesthetic of the production. A screen in Blade Runner 2049 has different constraints than one in Mr. Robot — the former requires retrofuturist coherence, the latter technical plausibility for a hacker character. The interviews explore how designers navigate these creative and technical tradeoffs.

Productions covered include Blade Runner 2049, The Martian, Tron: Legacy, Ghost in the Shell, Black Mirror, The Expanse, Mr. Robot, Avengers: Age of Ultron, and dozens more across Hollywood films and streaming series. The archive spans 15+ years of production design history.

## Key points

- FUI designers work at the intersection of graphic design, motion design, and narrative craft — the interface must tell a story, not just look cool.
- Mr. Robot is notable among productions for its technical authenticity — the show's creators famously consulted real security professionals, which shows in the screen design.
- The Pushing Pixels interviews reveal how FUI work is often done in relative isolation from other VFX — a small team or solo designer working from reference packages and director notes.
- These interfaces often become cultural touchstones: the aesthetic of a film's UI leaks into real-world design trends (see: skeuomorphic blue-on-black hacker UI tropes).
- Resource for interaction designers and motion designers interested in speculative UI or design fiction.
