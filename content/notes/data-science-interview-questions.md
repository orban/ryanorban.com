## Summary

alexeygrigorev/data-science-interviews is a community-maintained GitHub repository collecting data science and machine learning interview questions with answers. The project is maintained by Alexey Grigorev (DataTalks.Club founder) and covers the range of topics that appear in data science and ML engineering interviews: statistics, machine learning theory, algorithms, probability, and coding.

The repository serves two audiences: candidates preparing for interviews and hiring managers looking for what to ask. For candidates, the collection reveals what the community considers important conceptual ground — not just technical trivia, but the underlying reasoning that experienced practitioners can assess quickly. Questions like "what's the difference between L1 and L2 regularization? or when would you use a tree ensemble vs. a linear model?" probe for conceptual understanding, not memorized formulas.

Beyond interview prep, the repository functions as a self-assessment tool — working through the questions reveals gaps in your own understanding. It also reflects the ML community's implicit consensus about what a competent data scientist should know, which shifts over time as the field evolves.

## Key points

- The question set reveals field consensus on what competent data scientist means at a given point in time — useful for calibrating your own knowledge map.
- Strong emphasis on statistics foundations: confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, A/B testing — these appear because practitioners use them constantly and many candidates have weak foundations.
- Bias-variance tradeoff, regularization, cross-validation — the conceptual core of classical ML that appears in virtually every interview.
- The community-maintained format means it evolves: deep learning and MLOps questions have grown relative to classical statistics as the field has shifted.
- Complementary resource to ML from Scratch and Chip Huyen's ML interviews book — different formats for similar preparation goals.
