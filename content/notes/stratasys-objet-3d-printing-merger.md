## Summary

The merger of Stratasys and Objet in April 2012 created one of the largest 3D printing companies in the world, combining Stratasys's FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) technology with Objet's PolyJet multi-material printing capabilities. The deal was structured as a merger of equals, with the combined company retaining the Stratasys name and listing on NASDAQ.

This happened at the peak of the first major 3D printing hype cycle. MakerBot had popularized desktop printing for enthusiasts, while the industrial segment — where Stratasys and Objet competed — was growing rapidly for prototyping and short-run manufacturing. The merger reflected the competitive dynamics of a market where scale in intellectual property, materials science, and manufacturing processes mattered.

Objet's PolyJet technology was complementary to Stratasys's FDM base: PolyJet could print in multiple materials simultaneously and achieve much higher resolution for medical and dental applications. The combined company could now serve customers across the full spectrum from rough industrial prototypes to precision multi-material parts.

## Key points

- Stratasys (FDM technology) + Objet (PolyJet) = combined company covering two complementary professional printing technologies.
- Merger timed to the peak of 2012 3D printing hype, when the sector was attracting both mainstream and investor attention.
- Desktop 3D printing (MakerBot) and industrial 3D printing (Stratasys/Objet) were separate markets with different price points and use cases.
- 3D printing consolidation pattern: large players acquiring or merging to own IP breadth, while the consumer market commoditized.
- The combined company later acquired MakerBot in 2013 — a move that didn't go well, highlighting the gap between hobbyist and professional markets.
