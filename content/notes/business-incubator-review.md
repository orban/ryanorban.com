## Summary

This 2007 paper by Mark Maglana evaluates BLS-3G, a private business incubator in Davao City, Philippines, using the Hackett and Dilts incubation concept map as an analytical framework. BLS-3G was a joint venture between Brokenshire Learning Systems and 3G Communications, created when 3GCom's container van phone kiosks became obsolete due to GSM expansion. The paper situates BLS-3G against the broader literature on business incubation, which traces the concept back to a 1959 New York building refurbishment and its subsequent acceleration following the Bayh-Dole Act.

The framework structures the analysis across four interdependent levels: community, incubator organization, incubation process, and incubatees. At each level, the paper benchmarks BLS-3G against success correlates drawn from researchers like Rice, Hansen et al., and Allen and McCluskey. A key theoretical contribution discussed is the real options theory of incubation by Hackett and Dilts, which models incubatee selection as option creation and staged investment as option exercise — formalizing incubation performance as a function of selection quality, monitoring intensity, and resource munificence.

BLS-3G underperforms across nearly every dimension. It is not embedded in a local economic development strategy, has no formal university ties despite being on the Brokenshire College campus, has a self-described inexperienced advisory board, runs no selection process (accepting all applicants), and is still in the start-up lifecycle phase — focused on filling vacant container vans rather than developing incubatees. The paper is honest about its own limitations: data was gathered via email interviews and document review, not field observation.

## Key points

- The Hackett and Dilts incubation concept map defines an incubator as a shared workspace plus a strategic value-adding intervention system — not just real estate — operating across community, organization, process, and incubatee levels
- Real options theory applied to incubation: initial acceptance is option creation; staged investments and exits are option exercise; BLS-3G's lack of selectivity undermines this model entirely
- National Business Incubation Association (NBIA) identifies the incubation process (not structure or physical facility) as the primary determinant of success — structural taxonomies (for-profit vs. non-profit, etc.) show weak predictive power
- BLS-3G's failure to attract applicants is partly self-reinforcing: low occupancy pressure leads managers to accept all comers, which produces weak graduates, which reduces the incubator's reputation and future applicant flow
- Incubatee interaction (both informal and formal cooperation) is theorized as critical for problem-solving and trust-building — BLS-3G has informal contact among tenants but no documented formal cooperation
