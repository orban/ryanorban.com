## Summary

The topic-first meeting principle from Remeet is simple to state: schedule a meeting only after a specific topic that warrants a verbal discussion has surfaced, rather than scheduling a meeting and then soliciting agenda items to fill it. This inverts the standard approach of recurring standing meetings where the slot exists first and the content is retrofitted.

The framing captures something real about how most meetings dysfunction: the slot owns the conversation rather than the conversation owning the slot. A standing weekly sync exists because it was useful at some point; it persists through inertia. The topic-first approach forces a per-meeting justification — does this topic actually require synchronous verbal discussion, or could it be handled asynchronously?

The principle also implies that the right participants for a topic-first meeting are only those needed for that specific topic, not the standing membership of whoever was originally invited to the recurring slot. This naturally produces smaller, more focused meetings. The adjacent practices are async-first communication and the idea that meetings are expensive — they block multiple calendars and interrupt deep work. Topic-first is a structural mechanism for reducing meeting overhead without requiring cultural change around meeting culture.

## Key points

- Schedule meetings after a topic warrants discussion — don't retroactively find topics to fill scheduled slots.
- The standing meeting's failure mode: the calendar slot creates artificial urgency to fill time, rather than real content driving the meeting.
- Topic-first meetings naturally include only the relevant participants for that topic — smaller, more focused.
- Requires async-first defaults: if synchronous discussion is the last resort rather than the first, more topics resolve without meetings.
- Related to meeting culture reform: this is a structural rule, not just a cultural value — easier to enforce.
