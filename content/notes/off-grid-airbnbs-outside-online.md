## Summary

Outside Online (the digital arm of Outside Magazine) compiled a list of the most-wishlisted off-grid Airbnb properties in the United States — remote cabins, treehouses, yurts, and other alternative accommodations designed for disconnection. The article was published in the context of pandemic travel patterns, when many people were seeking remote outdoor escapes that allowed for social distancing while still accessing nature.

The timing matters: in 2020, social distancing and remote work had fundamentally changed travel demand. Properties in rural, low-density areas saw demand spikes. Off-grid accommodations — which typically lack reliable cell service and are deliberately removed from urban infrastructure — became attractive precisely because of the features that would previously have been drawbacks. Being unreachable was now a feature, not a bug.

Airbnb's wishlist data is a reasonable proxy for aspirational demand: it shows where people want to go rather than where they've already booked, capturing the set of properties people consider but haven't yet committed to.

## Key points

- The off-grid Airbnb category grew significantly during 2020-2021 pandemic travel — demand for remote, low-density properties spiked as urban areas became less desirable.
- Outside Magazine's coverage of Airbnb properties signals a shift: outdoor recreation and alternative accommodation had merged in the editorial frame.
- Wishlist data captures aspirational rather than actual travel behavior — useful for spotting trends before they show up in booking numbers.
- Properties in this category often exist in tension with conservation: increased demand for remote areas creates impact on the ecosystems they're surrounded by.
