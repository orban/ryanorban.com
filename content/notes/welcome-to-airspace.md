## Summary

Kyle Chayka's 2016 essay for The Verge coins the term "AirSpace" to describe the homogenized aesthetic that has spread globally through Airbnb listings, WeWork offices, and Instagram-friendly coffee shops. The look: exposed brick or raw concrete, Edison bulbs, reclaimed wood, minimalist sans-serif signage, a pour-over coffee bar, maybe a succulent or some monstera. You've seen it everywhere from Brooklyn to Bali to Berlin because it has spread everywhere.

The mechanism: Airbnb created a global marketplace for spaces, and hosts optimizing for bookings converged on the aesthetic that photographs well and appeals to the widest demographic of international travelers. Coffee shops optimizing for Instagram followability converged similarly. The result is that walking into a curated space in any major city gives you roughly the same visual experience — the distinctiveness of place has been flattened into a single portable aesthetic.

Chayka connects this to a broader observation about how platform optimization and global mobility homogenize culture. When the feedback signal is "photos that do well on Instagram or listings that book on Airbnb," and this signal is received identically everywhere, the local idiosyncrasies that make places interesting get optimized away. AirSpace is gentrification as aesthetic colonialism — the export of a particular upper-middle-class Brooklyn sensibility to the world.

## Key points

- AirSpace = the global homogenized aesthetic spread through Airbnb, WeWork, Instagram optimization.
- Mechanism: platforms create uniform feedback signals (bookings, likes) that reward the same visual language everywhere.
- Platform optimization kills local specificity: hosts everywhere converge on the aesthetic that books, not the authentic local look.
- Written in 2016, fully prophetic — by 2021 the Airbnb aesthetic was a cliché precisely because Chayka named it.
- Relates to filter bubbles and algorithmic culture: recommendation engines and marketplace incentives shape taste at population scale.
