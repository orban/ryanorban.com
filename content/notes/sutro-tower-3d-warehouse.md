## Summary

This was a SketchUp model of Sutro Tower — San Francisco's distinctive triple-pronged television transmission tower visible from nearly anywhere in the city — shared in Google 3D Warehouse. Google 3D Warehouse was a community repository where SketchUp users uploaded models of buildings and landmarks, which could then be placed in Google Earth to build out its 3D city layer.

Sutro Tower (built 1973, standing at 977 feet including the antenna) is one of San Francisco's most recognizable landmarks — it often appears to float above the fog bank covering Twin Peaks, giving it an otherworldly visual quality that makes it popular for photography and city identity. Modeling it in SketchUp required capturing the complex structural geometry: the three-pronged antenna array at the top, the tapering lattice structure, and the base.

The context: bookmarked alongside Three.js demos and [Collada Animation Viewer](/notes/collada-animation-viewer/) suggests this was part of exploring 3D models for web rendering. A COLLADA export of a 3D Warehouse model could be loaded into Three.js — connecting the two streams.

## Key points

- Google 3D Warehouse: community-contributed 3D models that populated Google Earth's 3D city layer — an early crowdsourced geospatial content system
- SketchUp was the accessible 3D modeling tool of the era — acquired by Google in 2006, made free, and used extensively for architectural and urban modeling
- Sutro Tower is a San Francisco landmark: built 1973 by a consortium of SF broadcasters for city-wide TV/FM transmission — the familiar silhouette that defines the Twin Peaks skyline
- 3D Warehouse models could be exported as COLLADA and loaded into Three.js — potentially the connection to the other 3D bookmarks
- Google eventually sold SketchUp to Trimble in 2012; the 3D Warehouse remains part of Trimble's portfolio
