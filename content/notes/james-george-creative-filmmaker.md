## Summary

James George is a filmmaker and creative technologist whose work explores the intersection of cinema and real-time 3D graphics. He co-created RgbdToolkit — an open-source toolkit for synchronized color and depth capture using a DSLR camera and Kinect sensor — that allowed filmmakers to capture footage where every pixel has a depth value, enabling 3D reconstruction of scenes after the fact.

His major project was CLOUDS, a non-linear documentary made with director Jonathan Minard about the intersection of art and technology in the creative coding community. CLOUDS was filmed entirely with depth cameras, allowing viewers to navigate through interviews and segments in 3D space — literally moving through a volumetric documentary. The project interviewed many key figures in Processing, openFrameworks, and the broader creative coding world.

George was part of the 2012-2013 generation of artists using depth cameras (Kinect, Xtion) to explore new documentary and cinematic possibilities. The RgbdToolkit was notable because it made depth-captured film practical: the Kinect's native depth sensor was low resolution, but synchronized with a DSLR gave much higher quality depth reconstruction.

## Key points

- RgbdToolkit: synchronized DSLR + Kinect capture; open-source; enables volumetric video where scenes can be relit and re-viewed in 3D post-capture
- CLOUDS documentary: non-linear volumetric documentary about creative coding; allows viewers to navigate 3D space through the interviews
- Depth camera filmmaking: the technique captures color + depth simultaneously, enabling 3D reconstruction and interactive viewing after capture
- Work appeared alongside Theodore Watson, Kyle McDonald, and other openFrameworks community members
- The volumetric video technique George pioneered is now mainstream in VR/AR production (HoloLens volumetric capture, etc.)
