## Summary

This post by Stephen addresses the question engineers face when evaluating a new team or interviewing: how do you tell if the product leader knows what they're doing? The roadmap is the visible artifact of product thinking — but reading it requires knowing what to look for. Good product management produces certain kinds of roadmaps; bad product management produces others, and they look different when you know the signals.

The framework contrasts: roadmaps organized around outputs (features, releases) vs. outcomes (user problems, metrics to move); roadmaps with explicit trade-offs and stated reasoning vs. lists with no explanation of prioritization; roadmaps that show understanding of the user's actual workflow vs. those that address surface-level feature requests. A roadmap full of add X feature with no rationale is a red flag. A roadmap that says "we believe users struggle with Y because Z, and we're testing W as a solution" shows a product mindset.

Engineers can evaluate this because the roadmap determines what they'll build — shipping features with no clear outcome hypothesis wastes months of engineering time. The post is honest about the power dynamic: engineers often don't get to reject a PM's roadmap, but they can factor roadmap quality into where they choose to work.

## Key points

- Good roadmaps are organized around outcomes (problems/metrics) not outputs (feature lists).
- Red flag: prioritization with no stated reasoning — suggests political or arbitrary decisions.
- Evaluate: does the PM understand the user's actual workflow, or just surface-level requests?
- Engineers have standing to evaluate roadmaps — they're the ones building what's on them.
- Roadmap quality is a legitimate signal when choosing a team or job.
- Part of the "engineering career" and staff engineering reading tradition on professional judgment.
