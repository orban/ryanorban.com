## Summary

musicForProgramming() is a long-running website hosting a curated series of audio mixes intended specifically for programming and deep focus work. Each episode is a single continuous mix (typically 1-2 hours), spanning ambient, drone, minimal techno, experimental electronic, and occasionally classical — genres that provide sonic texture without demanding attention.

The site's aesthetic is deliberately minimal: no accounts, no algorithm, no recommendations, just a numbered list of episodes with a play button. This is a direct counterpoint to services like Spotify's focus playlists that interrupt concentration with transitions and suggestions. The mixes themselves are curated with care — episode selection tends toward music with long attack/release, minimal vocals, and enough variation to remain interesting without becoming distracting.

The project has been running since 2012 and has accumulated a library of over 60 episodes, making it a reliable fallback for anyone who needs something to play but doesn't want to make a decision. The filename convention `musicforprogramming(); episode N` mirrors function call syntax — a small piece of programmer humor that ages better than most.

## Key points

- Long-form continuous mixes (1-2 hours each), no interruptions, no algorithm — pure listening for focus
- Spans ambient, minimal techno, drone, experimental electronic — genres with low attention demand
- No accounts, ads, or recommendations; deliberately minimal UI design
- Over 60 episodes (as of 2022) covering a wide range of tones from serene to industrial
- Contrast with Spotify's algorithmic curation: this is curator selection, not personalization
