## Summary

A personal video from Maker Faire 2012 capturing a 100% human-powered stage — concert-scale sound and lighting powered entirely by stationary bikes being pedaled by audience members. This was a recurring Maker Faire installation designed by various groups to demonstrate human power generation at a tangible scale and make physics intuitive: how many watts does a human produce? How many people does it take to power a PA system?

The human-powered stage format has been used by Kickstart Power, various festival sustainability projects, and educational programs. The typical setup: a bank of stationary bikes connected to generators and a battery buffer, feeding into a standard audio/lighting rig. The experiential point is that energy is physical and finite — you feel the resistance when the PA needs more power.

Maker Faire installations like this embodied the event's philosophy: not just showing you something cool, but letting you participate in and understand the mechanism. The bike stage was maker culture applied to energy literacy.

## Key points

- Human-powered stage: stationary bikes → generators → battery buffer → PA and lighting.
- An average adult generates 75-200W cycling; a full PA system typically requires 2-5 kW — hence needing many riders.
- Maker Faire used participatory installations to make technical concepts (energy, watts, power) experiential.
- Path was used for private sharing; this was shared from the May 2012 Maker Faire weekend.
