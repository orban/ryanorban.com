## Summary

Week 2 at Zipfian Academy tackled statistics head-on, earning the memorable subtitle. The curriculum confronted students with the frequentist vs. Bayesian divide — not as an abstract philosophical debate, but as two different ways of answering the same practical questions about data. The title's punchline is that the joke writes itself: frequentist statistics and Bayesian statistics walk into a bar and disagree about what the data means.

The frequentist approach treats probability as long-run frequency of events — you can't assign probability to a hypothesis, only to data. p-values, confidence intervals, and null hypothesis significance testing are frequentist constructs. The Bayesian approach treats probability as degree of belief — you maintain a prior distribution over hypotheses and update it with data to produce a posterior distribution.

For a data scientist, the practical difference shows up in A/B testing: a frequentist declares significance when p < 0.05; a Bayesian asks "what's the probability this variant is better, and by how much?" The Bayesian answer is more directly useful for business decisions. The Zipfian Academy curriculum forcing students to confront this divide early gave them vocabulary for reasoning about uncertainty throughout the rest of the program.

## Key points

- Frequentist statistics: probability = long-run frequency; no probability assigned to hypotheses; p-values test "is this data surprising under the null?"
- Bayesian inference: probability = degree of belief; prior + likelihood → posterior; directly answers "what should I believe about the hypothesis?"
- The practical divide in A/B testing: frequentist gives p-values and significance thresholds; Bayesian gives posterior probability of winning and expected lift.
- Zipfian Academy week 2 covered probability theory, distributions, hypothesis testing, and introduced the Bayesian framework — a lot to absorb in one week.
- The title captures a genuine culture clash: many practitioners in 2014 had strong tribal affiliations to one camp, which the bootcamp deliberately punctured.
