## Summary

T2D3 stands for triple, triple, double, double, double — the ARR growth trajectory that Neeraj Agrawal of Battery Ventures observed as a common pattern in SaaS companies that reached $1B valuations. Starting from roughly $2M ARR: triple to $6M, triple to $18M, then double to $36M, $72M, $144M. The math is arithmetic, but the implication is strategic: this rate of growth, sustained for five years, is what separates the companies that reach scale from those that plateau.

T2D3 became a useful benchmark in the venture capital community because it translated abstract growth expectations into concrete year-by-year targets. If you're at $3M ARR and growing 2x instead of 3x, you know you're tracking below the benchmark — which affects your funding options and exit timeline. The benchmark also implied a cadence of go-to-market investment: you have to be adding distribution capacity before you hit the growth targets, not after.

The limitations of the framework are worth noting: it was derived from enterprise SaaS with high ACV and long sales cycles. PLG (product-led growth) companies, consumer SaaS, and developer tools have different growth profiles. But as a directional heuristic for B2B SaaS with a sales motion, T2D3 remains a standard reference point for board conversations, fundraising benchmarks, and growth planning discussions.

## Key points

- T2D3: triple, triple, double, double, double ARR over 5 years from ~$2M to ~$144M ARR.
- Coined by Neeraj Agrawal of Battery Ventures as a benchmark for SaaS companies targeting unicorn outcomes.
- Useful for setting growth expectations, calibrating fundraising timelines, and planning go-to-market investment.
- Derived from enterprise SaaS with sales motions — less applicable to PLG or consumer SaaS.
- The benchmark implies investing in distribution *ahead* of the growth targets, not reactively.
- At any ARR level, tracking against T2D3 reveals whether you're on a $1B trajectory or not.
