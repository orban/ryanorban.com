## Summary

IBM's annual 5 in 5 predictions for 2018, published December 2013, listed five areas where they projected technology would fundamentally change daily life within five years. These predictions were part IBM Research roadmap, part marketing, and part genuine speculation — made by IBM's top scientists with some grounding in their own R&D pipeline.

The 2013 edition predicted: (1) **The classroom will learn you** — adaptive educational systems that personalize instruction by modeling each student's learning patterns; (2) **Buying local will beat online** — physical retail enhanced by local data and personalization will reclaim ground; (3) **Doctors will routinely use your DNA information** — genomic medicine going from research to routine clinical practice; (4) **A digital guardian will protect you online** — AI security systems that learn your normal behavior and detect anomalies; (5) **The city will help you live in it** — smart city infrastructure that responds to residents' needs.

Looking back from 2026, personalized learning, genomic medicine, and AI security (items 1, 3, 4) were directionally correct but slower than predicted. Local retail lost to online, not the reverse. Smart cities progressed unevenly.

## Key points

- IBM "5 in 5" is an annual tradition since 2006 — five predictions for five years out, released at year's end.
- 2013 predictions for 2018: adaptive learning, local retail resilience, routine DNA medicine, AI security, smart cities.
- Directionally correct on AI-powered personalization, genomics mainstreaming, and security monitoring.
- Wrong on local retail beating online — e-commerce continued to gain share through 2018 and beyond.
- The format reveals what IBM Research was betting on internally: mostly machine learning, genomics, and IoT.
- Useful as a retrospective calibration exercise in technology forecasting.
