## Summary

[Anatomy of an AI System](/notes/anatomy-of-an-ai-system/) is a large-scale research and visualization project by Kate Crawford and Vladan Joler, published in 2018. It uses the Amazon Echo as a case study to map the complete infrastructure, labor, data flows, and supply chains required to produce and operate a seemingly simple AI device. The essay is both an academic paper and a visual artifact — a sprawling diagram tracing raw materials through manufacturing through data centers through algorithmic systems to the end user.

The project makes visible what is usually invisible about AI systems: the rare earth minerals extracted in mines, the warehouse workers and gig economy data labelers, the massive energy consumption of cloud computing infrastructure, the surveillance-like data collection from users, and the corporate structures that extract value from all of these. The Amazon Echo is deconstructed as a node in an enormous global system involving geology, geopolitics, logistics, labor, and computation.

Crawford and Joler argue that AI is neither autonomous nor disembodied — it is deeply material, deeply labor-intensive, and deeply embedded in existing systems of extraction and inequality. The visual form of the essay matters: showing the full system at once makes it harder to wave away any single component as incidental.

This sits alongside Crawford's later book Atlas of AI as a foundational text in critical AI studies — the scholarly tradition examining AI not as a neutral technology but as a sociotechnical system with political economy. It directly influenced how researchers and journalists talk about the environmental cost of AI, data labeling labor, and hardware supply chains.

## Key points

- Uses Amazon Echo as a lens to trace the full global supply chain of a consumer AI product — from mineral extraction to cloud data centers to user behavior.
- Makes the material basis of AI visible: rare earths, factory labor, energy consumption, surveillance.
- Data labeling and Ghost work — the often-invisible human labor that trains AI systems — is central to the analysis.
- Situates AI within systems of extraction and inequality, not as a neutral or disembodied technology.
- Complementary text: Atlas of AI (Crawford, 2021) expands the same analysis into a full book.
- Influential in AI ethics and critical AI studies — frequently cited in policy discussions about AI regulation and environmental cost of AI.
