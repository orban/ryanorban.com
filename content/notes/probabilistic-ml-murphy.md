## Summary

Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction by Kevin Murphy is the modern successor to his 2012 book *Machine Learning: A Probabilistic Perspective*, updated to cover contemporary deep learning alongside classical Bayesian methods. The book is free online and covers the full ML landscape through a unified probabilistic lens: everything from linear regression and Naive Bayes through neural networks, VAEs, and normalizing flows is treated as a form of probabilistic inference or density estimation.

The probabilistic framing is the book's defining feature. Unlike survey books that treat each algorithm as a standalone technique, Murphy roots everything in probability theory and Bayes' theorem. This makes it easier to reason about uncertainty, compare models, and understand when classical and deep approaches are solving the same underlying problem differently. Bayesian linear regression and ridge regression, for instance, turn out to be the same thing from different philosophical framings.

The book exists alongside a second volume, *Probabilistic Machine Learning: Advanced Topics*, which covers topics like deep generative models, reinforcement learning, and [[causal inference]. Volume 1 is the one to read first — it's genuinely introductory at the graduate level, assuming math comfort but not domain expertise. This is the book to read after CS231n if you want a principled theoretical foundation, or alongside [fast.ai](/notes/fastai/) if you want the why behind the what.

## Key points

- Free online; the modern definitive ML textbook, updated to include contemporary deep learning alongside classical probabilistic methods.
- Unifying probabilistic framing: linear regression, logistic regression, Gaussian processes, VAEs — all treated as instances of probabilistic inference.
- By Kevin Murphy (Google Research) — authoritative and thorough, not a quick read.
- Volume 1 covers the core; Volume 2 (*Advanced Topics*) covers deep generative models, reinforcement learning, causal inference.
- Pairs well with CS231n (vision intuition) and [fast.ai](/notes/fastai/) (practical approach) as a theoretical complement.
