## Summary

This TechCrunch piece by rjrogers87 pushed back on the 2012 tech culture that treated consumer tech startups as inherently more interesting than enterprise infrastructure companies. The piece was saved by the author with a note pointing to Nutanix and its distributed hypervisor tech as a counterexample to the assumption that enterprise infrastructure is boring plumbing.

The argument runs against the dominant narrative of 2012, which was obsessed with social apps, mobile consumer products, and the Facebook IPO hangover. Enterprise software had a reputation for bad UX, slow sales cycles, and unglamorous work. The author's counter was that the *technical* problems in enterprise infrastructure — building distributed storage systems, creating new hypervisor architectures, reinventing how data centers work — were at least as hard and as interesting as the social and consumer problems getting most of the press.

Nutanix was a particularly apt example because it was doing something genuinely novel: not just building another storage product, but rethinking the architecture of the data center by collapsing separate compute, networking, and storage layers into a unified software-defined appliance. The hyperconverged infrastructure category it was creating didn't exist in 2012 as an established term — Nutanix and the analyst community were still coining the vocabulary. That kind of category creation is arguably more ambitious than competing in an established consumer app market.

## Key points

- The 2012 enterprise prestige gap: investor and press attention heavily skewed toward consumer/mobile, leaving enterprise infrastructure undervalued.
- Nutanix's distributed hypervisor approach as a technically sophisticated counterexample — category creation, not feature competition.
- The hyperconverged infrastructure term was still nascent in 2012; Nutanix was defining the category in real time.
- Enterprise sales cycles and B2B dynamics make traction less visible than consumer growth metrics — distorting perceived innovation levels.
- The enterprise unsexy narrative proved wrong: Nutanix IPO'd at $9B in 2016; Twilio, Stripe, GitHub all validated enterprise/developer-focused models.
