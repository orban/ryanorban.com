## Summary

This essay by ryxcommar is a practitioner's exit critique of data science as a job function. The core argument: data science as a role sits structurally between software engineering and business analysis in a way that makes it chronically undervalued. Data scientists are expected to code like engineers but deployed on work that produces no shippable product — analysis, dashboards, models that inform decisions but don't directly generate revenue. This positioning means they lack the leverage of engineers (who ship things) and the organizational access of analysts (who talk to decision-makers).

The essay identifies several failure modes: data scientists hired as magic insight generators who end up doing data janitor work (cleaning pipelines, fixing ETL jobs); data science teams that produce rigorous analyses no one acts on; models that get built but never deployed; and the peculiar status anxiety of a field that borrowed credibility from academia (scientists) while doing work that looks like neither science nor engineering in practice. The title of the post implies career transition — moving to engineering or MLE rather than staying in the poorly-defined middle.

The timing matters. This was written in November 2022, right before ChatGPT launched and LLMs began reorganizing the ML job market. The essay anticipated dynamics that became clearer in 2023: "data scientist" as a title fragmented into more specific roles — ML engineer, analytics engineer, data analyst, AI engineer — and many of the grievances in the essay were addressed by role specialization. The broader critique of data science as an undifferentiated role that promised too much and delivered ambiguously continues to be discussed.

## Key points

- Data science structurally between engineering and analysis — lacks leverage of either.
- Common failure modes: becoming data janitors, producing unactioned analysis, building models never deployed.
- Role identity problem: borrowed scientific credibility for work that is neither science nor engineering.
- Written November 2022, just before LLMs reorganized the ML job market.
- Anticipates 2023 role fragmentation: data scientist splitting into ML engineer, analytics engineer, AI engineer.
- Widely resonant in the data community — captured real structural frustrations.
