## Summary

Nutanix raised $33 million in August 2012, with TechCrunch noting early signs the company was preparing for an IPO. At this point Nutanix was establishing itself as a leader in hyper-converged infrastructure (HCI) — converging compute, storage, and networking into a single appliance managed by software rather than separate hardware silos.

The raise came during a period of rapid growth. Nutanix was positioned as a disruptor to traditional three-tier enterprise data center architecture: separate compute servers, dedicated SAN storage, and a separate networking fabric. Their Nutanix Distributed File System (NDFS) distributed data across the cluster's local drives, eliminating the need for a separate SAN. Key customers were running VMware virtualization workloads on the platform.

Nutanix did eventually IPO in September 2016 at roughly a $9 billion valuation — one of the larger tech IPOs of that year. The 2012 raise was likely a Series C; the company had previously raised from Lightspeed Venture Partners and Khosla Ventures. Ryan was working at Nutanix during this period, as part of the team building and selling the platform.

## Key points

- $33 million raised in August 2012 with early signals of IPO preparation.
- Nutanix positioning as a hyper-converged infrastructure disruptor to traditional SAN + compute architectures.
- NDFS (Nutanix Distributed File System) as the core software layer, distributing data across commodity hardware nodes.
- Target: enterprises running VMware workloads who wanted to simplify data center operations.
- Nutanix eventually IPO'd in September 2016 at ~$9 billion valuation.
