## Summary

Introduction to Probability for Data Science by Stanley Chan (Purdue) is a free online textbook designed for undergraduate data scientists who need rigorous probability theory without the pure math packaging. The text is freely available at the URL and comes with accompanying lecture videos (2020 and 2022 editions) and code examples in Python, R, MATLAB, and Julia.

The curriculum spans the foundational arc: probability axioms and set theory, discrete and continuous random variables, joint distributions, sampling theory, and then applied statistics — regression, estimation, and hypothesis testing. It's positioned as a bridge between the here's some math approach of pure probability texts and the here's some code approach of data science tutorials — giving practitioners the theoretical grounding that makes ML concepts actually make sense.

The multi-language code examples (Python, R, MATLAB, Julia) are a practical choice: data science practitioners work across these environments, and seeing the same concept expressed in multiple languages reinforces the math rather than conflating it with a specific library's API. This is a book worth working through if you find yourself cargo-culting p-values or treating probability as black-box scikit-learn calls.

## Key points

- Free textbook covering probability foundations through statistical inference — bridges math and applied data science practice.
- Topics: discrete/continuous random variables, joint distributions, sampling, estimation, hypothesis testing, regression.
- Code examples in Python, R, MATLAB, Julia — the same math across multiple languages reinforces concepts over syntax.
- Lecture videos available (2020, 2022 editions) — structured for self-study without an instructor.
- Fills the gap between pure probability texts (too abstract) and ML tutorials (too applied without foundations).
