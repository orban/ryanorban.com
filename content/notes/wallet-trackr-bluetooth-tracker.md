## Summary

Wallet TrackR was an early consumer Bluetooth item tracker — a credit-card-sized device that slipped into a wallet and paired with a smartphone app to help locate lost wallets. The original tweet context: "I need one of these for everything I own." The Mashable coverage reflected the 2012 consumer tech enthusiasm for small connected hardware.

Wallet TrackR was among the first consumer BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy) item trackers, occupying a category that Tile (launched via Kickstarter in 2013, shipping in 2014) would popularize and Apple AirTag (2021) would bring to mass market. The core technology — Bluetooth 4.0 / BLE enabling coin-battery life in tiny form factors — had become available to product developers around 2010-2011, and 2012 saw a wave of startups applying it to item tracking.

The tracking everything you own intuition proved correct as a product category, but the path there was longer than the early enthusiasm suggested. Tile succeeded through a network effect approach (other Tile users help locate your lost item). AirTag succeeded through Apple's Find My network of 1 billion+ iOS devices. The standalone Bluetooth trackers like early TrackR struggled against these network-dependent models — individual device range is 30-100 feet, barely useful alone.

## Key points

- Wallet TrackR: credit-card Bluetooth item tracker — one of the earliest BLE consumer trackers (2012)
- BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy 4.0) enabled coin-battery-powered trackers — the key enabling technology
- Category predated Tile (2013-2014) and Apple AirTag (2021) by years — same idea, earlier execution
- Network effect is decisive: Tile's crowdsourced network and AirTag's Find My network beat standalone range limits
- Reflects broader 2012-2013 hardware startup enthusiasm: BLE opened new product categories overnight
