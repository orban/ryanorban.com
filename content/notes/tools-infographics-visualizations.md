## Summary

This SEO-focused Australian blog published a tools roundup in 2013, targeting content marketers and non-designers who wanted to create shareable infographics — a format that was at peak popularity as a content marketing strategy. Infographics were viral on early Pinterest and were being used heavily in SEO link-building campaigns.

The tools landscape for data visualization in 2013 was bifurcating: on one end, no-code tools like Infogr.am, Piktochart, and Visual.ly that gave non-designers drag-and-drop infographic creation; on the other, developer tools like D3.js and Google Charts for custom data visualization. Tableau Public sat in the middle, offering sophistication without requiring code.

The article's SEO origin context is worth noting: much of the 2013 infographic boom was driven by link-building strategies rather than genuine data communication. The visual format was compelling and shareable, but the content was often thin — a problem that contributed to the eventual backlash against infographics as a genre.

## Key points

- No-code infographic tools in 2013: Infogr.am, Piktochart, Visual.ly, Easelly — all offering templates for non-designers
- Tableau Public was the most powerful self-service visualization tool for the technically inclined non-programmer
- D3.js and Highcharts were the developer options — maximum flexibility, significant learning curve
- The infographic SEO strategy: create a data-rich image that others would embed with a backlink — content marketing that produced actual link juice
- The infographic boom peaked around 2013-2014; oversaturation and low-quality content led to declining effectiveness as a marketing channel
