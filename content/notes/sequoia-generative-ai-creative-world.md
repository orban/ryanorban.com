## Summary

Sequoia Capital's October 2022 essay by Sonya Huang and Pat Grady is the canonical early VC framing of the generative AI wave. Published before ChatGPT's release, it mapped the emerging landscape as a new creative layer on top of existing software — models that could generate code, images, text, and audio would transform every creative and knowledge-work workflow.

The central claim was that generative AI wouldn't just automate existing tasks but enable entirely new creative capabilities — the gap between imagining something and producing it would collapse. The essay coined or popularized the term "generative AI" as the preferred umbrella, displacing AI-generated content (AIGC) and the narrower text-to-image framing. Sequoia argued value would accrue to both the model layer (foundation models) and the application layer — though later analyses by Elad Gil and others were more skeptical of application-layer defensibility.

The market map they drew — grouping companies by modality (text, code, image, video, audio, 3D) — became a standard template. The piece reads as prescient from 2024: the wave was real, the categories held. But the defensibility assumptions and the timeline (they expected rapid value creation) were more optimistic than what played out in the first two years.

## Key points

- Sequoia's October 2022 framing positioned generative AI as a new creative platform, not just an automation tool.
- Published before ChatGPT, drawing on Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2, GitHub Copilot, and GPT-3 as evidence.
- Market map organized by modality: text, code, image, video, audio, 3D — became standard taxonomy.
- Central thesis: the gap between imagining and producing creative output will collapse.
- Value creation framing was optimistic about application layer; later analyses were more skeptical.
- Authored by Sonya Huang and Pat Grady at Sequoia.
