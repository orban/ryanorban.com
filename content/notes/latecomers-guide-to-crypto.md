## Summary

Molly White's annotated response to Kevin Roose's 2022 New York Times piece "The Latecomer's Guide to Crypto" — one of the highest-profile pro-crypto explainers in mainstream media at the time. Rather than writing a separate critique, White embedded her counter-arguments directly into the original text using annotation software, producing a point-by-point rebuttal that makes the disagreements specific rather than rhetorical.

White runs Web3 is Going Just Great, the ongoing chronicle of crypto failures, exploits, and scams. Her annotations on the NYT piece draw on that background: where Roose describes DeFi as democratizing finance, White provides context about the exploits and rug pulls that occurred that same month; where he describes DAOs as new forms of democratic organization, she notes the voting concentration problems; where he characterizes crypto as uncensorable, she points to the many cases where transactions were reversed, wallets frozen, and users deplatformed.

The meta-point is about how journalism covers emerging technology: the NYT piece is structurally a FAQ written with the framing of crypto advocates, and the annotations expose how the framing itself obscures important facts. This is worth reading alongside genuinely constructive crypto takes (like Evan Conrad's crypto use cases analysis) — both are trying to be accurate, but start from different priors and ask different questions.

## Key points

- Inline annotations directly in Roose's NYT text — disagreements are specific, tied to exact claims.
- Molly White brings the Web3 is Going Just Great evidence base: real incidents, not abstract skepticism.
- Challenges common framings: DeFi democratization, DAO governance, Bitcoin as inflation hedge, crypto as censorship-resistant.
- Meta-critique: journalistic both-sidesism in tech coverage often defers to industry framing even in "neutral" explainers.
- 2022 context: published at peak crypto prices, before the FTX collapse and broader 2022 bear market validation of many concerns.
- Useful alongside Evan Conrad's crypto use cases piece — the honest version of the same question.
