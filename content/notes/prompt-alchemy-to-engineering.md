## Summary

This essay by William Cotton argues for moving beyond intuitive prompt tweaking — "prompt alchemy," where practitioners try variations until something works — toward principled prompt design grounded in analytic philosophy and formal reasoning. The key concept is "analytic augmentation": structuring prompts to leverage the LLM's reasoning by making logical structure explicit, using techniques from analytic philosophy like decomposition, disambiguation, and explicit inference chains.

The core observation is that LLMs are trained on a vast body of human reasoning and respond better to prompts that follow the logical structure of clear human reasoning. A prompt that explicitly lays out premises, rules, and expected inference steps gets better results than a vague natural language instruction — not because the model is doing logic but because clear logical structure in text is what high-quality reasoning samples look like in the training data.

The essay situates this in the philosophy of language and formal reasoning — drawing on Wittgenstein, Russell, and the analytic tradition's emphasis on clarity and logical form. This is an unusual lens for a technical subject, but the argument is that the people best positioned to understand what makes prompts work are philosophers of language, not just ML engineers. It predates the formalization of chain-of-thought prompting but arrives at similar conclusions through a different route.

## Key points

- "Prompt alchemy" (intuitive tweaking) vs "prompt engineering" (principled design based on logical structure).
- "Analytic augmentation": explicit logical decomposition in prompts leverages LLM training on clear human reasoning.
- Philosophical lineage: analytic philosophy (disambiguation, logical form) applied to prompt construction.
- Arrives at chain-of-thought prompting-adjacent conclusions through a philosophical rather than empirical route.
- Published Feb 2023 — ahead of the formal empirical literature on structured prompting methods.
