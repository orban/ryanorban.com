## Summary

This Hacker News thread discusses the best approach for a CS student or programmer to enter ML/DL research and development in 2022. The debate reflects the state of ML education at the time: an explosion of learning resources creating genuine choice paralysis, competing opinions about how much statistics vs. deep learning intuition vs. engineering fundamentals are needed first.

The community's recommended resources cluster around [Fast.ai](/notes/fastai/) (Deep Learning for Coders — top-down, code-first), Andrew Ng's Coursera specialization (systematic, broad), and theory-heavy texts like Elements of Statistical Learning and Bishop's Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning. The practical question of whether "understand the statistics first or build things first and fill in theory" is the right order generates most of the disagreement.

Career track debates are notable: the thread distinguishes ML researcher (PhD-level, highly competitive), data scientist (applied ML to business problems), ML engineer (infrastructure + modeling at scale), and data engineer (pipeline building — debated as to whether it leads to ML roles). The disagreement about data engineering as a dead end or legitimate specialization reflects real market confusion in 2022, before the LLM wave reshuffled role definitions.

## Key points

- Resources debated: [Fast.ai](/notes/fastai/), Andrew Ng Coursera, ESL, PRML (Bishop), Georgia Tech OMSCS.
- Career tracks: researcher (PhD), data scientist (applied), ML engineer (infra+modeling), data engineer (pipelines).
- Core tension: theory-first vs. code-first learning paths — slight community consensus favors top-down ([Fast.ai](/notes/fastai/)).
- Statistics fundamentals seen as prerequisite by experienced practitioners; debated by newcomers.
- Data engineer path debated as dead end vs. legitimate specialization — real market ambiguity at the time.
- Snapshot of ML education community wisdom in 2022 before ChatGPT shifted mainstream attention to LLMs.
