## Summary

Thumbtack — a services marketplace — published their data scientist hiring challenges publicly. These were real analysis and modeling problems used to evaluate candidates, covering things like predicting service provider success, building recommendation features, and analyzing marketplace dynamics. Publishing them was a signal about what kind of work the role involved and a way to self-select candidates who found the problems interesting.

The challenges are representative of applied data science at a 2014 marketplace startup: the inputs are noisy event logs and user behavior tables, the targets are business-meaningful outcomes (provider retention, booking conversion), and the evaluation is both technical (model quality) and communicative (explanation of findings).

## Key points

- Problems involve real marketplace data modeling: predicting which providers will succeed, optimizing matching between customers and providers
- Evaluation is split between technical execution (feature engineering, model selection, evaluation methodology) and communication (explaining findings to a non-technical audience)
- Self-selection mechanism: publishing challenges lets candidates assess whether the work matches their interests before applying
- Representative of the 2014 data scientist role: SQL + Python + stats + enough product sense to frame the right question
- Contrasts with pure algorithmic interview formats — closer to a take-home project than a whiteboard problem
