## Summary

Eric Jang (robotics researcher at Google Brain / DeepMind) wrote this essay in April 2022 at a moment of peak hype about the machine learning job market. The all roads lead to Rome thesis: the anxiety people have about choosing the "optimal" entry point into ML careers (top research lab vs. FAANG vs. startup vs. PhD) is largely misplaced, because with sufficient technical SKILL, paths between these converge. The market for strong ML practitioners was so hot in 2022 that the bottleneck was skill, not the specific credential or company logo.

Jang's practical advice centers on what actually differentiates ML researchers and engineers: the ability to iterate rapidly on experiments, strong intuitions about what's worth trying, comfort with ambiguity, and the skill of knowing when an approach is fundamentally broken vs. just needing tuning. The supply of people with good ML taste (which he distinguishes from ML knowledge) was far lower than the supply of people who had completed the standard curriculum. This is consistent with broader observations about how quickly the field was growing: the tweet he likely had in mind noted that staying current required reading 100+ papers per day.

The essay has aged in interesting ways. The 2022 ML job market was near its peak before the 2022-2023 correction reduced headcount at many companies. The all roads optimism made more sense at peak demand than at constrained budgets. But the underlying point about taste and rapid iteration remaining the scarce resource holds.

## Key points

- The bottleneck in ML careers is taste and rapid iteration, not credential or entry path — strong practitioners find their way regardless.
- "All roads lead to Rome": research lab, FAANG, startup, PhD all converge if the underlying skill is there.
- ML taste: ability to iterate rapidly, strong priors on what's worth trying, comfort with ambiguity — rarer than ML knowledge.
- Written April 2022 at peak ML market demand; the subsequent correction is relevant context.
- Eric Jang background: Google Brain robotics; perspective is shaped by what it takes to do serious research.
- The same week, the ML arXiv growth tweet circulated — 100+ papers/day to stay current. Both capture the 2022 ML moment.
