## Summary

Sriram Krishnan (former Twitter, Microsoft, Andreessen Horowitz) offers a set of practical principles for writing cold emails that actually work. The guide distills what separates cold emails that get responses from the vast majority that don't.

The core principles: make it short (if your email requires scrolling, it won't be read), make a specific ask (vague would love to connect requests get deprioritized vs. "can we do a 15-minute call next week"), and do your homework (referencing something specific about the person's work signals you're not mass-emailing). The framing is important — always lead with why the recipient would want to respond, not with your own background and needs.

Krishnan also addresses the meta-question of when to send cold emails at all: they work best for people who are genuinely accessible and interested in hearing from people like you, and poorly for people whose inbox is already overwhelmed. Sending to a VC with 500 inbounds a day is different from sending to an operator who writes publicly about being open to conversations.

## Key points

- Keep it short — if it requires scrolling, it won't be read.
- Specific ask beats vague request: propose a concrete time or action.
- Research the recipient — reference their actual work, not generic praise.
- Lead with value to them, not your own credentials or needs.
- The right cold email is actually a warm email: some mutual context or connection.
- From Sriram Krishnan, who has been on both sides of many cold emails.
