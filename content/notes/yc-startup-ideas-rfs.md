## Summary

Y Combinator publishes a Requests for Startups (RFS) list — problems where they believe the market is ready, the technology is tractable, and the impact would be significant. Unlike generic startup advice, the RFS is a direct signal of what YC partners will actively champion internally during funding decisions.

The current RFS focuses heavily on AI-native companies: startups that don't just add AI to existing software categories but rethink the category entirely. AI-native service companies — replacing human-staffed services in insurance, accounting, and compliance with AI agents rather than just software interfaces — is a central theme. Also prominent: building tools and infrastructure specifically for AI agents rather than humans ("the next trillion users won't be people, they'll be AI agents").

Other priority areas include personalized medicine using AI to deliver individual treatment plans, and hardware infrastructure plays — particularly inference chips and semiconductor supply chain companies that reduce dependence on concentrated manufacturing.

## Key points

- AI-native service companies: replace human-staffed services outright rather than building software tools for the humans doing those jobs.
- Software for agents: machine-readable interfaces (APIs, CLIs) designed for AI consumers rather than human ones.
- Personalized medicine: AI agents that analyze individual health data for customized treatment at scale.
- Inference chips: specialized silicon for agentic workloads, including space applications.
- Hardware supply chain: US-based manufacturing agility to compete with Chinese production speed.
- Company brain: systems that consolidate institutional knowledge for AI automation.
- The RFS functions as a public statement of conviction — ideas here will find receptive ears at YC demo day.
