## Summary

This primer on special relativity and general relativity takes a "modern" pedagogical approach — meaning it likely uses contemporary notation (four-vectors, index notation or differential forms), starts from physical principles rather than historical development, and connects to modern applications like GPS systems, gravitational waves, and black hole physics.

Special relativity rests on two postulates: the principle of relativity (physics laws are the same in all inertial frames) and the constancy of the speed of light. From these, you derive time dilation, length contraction, the relativity of simultaneity, and the equivalence of mass and energy (E = mc²). The modern approach teaches this through Minkowski spacetime and four-vectors, which make the mathematics more elegant and generalizable than Einstein's original algebraic derivations.

General relativity extends this to non-inertial frames and gravity. The equivalence principle — that locally, acceleration and gravity are indistinguishable — is the bridge. GR represents gravity not as a force but as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass-energy, described by the Einstein field equations. The mathematical machinery required — differential geometry, tensors, geodesics — is significantly more demanding than SR. A modern primer typically builds this from scratch but keeps the focus on physical intuition before formal derivations.

Key phenomena covered include gravitational time dilation (the basis for GPS corrections), black hole event horizons and the Schwarzschild solution, gravitational lensing, gravitational waves (directly detected by LIGO in 2015), and the expanding universe of cosmology. The relevance to broader intellectual interests: GR is the framework in which warp drives (Alcubierre metric) and exotic spacetime geometries are formally possible — connecting to other physics notes in this vault.

## Key points

- Special relativity: two postulates → time dilation, length contraction, E = mc², Minkowski spacetime
- General relativity: gravity as spacetime curvature, described by Einstein field equations
- The equivalence principle (gravity ≈ acceleration locally) is the conceptual bridge from SR to GR
- Key applications: GPS corrections (gravitational time dilation), black holes, LIGO's gravitational wave detections
- Mathematical prerequisites: differential geometry and tensor calculus for full GR treatment
