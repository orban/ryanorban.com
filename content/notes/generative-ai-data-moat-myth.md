## Summary

This tweet from @mathemagic1an (December 2022) argues that data moats for generative AI companies are a false promise — a counterpoint to the pitch that AI startups could defensibly differentiate by accumulating proprietary training data. The timing is notable: just weeks after ChatGPT launched, when hundreds of ChatGPT for X pitches were being built around the assumption that domain-specific data would create defensible advantages.

The argument: large language model capabilities commoditize the value of data. When a generic model can produce medical text, legal language, or financial analysis at expert quality from a general internet corpus, the barrier to entry created by "we have more of that domain's data" collapses. The data moat that worked for earlier narrow ML applications (training a classifier that required labeled examples) doesn't transfer to the generative AI regime where foundation models are the starting point.

This connects to the a16z piece on data moats from 2019 (saved same day), which made a related argument in the context of traditional ML — that network effects in data are often not as defensible as assumed. In the generative AI context of 2022, the argument became even stronger: the scaling laws and pretraining had already captured most of the general knowledge, leaving relatively little marginal value for proprietary vertical data. By 2023-2024, this analysis proved largely correct for commodity AI features, though fine-tuning on proprietary data remains valuable for specific applications.

## Key points

- Data moats don't transfer from narrow ML to generative AI — foundation models commoditize domain knowledge.
- "ChatGPT for X with our proprietary data" was a flawed competitive moat argument.
- Connects to a16z's 2019 data moats analysis and applies it to the LLM regime.
- Prescient: by 2023-2024, vertical GenAI differentiation proved harder than anticipated.
- Fine-tuning on proprietary data still valuable for specific tasks, but not a defensible moat by itself.
