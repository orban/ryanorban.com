Roppongi is Tokyo's most internationally-oriented district: home to embassies, expat bars and clubs, the Roppongi Hills complex (2003, developed by Mori Building), and a concentration of art institutions including the Mori Art Museum, National Art Center Tokyo, and 21_21 Design Sight. By night it's known for its club and bar scene that caters heavily to foreign visitors and residents.

Japan-guide.com was (and remains) one of the more reliable English-language travel resources for Japan — community-maintained with practical information on transport, hours, costs, and neighborhood character.

## Key points

- Roppongi Hills (2003) transformed the district from a purely nightlife-focused area to a mixed-use development with offices, residences, and cultural institutions
- Mori Art Museum at the top of Roppongi Hills is among the better contemporary art venues in Tokyo — panoramic city views and internationally curated exhibitions
- The neighborhood's international character makes it accessible for first-time visitors to Tokyo but somewhat removed from the typical Japanese neighborhood experience
- Midtown Tokyo (2007) added another anchor development near Roppongi, with the Suntory Museum of Art and 21_21 Design Sight (Issey Miyake and Tadao Ando)
- Roppongi at night: the clubs and bars are legitimate but the street touts around Roppongi crossing are aggressive — a well-documented friction point for visitors
