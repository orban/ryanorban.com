## Summary

This AnalyticBridge holiday post was circulated primarily because of a bon mot from Kirk D. Borne that was retweeted widely: "A #DataScientist is a machine for turning coffee into insights." The joke is a riff on the mathematician's cliché ("a mathematician is a machine for turning coffee into theorems," attributed to Alfréd Rényi).

The post itself collected gift ideas for data scientists — likely books, courses, or tools — but the cultural artifact here is the joke. Its virality in late 2013 says something about the data science moment: the term was new enough that data scientist still felt like an identity marker worth joking about, and the coffee/insights formula acknowledged the combination of grind (pun intended) and intellectual output that defined the job.

The Rényi original about mathematicians is more than 60 years old. The adaptation to data scientists marks the moment when data science had enough cultural mass to attract its own professional folk humor.

## Key points

- The post circulated primarily for the quote: "A #DataScientist is a machine for turning coffee into insights" — riff on Alfréd Rényi's mathematician joke.
- Kirk D. Borne: one of the most widely followed data science Twitter voices in 2013 (@KirkDBorne), known for sharing community resources.
- Cultural marker: professional folk humor about "data scientist" identity in 2013, when the job title was still novel.
- AnalyticBridge: an early data science community/social network, active 2012-2015 before LinkedIn groups and Slack communities superseded it.
- The Rényi original: "A mathematician is a machine for turning coffee into theorems" — the data science version was quick to emerge as the field gained critical mass.
