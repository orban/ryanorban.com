## Summary

John Metcalfe (Atlantic Cities) covered a simplified visual map of San Francisco's bicycle infrastructure — lanes, routes, and paths — that prioritized design clarity over strict geographic accuracy. The map followed the tradition of Harry Beck's 1933 London Underground map, which abstracted the Tube network into a clean diagram by removing geographic fidelity in favor of readability.

San Francisco's bike infrastructure in 2013 was expanding rapidly but remained confusing to navigate — dedicated lanes, sharrows, bike routes, and gaps made it hard to plan routes without detailed knowledge. A simplified schematic map made the network comprehensible as a system rather than a collection of disconnected segments.

The piece reflects the early 2010s moment when cycling infrastructure and urban mobility data were becoming subjects of serious data journalism and information design. Cities were starting to treat cycling networks as designed systems worth visualizing.

## Key points

- Simplified schematic map of SF bike infrastructure — prioritizes network legibility over geographic accuracy.
- Design tradition: Harry Beck's London Underground map established that transport network maps should be topological, not geographic.
- Context: SF's cycling network was growing but fragmented in 2013; a clear map made it navigable as a system.
- Part of the broader urban data journalism trend — cities publishing bike/transit data enabling citizen visualization projects.
- Atlantic Cities (now CityLab) was the primary outlet for design and urban planning journalism at this time.
