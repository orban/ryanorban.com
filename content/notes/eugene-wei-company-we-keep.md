## Summary

Eugene Wei (Remains of the Day blog) extends his status-as-a-service framework to examine how social graphs function as status signals. The title is a riff on the idea that your peer group is itself a signal about you — in social contexts, who you're associated with communicates status before you've done or said anything. Social platforms understand this implicitly: the value of a network is partly the people in it, and being in the right network is itself a status signal.

The essay explores how this dynamic shapes social product design. Features that seem about individuals are actually about groups: follower counts signal social endorsement, reply threads signal whose ideas are worth engaging with, mutual connections signal your access to a peer group. This is why follower count became so important even though it's a crude measure — it's a rough proxy for "who thinks this person is worth knowing."

Wei connects this to why social network bootstrapping is hard: a platform's value is a function of who else is already there. The cold-start problem for social isn't just about having content — it's about having the right people whose presence signals that this is a credible venue. Instagram's early growth among photographers and designers made it aspirationally attractive. The same mechanism works at smaller scales: inviting the "right" early users (influential in their communities) creates status pull for the next wave.

## Key points

- Social graphs are status communication systems — who you're connected to signals who you are before any content does.
- Follower count as the canonical crude status proxy: a rough measure of "how many people think this person is worth following."
- The cold-start problem in social products is fundamentally a status problem — you need credible people whose presence makes the platform feel like the right venue.
- Network effects are partly about content volume but significantly about the status and identity of who's in the network.
- Part of Eugene Wei's broader "status as a service" framework — social platforms are status ledgers as much as communication tools.
