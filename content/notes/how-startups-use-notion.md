## Summary

Kopa (a rental marketplace) shares their practical Notion setup as a startup — one of many how we use Notion posts from the 2020–2022 era when Notion was rapidly displacing Confluence, Google Docs, and Asana for small teams. The post covers the actual structure they use rather than abstract advice: how pages are organized, what templates they rely on, and which parts of their workflow Notion owns.

The typical startup Notion setup follows a pattern: a company wiki (onboarding, policies, how-we-work), an engineering area (sprint planning, project specs, RFCs), a product area (roadmap, user research), and a team area (meeting notes, 1:1 templates, OKRs). Notion databases with filtered views serve as lightweight project management — kanban boards, roadmaps, and task trackers that stay connected to the context (specs, notes) rather than existing in a separate tool.

The honest tension: Notion as a single source of truth works best for small teams that actively maintain it. As teams grow, the flexible structure that made Notion powerful becomes unmaintainable without discipline. By 2023 many teams were splitting: Notion for documentation and knowledge, Linear or Jira for issue tracking, Slack for communication — each tool doing fewer things better.

## Key points

- Company wiki pattern: onboarding docs, policies, how-we-work, engineering standards all in one place.
- Notion databases replace lightweight project management — kanban with linked context.
- OKRs in Notion: quarterly goals linked to specific projects and meeting notes.
- Discipline requirement: flexible structure requires active curation or it decays quickly.
- The 2022 pattern: all-in-Notion for small teams, then fragment when headcount grows.
