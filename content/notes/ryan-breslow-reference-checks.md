## Summary

Ryan Breslow (CEO of Bolt) published this thread after conducting over 10,000 reference checks at Bolt, arguing references are the most powerful and most underused tool in the talent acquisition process. Most reference checks are ineffective theater — you ask soft questions, get softball answers, and leave with no new signal. Breslow's framework is about getting real information.

The most important technique is the numerical scale question: "On a scale of 1–10, how would you rate this person?" The key isn't the number — it's the follow-up. A 9 or 10 is signal. An 8 followed by great! is a polished non-answer. When someone gives an 8 or 7, you ask: "What would it take for them to be a 10?" This opens the door to honest feedback that wouldn't come from a direct what are their weaknesses? question.

The framework also emphasizes working backwards through the reference network: the listed references are the people the candidate prepared. Ask those references for other people who worked with the candidate — you want the unprepared references who didn't get the coaching on what to say. The raw signal is there.

## Key points

- Reference checks are underused because most are conducted wrong — soft questions produce soft answers.
- Numerical scale question (1–10, how do you rate them?) + follow-up (what to get to 10?) extracts honest assessment.
- Work backwards: ask listed references for additional contacts — those unprepared references have raw signal.
- References tend to omit negatives voluntarily; you have to create permission for honest feedback explicitly.
- Breslow argues Bolt out-competed on talent partly by taking this more seriously than most early-stage startups.
