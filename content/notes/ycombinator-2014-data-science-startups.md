## Summary

MLWave compiled a survey of Y Combinator's 2014 cohort to identify data science and machine learning startups. This type of snapshot is valuable as historical record: it shows which ML application domains had reached commercial viability by mid-2014, before deep learning completely reshaped the landscape.

The 2014 YC cohort ML startups would have reflected the state of applied ML at the time: classical methods (random forests, logistic regression, SVMs, k-means) applied to specific domain problems. The common application areas: predictive analytics for sales and marketing, NLP for text classification and sentiment, recommendation systems, fraud detection, medical records analysis, computer vision using pre-deep-learning methods, and financial modeling.

What's telling about this list is what it doesn't contain: no large language models, no generative AI, no diffusion models. The foundation model era was still years away. AlexNet had won ImageNet in 2012 but deep learning hadn't yet translated into a wave of startups — that would come with the 2015-2016 wave of GPU-native companies.

## Key points

- 2014 YC data science startups reflect classical ML applied to specific domains — not foundation models.
- Common domains: predictive sales analytics, NLP classification, fraud detection, medical records, recommendation.
- deep learning startups were rare — the post-AlexNet startup wave hadn't yet materialized.
- Y Combinator as a signal: which problem domains had enough market signal to attract seed-stage investment.
- MLWave (Kaggle-adjacent community) was an early aggregator of applied ML resources and analysis.
- Historical context: 2 years post-AlexNet, 1 year before the GPU startup wave, 9 years before ChatGPT.
