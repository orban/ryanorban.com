## Summary

By April 2012, Y Combinator had become the most prestigious startup accelerator in the world, and its demo days had become competitive events where investors would commit to investments at the door before companies even presented. YC companies were getting seed valuations of $4-8M on little more than a prototype and a team. Jason Calacanis of LAUNCH asked whether this was justified or whether it was a valuation bubble inside the broader startup ecosystem's frothiness.

The case for the valuations: YC's track record by 2012 included Dropbox, Airbnb, Heroku, Reddit, Disqus, and others. Admission to YC was a genuine signal — it implied the team had passed Paul Graham's filter, which correlated with certain qualities (intelligence, execution ability, coachability). The YC alumni network was a real asset. And with Facebook's IPO looming, there was enormous capital looking for exposure to the consumer internet.

The case against: YC had significantly expanded its batch sizes (from ~20 to ~60+ companies per batch), which meant the signal quality was diluting. Demo day dynamics created FOMO-driven decisions. Seed-stage valuations don't matter much if the company finds product-market fit and raises a Series A — but they create a cap table problem if a promising team takes a high seed valuation and then struggles to raise at a higher price later.

## Key points

- Y Combinator demo day valuations of $4-8M seed on prototype-stage companies had become standard by early 2012.
- YC's track record (Dropbox, Airbnb, Heroku) created a premium signal for admission — justifying some price premium.
- Batch size expansion was diluting the signal: more companies meant the YC filter was less selective.
- FOMO-driven seed investing at demo day was producing decisions that weren't based on real analysis.
- The Facebook IPO froth was flowing downstream into earlier-stage investments.
- High seed valuations can be a trap: they require a higher Series A price to avoid a down round, which is damaging to team morale and cap table.
