## Summary

A practical, non-sensationalist guide to emergency preparedness by lcamtuf (Michał Zalewski), a prominent security researcher known for tools like AFL (American Fuzzy Lop) and foundational web security research. The guide applies clear-headed risk analysis to the question of what individuals and households should actually prepare for — not apocalyptic scenarios, but the realistic disruptions that statistically happen to people: extended power outages, natural disasters (earthquakes, floods, storms), infrastructure failures, and short-term supply disruptions.

The approach is explicitly **rational and proportionate** — Zalewski argues against both the nothing will happen to me head-in-sand attitude and the prepper movement's expensive, paranoid over-preparation. The goal is building resilience for realistic scenarios without investing in gear or plans you'll never use.

The guide covers: water storage (the most critical, least prepared-for resource), food reserves (72 hours minimum, 2 weeks is practical), communication plans (if power and cell networks fail), first aid fundamentals, financial resilience (cash when ATMs are down), and home inventory of critical items. The tone is direct — here's what to actually do, here's why, here's where to stop before you've gone overboard.

The 2022 context: bookmarked shortly after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, when practical resilience and infrastructure vulnerability were suddenly very salient topics for people who hadn't thought about them before. The guide was originally written well before this and has been cited widely in tech communities for years.

## Key points

- Rational, proportionate emergency preparedness — not prepper culture, not ignoring risk.
- Priority hierarchy: water first (3 days = minimum, 2 weeks = good), then food, communication, finances.
- Written by lcamtuf (Michał Zalewski) — security researcher applying adversarial/risk analysis to personal resilience.
- Focus on realistic disruptions: power outages, earthquakes, supply chain disruptions — not apocalyptic scenarios.
- Communication plan: critical if cell networks fail (meeting points, out-of-area contacts).
