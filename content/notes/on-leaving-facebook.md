## Summary

Alex Kotliarskyi's essay on leaving Meta after 8.5 years is honest about both the pull to stay and why he eventually left. The compensation structure is the first thing he names: top-5% market pay, stock refreshers that reset the vesting clock, premium benefits. These aren't just nice-to-haves — they're mechanisms that make the opportunity cost of leaving feel concrete and immediate in a way that job satisfaction doesn't.

The reason he eventually left is less concrete: "the magic was gone." He describes a slow cultural drift toward scale and metrics optimization, away from the quality and craftsmanship that originally attracted him to engineering. This is a common arc at large tech companies — as they scale, the work shifts from building things that feel exciting to operating and incrementally improving things that already exist. The transition from Replit confirmed what he suspected: the satisfaction gap between well-compensated scale work and smaller, higher-ownership work is real.

His practical advice for engineers considering similar transitions: approach the search as exploration rather than escape, invest in physical wellness during the process rather than grinding algorithm prep, build a clear narrative about your values and goals to resist recruiter pressure toward familiar prestige, and recognize that job satisfaction affects quality of life more than incremental compensation differences at this income level.

## Key points

- Golden handcuffs are structural: refreshing stock grants reset vesting inertia every few years — deliberate retention mechanism.
- The craft satisfaction decline at scale: work shifts from building to operating and optimizing at the margin.
- Practical job search advice: exploration framing, physical wellness, clear values narrative, resisting prestige gravity.
- Meta → Replit transition: smaller company, more ownership, different kind of craft.
- The implicit claim: past a certain compensation level, satisfaction drives quality of life more than additional income.
