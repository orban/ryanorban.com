## Summary

[Logseq](/notes/logseq/) is an open-source personal knowledge management tool with a distinctive outliner-first structure. Unlike Obsidian (which treats each note as a flat markdown document), Logseq treats every entry as a block in a hierarchical outline, and each block is individually linkable with bidirectional links. The daily journal is the primary entry point — you write each day, and the system builds a graph from the connections you make.

The privacy angle is real: [Logseq](/notes/logseq/) stores all data locally as plain text Markdown and Org-mode files. There's no proprietary format, no cloud dependency by default, and no vendor lock-in. The entire database is reconstructed on the fly from files on your filesystem, which means you can back up, version control, and migrate your notes without any special tooling.

[Logseq](/notes/logseq/) competes directly with Roam Research (which pioneered the daily journal + graph approach) and Obsidian (which also uses local Markdown). The key differentiator from Obsidian is the block-based structure — linking to specific blocks rather than just notes. This makes it closer to Roam Research philosophically, but open-source and without the SaaS pricing model.

## Key points

- Outliner-based PKM — every paragraph is a block, blocks are individually linkable.
- Daily journal as the primary entry point — notes flow from days into the graph.
- Local-first: all data stored as plain Markdown or Org-mode files you control.
- Bidirectional links build a graph connecting pages and blocks.
- Open-source under AGPL — free to use, with optional paid sync.
- Competes with Roam Research, Obsidian, Notion in the PKM space.
- Queries let you filter and surface notes by tag, property, or date.
