## Summary

This CityLab piece covers a conceptual art dinner where data visualizations were rendered as edible food — charts and graphs translated into physical objects you could taste and consume. The project pushes on a question that information design usually treats as purely visual: what does it mean to make data tangible? Embodied cognition research suggests that physical interaction with information changes comprehension; literally eating it is the extreme case.

The concept connects to a broader movement in data physicalization — representing data through non-screen media. Tactile maps for the visually impaired, sound-based data (sonification), and haptic feedback in data tools all share the same premise: our sensory systems other than vision are underused in data communication. Food adds the dimensions of taste, smell, and the social ritual of eating together.

It's an art project more than a research finding — but the question it raises is genuinely interesting for UX design and science communication: when does the medium through which data is experienced affect what people understand and remember?

## Key points

- Data physicalization: representing data as non-screen, non-2D objects.
- The edible version is an extreme case of making abstract information tangible.
- Embodied cognition premise: physical interaction with information changes how it's processed.
- Connects to sonification, tactile maps, and haptic data feedback as alternatives to visual-only design.
- Art project, not empirical research — but raises legitimate questions for science communication.
