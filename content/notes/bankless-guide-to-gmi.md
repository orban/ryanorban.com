## Summary

Bankless is one of the most prominent DeFi-native media properties, founded by Ryan Sean Adams and David Hoffman. Their newsletter and podcast consistently framed crypto and DeFi participation not just as investment but as opting out of the traditional financial system — going bankless. The GMI (Gonna Make It) guide is a piece from the cultural peak of the 2021-2022 crypto bull run, unpacking the slang, philosophy, and identity markers of crypto-native culture.

GMI and its opposite NGMI (Not Gonna Make It) are crypto Twitter terms that encode a specific belief: that crypto represents a once-in-a-generation wealth transfer, and only those who understand and act early will benefit. The terminology creates an in-group identity — people who are going to make it understand DeFi, hold ETH, participate in DAOs, and don't sell. NGMI marks those who don't get it, whether skeptics, those who panic-sell, or those who hold legacy assets.

The guide likely covers: the vocabulary (GMI, NGMI, wagmi, HFSP — have fun staying poor), the philosophical underpinnings (separation from legacy finance, hyperbitcoinization), and the behavioral norms (diamond hands, DCA, yield farming participation). As a cultural document it's more revealing than it intends — the in-group terminology, the messianic framing, and the scorn for non-participants tell you a lot about the social dynamics driving the 2021 speculation.

## Key points

- GMI/NGMI taxonomy encodes belief that crypto is a sorting event — winners and losers determined by early, correct action
- Bankless philosophy: DeFi participation is opting out of the legacy financial system, not just investing
- WAGMI (We're All Gonna Make It) is the collectivist variant — solidarity framing for the crypto community
- Cultural artifacts: HFSP, diamond hands, "gm" (good morning) as community signal — crypto Twitter in-group markers
- Saved January 2022 at the peak; by end of 2022 the vocabulary looked different in context of 70%+ market drawdowns
