## Summary

Published in *Nature* (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-022-05028-x), this paper from Gavin Wright's lab (Wellcome Sanger Institute) and Berend Snijder's lab (ETH Zurich) presents the most comprehensive map to date of direct protein-protein interactions across human leukocyte surface proteins. Using a high-throughput surface receptor screening method, the team systematically tested interactions across a recombinant library covering most detectable surface proteins on immune cells — producing a quantitative protein interactome for the human immune system.

The immune system is unusual in that its organ is distributed: immune cells circulate and must dynamically form physical associations using cell-surface receptor interactions. Despite the therapeutic importance of these interactions, the map was previously incomplete. This paper fills much of that gap with independent biophysical validation of each novel interaction, yielding confidence scores and quantitative parameters rather than just presence/absence calls.

The paper integrates the interactome with single-cell RNA sequencing expression data to build a reductionist mathematical model predicting cellular connectivity from first principles, and packages results as an interactive multi-tissue single-cell atlas. Functional validation using targeted protein stimulation and multiplex imaging links specific receptor pairs to immune response modulation.

## Key points

- Systematically maps direct interactions across the surface proteome of human leukocytes — the most complete such map published
- Each interaction independently validated with biophysical parameters (binding affinity, kinetics) — not just high-throughput hits
- Mathematical model predicts cellular connectivity from expression data + receptor interactions, enabling inference across tissue types
- Interactive single-cell atlas allows browsing immune interactions in context of tissue-level expression
- Links receptor interactions to functional roles: modulating immune responses and maintaining normal intercellular associations
