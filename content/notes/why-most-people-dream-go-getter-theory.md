## Summary

The distinction between people who have goals and people who achieve them isn't primarily about talent, intelligence, or luck — it's about action initiation. This post's go-getter theory frames the gap as a difference in tolerance for the discomfort of starting: taking the first step before there's certainty of outcome, before anyone is watching, before it feels safe to begin.

Most people dream and plan and research until the conditions feel right. Go-getters start before conditions are right, accepting that the first iteration will be imperfect. The first article is bad. The first version ships with bugs. The first gym session is embarrassing. The difference is in treating that discomfort as the cost of entry rather than a signal to wait.

This maps onto deliberate practice research: the people who achieve mastery aren't more comfortable with failure — they've simply developed a higher tolerance for starting and persisting through the uncomfortable early stages. The psychologist Carol Dweck's growth mindset research supports this: people who believe abilities develop through effort are more likely to initiate (and re-initiate after failure) than those with fixed-ability beliefs.

## Key points

- The dreamer/doer gap is primarily about initiation tolerance, not capability or talent.
- Starting when conditions aren't right is the SKILL — not waiting until it feels safe.
- Growth mindset (Dweck) predicts higher initiation rates: effort improves ability, so starting is always worth it.
- Perfectionism is often initiation avoidance in disguise — waiting for readiness that never arrives.
- The first version of anything is bad; the only way past it is through it.
- Related to activation energy: reducing the cost of starting (small commitments, public accountability) raises completion rates.
