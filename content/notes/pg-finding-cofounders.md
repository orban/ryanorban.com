## Summary

Garry Tan (then a Y Combinator partner, later YC president) shared excerpts from a Paul Graham interview on the mechanics of finding cofounders and what strong founding teams look like. PG's view is unambiguous: the founding team is the most important variable in startup success, and a single founder is usually a significant disadvantage.

The core argument: a single founder making a hard decision has only their own perspective. Two or three co-founders have someone to argue with, to catch mistakes, to provide energy when one person is down. The stress of a startup is high enough that the psychological support alone matters — but the practical benefit is more fundamental. A two-person team with complementary skills (technical + business, or backend + frontend) can move faster and make better decisions than either person alone.

PG's cofounder matching advice was pragmatic: the best place to find a cofounder is your existing network (worked together, went to school together, built something together). The chemistry and trust that comes from prior collaboration is hard to replicate with someone you just met at a networking event or through a cofounder matching service. The analogy he used repeatedly: marrying someone after a blind date — you're staking your next several years on someone whose stress responses you don't know.

## Key points

- Paul Graham consistently argued that a single founder is a significant handicap — YC would accept solo founders, but the bar was higher.
- Best cofounder source: people you've already worked with — trust is already established, working styles are known.
- Complementary skills matter: a team where both founders are engineers is better than one founder alone, but a tech + business split can cover more ground.
- The strong team signal YC looked for: do they finish each other's sentences? Do they disagree well? Do they have a track record together?
- Garry Tan later built Initialized Capital and became YC president — an interesting perspective, given he himself had started as a technical cofounder.
