## Summary

Sequoia Capital's May 2022 all-hands presentation to portfolio founders, delivered by Roelof Botha, Doug Leone, Alfred Lin, and Carl Eschenbach, addresses the sharp macroeconomic shift underway as the Federal Reserve raised interest rates to combat inflation after a two-year zero-rate era. The opening framing by Botha references his experience as CFO of PayPal in March 2000 — when Sequoia's Michael Moritz counseled the team to recognize the moment, extend runway, and become a real business. PayPal had 7 months of runway; they cut costs, introduced fees in June 2000, and reached $250M revenue and profitability by 2002.

The core message is structural: the cost of capital has fundamentally changed. During 2020-2021, negative real interest rates drove a fundraising environment where growth companies could raise easily at high valuations. That environment is over. The presentation covers the macro environment (Kevin Kelly), public market repricing (Jeff Wang), historical patterns (Doug Leone), and specific operating advice (Alfred Lin). The operating advice is direct: calculate default alive vs. default dead, extend runway to 24+ months, cut burn aggressively, and avoid assuming the markets will recover quickly enough to bail you out.

The document is historically significant as an artifact of the 2022 correction — the moment when venture capital and the broader tech sector acknowledged that the zero-rate era was over. Sequoia's reputation meant this presentation became a widely read reference for founders and investors navigating the shift. The growth at all costs mentality that characterized 2020-2021 startup culture was explicitly repudiated in favor of profitability and capital efficiency as primary virtues. The PayPal framing also exemplifies Sequoia's recurring playbook: contextualize the current crisis within prior crises to project that survival + focus = emergence stronger.

## Key points

- Core message: the cost of capital has fundamentally changed — treat this as a structural shift, not a dip to wait out
- **Default alive vs. default dead**: calculate your runway and understand which position you're in before anything else
- Operational advice: extend runway to 24+ months, cut burn, achieve profitability — don't rely on the next fundraise
- Historical contextualization: 2000 (dot-com), 2008 (financial crisis), 2022 — Sequoia's recurring playbook
- Artifact of the 2022 tech correction: the moment the VC community publicly acknowledged the zero-rate era was over
