## Summary

MicroConf is an annual conference in Las Vegas organized by Rob Walling and Mike Taber, designed specifically for self-funded software founders — people building SaaS products and other software businesses without venture capital. The 2012 edition was an early one, when the conference was still small enough (a few hundred attendees) to feel like a tight community. Whitetail Software's recap captured both the content and the ethos.

The conference explicitly positioned itself against the dominant startup narrative of the era. In 2012, Y Combinator had just graduated its largest batch, TechCrunch covered Series A and B rounds daily, and startup had become synonymous with VC-funded companies aiming for large exits. MicroConf was a deliberate alternative: profitable businesses, no outside investors, founders who owned 100% and set their own terms. Rob Walling's tagline was startups for the rest of us — software companies that didn't need to be unicorns.

The talks covered topics like pricing strategy for small SaaS businesses (charge more, annual plans), customer acquisition with no marketing budget (content marketing, SEO, targeting a niche), and how to build sustainably without the pressure of investor timelines. Hiten Shah, Patrick McKenzie (patio11), and others in the Hacker News bootstrapper community were key influences. This community eventually evolved into Indie Hackers, which Courtland Allen launched in 2016 as a content platform for the same audience.

## Key points

- MicroConf organized by Rob Walling (Drip, Tinyseed) and Mike Taber for self-funded SaaS founders.
- Counter-narrative to VC-track startups: profitable, owner-operated, no external timeline pressure.
- Key figures in this ecosystem: Patrick McKenzie (patio11), Hiten Shah, Nathan Barry (ConvertKit).
- Indie Hackers (2016, Courtland Allen) became the content/community extension of the same movement.
- MicroConf grew significantly over the decade — by 2020 it had remote events and multiple city editions.
