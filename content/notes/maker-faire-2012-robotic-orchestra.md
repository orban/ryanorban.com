## Summary

A personal video from Maker Faire 2012 showing a robotic orchestra — mechanical actuators or purpose-built machines playing real instruments autonomously. Robotic musical instruments have a tradition at Maker Faire and in computer music research: they occupy an interesting space between MIDI sequencing (purely electronic) and human performance (fully physical), and they make sound generation mechanisms visible and tangible.

Notable robotic instrument projects of the era: Trimpin's complex pneumatic and solenoid-driven installations, Pat Metheny's Orchestrion (a touring robotic backing band), and various Arduino-driven solenoid drum machines. The Maker Faire version was likely a community project using solenoids to strike xylophones, marimbas, or drum kits under MIDI or custom sequencer control.

The interest in robotic orchestras at events like Maker Faire connects to a broader fascination with mechanical music — player pianos, music boxes, and barrel organs represent centuries of attempts to automate musical performance. Modern Arduino-driven versions democratize what previously required significant engineering resources.

## Key points

- Robotic musical instruments: solenoids/pneumatics trigger physical instruments under electronic control.
- Bridges MIDI sequencing with acoustic instrument physics — different from purely electronic music.
- Notable examples: Trimpin (installation artist), Pat Metheny's Orchestrion, countless Maker Faire community builds.
- Arduino-controlled solenoids + MIDI input = accessible entry point for DIY robotic instruments.
