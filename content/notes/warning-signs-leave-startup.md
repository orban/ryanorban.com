## Summary

This guide from Jobstart addresses one of the hardest career decisions in the tech world: when the discomfort of working at a startup crosses from normal early-stage chaos into genuine warning signs worth acting on. The core challenge is that many legitimate warning signs — uncertainty, shifting priorities, founder stress, cash pressure — are also just features of working at an early-stage company. Distinguishing signal from noise requires looking at patterns, not incidents.

The actionable signals tend to cluster around a few categories. Financial: runway is less than six months with no clear fundraising path, payroll has been late, or major customers are churning faster than they're being replaced. Leadership: founders are withholding information that should be shared with the team, or the team's stated values diverge sharply from observable behavior. Equity: significant dilution events happened without explanation, or the cap table is more complex than it was represented during your offer. Growth: key technical or business hires are leaving faster than they're being replaced.

The emotional dimension is underappreciated. People stay at startups long past the warning signs because of sunk cost reasoning ("I've already invested two years"), social loyalty to founders, and the optimism bias that's partly necessary to work there in the first place. The guide reframes the question from "is this startup going to succeed? to is this the best use of my time given what I know now?" — a cleaner decision framework.

## Key points

- Financial warning signs: short runway with no clear path, late payroll, major churn.
- Leadership warning signs: information withholding, values-behavior divergence, key hires departing.
- Equity warning signs: unexplained dilution, cap table complexity, vesting cliffs not clearly communicated.
- Sunk cost fallacy is the biggest trap — past investment doesn't predict future return.
- Reframe: not will this succeed? but "is this still the best use of my time?"
