## Summary

The Miniature Earth is a short educational video that reframes global population statistics by scaling the world down to 100 people. The exercise makes abstractions concrete: if the world had 100 inhabitants, how many would have access to clean water, electricity, or a computer? How many would be malnourished? What percentage would control the majority of wealth?

This format — the statistical village or 100 people thought experiment — has roots in academic writing going back to Donella Meadows' 1990 essay State of the Village Report, but the video format popularized it widely in the early 2000s. The Miniature Earth video (originally created in 2001, updated through 2010) was one of the early viral edu-clips, traveling through email chains and early YouTube.

The personal note — "really made me appreciate my American privilege" — reflects the video's intended effect: confronting global inequality through proportional thinking rather than raw numbers. 67 people out of 100 can't read; 1 person owns more than all others combined. Numbers at that scale land differently.

## Key points

- A thought experiment: if the world were a village of 100, what would the demographics look like?
- Makes global inequality visceral — wealth, literacy, food access all rendered proportionally.
- Based on Donella Meadows' State of the Village framework; video format popularized it.
- Part of the early wave of viral educational content before formal platforms like Khan Academy.
