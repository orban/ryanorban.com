## Summary

Darius Kazemi (tinysubversions) is one of the most prolific and thoughtful creators of Twitter bots and generative art systems. His projects page catalogs a range of work: bots that recombine news headlines (TwoHeadlines), bots that generate sorting algorithm descriptions (SortingBot), bots that create glitched logos (GlitchLogos), and bots that produce fake wire-service scene descriptions (WireScenes). Each project plays with the gap between algorithmic process and apparent meaning.

The bookmark was saved from his Eyeo 2016 talk, a festival at the intersection of art, technology, and data. Kazemi's work embodies what generative art theorists call constrained outputs — rules tight enough to produce coherent outputs, loose enough to produce surprise. His bots aren't impressive because they're sophisticated; they're interesting because they're simple rules that produce unexpected meaning. A Markov chain recombining real headlines produces things that feel true even when they're fabricated.

The tiny subversions name is a statement of intent: not grand AI art, but small interventions that make you see familiar forms (news, product names, wire copy) from an unexpected angle. His writing on bot ethics — how to make bots that are funny and strange without being harmful — established many of the informal norms for the Twitter bot community of that era.

## Key points

- Darius Kazemi creates bots that exploit the gap between algorithmic recombination and apparent meaning.
- TwoHeadlines, SortingBot, GlitchLogos, WireScenes — each subverts a recognizable genre.
- Works through constrained systems, not sophisticated AI — surprise comes from rule simplicity, not complexity.
- Bot ethics writing established norms for creative/humorous bots vs. harmful/deceptive ones.
- Presented at Eyeo 2016, a festival pairing art, technology, and data visualization communities.
