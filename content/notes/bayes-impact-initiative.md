## Summary

Bayes Impact is a nonprofit organization founded in San Francisco that applies data science and machine learning to social problems. The organization pairs data scientists with nonprofit and government partners to tackle challenges in healthcare access, criminal justice, and public policy. The name is a reference to Bayesian inference — and a signal about the technical orientation of the founders.

The launch of Bayes Impact in 2014 coincided with a surge of interest in "data for good" applications. The Zipfian Academy community had significant overlap with this movement — data science students who came from non-traditional backgrounds often had strong social motivation for learning quantitative skills. The San Francisco data science community in 2014 was actively debating whether data skills could be redirected from consumer optimization toward social benefit.

Bayes Impact distinguished itself from tech-company-funded social good initiatives by being an independent nonprofit — it could work with organizations that might be wary of tech company involvement. The model became influential: several similar data science for nonprofits organizations launched in the following years.

## Key points

- Nonprofit applying data science and machine learning to healthcare, criminal justice, and public policy.
- Founded in San Francisco in 2014 alongside the boom in data science bootcamps like Zipfian Academy.
- "Data for good" framing: redirecting quantitative skills from consumer optimization to social problems.
- Pairs data scientists with nonprofits and government — different model from corporate CSR programs.
- Part of a broader 2014 movement connecting machine learning expertise with public interest work.
