## Summary

This Quora thread collected community knowledge about public data sources at a time (2014) when the landscape was more fragmented than it is today. Finding large, clean datasets required knowing which agency, university, or company had published what — there was no single discovery layer.

The major categories that appeared: US government open data (Data.gov, Census Bureau, BLS, NOAA weather data), academic repositories (UCI Machine Learning Repository — still one of the best sources of benchmark datasets), web-scale data from social platforms (Twitter streaming API, Wikipedia dumps), Kaggle datasets (which was early in its community-contributed dataset era), and specialized sources like Freebase, DBpedia, and financial data from Quandl.

The thread is also a time capsule: many resources mentioned (Freebase, early Quandl) have since changed significantly. Google Dataset Search (launched 2018) and Hugging Face Datasets (2020+) later created the centralized discovery layer that was missing in 2014.

## Key points

- UCI ML Repository: still the canonical benchmark dataset source for ML research — consistently referenced from 1987 to present.
- Government open data portals (Data.gov, Eurostat, World Bank) provide real-world tabular data for economic and social analysis.
- Wikipedia database dumps: full text + structure of one of the world's largest knowledge bases — standard for NLP pretraining and knowledge graph research.
- Kaggle as dataset discovery layer: the community-contributed datasets feature was early in 2014; now one of the primary discovery mechanisms.
- The fragmentation in 2014 is why data collection was a significant part of data science work — data engineering was often just finding and cleaning public data.
