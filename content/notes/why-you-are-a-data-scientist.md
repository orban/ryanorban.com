## Summary

This Slanted Windows post argues that data science is fundamentally about how you think — designing experiments, asking questions of data, and using whatever tools help you answer them — rather than a specific set of credentials or technologies. The retweeted framing from @adailyventure: "Data science isn't about having the tools, but how you use the tools to conduct the experiments you creatively design."

The argument pushes back against a credentialing view of the field that was emerging in 2014: you need a PhD, you need to know deep learning, you need specific tools. Instead, the post locates the core SKILL in experimental design and scientific reasoning — if you naturally ask how do I know this is true? and what would disprove this?, you're already doing data science regardless of title.

This was a useful corrective to the gatekeeping impulse in a field that was still defining its own norms. The countervailing pressure (from the bioinformatics post "You're Not Allowed Bioinformatics Anymore") was that soft skills without coding ability leave you unable to actually execute. Both perspectives were active in 2014's debate about what the field required.

## Key points

- Data science as mindset: hypothesis formation, experimental design, and evidence-based reasoning are the core — tools are means, not ends.
- Contrasts with the technical gatekeeping view: the field needs people who can think scientifically, even if they're still learning the tools.
- Pairs with inbox/2014-07-24-bioinformatics-you-cant-do-it for the counterargument: mindset without coding ability can't execute on its own.
- The democratization argument: many people in other roles are already doing informal data analysis and A/B testing — the label shouldn't be a barrier.
- 2014 context: the field was figuring out its own entry criteria; these competing positions shaped how bootcamps like Zipfian Academy defined their prerequisites.
