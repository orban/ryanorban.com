## Summary

Moves was a quantified self app that automatically tracked physical activity using the iPhone's built-in accelerometer and GPS — no manual logging, no wearable device required. The content field frames it explicitly as an alternative to dedicated fitness trackers like Fitbit or Jawbone UP that cost $100-150: Moves was free.

The app ran continuously in the background and classified movement into walking, running, cycling, and transport (sitting). It built a storyline of your day — where you went, how long you were there, how you got between places. This was distinct from dedicated fitness trackers in that it captured both activity and place, not just step counts.

Moves was significant in the 2013 wearable and fitness tracking landscape because it demonstrated that acceptable passive activity tracking was possible with existing smartphone hardware, no additional device needed. It was acquired by Facebook in April 2014 — less than two years after launch — for reported $100M, primarily for the location and activity data.

## Key points

- **Passive tracking**: Moves required no manual logging — it ran in background and used accelerometer + GPS to classify activity automatically
- Competed with Fitbit, Jawbone UP, and Nike FuelBand — offered similar data at $0 vs $100-150 for dedicated hardware
- The "storyline" feature was ahead of its time: a visual log of where you went and how, not just step counts
- Acquired by Facebook in April 2014 — the location/activity data was the strategic asset, not the app itself
- Highlights the 2013 tension: dedicated fitness wearables vs. smartphone-only approaches — Apple Watch (2015) eventually settled this by making the wearable itself a smartphone companion
