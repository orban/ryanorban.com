## Summary

In 2015, Galvanize announced a partnership with Google to expand to London — its first international campus. The partnership was significant: Google wasn't just lending its brand, but actively partnering on the educational model, likely providing curriculum resources or access to Google's UK tech ecosystem.

The strategic logic for Galvanize was clear: London was (and remains) Europe's dominant tech hub, with a large population of career-changers wanting to enter the technology sector and a developer talent shortage comparable to San Francisco. The coding bootcamp model that worked in SF, Denver, and Seattle was straightforwardly exportable.

The Google partnership reflected a broader pattern in 2015 where major tech companies began treating coding education as infrastructure worth investing in — not primarily for PR, but because the developer talent pipeline was genuinely constraining their own growth. Google, Microsoft, and others were all running or funding developer education programs at this time.

## Key points

- Galvanize's first international expansion — London as Europe's tech hub.
- Google partnership: credibility, curriculum resources, ecosystem access.
- Context: tech talent shortage in the UK mirrored US, making bootcamp model exportable.
- Major tech companies (Google, Microsoft) investing in developer education in 2015 as a talent pipeline strategy.
- By Dee Dee Skinner (likely CEO/co-founder of Galvanize at the time).
