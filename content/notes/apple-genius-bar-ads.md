## Summary

In summer 2012, Apple ran a series of television ads around the 2012 London Olympics featuring a bumbling customer being helped by a Genius Bar employee. The Verge's critique was pointed: the ads were unfunny, condescending to customers, and completely inconsistent with the confident, aspirational advertising that defined the Apple brand under Steve Jobs. The piece captured a broader anxiety about what Apple would become after Jobs.

The specific failure of the ads was tonal. Apple's advertising from the 1984 Macintosh spot through the Think Different campaign through the iPod silhouettes had always positioned the user as someone special, capable, and ahead of the curve. The Genius ads positioned the user as confused and helpless — someone who needs to be saved by the store. It's the opposite of the brand's historic identity, and audiences noticed.

This came 9 months after Jobs died and about 3 months after Tim Cook's first full year as CEO. The post-Jobs Apple anxiety was intense in 2012: would the company maintain its design and marketing standards without its chief taste-maker? The Genius ads were held up as evidence that the answer was no. In retrospect, the ads turned out to be an anomaly rather than a trend — Apple pulled them after the Olympics and returned to its more typical minimalist aesthetic.

## Key points

- Apple briefly ran slapstick Genius Bar TV ads during the 2012 London Olympics — a significant tonal departure from the brand's historic style.
- The criticism wasn't about production quality but about positioning: the ads made Apple customers look helpless, undermining the brand's "Think Different" identity.
- The post-Steve Jobs transition raised genuine questions about whether Apple's taste standards were institutionalized or personally held.
- Apple pulled the Genius ads after the Olympics — the episode remained an outlier, not a new direction.
- Illustrates how brand identity can be maintained as a set of norms or can be driven by a single person — Apple in 2012 was testing which it was.
