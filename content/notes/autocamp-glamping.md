## Summary

AutoCamp is a glamping company that operates premium outdoor hospitality properties centered on vintage and custom Airstream trailers. The positioning bridges two markets: the outdoor/national parks experience that traditional camping provides, and the boutique hotel comfort level that travelers accustomed to quality accommodations expect. Properties are located at scenic destinations — Joshua Tree, Yosemite, Zion, Catskills, Cape Cod, Sonoma — with each designed to complement the surrounding landscape.

The Airstream as product choice is deliberate. Airstreams carry strong brand associations: mid-century Americana, road trip culture, classic design that reads as premium without being ostentatious. AutoCamp's custom units add modern interiors (quality bedding, kitchenette, curated design) while preserving the Airstream aesthetic. The result is a product that photographs well, differentiates from generic glamping tents, and appeals to design-conscious travelers.

The business model is asset-intensive (Airstreams + land + hospitality operations) but defensible in location: a property adjacent to Yosemite with the right permits is genuinely difficult to replicate. The company competes with Under Canvas (safari tents near national parks) and increasingly with traditional resort hotels as the outdoor luxury category matures. AutoCamp received significant VC backing around 2019-2021 on the thesis that the outdoor hospitality market was underserved relative to demand.

## Key points

- Glamping at national park and scenic locations — Airstream trailers as the premium outdoor accommodation product.
- Properties: Joshua Tree, Yosemite, Zion, Catskills, Cape Cod, Sonoma — proximity to natural attractions is core to value proposition.
- Airstream aesthetic signals quality without luxury hotel price point — strong design brand associations.
- Competes with Under Canvas (tents near parks) and boutique hotel brands extending outdoors.
- Asset-heavy, location-defensible business model — land permits near major parks are genuinely scarce.
- Part of the broader outdoor/adventure travel boom accelerated by COVID-era travel behavior shifts.
