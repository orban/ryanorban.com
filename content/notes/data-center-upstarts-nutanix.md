## Summary

This Businessweek piece from December 2013 covered the emerging hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) wave, led by Nutanix and other startups taking market share from incumbents HP and EMC in enterprise data centers.

Nutanix (founded 2009) was the standout example: its approach packaged compute, storage, and networking into a software-defined appliance that was simpler to manage than the traditional three-tier enterprise architecture (separate servers, storage arrays, and networking from different vendors). The pitch was consolidation and operational simplicity — one vendor, one management interface, linear scale-out.

The article notes that the disruption pattern was classic: startups addressed a real pain point (data center complexity and cost) that incumbents weren't solving, often because solving it would cannibalize their own product lines. HP's server and storage divisions had conflicting incentives to integrate. EMC was similarly constrained by its position in the existing sales motion.

## Key points

- Hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI): compute + storage + networking in a single software-defined appliance — the category Nutanix popularized.
- Nutanix was the category leader; others included SimpliVity and Scale Computing.
- Displacing HP (ProLiant servers + 3PAR storage) and EMC (VMAX, VNX storage) which dominated enterprise data center budgets.
- Disruption dynamic: incumbents' product line incentives prevented them from self-cannibalizing into HCI.
- VMware eventually entered with vSAN (2014) — the standard move of a platform vendor entering an adjacent layer.
- Nutanix IPO'd in 2016 at $1.9B valuation; HCI is now mainstream enterprise infrastructure.
