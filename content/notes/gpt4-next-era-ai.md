## Summary

GPT-4 represented a qualitative jump from GPT-3 that surprised even many practitioners — not just better on benchmarks but demonstrably different in the kinds of tasks it could handle. This HackerNoon overview covers the architectural and capability advances that made GPT-4 a step-change in the LLM landscape when it was released in March 2023.

The headline advances: GPT-4 is multimodal (accepts image inputs alongside text), has a dramatically larger context window (8K or 32K tokens vs. GPT-3's 4K), scores near human-level on professional exams (bar exam, medical licensing, GRE), and shows significantly improved instruction following and reasoning on complex multi-step tasks. The reasoning improvement was the most practically significant — GPT-3.5 could follow instructions but often lost track of constraints in complex tasks; GPT-4 maintained coherence over longer reasoning chains.

OpenAI was characteristically opaque about the architecture — no parameter count, no architecture details released. The widely cited estimate of 1.8 trillion parameters in a mixture of experts configuration comes from leaks and third-party analysis, not official disclosure. What was clear from the [Foundation Model Transparency Index](/notes/foundation-model-transparency-index/) is that this opacity extended to training data and compute as well. GPT-4 became the reference against which all other models were measured in 2023, defining the frontier model category.

## Key points

- GPT-4 added multimodal input (images), extended context (32K), and step-change reasoning quality.
- Near-human performance on professional exams (bar, medical licensing, GRE).
- Architecture undisclosed — parameter count from leaks only, not official disclosure.
- Defined the "frontier model" benchmark category for the rest of 2023.
- Instruction following and coherence on complex multi-step tasks was the practical differentiator vs. GPT-3.5.
- Precursor to the cascade of models trying to match or exceed GPT-4 (Claude 2, Gemini, Llama 2 70B).
