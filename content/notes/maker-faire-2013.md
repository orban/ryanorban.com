## Summary

A personal photo from Maker Faire 2013 at the San Mateo County Event Center, the annual gathering organized by Make Magazine that brings together hardware hackers, DIY electronics builders, 3D printing enthusiasts, roboticists, and crafters. In 2013, Maker Faire was at its peak cultural relevance — the maker movement was being seriously discussed as a path to reindustrialization and STEM education reform.

Maker Faire in 2013 featured Arduino-based projects, early consumer 3D printers (MakerBot was having its mainstream moment), large-scale art installations, and science demonstrations. The Bay Area event (World Maker Faire was in New York) attracted ~100,000 attendees across the weekend.

The maker movement's 2013 moment was genuine: crowdfunding (Kickstarter, Indiegogo) was enabling hardware startups, Arduino had democratized microcontroller programming, and affordable 3D printing was making custom fabrication accessible. The subsequent years would see both a commercialization of maker culture and a pullback from some of the more utopian claims about reindustrialization.

## Key points

- Maker Faire Bay Area: annual San Mateo event organized by O'Reilly Media / Make Magazine
- 2013 was peak maker movement moment: Arduino, affordable 3D printing, crowdfunding hardware
- MakerBot Replicator 2 was the prominent consumer 3D printer of that era
- Path was a private social network (max 150 connections) popular in 2012-2013 before declining
- The maker → startup pipeline was real: Pebble, Oculus, and others came from maker/hardware hacker backgrounds
