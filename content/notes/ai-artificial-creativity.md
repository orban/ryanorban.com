## Summary

This NessLabs essay by Anne-Laure Le Cunff examines what the arrival of powerful generative AI tools means for human creativity and knowledge work. Published in November 2022 — right as ChatGPT was about to launch and Stable Diffusion had just gone public — it's an early attempt to think through questions that would become central discourse for the next few years.

The central argument: AI tools don't replace creativity, they change what creativity consists of. When AI can generate text, images, and code, the creative task shifts from generation to curation, direction, and taste. The human role becomes the one who asks the right questions, judges the outputs, and integrates results into something coherent and meaningful. Metacognitive skills — knowing what you want, recognizing quality, directing the process — become more important relative to the raw generative skills that AI can now approximate.

NessLabs focuses on the science of learning and productivity, so the framing is practical rather than philosophical: how do you adapt as a knowledge worker when AI can do more of the routine cognitive tasks? The essay doesn't reach a settled conclusion so much as map the territory — this was early, and the right answers weren't yet clear.

## Key points

- Generative AI shifts creative work from generation toward curation, direction, and taste.
- Metacognitive skills become more valuable: knowing what you want and recognizing quality matter more when generation is easy.
- Published at the start of the generative AI wave — early framing of questions that remain contested.
- From NessLabs by Anne-Laure Le Cunff, focused on practical knowledge worker implications.
- The human role: not competing with AI on output volume, but bringing judgment and intent to the process.
