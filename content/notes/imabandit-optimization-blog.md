## Summary

I'm a Bandit is Sébastien Bubeck's research blog, started while he was at Princeton (later at Microsoft Research). The name is a pun on multi-armed bandit problems — the core subject of Bubeck's research — and positions the blog as written by a practitioner of that research domain. The content sits at the level between textbook pedagogy and formal research papers: rigorous enough to be useful to researchers, accessible enough to build intuition without requiring complete paper-level context.

Bubeck's research sits at the intersection of convex optimization, online learning, and bandit algorithms. The blog covers gradient descent variants, mirror descent, online convex optimization, stochastic gradient descent convergence analysis, and randomized algorithms — all topics that became central to machine learning theory as deep learning's convergence properties became a major research question. His 2012 survey on regret analysis of stochastic and nonstochastic multi-armed bandit problems (with Nicolò Cesa-Bianchi) remains a foundational reference.

The blog represented a style of mathematical exposition that was rare in 2013: genuinely expert practitioners sharing technical content online at a level of depth that reflected their actual research thinking. It was part of a small ecosystem of influential ML/optimization blogs (alongside Francis Bach's notes, Moritz Hardt's work, and the Off the Convex Path blog) that helped communicate theoretical results to a wider audience of practitioners.

## Key points

- Sébastien Bubeck: Princeton → Microsoft Research; leading researcher in convex optimization and bandit algorithms.
- Multi-armed bandit algorithms: the explore-exploit tradeoff formalized — fundamental to reinforcement learning and online decision-making.
- Blog style: research-level rigor without requiring full paper context — proof sketches, intuitions, and connections between ideas.
- Regret analysis: the mathematical framework for online learning — how much worse is an algorithm than the best fixed strategy in hindsight?
- Part of a small ecosystem of expert technical blogs that helped diffuse theoretical ML research to practitioners in the early 2010s.
