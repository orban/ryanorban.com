## Summary

By 2013, cloud computing had established its core delivery models — IaaS (Infrastructure-as-a-Service, led by AWS), PaaS (Platform-as-a-Service, led by Heroku and Google App Engine), and SaaS (Software-as-a-Service, led by Salesforce). This ReadWrite piece identified the next wave of specialization: cloud services targeting specific pain points in enterprise IT that didn't fit neatly into the existing categories.

Desktop-as-a-Service (DaaS): virtual desktops hosted in the cloud rather than on premises — users access a Windows or Linux desktop through a thin client or web browser, and all compute happens in the data center. Amazon WorkSpaces launched in late 2013; VMware and Citrix had on-premises VDI predecessors. The appeal for enterprises: centralizes desktop management, simplifies compliance (data never leaves the cloud), enables BYOD without security risk.

Monitoring-as-a-Service (MaaS): infrastructure and application monitoring delivered as SaaS rather than deployed on-premises. New Relic and AppDynamics were the 2013 leaders — you installed a small agent, they handled the monitoring infrastructure and dashboards. This is the model that later became observability infrastructure. Disaster Recovery-as-a-Service (DRaaS): replication and failover to cloud as a managed service, replacing the traditional second data center. Companies like Zerto and VMware vCloud Air were early players. The economics were compelling — pay for recovery capacity only when you need it rather than maintaining idle standby infrastructure.

## Key points

- DaaS (Desktop-as-a-Service): cloud-hosted virtual desktops — centralized management, BYOD compatibility, compliance simplification. Led to Amazon WorkSpaces launch (2013).
- MaaS (Monitoring-as-a-Service): SaaS monitoring with lightweight agents — New Relic, AppDynamics; precursor to the observability platform category.
- DRaaS (Disaster Recovery-as-a-Service): cloud replication replacing second data centers — pay-per-use disaster recovery capacity.
- The proliferation pattern: every IT function that had been a capital expense was being turned into an operational subscription — even functions that enterprises had previously considered too sensitive to outsource.
- Anything-as-a-Service (XaaS): the general principle that cloud commoditizes delivery of any IT capability that was previously owner-operated.
