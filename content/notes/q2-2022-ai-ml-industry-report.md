## Summary

Gradient Flow's Q2 2022 preview report surveys the state of artificial intelligence and machine learning adoption across enterprise and research contexts as of mid-2022 — just before the ChatGPT moment reshaped the entire industry's priorities. The report covers infrastructure tooling (where MLOps and feature stores dominated enterprise conversations), deployment patterns, and where organizations were investing in ML engineering versus pure research. At the time, transformer models were primarily discussed in the context of NLP and computer vision, with generative AI still a niche concern.

The survey data captures the gap between research and production: most organizations struggled with model deployment, data quality, and monitoring rather than with novel architectures. The dominant pain points were operational — model drift, training-serving skew, data pipeline reliability — rather than the capability questions that would come to dominate the field by Q4 2022. AutoML and low-code ML tools were gaining traction as organizations tried to reduce dependency on scarce ML engineering talent.

As a historical artifact, the report is interesting for what it doesn't emphasize: large language models appear but as specialized tools, not as the central organizing technology. Foundation models were discussed in research circles but not yet on enterprise roadmaps. Reading it in retrospect, the industry was about 6 months from a complete reorientation of priorities.

## Key points

- Mid-2022 snapshot: MLOps, feature stores, and deployment reliability dominated enterprise AI conversation
- Generative AI and large language models were not yet the central enterprise concern — that shifted sharply in late 2022
- Key bottleneck was ML engineering talent, driving AutoML and low-code ML adoption
- Model monitoring and data quality were top operational pain points
- The report serves as a before-picture: what the AI/ML industry looked like before the ChatGPT disruption
