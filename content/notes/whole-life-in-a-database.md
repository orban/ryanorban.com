## Summary

Felix Krause (creator of Fastlane) describes his practice of logging nearly every aspect of his life into a single structured database — a form of extreme quantified self combined with a personal data infrastructure project. The data categories include health metrics (sleep, exercise, weight), mood and emotional state, social interactions (who he talked to, for how long), work time, finances, travel, and media consumed. The database is a single source of truth that he queries for self-reflection and personal analytics.

The technical setup is one part of it — choosing a schema that can flexibly accommodate new tracking categories, writing the logging tooling, building dashboards. But the more interesting part is the phenomenology: what actually changes when you track everything? Krause reports that awareness itself shifts behavior (the Hawthorne effect at personal scale), that retrospective analysis reveals patterns invisible to day-to-day perception, and that certain categories turn out not to matter once the novelty of tracking them wears off.

The essay connects to a broader quantified self movement that was active from the late 2000s through the 2010s — Kevin Kelly and Gary Wolf were key advocates, the movement held regular meetups, and tools like Exist.app, RescueTime, and various health trackers emerged to serve it. The honest version of this practice acknowledges that most tracking habits don't produce lasting behavioral change; the rare exceptions involve specific goals with clear feedback loops. Krause's database is the maximalist, technically-capable version of an impulse most people satisfy with a Fitbit.

## Key points

- Single database for all personal life tracking: health, mood, social, financial, work, travel, media.
- Technical challenge: designing a schema flexible enough for new categories without losing historical consistency.
- Hawthorne effect at personal scale: awareness of tracking influences behavior, not just insight from analysis.
- Quantified self movement context: Kevin Kelly, Gary Wolf, the mid-2010s peak of personal data enthusiasm.
- Felix Krause is best known for Fastlane (iOS CI/CD automation) — this is a personal project.
- Meta-question: most granular tracking doesn't produce behavioral change; specificity + feedback loop is what matters.
