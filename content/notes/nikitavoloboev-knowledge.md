## Summary

Nikita Voloboev (developer at Linear) maintains one of the most impressive public personal knowledge management systems on the internet — a GitBook-style wiki hosted on GitHub containing thousands of notes spanning programming languages, algorithms, mathematics, physics, productivity, philosophy, and tools. It's a living document updated continuously, organized by topic rather than chronologically. The project demonstrates what a second brain looks like when built seriously over years rather than as an experiment.

The structure is topic-first: each subject has its own markdown file with curated links, personal notes, and cross-references. Categories range from programming (Rust, Python, TypeScript, functional programming) to sciences (biology, physics, chemistry) to tools (macOS, NixOS, editors) to abstract topics (consciousness, creativity, learning). The density varies — some pages are deep dives, others are link collections. What makes it valuable isn't any individual page but the accumulated cross-connections: the wiki becomes a map of how one thoughtful developer has organized their understanding of a large swath of human knowledge.

The relevance to personal knowledge management practice: Voloboev's system is built with Obsidian-style linking but pre-dates the Obsidian era, having started as a GitBook then migrated formats. The lesson it demonstrates is that consistency over time matters more than the tool — the wiki has value because it's been maintained for years, not because of any particular technical choice. It also exemplifies learning in public, a practice of sharing knowledge as it's acquired rather than only after mastery, which creates accountability and attracts collaborators.

## Key points

- One of the most complete public second brain implementations — thousands of notes across years of continuous maintenance.
- Topic-first organization: each domain has a dedicated page with notes, links, and cross-references.
- Demonstrates that tool choice matters less than consistency — started in GitBook, survived tool migrations.
- Learning in public at scale: the wiki is findable and has influenced how many developers think about PKM.
- By Nikita Voloboev, developer at Linear; also known for Learn Anything (knowledge graph project).
- Source of inspiration for Obsidian vault structures and digital garden practices.
