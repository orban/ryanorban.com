## Summary

This article addresses the organizational challenge that companies hit when we should do data science becomes we need to hire data people. The core argument is that most companies make the same mistakes: hiring a single "data scientist" who is expected to do everything (data engineering, analysis, modeling, visualization), then being confused when they're overloaded or underperforming.

Building a functional data team requires sequencing hires correctly and understanding that data scientist, data engineer, and data analyst are distinct roles with different SKILL sets and responsibilities. A common antipattern is hiring a sophisticated modeling person before you have clean, accessible data — the modeler spends 80% of their time on data engineering work they're not optimized for. The article recommends starting with data infrastructure before analytics or modeling.

The organizational question of where the data team sits — centralized vs. embedded in business units — also affects output. Centralized teams produce methodological consistency but can become bottlenecks; embedded teams move faster but create fragmented practices. The right choice depends on company size, data maturity, and whether the business questions are uniform or heterogeneous across divisions.

## Key points

- Common mistake: hiring a lone data scientist to own everything from pipelines to models.
- Correct sequencing: data infrastructure first, then analytics, then modeling — don't hire modelers into a data desert.
- Data scientist, data engineer, data analyst are distinct roles with different primary skills.
- Organizational placement: centralized vs. embedded teams involves a consistency vs. speed tradeoff.
