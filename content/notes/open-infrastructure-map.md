## Summary

[Open Infrastructure Map](/notes/open-infrastructure-map/) is an interactive web map that visualizes physical infrastructure drawn from OpenStreetMap data — power lines and substations, oil and gas pipelines, telecommunications towers and cables, wind farms, solar installations, and water pipelines. The data comes from OpenStreetMap's community-contributed infrastructure tags, rendered on a dedicated map layer that makes the invisible physical systems underlying civilization visible.

The tool is useful for anyone thinking about energy systems, infrastructure resilience, or physical network topology. Zooming into a region reveals the actual power grid topology — which lines are high-voltage transmission versus distribution, where substations cluster, how generation connects to load centers. For telecoms, you can see where fiber runs along highways and rail corridors, and where tower coverage overlaps. This kind of data was previously only available in fragmented utility company datasets.

OpenStreetMap's infrastructure mapping varies in completeness by region and infrastructure type. Power lines in Europe and the US are often well-mapped; gas pipeline data is patchier. The map is not authoritative for engineering decisions but provides a remarkable bird's-eye view of the physical substrate that most digital work depends on.

## Key points

- Visualizes power lines, substations, gas pipelines, telecom towers from OpenStreetMap data.
- Distinguishes high-voltage transmission lines from distribution networks.
- Shows generation sources: wind farms, solar installations, hydro plants.
- Useful for infrastructure resilience analysis, energy research, and physical network topology understanding.
- Data quality varies by region — Europe and US power grid coverage tends to be good.
- All data from the OpenStreetMap community; completeness depends on contributor coverage.
