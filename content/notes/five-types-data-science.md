## Summary

This piece tackled a practical confusion in 2013: data scientist was a single job title covering wildly different work. A company that needed someone to build dashboards in SQL was advertising for the same role as a company building recommendation systems with deep learning. This taxonomy tried to slice the space usefully for startups deciding what to hire for.

The five types roughly correspond to: (1) **Business intelligence / analytics** — dashboards, reporting, understanding what happened; (2) **Product analytics** — user behavior analysis, funnel optimization, A/B testing; (3) **Growth data science** — acquisition channel analysis, marketing attribution; (4) **Machine learning engineering** — building models for product features (recommendations, ranking, NLP); (5) **Research** — advancing methods, publishing, longer-horizon work.

These require very different skills. A BI analyst needs strong SQL and data visualization instincts. A machine learning engineer needs algorithmic depth and production engineering skills. A researcher needs theoretical training and academic instincts. Conflating them under one title was causing both hiring mismatches (companies hiring researchers when they needed analysts) and compensation confusion (ML engineers commanding researcher salaries).

## Key points

- "Data scientist" in 2013 covered at least five distinct job functions with different skills, tools, and organizational roles.
- BI/analytics and ML engineering are on different SKILL spectra — the former is about clarity and communication, the latter about model building and deployment.
- Startup hiring mistake: hiring a PhD researcher when you need someone to build dashboards and answer business questions with SQL.
- The taxonomy problem was real and persisted — companies still routinely conflate these roles in job postings.
- Zipfian Academy was teaching the applied/engineering end of this spectrum: Python, SQL, machine learning for product decisions.
