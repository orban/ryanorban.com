## Summary

The Ethereum Foundation (EF) is a non-profit that supports the Ethereum ecosystem without controlling it. Its 2022 annual report (covering 2021 activities) outlines the philosophy, structure, and financial allocations behind one of the most unusual organizations in the blockchain space — a foundation that describes itself as "highly aligned, loosely coupled," deliberately avoiding the top-down structure of traditional organizations.

Executive Director Aya Miyaguchi introduced the "Infinite Garden" concept as the EF's vision — a metaphor for Ethereum as a cultivated but open ecosystem that grows beyond any single actor's control. The report emphasizes that the EF's most important role is often quiet: coordinating core protocol conversations, connecting EF Fellows' projects from emerging economies with the wider community, and facilitating educational efforts. The financial numbers are a small fraction of what the broader Ethereum community does.

The EF operates as a community of semi-independent teams, each with their own processes and goals but sharing alignment on Ethereum's long-term direction. This organizational design deliberately distributes decision-making — including decisions about how to allocate the EF's treasury — to third-party ecosystem actors wherever possible, reflecting a belief that decentralized funding is important for the ecosystem's health.

## Key points

- The EF explicitly rejects the tech company or normal non-profit frame — it is a new kind of institution built to support a decentralized protocol without controlling it
- "Highly aligned, loosely coupled" describes the internal team structure: shared vision, independent execution
- The Infinite Garden concept positions Ethereum as something tended rather than owned — the EF is a gardener, not an owner
- 2021 financial summary documents EF grants to protocol development, community building, and ecosystem research
- Aya Miyaguchi's letter acknowledges the difficulty of the EF's role and invites community feedback (questions@ethereum.org)
- More decentralized funding from outside the EF is explicitly a goal, not a threat to the EF's relevance
