## Summary

Patented Futures is a design research project by MODEM that takes patent filings from major tech corporations and reimagines them as rendered product designs. The premise: patents are forward-looking documents that describe technologies companies plan to build or want to control, but the legal language makes them unreadable as visions of the future. By translating them into designed objects, the project makes the implied corporate roadmap legible and critically examinable.

The focus is wearable technology — an area where companies like Apple, Google, Meta, and Microsoft have filed thousands of patents for glasses, haptic devices, neural interfaces, and body-mounted sensors. The rendered designs make strange things visible: what it would actually mean to wear these devices, what surveillance or behavioral modification they imply, what body-relationship they assume. Patent drawings are technically required to be enabling but are often deliberately abstract; MODEM's design renderings are deliberately concrete.

This is design fiction as a critical practice — using the aesthetics and materials of product design to make speculative technology real enough to critique. By taking the spec seriously and designing the actual object, the project reveals implications that patents obscure through abstraction. Similar work by Near Future Laboratory and Superflux has explored how futures-facing design can function as critical commentary on technology trajectories.
