## Summary

[Future Funding List](/notes/future-funding-list/) is a curated directory of alternative startup funding sources — everything beyond the standard venture capital → Series A path. The premise: most founders know about Y Combinator and Sequoia, but the funding landscape is much broader: non-dilutive government grants, revenue-based financing, equity crowdfunding via Reg CF, accelerators with specific domain focus, and angel networks that operate outside major tech hubs.

The resources are organized by type: grants (NSF SBIR, ARPA-E, USDA programs for relevant sectors), revenue-based financing providers (Clearco, Capchase, Lighter Capital), equity crowdfunding platforms (Republic, Wefunder, StartEngine), accelerators beyond YC/Techstars, and angels who publish their investment thesis. The diversity matters because different funding sources make sense at different company stages and for different business models.

Non-dilutive funding is particularly useful for capital-efficient companies: a SaaS business with predictable MRR can use revenue-based financing to fund growth without giving up equity. Government grants can fund R&D for deep-tech companies where VC wouldn't touch the risk profile. These funding forms were underutilized in 2022 partly because founders defaulted to the VC path without exploring alternatives.

## Key points

- Aggregates grants, RBF, crowdfunding, and accelerators alongside traditional VC — covering the full funding spectrum
- SBIR/STTR grants from NSF, NIH, DARPA: major non-dilutive funding for deep-tech and life sciences startups
- Revenue-based financing suits SaaS and e-commerce companies with predictable cash flows
- Equity crowdfunding under Reg CF allows raising from non-accredited investors — useful for consumer brands with large fan bases
- Practical complement to AngelList and Crunchbase for founders doing funding research
