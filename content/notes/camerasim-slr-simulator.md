## Summary

CameraSim by David Cshed is a browser-based simulator that replicates the behavior of a DSLR camera. You adjust aperture (f-stop), shutter speed, ISO, focal length, and subject distance, and see a simulated photograph update in real time. The simulation shows the effect of each parameter on exposure (brightness), depth of field (what's in focus), and motion blur.

The value is pedagogical: the relationship between aperture, shutter speed, and ISO — the exposure triangle — is counterintuitive until you've seen it in action. A fast shutter speed freezes motion but requires a wider aperture or higher ISO to compensate. A narrow aperture (high f-number) gives deep depth of field but lets in less light. The simulator makes these tradeoffs visceral in a way that reading about them doesn't.

CameraSim was a popular learning tool in the early 2010s when DSLR cameras were becoming affordable for enthusiasts (Canon's Rebel series, Nikon D3100 range) and many new owners wanted to move beyond auto mode without wasting a day's shooting practice. The simulator let you build the intuition safely.

The limitations of the simulation are obvious — it can't replicate the feel of holding a camera, the split-second decisions of live shooting, or the variation across different lenses and sensors. But for learning the vocabulary and causal relationships between settings, it's effective.

## Key points

- Interactive exposure triangle simulation: aperture, shutter speed, ISO adjustments with instant visual feedback.
- Shows depth of field and motion blur effects alongside exposure — the full range of tradeoffs.
- Pedagogical target: DSLR newcomers wanting to understand manual/priority modes without needing a live shoot.
- Built in Flash (2012 era), later ported to HTML5.
- Subject distance and focal length simulated — shows compression effects at telephoto vs. wide angles.
