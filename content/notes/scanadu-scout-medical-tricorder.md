## Summary

Scanadu Scout launched on Indiegogo in 2013 as a handheld device that measures a full panel of vital signs — heart rate, blood pressure, SpO2 (blood oxygen), temperature, and ECG — by touching the forehead for 10 seconds. The Star Trek tricorder was the explicit design inspiration, and the campaign raised $1.66 million (664% of goal), one of the largest health-tech crowdfunding campaigns at the time.

The device was part of the wave of quantified self hardware enabled by miniaturized sensors and smartphone connectivity. Unlike dedicated medical devices, Scanadu Scout was designed for continuous self-monitoring by health-conscious consumers — not clinical diagnosis. The data synced to an iOS app where users could track trends over time.

Scanadu's regulatory path was the hard part. The FDA required clinical trials and 510(k) clearance for a device making medical measurements, and the company navigated this with a research-use-only positioning during the crowdfunding phase. Scanadu eventually shut down in 2017 after failing to receive FDA clearance for the Scout as a consumer device — a cautionary tale about the gap between a compelling hardware demo and a cleared medical product.

## Key points

- Measures: heart rate, ECG, SpO2, temperature, HRV via single forehead touch in ~10 seconds
- Crowdfunding milestone: $1.66M raised on Indiegogo in 2013 — validated consumer demand for personal health monitoring
- Regulatory challenge: FDA 510(k) clearance required for medical device claims; Scanadu used research use only positioning
- Shutdown 2017: couldn't achieve FDA clearance for consumer medical use while maintaining clinical accuracy
- Preceded the mainstream success of Apple Watch health features (ECG in 2018, SpO2 in 2020)
- The tricorder concept: Star Trek's diagnostic device as a 50-year design aspiration finally achievable with modern sensors
