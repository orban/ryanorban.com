## Summary

[Revenue Watcher](/notes/revenue-watcher/) is a benchmarking resource for SaaS and software company revenue growth — tracking how companies' ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) growth rates compare across different stages and time periods. The core value: knowing whether your company is growing at the median, top quartile, or bottom quartile for its ARR range helps contextualize fundraising conversations and internal planning.

Growth benchmarking for private software companies is genuinely difficult. Public company data (from SEC filings) is well-analyzed, but private company data is sparse and often self-reported. Resources like T2D3 (triple-triple-double-double-double growth) give rough heuristics, but they're aspirational metrics from the best-performing companies. [Revenue Watcher](/notes/revenue-watcher/) aims to show the actual distribution across a broader set of companies.

This kind of reference is particularly useful when raising a Series A or Series B: investors will benchmark you against cohorts of companies that raised at similar stages and revenue levels. Knowing that $2M ARR growing at 150% YoY puts you in the top quartile for your cohort — vs just knowing your own number — changes how you frame the narrative. The benchmarks also shift with market conditions: growth rates that were table stakes in 2021 were considered strong in 2023.

## Key points

- Benchmarks SaaS ARR growth rates by stage — useful for understanding relative performance vs. cohort
- Top quartile at seed/Series A typically requires 150-200%+ YoY growth; median much lower
- Growth rates compressed post-2021: companies that looked "average" in 2021 looked strong in 2023 on the same metrics
- Complements Bessemer's State of the Cloud and Openview SaaS Benchmarks as reference materials for fundraising prep
- Useful for burn multiple and Rule of 40 context alongside raw growth figures
