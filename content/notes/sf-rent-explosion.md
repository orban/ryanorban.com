## Summary

Priceonomics published this data-driven analysis of San Francisco rent prices in July 2013, during the peak of the first major tech-driven housing crisis. Ryan's bookmark note called out SoMa (South of Market) as the most expensive neighborhood for a 1-bedroom — notable because SoMa had historically been an industrial district, and its transformation into a tech hub had driven up rents faster than traditional desirable neighborhoods like Pacific Heights or the Marina.

The piece landed during a period of active public debate about housing affordability in San Francisco. The tech industry's second wave — post-2008 recovery, with companies like Twitter, Yelp, Airbnb, and Dropbox based in SoMa — was visibly displacing existing residents and businesses. Priceonomics made the data argument concrete: nominal rents for specific unit types by neighborhood, showing both the absolute costs and the rate of increase.

The bookmark is personal context: Ryan was likely living or working in San Francisco at the time, paying these rents directly. SoMa being the most expensive 1BR market made sense given Nutanix's San Jose headquarters, but the broader SF market was the reference point for tech compensation and cost-of-living calculations.

## Key points

- SoMa (South of Market): highest 1BR rents in SF in 2013, driven by proximity to Twitter, Yelp, Airbnb, and other tech employers.
- Priceonomics's data journalism approach: Craigslist rental data scraped and analyzed to produce neighborhood-level price maps.
- The 2013 SF housing crisis foreshadowed the larger affordability crisis that worsened through 2015-2019 and became a major political issue.
- San Francisco's supply constraints (zoning, slow permitting, anti-density politics) meant demand from tech employment growth had nowhere to go but prices.
