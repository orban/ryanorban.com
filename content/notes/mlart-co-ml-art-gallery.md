## Summary

mlart.co is a curated gallery of machine learning art projects — works created using neural networks, GANs, style transfer, and other ML-based techniques by artists and researchers working at the intersection of art and machine learning. The site functions as a discovery layer for this community, surfacing projects that demonstrate creative and aesthetic applications of ML beyond purely functional uses.

The ML art field was expanding rapidly in 2020: StyleGAN had produced photorealistic faces and other imagery, DALL-E was months away from its first public demo, and DeepDream had already established neural networks as creative tools. Galleries like mlart.co served to collect and showcase the range of aesthetic possibilities that researchers and artists were exploring.

Projects typically include: GANs trained on specific visual corpora, style transfer applied to video or interactive experiences, text-to-image experiments, music generation, and data visualization using neural embeddings. The work sits in a tradition connecting to generative art (Sol LeWitt, Vera Molnár) and computational creativity research.

## Key points

- ML art accelerated dramatically from 2019-2022: StyleGAN → CLIP → DALL-E → Stable Diffusion each opened new creative possibilities in sequence.
- Style transfer was the first widely accessible creative ML technique (fast-style-transfer, Prisma app) — made ML art accessible to non-researchers.
- GANs are the dominant technique in this gallery era (2020), though diffusion models have since eclipsed them for generative image quality.
- The ethical questions around ML art — training data sources, artist compensation, authenticity — were beginning to emerge around this time.
- Resources like Runway ML and Artbreeder democratized access to these techniques for non-programmers.
