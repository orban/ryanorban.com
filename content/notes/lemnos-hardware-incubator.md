## Summary

Lemnos Labs (later just Lemnos) was one of the first hardware startup incubators in Silicon Valley, profiled by Businessweek in late 2012. Founded by Jeremy Conrad and Helen Zelman, it focused exclusively on companies building physical products — hardware, robotics, sensors, and devices — in a VC environment that had historically preferred software for its lighter capital requirements and faster iteration.

The 2012 timing was meaningful. Kickstarter had proven that hardware products could attract pre-sales and community funding (Pebble raised $10M on Kickstarter in 2012; Ouya raised $8.5M). Arduino and Raspberry Pi had lowered the barrier to prototyping. Manufacturing in Shenzhen had become more accessible to small teams through firms like Dragon Innovation. The hardware is hard objection — it requires inventory, supply chains, and longer time-to-market than software — was becoming more manageable.

Lemnos provided not just funding but the mentorship and physical resources specific to hardware: manufacturing relationships, supply chain guidance, and a community of engineers who'd shipped physical products. Portfolio companies included robotics, sensor, and connected device startups. The incubator model for hardware predated broader recognition of the space; by 2015, HAX (in Shenzhen), Bolt, and others had followed with similar models.

## Key points

- Lemnos Labs (2012): one of the first hardware-focused startup incubators in Silicon Valley
- Hardware startup renaissance drivers: Kickstarter pre-sales, Arduino/Raspberry Pi prototyping, accessible Shenzhen manufacturing
- "Hardware is hard" objection being overcome by: componentization, reference designs, faster prototyping tools
- Lemnos differentiators: physical resources, manufacturing mentorship, supply chain expertise — not just money
- The model validated: HAX, Bolt, Highway1 all followed as hardware-focused investors proliferated after 2013
