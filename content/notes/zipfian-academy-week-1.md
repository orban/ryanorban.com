## Summary

This blog post by a Zipfian Academy student documents the first week of the data science bootcamp curriculum, with the title flagging that SQL window functions appeared on day 3 — before most students were comfortable with basic SQL. It's a primary source for the Zipfian Academy pedagogical philosophy: move fast, trust students to catch up, throw advanced material early to anchor later learning.

Zipfian Academy was a 12-week intensive data science bootcamp in San Francisco, co-founded by Jonathan Dinu and Katie Kent. It was one of the first data science bootcamps — predating the wave of competitors that followed and occupying a moment when the data scientist job title was new enough that there was no established training path. The curriculum compressed what a statistics or CS grad program might teach over two years into three months of full-time work.

SQL window functions (OVER, PARTITION BY, ROW_NUMBER, LAG, LEAD, RANK) are powerful but non-obvious — they let you compute aggregations within groups without collapsing rows, and they're essential for cohort analysis, session attribution, and running totals. Teaching them in week 1 signals the curriculum's priorities: you're going to work with data immediately, and that requires SQL fluency first.

## Key points

- Zipfian Academy used high-velocity exposure: introducing advanced material early (window functions day 3) to create a reference frame even before full comprehension.
- SQL window functions are the workhorse of analytical SQL — OVER/PARTITION BY enables cohort analysis, funnel metrics, and time-series calculations that basic GROUP BY can't express.
- The blogging culture at Zipfian Academy was intentional: students wrote publicly about their learning, which served as marketing, accountability, and knowledge sharing.
- Week 1 curriculum in 2014: SQL fundamentals → advanced SQL (window functions, CTEs) → Python for data manipulation — moving immediately to working with real data.
- Historical artifact: documents the early days of formal data science education before the field standardized on curricula.
