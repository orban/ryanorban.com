## Summary

Joel Gascoigne, co-founder of Buffer, wrote extensively about habits, productivity, and self-improvement as part of Buffer's early radical transparency blogging strategy. This piece focuses on what separates a morning routine that sticks from one that doesn't — specifically, two factors that most productivity advice ignores.

The typical morning routine advice focuses on *what* to do: exercise, journaling, meditation, reading. Gascoigne's insight is about the *design* of the routine itself: making individual elements genuinely enjoyable (not just virtuous-feeling) and starting with something so small it requires no willpower to begin. A five-minute walk beats an ambitious hour of exercise you'll skip.

This connects to Charles Duhigg's habit loop framework (cue → routine → reward) and BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits work, which would be published in book form in 2019 but whose ideas were circulating in this form in 2012. Gascoigne's posts from this period were widely read because Buffer published everything openly — their own habits, struggles, revenues — and it built genuine trust with an early tech audience.

## Key points

- Making routines enjoyable (not just useful) is a prerequisite for sustainability — willpower runs out
- Start with minimum viable versions of each habit to reduce the activation energy required
- Joel Gascoigne used Buffer's blog as a transparency platform — personal habit content built founder audience
- Connects to BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits (starting small) and habit loop frameworks
- The two overlooked aspects framing: enjoy the process + start tiny, both underweighted in standard productivity advice
