## Summary

Jack West's blog documented his experience as a high school teacher integrating educational technology (EdTech) into classroom practice. The blog was saved alongside a retweet about a child finding his community at Maker Faire, suggesting interest in the intersection of maker culture and formal education — a live question in 2012 about whether the hands-on, project-based learning ethos of the maker movement could translate into school curricula.

In 2012, the EdTech space was buzzing. Khan Academy had gone viral, Coursera and Udacity had just launched MOOCs, and iPads were entering classrooms through 1:1 device initiatives. The debate was less "should technology be in schools and more which technologies, and for what purposes." Teacher-practitioners like West provided a valuable counterweight to the techno-optimist claims coming from product companies and foundations — someone actually trying to make things work in a real classroom had different and often more grounded views.

The teacher-as-blogger format of this era (2010-2014) was a distinct phenomenon: educators who were early adopters using personal blogs as professional development and peer communication channels before dedicated platforms like Teachers Pay Teachers or the current Twitter/X edtech discourse community existed.

## Key points

- Teacher-practitioner perspective on EdTech: more grounded than vendor claims, more granular than policy research.
- 2012 EdTech landscape: Khan Academy viral success, MOOCs launching, iPad classroom rollouts proliferating.
- Tension between maker/project-based learning ethos and standardized testing pressures in formal education.
- EdTech teacher blogs (2010-2015) formed an informal professional learning network before today's platforms.
- Related: ISTE (International Society for Technology in Education) was the main institutional voice for this community.
