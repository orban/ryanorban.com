## Summary

This Nature News article from January 2014 is an early mainstream science piece on machine learning and deep learning, written as the field was accelerating out of academic obscurity. It covers the core idea — systems that learn representations from data rather than following hand-coded rules — and profiles the researchers behind the wave: Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun, and Yoshua Bengio, who later shared the Turing Award for their contributions to deep neural networks.

The article appeared just after Google acquired DeepMind and Google Brain was publishing results on large-scale neural networks. It represents the moment deep learning shifted from "something interesting happening in ML research to something Nature thinks the broader scientific community should know about."

## Key points

- Profiles the ImageNet moment — the 2012 AlexNet win that sparked the deep learning revival and demonstrated that convolutional neural networks at scale beat everything else on visual recognition
- Covers the Hinton, LeCun, Bengio research lineage — the group that kept neural networks alive through the 1990s–2000s winter
- Documents Google, Facebook AI Research, and Microsoft Research racing to hire deep learning researchers and apply the results commercially
- Explains representation learning in accessible terms: layers learn increasingly abstract features, from edges to shapes to objects
- Written at the exact inflection point before the field became crowded — a useful historical snapshot of what cutting edge looked like in early 2014
