## Summary

Jon Stokes (formerly of Ars Technica) launched this Substack series in September 2022 to explain machine learning and generative AI to non-technical readers at exactly the moment when Stable Diffusion and GPT-3 were entering mainstream awareness. Part 1 establishes the conceptual foundation: what machine learning actually is, how it differs from traditional programming, and why statistical learning from data produces systems that can do surprising things.

The framing centers on the shift from rules to examples: classical programming requires humans to specify rules explicitly (if X then Y). Machine learning inverts this — you provide many examples, and the system infers the rules. This is why ML can do things like image recognition that are trivially easy for humans to do but nearly impossible to specify as explicit rules. The same statistical learning approach, scaled up and applied to text and images, produces large language models and diffusion models.

Stokes' contribution in 2022 was clarity aimed at policy-minded, technically curious but not technically expert readers — journalists, VCs, regulators, people who needed to understand what was happening without reading papers. His series sat between the deeply technical (arXiv papers, ML blogs) and superficial (mainstream press) — similar to what Ben Thompson does for business strategy. The series continued through tokenization, embeddings, diffusion, and the emerging generative AI landscape.

## Key points

- Conceptual on-ramp to machine learning: statistical learning from examples vs. explicit rule programming.
