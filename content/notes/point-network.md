## Summary

[Point Network](/notes/point-network/) positioned itself as a more radical decentralization than typical web3 projects. The critique embedded in its pitch was accurate: most web3 applications in 2022 still used centralized infrastructure — AWS-hosted frontends, Ethereum Name Service for domain resolution, IPFS pinning services managed by central operators. You could have a fully on-chain smart contract with a frontend served from a company's cloud. Point Network aimed to eliminate this by decentralizing DNS, storage, and content delivery simultaneously.

The architecture replaced standard internet components: decentralized domain registration (like ENS but as the entire naming layer), Arweave-style permanent decentralized storage, and a purpose-built browser that could navigate this alternative infrastructure natively. The idea was that if you browsed the web through Point's browser and DNS, you'd interact with genuinely decentralized applications end to end. This required more than a smart contract layer — it required an alternative network stack.

The project launched mainnet and a web3 browser in September 2022. But the FTX collapse in November 2022 devastated crypto funding markets and consumer appetite for novel infrastructure. [Point Network](/notes/point-network/) struggled to find adoption — decentralized DNS requires significant ecosystem buy-in to be useful — and ultimately failed to achieve product-market fit. By 2024, its assets were acquired by Arweave/AO entities. It's an instructive case study in the difference between technically coherent decentralization and what users will actually adopt.

## Key points

- Aimed to decentralize DNS, storage, and content delivery — the full internet stack, not just contracts
- Critique: most "web3" projects still use centralized domains, hosting, and storage
- Required its own browser to navigate the alternative network infrastructure
- Mainnet launched September 2022; failed to gain adoption post-FTX collapse
- Assets acquired by Arweave/AO entities by 2024
- Lesson: technically coherent decentralization doesn't guarantee adoption if it requires ecosystem coordination
