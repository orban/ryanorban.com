## Summary

Full Stack Deep Learning (FSDL) is a free course originally taught at UC Berkeley that addresses a specific gap: most deep learning education ends at model training, leaving practitioners without the skills to actually deploy and maintain systems. FSDL covers the full pipeline — from problem setup and data management through production deployment and monitoring.

The curriculum spans CNNs and RNNs for the fundamentals, then moves into transformers and transfer learning, and devotes the second half to engineering practice: infrastructure setup, debugging neural networks, data labeling, ML ethics, testing, explainability, CI/CD, and model monitoring. There's also a week on ML team structures and roles — the organizational side of applied ML that pure ML courses ignore.

What makes FSDL distinctive is that it targets the intersection. Researchers who know how to train models but not how to ship them will get the engineering half. Engineers who know how to build systems but not how to think about ML will get the fundamentals. The final weeks involve capstone projects. The 2021 edition was run at UC Berkeley and offered both live and self-paced tracks.

## Key points

- Bridges the gap between ML research and production ML — both model training fundamentals and engineering practice.
- Covers data labeling, experiment tracking, ML testing, model monitoring, and CI/CD for ML — areas most ML courses omit.
- Dedicated week on ML ethics and responsible AI development alongside technical content.
- Discusses ML team structures — staffing, role definitions, and organizational approaches to applied ML.
- Free and self-paced; recorded lectures available online. Taught by practitioners at companies like Weights & Biases and Andrej Karpathy.
- Companion to the MLOps landscape: teaches the practices that tools like MLflow, DVC, and Weights & Biases implement.
