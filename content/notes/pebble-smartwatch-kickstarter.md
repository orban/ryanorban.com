## Summary

In April 2012, Pebble became the most-funded project in Kickstarter history, raising over $3 million (eventually reaching $10M+) for a smartwatch that connected to iPhone and Android phones. The Wired Q&A with co-founder Eric Migicovsky captures the moment when crowdfunding proved it could bootstrap an entire hardware product category. Migicovsky's quote — We're basically leveling up — reflects the genuine surprise of a small team suddenly managing tens of thousands of pre-orders.

Pebble was notable for several reasons: it was one of the first smartwatches with real consumer traction, it used an e-paper display for week-long battery life (vs. the day-long life that would plague later smartwatches), and it had a developer SDK that built a small app ecosystem before the product shipped. The Kickstarter success proved a market existed that Apple and Samsung hadn't yet addressed — a proof-of-concept that directly influenced both companies' subsequent smartwatch efforts.

The leveling up framing reflects a pattern in hardware crowdfunding: campaigns succeed beyond any reasonable operational plan, and the founders suddenly have to become a real company overnight. Supply chain, manufacturing scale, customer support — none of which Kickstarter had prepared them for.

## Key points

- Pebble raised $10M+ from over 85,000 backers — the most successful Kickstarter project to that date.
- E-paper display was the key hardware differentiator: week-long battery life vs. one-day for OLED-based competitors.
- The campaign proved consumer demand for smartwatches years before Apple Watch launched in 2015.
- Crowdfunding as product validation: Pebble's success influenced the investment thesis of hardware startups for years.
- Pebble was eventually acquired by Fitbit in 2016 for ~$23M — a cautionary tale about the gap between Kickstarter success and durable business.
