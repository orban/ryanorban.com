## Summary

Wonkblog (Washington Post's data-driven policy blog) published this piece in April 2012 during the slow recovery from the 2008 financial crisis. The headline finding — that hot sauce was among the fastest-growing industries — was the hook, but the underlying data painted a broader picture of where the US economy was expanding. The piece drew on IBISWorld industry data.

The 2012 context matters: overall GDP growth was sluggish (about 2-3%), unemployment was still elevated, and most traditional manufacturing was flat. The fastest-growing categories tended to be service industries, specialty food products, and sectors benefiting from demographic shifts. Hot sauce's growth was partly a cultural story: the Sriracha moment (Huy Fong Foods' Sriracha had become mainstream) reflected both the growth of Hispanic and Asian food culture in America and a broader trend toward bolder flavors that food manufacturers were tracking.

The piece is an artifact of Wonkblog's early period when it was pioneering data journalism on wonky economic topics for a general audience — the approach that would later influence FiveThirtyEight, Vox, and similar outlets.

## Key points

- Specialty food categories (hot sauce, ethnic condiments) were growing partly on demographic shifts — Hispanic and Asian-American populations growing faster than average.
- The post-2008 financial crisis recovery was uneven: service sectors and niche consumer products outgrew traditional manufacturing.
- IBISWorld data as a source for fastest growing rankings — the methodology was annualized revenue growth, which favors small-base industries.
- Wonkblog (Ezra Klein's operation) was pioneering the explain-economics-to-general-readers model in 2012.
- The hot sauce economy framing became a minor meme — showing up in business media as shorthand for niche-market wins in a slow-growth macro environment.
