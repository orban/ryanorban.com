## Summary

Canonical launched the Ubuntu Edge Indiegogo campaign in July 2013, attempting to crowdfund a high-specification smartphone that doubled as a desktop computer — plug it into a monitor and keyboard and you got a full Ubuntu Linux desktop environment. The device spec was ambitious: 4GB RAM, 128GB storage, a sapphire crystal display, and a silicon-anode battery, all at a time when flagship Android phones were shipping with 2GB RAM.

The campaign set a $32 million funding target — extraordinarily ambitious for crowdfunding. It offered early backers a device for $600, then raised the price to $830 for later backers, positioning it as a premium developer and power-user device rather than a mass-market consumer product. At peak, the campaign was raising ~$1M/day and briefly became the highest-funded Indiegogo campaign in history.

This bookmark was saved just days into the campaign when funding levels were tracked closely by the tech press. The coverage captured the genuine excitement: a vertically integrated Linux phone from the company that shipped Ubuntu was a real alternative to the iOS/Android duopoly. The campaign ultimately raised $12.8M — a record for crowdfunding at the time, but less than half the goal, so all backers were refunded.

## Key points

- Ubuntu Edge design: smartphone + desktop convergence — the same hardware ran Android apps and full Ubuntu, connected to external displays via MHL or SlimPort.
- $32M Indiegogo goal: designed to be achievable only at sufficient scale to make manufacturing viable — Canonical structured it explicitly as pre-orders, not donations.
- Raised $12.8M: a record-breaking campaign that still failed its target — a notable data point about the limits of crowdfunding for hardware projects requiring manufacturing scale.
- Canonical's phone strategy: the Ubuntu Edge was the premium tier; Ubuntu Touch was the software platform targeting mid-range devices. Both were eventually discontinued.
