## Summary

David Rees — political cartoonist and author — launched a genuine artisanal pencil sharpening service: he would sharpen your pencil by hand using specialized tools (hand-cranked sharpeners, sandpaper pads, leather strops) and return it with a certificate of authenticity, wood shavings, and eraser detritus preserved. The price: $15 per pencil. A documentary was later made. He published a book, *How to Sharpen Pencils*.

The appeal was genuinely ambiguous: it was satirical, but it was also real. Rees wasn't faking the sharpening — he developed actual expertise in the craft and could distinguish between different pencil grades, graphite compositions, and optimal tip geometries. The project occupied that productive space where something is simultaneously a joke and a serious pursuit.

It arrived at the peak of the artisanal movement — the cultural moment when artisanal everything (bread, pickles, cocktails, coffee) had become both a genuine market phenomenon and a target of satire. Portlandia was skewering the same territory on TV. The maker movement and farm-to-table ethos were expanding what counted as worth making carefully and by hand.

The deeper question Rees was asking: what does it mean to do something with full attention and craft, when the object is so quotidian that automation seems obviously correct? The answer his project gave: attention itself is the product. A sharpened pencil returned with documentation of its sharpening is not a better pencil — but it's an artifact of an intentional act.

## Key points

- David Rees: political cartoonist who turned pencil sharpening into a genuine craft practice and business.
- Service was real: $15/pencil, hand-sharpened, with certificate, wood shavings, and eraser dust returned.
- Arrived at the peak of artisanal culture satire — simultaneously mocking and embodying the movement.
- Published *How to Sharpen Pencils* (2012) — a deadpan instructional that became a cult object.
- Conceptual point: attention and intentionality are themselves a product, separable from the physical output.
