## Summary

Alex Ghiculescu (co-founder of Tanda, an Australian workforce management SaaS) reflects on 11 years of product decisions in this Substack post. Tanda is a long-running profitable B2B SaaS company serving SMBs in industries like hospitality and retail with shift scheduling, time tracking, and compliance tools — a context that shapes every lesson in the post.

The honest retrospective covers what worked and what the author wishes they'd done differently across product strategy, feature prioritization, customer segmentation, and competitive positioning. Key themes: early product decisions tend to be path-dependent in ways you don't realize until years later; building for a specific customer segment is more defensible than building for everyone; the temptation to add features to chase enterprise deals often dilutes the product for the customers who actually love it.

The post is valuable not for novel frameworks but for specificity — Ghiculescu is talking about a real product with real tradeoffs across a long timeframe. Most SaaS strategy writing is abstract or based on short time horizons. An 11-year retrospective on a bootstrapped profitable product gives different advice than the "raise a big round and grow fast" playbook. It pairs well with other long-form SaaS founder retrospectives like Basecamp/DHH's writing and the Buffer transparency posts.

## Key points

- Product decisions are path-dependent: early choices constrain future options more than expected.
- Customer segment clarity matters more than broad feature coverage.
- Adding features for enterprise deals can dilute the product for SMB customers who are your base.
- Bootstrapped profitable SaaS perspective — different lessons than VC-backed growth companies.
- 11 years of Tanda (workforce management) in hospitality/retail markets.
