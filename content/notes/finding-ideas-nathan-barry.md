## Summary

Nathan Barry (founder of ConvertKit, now Kit) wrote this post in early 2013 when he was building his career as a designer who shipped products independently. The guide addresses the problem of project idea generation for people who want to build something but don't know where to start.

Barry's core advice: the best ideas come from problems you personally experience, intersections of skills you already have, and communities you already participate in. Generic what should I build? brainstorming rarely produces good ideas; specific observation ("this tool I use every day is broken in this specific way") reliably does. The second major point: information products (ebooks, courses) were underused by designers and developers who could leverage their expertise for immediate revenue, funding the development of more ambitious products.

Barry's own trajectory illustrated the advice: he wrote design books (Designing Web Applications, Authority), used the revenue to build ConvertKit (email marketing for creators), and grew ConvertKit to $30M+ ARR. The "small products as stepping stones" approach he advocated in 2013 was his actual path.

## Key points

- Problem-first ideation: solve a problem you personally have — the frustration provides both the insight and the motivation to finish
- SKILL intersection: where two or more skills you have overlap is often where you can create value others can't easily replicate
- Information products as starter projects: ebooks, courses, screencasts — lower barrier to ship, immediate revenue test, builds an audience for future products
- Shipping discipline: the best ideas are worthless without execution; shipping small projects fast builds the habit and judgment to execute bigger ones
- Nathan Barry's path: design books → ConvertKit (email for creators) → the advice was autobiographical
