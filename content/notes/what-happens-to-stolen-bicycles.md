## Summary

Priceonomics used Craigslist data and field research (including a sting operation where they bought back a stolen bike) to trace what happens to bicycles after they're stolen. The finding was both obvious and underappreciated: bike theft is nearly a risk-free crime because the bikes are immediately sold through the informal market — particularly Craigslist — at deep discounts. The resale market is liquid, prices are well-known, and enforcement is minimal.

The piece is a good example of data journalism using publicly available market data to investigate something that generates folklore but few facts. By analyzing Craigslist bike listings — particularly the suspiciously cheap ones — Priceonomics could map the informal economy of stolen bike resale. The price distribution had a clear bimodal structure: a cluster of listings at near-retail prices (legitimate sellers) and a cluster at 20–30% of retail (stolen goods being moved quickly). The stolen-goods cluster correlated with certain listing characteristics: few or no photos, seller won't meet at their home address, vague provenance.

The structural problem is asymmetric incentives: the marginal cost of stealing a bike (minimal effort, minimal risk) versus the marginal benefit (quick $100–$200 cash) makes it economically rational for a certain class of opportunistic theft. The solution — better registration, more aggressive Craigslist enforcement, GPS tracking — all exist but none have made a significant dent, because the informal market was fast and the formal reporting/recovery system was slow and unmotivated.

## Key points

- Bike theft is nearly risk-free: bikes move through Craigslist informal market within hours at 20–30% of retail value
- Craigslist data as crime signal: cheap listings without photos, vague provenance, and non-home-address meetups correlate with stolen goods
- Asymmetric incentives: low effort + low risk + quick cash = rational for opportunistic thieves; high effort + low probability of recovery = irrational for victims to report
- Registration systems (Bike Index, police registration) exist but coordination failures mean they're rarely used and rarely checked
- Priceonomics pioneered the "use market data to answer mundane questions rigorously" format that later became a genre
