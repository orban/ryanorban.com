## Summary

Julia Evans published this list of questions she asks companies during technical interviews. The questions probe engineering culture, code quality, on-call practices, and whether the team actually learns from incidents. What makes the post useful is that Evans asks specific, concrete questions that reveal real information — not generic what's your culture like softballs, but questions like "what does your monitoring look like? and how do you handle technical debt?"

The implicit argument is that candidates should interview companies as hard as companies interview candidates. Joining a team with bad on-call hygiene or no post-mortems affects your daily life and growth as an engineer, so gathering that signal upfront is worth the awkwardness of asking directly.

## Key points

- Questions target concrete engineering signals: deployment frequency, monitoring depth, incident retrospectives, code review culture
- "How do you handle technical debt?" and "What do you wish you'd done differently?" often reveal more than the official culture pitch
- Asking about on-call load and escalation policies is essential — unexpected 3am pages are a major quality-of-life variable
- Julia Evans is known for making engineering topics approachable; this post reflects her direct, practical style
- The questions were influential — widely shared among engineers evaluating companies, especially at early-career stages
