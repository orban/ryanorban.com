## Summary

[Web3 Index](/notes/web3-index/) tracks real demand-side metrics for decentralized protocols — specifically fee revenue generated by each network as a measure of genuine economic activity. The premise: most crypto analytics in 2022 obsessed over TVL (total value locked) and token price, which are easily gamed with liquidity mining incentives. Fee revenue is harder to fake because it requires real users paying for real services.

The metrics tracked: protocol revenue (fees paid to protocol treasury), supply-side revenue (fees distributed to network participants like LPs, validators, or node operators), and P/F ratios (price-to-fees, a rough valuation multiple analogous to P/E in equities). Seeing that a protocol generates $2M/month in fees from real usage tells you more about its moat than knowing it has $500M TVL built on 40% APY incentives.

The site covered a range of protocols: Ethereum (the dominant fee-generating network), Uniswap (consistent DEX fee revenue), The Graph (indexing fee revenue), Livepeer (video transcoding), and various others. In the 2021-2022 period, fee revenue was one of the few metrics that separated protocols with genuine product-market fit from those running on speculation.

## Key points

- Tracks protocol revenue and supply-side revenue as metrics of genuine network demand, not speculation
- P/F ratio (price-to-fees) gives a rough valuation multiple for comparing protocol value relative to cash flow
- Contrast with TVL: total value locked is easily inflated by liquidity mining; fees require genuine user activity
- Covers both Ethereum L1 and major DeFi protocols, compute networks, and infrastructure services
- Context: during the 2022 bear market, protocols with real fee revenue (Ethereum, Uniswap) fared far better than yield-farming plays
