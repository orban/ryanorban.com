## Summary

The Differentiable Self-Organizing Systems workshop (hosted at selforglive.GitHub.io) is the academic hub for the research program centered on gradient-based approaches to self-organization — systems that develop structure through local interactions rather than top-down design. The work is primarily associated with Alexander Mordvintsev and collaborators at Google Brain, though the workshop draws broader participation from researchers interested in morphogenesis, swarm systems, and emergent complexity.

The central question the workshop addresses: can we learn the local rules that produce desired global behaviors, using gradient-based optimization? This is distinct from both hand-designing rules (classical cellular automata like Conway's Game of Life) and from top-down generative models. The Neural Cellular Automata framework is the flagship example — but the scope extends to any system where local agent behavior can be differentiated with respect to global outcomes.

The research thread connects to deep questions in biology and physics: how does a genome encode a body plan? How do slime molds find food? How do flocking birds maintain formation without a leader? The differentiable approach offers a computational tool for studying these phenomena — you can learn rules that produce observed biological patterns, then inspect those rules to understand the underlying mechanism. The practical applications extend to procedural generation, texture synthesis, morphogenetic engineering, and robust distributed systems that self-repair.

## Key points

- Workshop for the differentiable / gradient-based self-organization research thread — learning local rules from global objectives.
- Flagship work: Neural Cellular Automata series (Growing Neural Cellular Automata, [Self-Organising Textures](/notes/self-organising-textures/), Learning to See).
- Connects formal computation (CA, reaction-diffusion) to machine learning via backpropagation.
- Biological motivation: morphogenesis, Turing patterns, swarm behavior, developmental biology.
- Research from Google Brain / Mordvintsev group — see the related Distill publications for interactive explanations.
- Broader implication: differentiable self-organization as a design paradigm for robust distributed systems.
