## Summary

These slides from the University of Cambridge Computer Laboratory (author `av308` — likely Andreas Vlachos or another Cambridge NLP/ML researcher) cover the intersection of machine learning and social networks — a field sometimes called social machine learning or computational social science. The 2010 timestamp (smla10 in the filename) places this at a key moment when social network analysis was being enriched by ML techniques and vice versa.

The core topics in this area: link prediction (will two nodes form a connection?), community detection (identifying clusters in network graphs), influence modeling (how information spreads), sentiment analysis on social text, and behavior prediction from network structure. The Facebook platform and early Twitter API access had given researchers access to large-scale social graph data for the first time, enabling empirical study at scale.

The Cambridge Computer Lab (CL) was a significant center for this work — it sits at the intersection of NLP, machine learning, and systems research, with strong connections to industry. Social ML as a discipline eventually matured into what's now called computational social science — interdisciplinary work spanning CS, sociology, and economics that studies human behavior through digital trace data.

## Key points

- Social machine learning: applying ML to social network data — link prediction, community detection, influence modeling
- Cambridge CL's interdisciplinary position: NLP + ML + network science intersecting
- 2010 timing: just after Twitter API and Facebook platform opened up large-scale social graph data for research
- Link prediction and community detection are the core graph ML problems that GNNs now dominate
- Connects to modern graph neural network work — same problems, very different methods
