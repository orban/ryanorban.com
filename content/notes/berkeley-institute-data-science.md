## Summary

The Berkeley Institute for Data Science (BIDS) launched on December 12, 2013, marking a milestone in the institutionalization of data science as an academic discipline. The launch event brought together researchers across departments at UC Berkeley to formalize an interdisciplinary center that would bridge statistics, computer science, domain sciences, and the social dimensions of large-scale data analysis.

Peter Norvig, then Director of Research at Google, gave a notable quote that circulated widely: "The solution to job automation is that everyone will have to become a Data Scientist." This framing — data science as the mandatory human SKILL in an automated economy — captured the moment's anxiety and optimism simultaneously. It appeared in a tweet from Zipfian Academy (one of the first data science bootcamps in San Francisco).

BIDS was one of several similar institutional efforts in 2013 (alongside NYU's CDS and others) that signaled the transformation of data science from an informal industry label into an academic category with funding, faculty lines, and institutional infrastructure.

## Key points

- BIDS launch December 12, 2013 — formal academic institutionalization of data science at UC Berkeley.
- Peter Norvig quote: "The solution to job automation is that everyone will have to become a Data Scientist" — captured the era's techno-optimism about data skills as an employment hedge.
- Interdisciplinary charter: statistics, CS, domain sciences, social science of data — the standard BIDS framing.
- Zipfian Academy (SF data science bootcamp) tweeted the Norvig quote — shows the overlap between academic institution-building and the bootcamp industry in 2013.
- Part of the same wave as NYU Center for Data Science and Moore/Sloan Data Science Environments.
- Data science was moving from job title to academic department in 2013.
