## Summary

A Greylock Partners conversation between Fei-Fei Li (Stanford Professor, creator of ImageNet, co-director of the Stanford Human-Centered AI Institute) and Mira Murati (OpenAI CTO). Recorded in late 2022 — just before ChatGPT's release — it covers the human dimensions of AI development: who benefits, who gets left behind, how responsibility is distributed, and what it means to build AI that genuinely helps people.

Fei-Fei Li's perspective emphasizes human-centered AI: technology should augment human capability and be evaluated by its effects on human flourishing, not just benchmark performance. Her work on ImageNet and later advocacy for diversity in AI research informs her view that who builds AI determines what gets built. Murati's perspective from inside OpenAI addresses the challenges of iterative deployment — releasing capabilities responsibly while maintaining enough openness to get external feedback.

The discussion lands on questions that don't have clean answers: How do you balance safety and accessibility? What does beneficial AI mean when people disagree on what's beneficial? How do large labs maintain accountability as they grow?

## Key points

- Fei-Fei Li (Stanford / Stanford HAI) and Mira Murati (OpenAI CTO) in conversation.
- Covers AI ethics, access, responsibility, and human-centered design principles.
- Fei-Fei Li's human-centered AI framework: evaluate AI by effects on human flourishing.
- Touches on diversity in AI: who builds it determines what gets built.
- Recorded pre-ChatGPT — represents the 2022 discourse before public LLM explosion.
