## Summary

Zipfian Academy — one of the first data science bootcamps, founded in San Francisco in 2013 — published this piece articulating what they considered the essential mental habits of effective data scientists. The argument was that knowing Python, SQL, and machine learning algorithms wasn't sufficient; what separated good data scientists was a particular mode of thinking: comfort with uncertainty, a bias toward empiricism over intuition, and the ability to ask precise questions before reaching for tools.

The mindset framing was important in 2013 because the field was being defined in real time. Companies were trying to figure out what to hire for; bootcamps and universities were trying to figure out what to teach. Zipfian Academy argued that teachable skills (coding, statistics, ML algorithms) were necessary but not sufficient — the harder part was intellectual disposition: treating everything as a hypothesis to be tested, knowing when not to trust your data, and being willing to say I don't know rather than producing confident-sounding wrong answers.

Zipfian Academy was notable for being early and selective — their 12-week program required significant math and coding prerequisites and focused on the analytical side more than many later bootcamps. This piece reflected their view of what made their graduates effective: not just the technical toolkit but the skeptical, probabilistic thinking behind it.

## Key points

- Hypothesis-driven thinking: formulate a question before pulling data — don't mine for patterns without a prior about what you're looking for.
- Comfort with uncertainty: data science outputs are probabilistic; good practitioners communicate confidence intervals, not just point estimates.
- Skepticism toward data: understanding data provenance, collection biases, and missing data patterns before trusting results.
- Iteration as practice: a data science project is never "done" — it cycles through framing, exploration, modeling, and evaluation repeatedly.
- Zipfian Academy context: one of the earliest serious data science bootcamps, later acquired by Galvanize — their graduates went to top SF tech companies.
- The mindset argument has aged well: the 2013 frustration that people with tools but not judgment were being hired as data scientists resurfaced repeatedly as the field matured.
