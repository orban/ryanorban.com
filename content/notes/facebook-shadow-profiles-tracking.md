## Summary

Luis Aguiar, Christian Peukert, Maximilian Schaefer, and Hannes Ullrich (CESifo Working Paper 9571, Feb 2022) quantify the scale of Facebook's cross-web tracking infrastructure using a representative sample of US internet users. The core finding: **52% of websites visited** by US internet users, accounting for **40% of browsing time**, employ Facebook's tracking technology (Like buttons, Share buttons, pixel trackers). This network of engagement buttons placed on third-party websites lets Facebook follow users across the entire web and build shadow profiles — detailed behavioral profiles of internet users, including those who don't have Facebook accounts.

The shadow profile concept is critical: because Facebook's tracking fires on any visit to any site that has embedded Facebook's engagement buttons (not just facebook.com), Facebook accumulates behavioral data on non-users. The paper finds that tracking extent on privacy-sensitive domains (medical information, financial services, adult content) is similar to the general web — meaning Facebook's data collection is largely indiscriminate across content type. Demographic differences in tracking exposure are small and largely explained by differences in browsing activity, not structural differences in which sites different groups visit.

The paper's contribution is quantitative: prior work on web tracking established that third-party trackers exist, but this paper measures the actual coverage for a representative sample. 52% of sites is an extraordinary market penetration for a single company's tracking infrastructure. Combined with GDPR questions about legal basis for processing non-user data and the opacity of shadow profile construction, the paper provides empirical foundation for regulatory arguments about Facebook's data practices. The tracking network described predates Meta's rebrand but the infrastructure remains substantially unchanged.

## Key points

- 52% of websites (40% of browsing time) for representative US users employ Facebook's tracking technology — massive cross-web coverage
- **Shadow profiles**: Facebook builds behavioral profiles of non-users through embedded tracking on third-party sites
- Tracking is indiscriminate: privacy-sensitive domains (health, finance) tracked at similar rates as general web
- Small Facebook user vs. non-user differences in tracking exposure — primarily driven by browsing activity differences, not structural
- Provides empirical basis for regulatory scrutiny of third-party data collection under GDPR and similar frameworks
