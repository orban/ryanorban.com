## Summary

The Center for Applied Rationality (CFAR) runs multi-day workshops aimed at improving thinking by directly training specific cognitive skills rather than just teaching theory. The CFAR Handbook documents their curriculum as of 2021 — a collection of techniques, exercises, and framings for becoming a better reasoner in practice. It emerged from the LessWrong rationalist community, drawing on Eliezer Yudkowsky's sequences and the broader rationalism ecosystem, but focuses on exercises that can shift actual behavior rather than just intellectual understanding.

Core curriculum areas include: calibration (knowing how uncertain you actually are by making and tracking predictions), aversion factoring (breaking down why you're avoiding something, which often reveals the actual obstacle), trigger-action planning (forming implementation intentions that actually fire in context), inner simulator work (getting your gut-level predictions to register emotionally, not just abstractly), and CoZE (comfort zone expansion through deliberate low-stakes discomfort). Each technique targets a specific gap between knowing what good reasoning looks like and actually doing it under real-world conditions.

CFAR's theory of change is that most reasoning failures aren't about lacking information or logic — they're about predictable failure modes: motivated reasoning, scope insensitivity, availability bias, and the gap between System 1 and System 2 in dual process theory. The handbook doesn't just name these biases (a la Daniel Kahneman's Thinking, Fast and Slow) but provides specific exercises to train better habits in each domain. The approach is empirical: techniques are evaluated for whether they actually produce measurable change in participants.

## Key points

- CFAR workshops are the main delivery mechanism; the handbook documents the curriculum for participants and trainers
- Calibration training — using prediction platforms and feedback loops to make uncertainty estimates more accurate — is a core pillar
- Trigger-action planning adapts implementation intention research from psychology into practical habit formation
- Techniques bridge LessWrong rationalist theory (Bayesian reasoning, epistemic status norms) and behavioral activation
- Related to Julia Galef's *The Scout Mindset* and Anna Salamon's work on self-prediction and internal family systems
- Strong emphasis on distinguishing System 1 gut responses from System 2 deliberate reasoning and improving the accuracy of both
