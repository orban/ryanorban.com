## Summary

This ChainLinkGod analysis examines the capital rotation dynamics that defined the 2021 crypto bull market — when large pools of capital systematically moved from Ethereum to emerging layer-1 blockchains (Solana, Avalanche, Fantom, Terra, Harmony) chasing higher yields and earlier entry on newer ecosystems. Written in mid-2022, it's a retrospective with the advantage of knowing how most of these experiments ended.

The rotation thesis was simple: Ethereum was congested and expensive; newer L1s offered cheap transactions, high APY incentives, and the early-mover advantage. Capital flooded in, TVL spiked, native tokens pumped, and protocols deployed liquidity mining programs to retain it. The analysis shows this was largely circular: the APYs were denominated in inflationary native tokens, the TVL was bootstrapped by those same tokens, and the underlying economic activity was thin. When token prices declined, the yield math collapsed and capital rotated again — often back to Ethereum or out of DeFi entirely.

The Terra / LUNA collapse in May 2022 (just before this article's publication) is the most extreme example of this dynamic: a $40B stablecoin ecosystem built on unsustainable algorithmic stablecoin incentives that unwound in 72 hours. The analysis situates Terra as the logical endpoint of rotation-thesis thinking taken to its extreme.

## Key points

- 2021 L1 rotation was driven by liquidity mining incentives, not genuine user adoption or DeFi activity
- Solana, Avalanche, and Fantom each had genuine technology differentiation but saw their growth heavily distorted by mercenary capital
- The Terra collapse showed the catastrophic risk when a chain's growth depends on its own native token's price appreciation
- Ethereum's moat (developer ecosystem, network effects, ERC-20 liquidity) proved more durable than most expected
- Post-2021 context: rotation thesis capital largely exited; surviving L1s (Solana especially) rebuilt on genuine developer adoption rather than yield chasing
