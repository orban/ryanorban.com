## Summary

Derek Sivers gives characteristically direct advice on finding available .com domains: don't anchor on the first name you like, don't compromise by using hyphens or .net/.io alternatives for a brand name, and use a systematic approach to discover invented words or combinations that are both memorable and available.

The tactics include: combining two short words creatively (the Flickr/Tumblr vowel-drop pattern), invented compound words that suggest the meaning without stating it, nonsense syllables that are pronounceable and memorable, or reusing common words in unexpected combinations. The constraint is specificity: avoid generic words that compete with thousands of other sites, and avoid obscure words that people will misspell.

Sivers is worth reading on naming because he's opinionated and brief — he won't give you a decision tree, he'll tell you what he'd do. The underlying principle is that the domain name is for branding, not description; the best domains are distinctive enough to stick in memory and simple enough to spell after hearing them once. This is more design constraint than technical advice.

## Key points

- Don't use hyphens or alternative TLDs as a fallback — they signal "I wanted the .com but couldn't get it"
- Invented short words (Flickr, Tumblr, Etsy pattern) are often available and memorable
- Test: can someone spell it after hearing it once? If not, the domain name is working against you
- .com still matters for brand trust in 2022 despite the proliferation of .io, .co, .app
