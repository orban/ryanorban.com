## Summary

Lyn Alden's essay traces the history and current stress points of the petrodollar system — the arrangement that has underpinned dollar dominance since Nixon took the US off the gold standard in 1971. The deal: the US provides military security to oil-producing nations (especially Saudi Arabia), and in exchange, oil is priced and settled in dollars globally. This created structural demand for dollars that reinforced the US dollar's reserve currency status independent of US trade balances or debt levels.

The essay argues this system is fraying on multiple fronts: the US has become a net oil exporter (reducing the urgency of Middle East military commitments), other countries are increasingly settling trade outside the dollar, the US debt-to-GDP ratio has grown to levels that historically precede currency regime changes, and alternatives like the euro, Chinese yuan, and Bitcoin provide partial substitutes for dollar functions. None of these individually kills the system, but in combination they suggest the dollar's reserve share will decline from historical peaks.

Lyn Alden is careful to distinguish between the dollar losing dominance and the dollar collapsing — the former is likely, the latter is extreme and speculative. The practical investment implication she draws is toward inflation hedges: commodities, real assets, TIPS, and digital assets as partial portfolio protection against dollar debasement. The essay is notable for its rigorous sourcing and its avoidance of the sensationalism common in gold-bug and crypto-maximalist writing on this topic.

## Key points

- The petrodollar system (1974–present): oil priced in dollars globally creates structural demand for dollar reserves regardless of US fundamentals.
- Fraying factors: US energy independence, dedollarization trends, US debt expansion, rising alternatives (euro, yuan, Bitcoin).
- Key metric: dollar's share of global reserves has fallen from ~71% (2000) to ~59% (2020) — a slow decline, not a cliff.
- By Lyn Alden, one of the most rigorous macro analysts who engages seriously with both traditional and crypto markets.
- Connects to Bitcoin as a hedge narrative: if reserve currency status erodes, hard money alternatives benefit.
