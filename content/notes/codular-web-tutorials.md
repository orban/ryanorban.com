## Summary

Codular was an independent web development tutorial site active around 2012–2013, focused on practical guides for HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and PHP. The site published short, focused tutorials aimed at developers looking to learn specific techniques — typical content included CSS animations, jQuery plugins, PHP patterns, and responsive design techniques.

In 2012-2013, the web tutorial ecosystem was fragmented across dozens of independent sites — CSS-Tricks (Chris Coyier), Smashing Magazine, A List Apart, Net Tuts+ (Envato), and many smaller properties like Codular. These indie sites were often passion projects that built modest audiences before either being acquired (as Envato did with many tutorial networks) or shutting down as the market consolidated.

The economics were challenging: good tutorials took significant time to write, ad revenue was thin unless traffic was substantial, and the major platforms had structural advantages in SEO and audience. By 2015, the market had largely consolidated: CSS-Tricks for CSS/JS, Smashing Magazine for broader web design, and freeCodeCamp (2014) plus The Odin Project for structured learning paths. Codular-style indie tutorial sites largely disappeared.

## Key points

- Codular targeted working developers with focused technique tutorials — CSS, JavaScript, PHP, jQuery
- Part of the 2012-era indie tutorial site ecosystem, now largely consolidated around a few major platforms
- The economics of independent tutorial publishing were difficult: time-intensive content, thin CPM ad revenue
- Consolidation drivers: freeCodeCamp, The Odin Project, MDN Web Docs — high-quality free alternatives
- Reflects the era before YouTube tutorials dominated developer education
