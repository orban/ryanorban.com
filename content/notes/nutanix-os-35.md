## Summary

Nutanix released NOS (Nutanix Operating System) 3.5 in August 2013, a significant update to their hyper-converged infrastructure platform that added data deduplication, compression, and performance improvements across their virtualization and storage stack. The release was accompanied by updates to the Prism management UI, making cluster management more accessible to operators used to traditional SAN tools.

NOS 3.5 was part of Nutanix's strategy to close feature gaps with incumbent SAN and NAS vendors. EMC, NetApp, and HP had mature storage software stacks with deduplication and compression built in — these features were table stakes in enterprise storage procurement. By adding them to NOS, Nutanix removed common objections from enterprise procurement teams comparing them against traditional architectures.

The boasts performance and style tagline reflected the dual marketing strategy: the engineering credentials (performance benchmarks, enterprise features) paired with the operational simplicity story (the Prism UI, single-vendor support, scale-out rather than scale-up). The product page format — clean consumer-style landing page for enterprise infrastructure — was itself distinctive in a market accustomed to dense technical datasheets.

## Key points

- NOS 3.5 added deduplication and compression — closing feature parity gaps with EMC and NetApp that blocked enterprise deals.
- Prism UI improvements: centralized visibility into the entire cluster from a single pane of glass — operational simplicity was the main differentiator vs. traditional multi-vendor setups.
- Hyper-converged infrastructure pitch: one vendor for compute, storage, and networking; linear scale-out by adding nodes; no SAN or separate NAS required.
- Marketing approach: consumer-grade product pages for enterprise software — unusual in 2013, part of positioning Nutanix as the Apple-style choice for data center teams.
- Pre-IPO execution: Nutanix was in the phase of proving it could sell at enterprise scale, not just to cloud-native startups.
- Context connects to concurrent The Register coverage of Joe Tucci flying in to defend an EMC deal — Nutanix was winning those battles.
