## Summary

[House Stock Watcher](/notes/house-stock-watcher/) is a civic transparency tool that aggregates and displays stock trade disclosures filed by US House of Representatives members under the STOCK Act (Stop Trading on Congressional Knowledge). The STOCK Act requires members of Congress to disclose stock transactions over $1,000 within 45 days of the trade. The disclosures are public record, but the official filing system is cumbersome — [House Stock Watcher](/notes/house-stock-watcher/) makes them searchable, sortable, and browsable.

The tool was built during a period of heightened public interest in congressional trading, particularly following revelations that some representatives had made large trades in early 2020 around COVID briefings before the public market impact was understood. This raised questions about whether legislators with access to non-public information could trade on it without consequence.

The practical use of a tool like this ranges from journalists researching stories about legislative conflicts of interest, to retail investors following what Congress is buying as a rough (though imperfect) signal, to policy advocates arguing for stronger trading restrictions. The STOCK Act was a disclosure requirement, not a prohibition — many people who found these disclosures troubling argued for an outright ban on members of Congress trading individual stocks.

## Key points

- Aggregates mandatory trade disclosures from US House members under the STOCK Act.
- Makes congressmember trading activity searchable — official filing system is not browsable.
- Built following 2020 controversies around COVID-period trades by legislators with advance briefing access.
- Related: Senate Stock Watcher covers Senate disclosures under the same act.
- The STOCK Act required disclosure, not prohibition; the tool surfaced the gap between disclosure and restriction.
