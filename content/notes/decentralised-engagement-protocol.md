## Summary

The attention economy routes enormous value through a small number of platform intermediaries — Google, Meta, and a handful of others capture the majority of the economic value generated by human attention, returning almost nothing to the people whose attention is being sold. Incent's whitepaper v2.0 (2022) proposes a decentralized engagement protocol to restructure these flows: users receive tokenized rewards directly for their attention and engagement, cutting out the intermediary platforms and replacing centralized advertising networks and loyalty programs with on-chain protocol mechanics.

The protocol draws explicitly on DeFi as a template, positioning itself as DeMa — Decentralized Marketing — by analogy to how DeFi replaced centralized financial intermediaries with smart contracts and automated market makers. Brands and advertisers interact with the protocol directly, staking tokens that flow to users who engage with their content or products. The tokenomics design tries to align incentives: users are rewarded for genuine engagement rather than passive exposure, and brands pay only for verifiable attention rather than estimated impressions. Blockchain provides the audit trail that traditional affiliate marketing and loyalty programs lack.

The proposal sits within a cluster of Web3 projects from 2021–2022 that sought to apply DeFi mechanics to non-financial domains — often called token-incentivized networks or protocol-owned communities. The honest assessment is that these models faced a common failure mode: the token itself became the product, attracting speculators rather than genuine users, undermining the engagement signal the protocol was designed to create. Whether "DeMa" can avoid this trap depends on whether the engagement being rewarded is observable on-chain in a way that resists gaming.

## Key points

- Positions tokenized loyalty as a DeFi primitive: rewards flow through smart contracts rather than centralized loyalty databases
- The "DeMa" framing explicitly mirrors DeFi — decentralized financial infrastructure — but applied to the advertising and engagement layer
- Brands pay for verifiable attention (on-chain receipts) rather than estimated impressions — the key audit advantage over traditional programmatic advertising
- Token incentives create the same mercenary capital problem seen in DeFi yield farming: users optimize for reward extraction rather than genuine engagement
- Part of a 2021–2022 wave of Web3 social/attention projects including Brave Rewards, Rally, and BitClout that tried to tokenize creator-audience relationships
