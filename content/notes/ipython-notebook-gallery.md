## Summary

This was an early community gallery of IPython (later Jupyter) notebooks, sorted by views — a discovery mechanism for finding high-quality examples across machine learning, data analysis, scientific computing, and teaching materials. The gallery predates nbviewer's rise as the dominant notebook sharing platform.

In 2015, Jupyter notebooks were firmly established as the primary medium for data science work in Python but there was no canonical way to discover exemplary notebooks. This gallery filled that gap by tracking which notebooks were being shared and viewed most frequently, surfacing the community's collective judgment about quality.

The types of notebooks that tended to rank highly: interactive visualizations demonstrating D3.js or matplotlib capabilities, tutorial notebooks explaining algorithms from scratch, and research notebooks accompanying academic papers (the reproducible research movement was gaining traction). This era also saw the emergence of notebook-as-teaching-tool philosophy, with resources like the Probabilistic Programming and Bayesian Methods for Hackers notebook and Introduction to Statistical Learning adaptations.

## Key points

- Early discovery mechanism for high-quality Jupyter notebooks before dedicated platforms existed.
- Community curation via view counts — surfaces collective judgment about quality examples.
- Most-viewed categories: tutorials, visualizations, research supplements, algorithm explanations.
- Reflects the 2015 moment when notebooks had become primary data science medium but sharing infrastructure was still immature.
- Reproducible research movement: academic papers increasingly accompanied by notebooks.
- Superseded by nbviewer, GitHub rendering, and later Kaggle notebooks and Observable.
