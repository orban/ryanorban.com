## Summary

This Bogleheads forum thread, started by user HEDGEFUNDIE, became one of the most-discussed personal finance strategy threads of 2019-2020. The proposal: hold a simple two-fund portfolio of UPRO (3x leveraged S&P 500) and TMF (3x leveraged long-term Treasuries) in roughly a 55/45 split, rebalanced quarterly. The strategy is a retail implementation of risk parity — the idea, pioneered by Bridgewater's Ray Dalio via the All Weather Portfolio, that a balanced portfolio should equalize risk contribution across asset classes rather than weighting by dollar amount.

The logic for pairing leveraged equities with leveraged long-term bonds: equities and long-duration Treasury bonds have historically been negatively correlated during crises (bonds rally when equities crash), so the combination can be leveraged to amplify the Sharpe ratio without proportionally amplifying drawdowns. Risk parity as practiced by institutional funds like Bridgewater achieves this with derivatives and margin; HEDGEFUNDIE's innovation was observing that leveraged ETFs give retail investors a fee-accessible (if expensive) approximation.

The thread spawned extensive backtesting and community analysis. Critics note several risks: volatility decay (leveraged ETFs suffer from daily rebalancing math in choppy markets), sequence-of-returns risk in extended bear markets, and the assumption that equity-bond negative correlation holds — which historically has not been stable across all inflation regimes. The strategy performed spectacularly in backtests through a period of falling interest rates but the 2022 environment (equities and bonds both crashing) would have been brutal.

## Key points

- Portfolio: ~55% UPRO (3x S&P 500) + ~45% TMF (3x 20+ year Treasuries), quarterly rebalanced.
- Conceptual basis: risk parity — balance risk contribution, not dollar allocation.
- Key assumption: negative correlation between equities and long-duration bonds holds in crises.
- Risk: volatility decay compounds losses in choppy markets; 3x leverage magnifies both gains and losses.
- Historical backtests looked strong for 2000-2019 period but 2022 (bonds and stocks both cratering) exposed structural risk.
- Bogleheads community generated extensive analysis, variants, and critiques in the thread.
