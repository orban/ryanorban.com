## Summary

Desktop Genetics (later rebranded as Synthego-adjacent, operating at deskgen.com) was an early-stage synthetic biology startup building software tools for genome editing and CRISPR guide RNA design. This design competition brief from early 2013 reflects the company's need for visual identity and UX design — a common move for technical biotech startups that need design help but can't yet compete on salary.

The timing is notable: January 2013 is just months after the landmark Doudna-Charpentier CRISPR-Cas9 paper (Science, June 2012) and Zhang Lab papers demonstrating genome editing in mammalian cells (Science, January 2013). The era of CRISPR as a widespread laboratory tool was just beginning, and companies building software to simplify guide RNA design — the computational layer of CRISPR experiments — were in genuinely early territory.

Desktop Genetics positioned itself as the software layer for the CRISPR revolution: rather than selling reagents or doing editing services, they built the informatics tools that researchers used to design experiments. The design competition reflects an early-stage company building brand identity simultaneously with its technical product — a resource-constrained approach that revealed both the bootstrapped nature of biotech software startups and the moment's excitement.

## Key points

- Desktop Genetics built software for CRISPR guide RNA design — the computational layer of genome editing
- Saved in January 2013: just as the first mammalian CRISPR-Cas9 papers appeared — genuinely early in the CRISPR era
- Design competitions are a common resource-constrained approach for early biotech startups to get quality creative work
- The synthetic biology software layer (design tools, lab informatics) attracted its own wave of startups alongside reagent companies
- CRISPR democratized genome editing; the software tools to use it were still being invented in early 2013
