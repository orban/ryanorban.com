## Summary

Outset Capital published this data piece looking at pre-seed funding norms — the first institutional check most startups raise, typically $500K–$2M, before demonstrating product-market fit. The analysis draws on deal data to establish what "normal" looks like: valuation ranges, dilution percentages, round sizes, and how these shifted during the 2020-2022 funding environment.

Pre-seed funding sits in a gap: too early for most traditional seed funds (which want traction), too late to be bootstrapped in many sectors. The market in 2021-2022 saw significant inflation in pre-seed valuations driven by the low-rate environment — rounds that would have been $3-4M caps in 2019 were closing at $8-12M caps on SAFEs. This data from Outset provides calibration for founders trying to understand whether their terms are market-rate or outliers.

The practical questions this data answers: What equity should you give up in a pre-seed round? What valuation cap is defensible before you have revenue? How does round size affect your next raise? Pre-seed norms differ significantly from seed and Series A because the investor relationship is more concentrated, the company is more nascent, and the terms (often SAFEs rather than priced rounds) give less investor protection.

## Key points

- Pre-seed rounds (2020-2022): typically $500K–$2M on SAFEs, valuations inflated to $6-15M caps during the bull market
- 15-20% dilution target remains the rough rule of thumb even as check sizes grew
- Outset Capital focuses on pre-seed and seed in B2B SaaS and infrastructure — data reflects that lens
- SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity) dominates pre-seed; convertible notes used when investors want interest and maturity date
- Benchmarks aged quickly: post-2022 correction reset valuations significantly downward
