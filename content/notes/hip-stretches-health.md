## Summary

This Health.com guide by trainer Lauren Kanski (NASM-CPT) covers 15 hip stretches addressing the most common sources of hip tightness in people with desk jobs: tight hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris) from prolonged sitting, tight glutes and piriformis from inactivity, and IT band tightness from overuse or posture.

The stretches are organized by muscle group and include both pre- and post-exercise variations. Key stretches covered: the **kneeling hip flexor stretch** (targets iliopsoas — the primary culprit for desk-sitter hip flexor tightness), **pigeon pose** (deep piriformis and glute stretch), **figure-four** (accessible alternative to pigeon for those with knee sensitivity), **standing quad stretch** (hip flexor + quad), **butterfly stretch** (inner thigh and hip rotators), and **90/90** (addresses both internal and external hip rotation).

The clinical context: prolonged sitting causes the hip flexors to adaptively shorten and the glutes to become neurologically inhibited (gluteal amnesia, or dead butt syndrome). This contributes to lower back pain, knee tracking issues, and reduced athletic performance. Regular hip mobility work addresses these patterns. The recommendation is daily hip stretching for office workers, ideally bookending work sessions.

## Key points

- Tight hips from sitting: primarily hip flexor (iliopsoas) shortening + gluteal inhibition.
- Key stretches: kneeling hip flexor, pigeon/figure-four (piriformis), butterfly (adductors), 90/90 (hip rotation).
- 15 exercises cover the full hip complex — not just the flexors but glutes, piriformis, IT band, adductors.
- Gluteal amnesia: prolonged sitting causes the glutes to stop firing properly — stretching addresses one side; glute activation work addresses the other.
- Daily practice recommended for sedentary workers; particularly useful before and after exercise.
