## Summary

Tim Blair White wrote this account of a demo day or portfolio showcase at Lightspeed Venture Partners, featuring 10 emerging technology startups. The content field highlights a Nutanix mention — significant because Nutanix went on to become one of the more successful enterprise infrastructure companies of the decade, with a 2016 IPO at ~$2.5B valuation.

Lightspeed Venture Partners was a prominent Sand Hill Road firm focused on enterprise and consumer technology. In early 2013, their enterprise portfolio reflected the infrastructure trends of the period: hyper-converged infrastructure, cloud management, big data, and enterprise mobile. Nutanix represented the hyper-converged infrastructure bet — replacing traditional SAN + server architectures with scale-out appliances that combined compute and storage.

The significance of the Nutanix reference: in March 2013 Nutanix was still a startup (founded 2009, raised Series C in 2013). By 2016 it had IPO'd; by 2020 it was competing directly with VMware's VSAN and Dell/EMC. The early mention in a VC portfolio day is a useful marker of when the hyper-converged infrastructure category became visible to investors.

## Key points

- Nutanix in early 2013: still private, early in its trajectory toward becoming the leading hyper-converged infrastructure vendor
- Lightspeed Venture Partners has backed Snap, AppDynamics, and Nimble Storage — known for enterprise infrastructure alongside consumer bets
- Hyper-converged infrastructure: combines compute, storage, and networking in scale-out appliance nodes — disrupting the traditional server + SAN architecture
- The demo day format: portfolio companies presenting to each other, to analysts, and to potential partners or customers — a common VC ecosystem event
- Nutanix's 2016 IPO validated the hyper-converged category; VMware VSAN and Dell VxRail followed as incumbent responses
