## Summary

In September 2014, Zipfian Academy announced a partnership with Skymind to incorporate deep learning into its data science curriculum. Skymind was the company behind Deeplearning4j (DL4J), a JVM-based deep learning library that preceded TensorFlow and PyTorch as one of the first production-ready deep learning frameworks.

This partnership placed Zipfian Academy among the first bootcamps to formally teach neural networks and deep learning — a significant decision given that deep learning had only recently crossed from academic research into commercial application. AlexNet's 2012 ImageNet win had demonstrated that deep neural networks could achieve superhuman performance on image recognition, and by 2014 the technology was beginning to diffuse into industry.

Skymind was notable for being one of the few serious deep learning infrastructure companies before Google released TensorFlow in November 2015. Its founder Adam Gibson was building enterprise deep learning tooling before the term AI startup had reached its current ubiquity. The partnership with Zipfian Academy was a mutual visibility play: Skymind got curriculum exposure, Zipfian got to offer cutting-edge content.

## Key points

- September 2014: Zipfian Academy + Skymind partnership — one of the first bootcamp deep learning curricula.
- Skymind: maker of Deeplearning4j (DL4J) — JVM-based deep learning library, predated TensorFlow (Nov 2015).
- Context: 2 years post-AlexNet, pre-TensorFlow — deep learning was becoming commercially viable but tooling was nascent.
- Zipfian Academy positioned itself as curriculum leader by being early to deep learning content.
- Deeplearning4j was later folded into Eclipse Foundation as Eclipse Deeplearning4j.
- Historically notable: bootcamp curricula in late 2014 were the first mass-market pathway to neural network training.
