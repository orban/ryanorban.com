## Summary

Nutanix published this openly combative post in November 2012, written by (or attributed to) Sudheen Nair, attacking EMC's storage marketing as condescending to enterprise buyers. At the time, Nutanix was a well-funded startup (founded 2009, Series C had just closed) building hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) — combining compute, storage, and networking into a software-defined appliance stack that replaced traditional SAN and NAS architectures.

The competitive dynamic was sharp. EMC dominated enterprise storage with products like VNX and VMAX, and its VMware relationship gave it a near-lock on virtualized data center stacks. Nutanix was directly attacking this model: its proposition was that the separate storage array — the expensive, complex, specialized hardware that EMC sold — was unnecessary. Software-defined, x86-based, converged nodes could replace the whole stack at lower cost with better management.

The they think you're stupid framing was deliberately provocative and reflected the aggressive startup vs. incumbent narrative common in the enterprise infrastructure space of 2012–2015. Nutanix went public in 2016 at a $9 billion valuation; the HCI market it pioneered eventually forced EMC to respond with VxRail (built on Nutanix-rivaling technology). EMC itself was acquired by Dell Technologies in 2016.

## Key points

- Nutanix in 2012: early-stage HCI startup directly attacking EMC's legacy SAN/NAS storage business
- Hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI): compute + storage + networking in software-defined x86 nodes — no separate storage arrays needed
- Aggressive startup marketing strategy: "they think you're stupid" positions incumbent as patronizing, startup as advocate
- EMC responded eventually with VxRail; Nutanix IPO'd at $9B in 2016; EMC acquired by Dell in 2016
- Historical note: ryanorban (the bookmark author) was at Nutanix at the time — this was his employer's blog
