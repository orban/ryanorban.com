## Summary

Gurmeet Manku (a computer scientist at Google) maintained this collection of mathematical and logic puzzles on his personal site. The collection spans combinatorics, probability, geometry, and algorithm design puzzles — with an emphasis on problems where the solution reveals something surprising about the underlying structure.

These puzzles occupy a specific niche: they're harder than typical interview questions but not requiring research-level mathematics. Many circulate in technical interviews at companies like Google, Microsoft, and Jane Street — the kind of problems where a brute-force approach is possible but the elegant solution requires insight. The expected value and probability puzzles are particularly well-crafted: the answer is often counterintuitive (the birthday problem, Monty Hall, the envelope paradox) and rewards careful probabilistic reasoning.

For programmers, puzzles like these build the habit of looking for structural simplification before reaching for implementation. The aha moment in a well-designed puzzle is structurally similar to recognizing that a problem has dynamic programming substructure, or that a seemingly O(n²) problem has an O(n log n) solution via a different representation.

## Key points

- Mathematical puzzles as training for insight: the goal isn't getting the answer but noticing the structure that makes the elegant solution obvious in retrospect.
- Probability puzzles feature prominently — many with counterintuitive answers that test understanding of conditional probability and expected value.
- Algorithm design puzzles: problems that map to classic CS problems once the right representation is found — sorting, graph search, dynamic programming.
- The collection was widely shared as interview preparation material for software engineering roles at top-tier tech companies in the 2010s.
- Recreational mathematics tradition: from Martin Gardner's Scientific American columns to Project Euler to competitive programming — puzzle collections as a genre of mathematical education.
