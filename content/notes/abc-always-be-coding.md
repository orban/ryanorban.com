## Summary

David Byttow's ABC: Always Be Coding became one of Medium's most-shared engineering career posts when it was published in 2013. The central argument: the best way to prepare for technical interviews at companies like Google, Facebook, and Twitter is not to cram LeetCode-style problems but to code constantly — side projects, open-source contributions, and deliberate practice on real problems.

The post pushed back against the "interview prep industrial complex" that was already forming around books like *Cracking the Coding Interview*. Byttow's view: someone who codes every day develops the pattern recognition and problem-solving intuition that interview questions test — but as a byproduct of genuine engineering work, not as a memorized repertoire. Side projects also produce portfolio artifacts that signal capability more convincingly than a polished interview performance.

The ABC framing resonated widely because it reframed interview preparation as a lifestyle rather than a sprint. The advice to write code every day, ship things to production, and read others' code aligns with the broader deliberate practice literature (Anders Ericsson's work on expertise).

## Key points

- Always be coding: daily practice builds pattern recognition that interview prep alone doesn't
- Side projects > cram sessions: they produce portfolio artifacts and genuine problem-solving intuition
- Read code from good engineers — open source is an apprenticeship available to everyone
- Breadth first at the start of your career, then depth; the reverse is harder to reverse
- Technical interviews measure pattern recognition, not just knowledge — pattern recognition requires practice over time
- 2013 context: the post predated LeetCode's dominance; the advice has become more contested as the interview landscape has shifted
