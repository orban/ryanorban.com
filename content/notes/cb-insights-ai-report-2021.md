## Summary

CB Insights' annual State of AI report for 2021 is a data-rich snapshot of private market artificial intelligence investment at what turned out to be a pivotal inflection year. The report covers dealmaking, funding, and exits by private AI companies globally — tracking unicorn creation, sector breakdowns, geographic distribution, and the competitive dynamics between the US, China, and Europe. 2021 was a record year by nearly every metric: funding, deal count, and unicorn formation all hit highs not seen in prior years.

The categories the report tracks reflect the AI landscape of the time: computer vision, NLP, robotics, autonomous vehicles, and AI infrastructure dominate. Generative AI appears as a small category and large language models are barely mentioned — this is pre-ChatGPT, pre-Stable Diffusion, and the GPT-3 moment is just under a year old. Reading the report now is instructive precisely because of what's absent: the categories that would dominate 2023–2025 investment are either missing or marginal.

The corporate AI investment section tracks Google, Microsoft, Salesforce, and other strategics making acquisitions and minority investments. The exit environment in 2021 was frothy: both IPO and SPAC activity was at peaks that wouldn't be repeated. This report captures the moment just before rising interest rates and the tech market downturn of 2022 reset valuations across the sector.

## Key points

- 2021 was a record year for private AI investment by deal count, dollar volume, and unicorn formation — the report documents the peak of the pre-LLM-era AI investment cycle.
- Generative AI and foundation models are minimal in the 2021 categories, making this a useful historical baseline for how rapidly the investment landscape shifted in 2022–2023.
- Geographic breakdown shows US dominance with China second, though US-China competitive dynamics in AI chips and autonomous vehicles are flagged as a growing strategic tension.
- Corporate strategic investment from Google, Microsoft, and others is tracked alongside pure venture capital — anticipates the hyperscaler AI arms race that followed.
- Useful primarily as a historical document: the 2021 deal and category structure looks very different from 2024's, making it a benchmark for how dramatically ChatGPT and generative AI reshuffled investment priorities.
