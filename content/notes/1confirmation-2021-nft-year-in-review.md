## Summary

The 2021 NFT market hit $19.6B in total trading volume and $31.4B in total market cap, with 2M active users on Ethereum — growth that turned what began in 2017 as a niche experiment in digital ownership into a multi-billion dollar global asset class. 1confirmation, a crypto-focused VC fund with investments in OpenSea, SuperRare, and artists like Pak and Beeple, published this annual review as a comprehensive data-driven snapshot of that year. The report is written from an insider perspective: bullish on the long-term trajectory, but grounded in actual trading volumes rather than hype.

The four primary categories that drove NFT activity in 2021 were visual art ($2.5B trading volume, 66.6k active traders), PFP projects ($8.7B trading volume, 376.6k active traders), cultural collectibles ($4.4B, 482.1k traders), and gaming ($4.0B via Axie Infinity and others). The ERC-721 standard, created in mid-2017, was the key technical catalyst — it gave developers a common interface so marketplaces like OpenSea could support all NFT collections without custom integration per contract. ERC-1155 followed as an alternative that supports both fungible and non-fungible tokens in a single contract, with better gas efficiency for bulk transfers.

OpenSea dominated with $16B in trading volume and 1.26M active users. The other notable platforms — SuperRare (high-end art, avg. 10.6 ETH per sale), Foundation ($135M, cultural range), Rarible ($249M, collectibles), and Catalog (music NFTs, $1.65M) — each carved out distinct niches. Fractional introduced a new primitive: fractionalize an NFT into ERC-20 tokens, enabling shared ownership of high-value pieces. CryptoPunks generated $2.4B in trading volume; BAYC $979M; ArtBlocks $1.39B in secondary sales; Axie Infinity $3.7B in NFT trading alone.

## Key points

- ERC-721 standard (2017) enabled interoperability across all NFT marketplaces — the technical unlock that made the 2021 boom possible.
- PFP projects ($13.9B market cap) represent a new bottom-up brand model: globally owned from launch, incentive-aligned, vs. top-down corporate brands. BAYC gave holders commercial rights, a strategy that separated it from CryptoPunks.
- Beeple's *Everydays: The First 5000 Days* sold at Christie's for $69.3M — first NFT at a major auction house, 7th most expensive sale by a living artist.
- Axie Infinity pioneered play-to-earn: in-game NFT items convertible to cash, AXS fully diluted market cap $25.4B. The model creates new earning opportunities but also exposed players to speculative asset risk.
- Competing chains (Polygon, Ronin, Solana, Flow, Tezos) claimed some activity, but Ethereum + L2s still represented 90%+ of NFT volume. The report predicted L2 innovation in 2022 would extend Ethereum dominance.
- Music NFTs via Catalog and photography NFTs were emerging sub-categories; NounsDAO demonstrated NFT-governed treasuries as a new model for community-owned organizations.
- Hal Finney predicted NFT-like digital ownership in 1993 — a lineage the report traces: Colored Coins (2012) → Counterparty (2014) → CryptoPunks + ERC-721 (2017) → OpenSea beta → 2021 boom.
