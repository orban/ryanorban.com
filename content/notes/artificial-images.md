## Summary

[Artificial Images](/notes/artificial-images/) is the creative and educational practice of Derrick Schultz, spanning generative AI art, computational filmmaking, and teaching. His work explores algorithmic abstraction — GAN-generated prints, neural-network-processed video, and AI-assisted animation — treating machine learning as an artistic medium rather than a utility.

The educational component is practically significant: Schultz runs small-group workshops teaching diffusion models, ComfyUI, and generative techniques to artists, designers, and image-makers. This occupies a gap between ML papers (too technical) and YouTube tutorials (too shallow) — targeted instruction for creative practitioners who want real understanding of the tools they use.

The site documents a progression from 2015 through 2026 — spanning the GAN era (StyleGAN, BigGAN), the Stable Diffusion explosion, and current diffusion model workflows. This makes it a useful historical record of how generative AI tools evolved and how one artist incorporated them into a sustained practice.

## Key points

- Art practice + education: GAN-generated work, diffusion model workshops, computational filmmaking.
- Workshops target artists and designers, not developers — practical instruction in creative ML tools.
- Covers ComfyUI, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, and video generation techniques.
- Active from 2015 through present — a longitudinal record of the generative AI tool evolution.
- Community resources: YouTube, GitHub, Patreon, Slack — full ecosystem around the practice.
- Exemplifies the artist-technologist at the machine learning × creativity boundary.
