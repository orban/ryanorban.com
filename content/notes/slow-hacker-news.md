## Summary

Slow HN is an alternative interface for Hacker News that filters for quality over recency — showing stories that have accumulated engagement over time rather than stories that are currently trending. The standard Hacker News front page rewards rapid upvoting of new submissions, which means it surfaces content that is timely and attention-grabbing but not necessarily the most substantive. Slow HN inverts this.

The design philosophy connects to the broader slow information diet movement: the idea that the real value in places like Hacker News isn't the news but the accumulated knowledge and thoughtful discussion — and that the news format (recency-weighted ranking) is actually the worst way to surface it. Filtering for sustained engagement over multiple days acts as a quality signal: if something keeps getting upvoted and discussed well after the initial wave, it probably has enduring value.

Similar approaches exist for other platforms: Hacker News Classics surfaces all-time top posts, various Twitter/X tools try to filter out noise, newsletter digest formats summarize the week's best. The common thread is skepticism about real-time feeds as an information consumption model.

The tool is simple — it doesn't require an account or configuration, just visit the site instead of `news.ycombinator.com` when you want lower-noise, higher-quality content.

## Key points

- Alternative Hacker News reader that filters for sustained engagement rather than current trending.
- Quality signal: stories that keep getting upvoted days after submission have more enduring value than viral spikes.
- Part of the "slow information diet" philosophy — skepticism of real-time feeds and recency-weighted ranking.
- Similar tools: Hacker News Classics (all-time bests), weekly digest newsletters, curated read-later stacks.
- No account required — drop-in replacement for the standard HN front page.
