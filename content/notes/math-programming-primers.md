## Summary

Jeremy Kun's Math ∩ Programming blog maintains a primers index — self-contained posts that build the mathematical foundation needed to understand the more advanced algorithm and CS theory posts on the blog. The primers cover linear algebra, group theory, topology, graph theory, probability theory, and computational complexity, each written for a programmer audience that has CS intuition but not necessarily formal math training.

What distinguishes these primers from textbooks: they're written by someone who moves fluidly between mathematical rigor and programming intuition, and every concept is immediately connected to a computational application. The linear algebra primer, for example, explains vector spaces in a way that maps directly to how NumPy operations work. The group theory primer grounds the abstraction in symmetry operations on data structures.

Kun's approach embodies the belief that mathematicians and programmers are working on the same problems with different notation and priorities, and that the intersection is where the most interesting work happens. The blog became a reference for programmers interested in algorithms, cryptography, machine learning theory, and combinatorics who want mathematical depth without reading textbooks cover-to-cover.

## Key points

- Self-contained primers: each post assumes only high school math and programming experience
- Topics: linear algebra, group theory, topology, probability theory, graph theory, complexity theory
- Every mathematical concept is connected to a concrete computational or programming application
- Jeremy Kun went on to write "A Programmer's Introduction to Mathematics" (2018), a book-length expansion
- The blog is open and growing — primers from 2011 through present day
- Valuable as a reference: find the primer for the math you need for a specific algorithm, read it in 30 minutes
