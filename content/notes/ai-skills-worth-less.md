## Summary

Ryszard Szopa wrote this during the 2021 machine learning boom when ML engineers commanded extraordinary salaries and every company wanted to hire data scientists. His argument: this premium is partially illusory. Most ML work in industry is less about cutting-edge model development and more about data cleaning, pipeline engineering, and applying well-understood algorithms to standard problems — work that requires domain knowledge and software engineering SKILL at least as much as ML expertise.

The critique has two parts. First, the supply of ML talent is rising faster than most people expect — online courses, [fast.ai](/notes/fastai/), Coursera, and accessible tooling are democratizing ML skills, compressing the scarcity premium. Second, the bottleneck in most ML projects isn't algorithmic sophistication — it's problem formulation, data quality, and understanding the domain well enough to know what to predict and what success looks like. These skills are undervalued relative to model-building.

Viewed from 2026, this essay was prescient in some ways: AutoML tools, foundation models, and LLM-based coding tools have continued to commoditize the train a model portion of ML work, shifting value toward people who can identify the right problems, manage data infrastructure, and deploy systems reliably. The pure ML researcher role remains valuable but the generic ML engineer has faced more commoditization than 2021 salaries implied.

## Key points

- ML skills premium was partially driven by temporary scarcity — supply of practitioners was rising rapidly.
- Most industry ML work is pipeline engineering and domain translation, not algorithmic innovation.
- Domain knowledge and problem formulation skill are undervalued relative to model-building.
- AutoML and foundation models have since commoditized more of the build a model task.
- By Ryszard Szopa — contrarian view worth holding alongside the bullish ML career narrative.
