## Summary

This Medium essay (from Innovation Endeavors, a VC firm) argues that the primary constraint on large language model startups in late 2022 wasn't model quality, API access, or compute — it was UX design. GPT-3 had been available since 2020 and was demonstrably powerful, but most products built on it were clunky or confusing. The bottleneck was the ability to translate model capability into an interface that non-technical users could understand and value.

The claim resonated in the founder community (Ryan Orban, who bookmarked this, shared it as reflective of his own experience). The pattern was visible: the same underlying model could produce wildly different products depending on interface design, prompt framing, and interaction model. ChatGPT itself was perhaps the clearest evidence — OpenAI had the same GPT-3.5 capability as competitors, but the chat interface was the insight. A conversation metaphor users already understood made the capability immediately accessible.

The practical implication: LLM startups needed product designers and UX engineers as much as ML engineers, at a time when neither category had much experience building AI-native interfaces. This was a genuine supply/demand mismatch. What interaction patterns work for a text-generating model? When should the AI write vs. when should it ask? How do you convey confidence and uncertainty? How do you handle errors gracefully? These were open design questions, not engineering questions, and the teams that got them right pulled ahead. The [AI UX](/notes/ai-ux/) field has since developed significantly, but in 2022 most teams were figuring it out from scratch.

## Key points

- Core claim: UX design talent was scarcer than model access for LLM startups in 2022.
- GPT-3 was available since 2020 but most products were difficult to use — interface was the gap.
- ChatGPT demonstrated the insight: familiar conversation metaphor + good defaults = accessible capability.
- Open design questions: when does AI write vs. ask? how to handle errors? how to convey confidence?
- [AI UX](/notes/ai-ux/) skills (prompting interfaces, streaming UX, feedback loops) were nearly nonexistent as a specialty.
- The teams that solved interface design pulled ahead — this was a product advantage, not a model advantage.
