## Summary

Genius of the Modern World is a BBC documentary series presented by Bettany Hughes, covering the lives and ideas of major Western philosophers. The Karl Marx episode (Episode 1 of 3) traces his biography — from his youth in Prussia through exile in Paris and Brussels, to his long residence in London where he wrote Das Kapital — alongside an accessible account of his intellectual contributions.

Marx's core theoretical contribution is historical materialism: the idea that economic conditions (the "base") determine social, political, and cultural structures (the superstructure), and that history progresses through class struggle driven by contradictions in each mode of production. Capitalism, in his analysis, contains inherent contradictions — the tendency of the profit rate to fall, the extraction of surplus value from labor — that will eventually produce its own crisis and replacement.

The BBC format makes these ideas accessible without dumbing them down. Bettany Hughes visits Marx's actual locations and interviews scholars, giving historical texture to concepts that often get taught in the abstract. Part of the Masters of Money series context places this alongside episodes on Keynes and Hayek — the three major competing frameworks for thinking about economic organization that still structure political economy debates.

## Key points

- Karl Marx (1818-1883): historical materialism, surplus value, class struggle, alienated labor
- Das Kapital (1867): systematic critique of capitalism's internal logic — labor theory of value, capital accumulation
- The Communist Manifesto (1848, with Friedrich Engels): political program; historical materialist analysis of bourgeoisie/proletariat conflict
- BBC's Genius of the Modern World covers Nietzsche and Freud in Episodes 2-3 — the three thinkers most cited as shaping modern self-understanding
