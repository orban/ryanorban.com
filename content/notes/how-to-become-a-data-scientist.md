## Summary

This Quora thread became one of the most-referenced career guides in the nascent data science field circa 2012-2013 — bookmarked extensively during the period when data science was defined as "a person who is better at statistics than any software engineer and better at software engineering than any statistician" (Josh Wills' formulation). Formal data science degrees didn't exist yet; the paths in were varied and the requirements were not standardized.

The most-upvoted answers converged on a SKILL stack: strong statistics foundation (probability, hypothesis testing, regression, Bayesian inference), programming proficiency (Python or R), SQL and database fluency, machine learning algorithms (classification, regression, clustering, dimensionality reduction), and domain knowledge in a specific application area. The T-shaped framing was common: deep expertise in one area plus breadth across the others.

The thread was saved during the period when Zipfian Academy (one of the first data science bootcamps) was operating, and reflects the skills that bootcamps were designed to teach. The Quora answers also debated whether a PhD was necessary — the emerging consensus was "no for industry positions, yes for research roles" — which was itself a signal that data science was diverging from academic statistics and ML research into a distinct applied practice.

## Key points

- Core skills in 2013: statistics, Python/R, SQL, machine learning, communication — the same stack that remains central today
- Academic path: statistics, computer science, physics, or economics degrees were common backgrounds; no dedicated data science programs existed yet
- Practical path: work with data in adjacent roles (analyst, engineer) and self-study ML/stats; portfolio projects demonstrating skills matter more than credentials
- Kaggle was mentioned as a practical learning ground for machine learning competitions — becoming a visible portfolio mechanism for industry hiring
- The full-stack data scientist framing: individual contributors who could go from raw data to deployed model — rare and valuable in 2013, increasingly common by 2018
- This thread is a historical artifact of the field's formation — the skills emphasized reflect what was scarce (statistics + coding) rather than what would become commoditized (tooling)
