## Summary

Jeff Erickson is a professor at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign (UIUC) who has taught algorithms for decades and made his lecture notes freely available online. The materials evolved into a textbook, *Algorithms* (2019), which is notable for its mathematical rigor, clear presentation, and coverage of topics that undergraduate algorithms courses often skip or handle superficially.

The coverage spans the standard curriculum — sorting, dynamic programming, graph algorithms (DFS, BFS, shortest paths, minimum spanning trees), network flow, NP-completeness — plus Erickson's specialty: computational geometry. The geometry sections (convex hull, triangulation, range trees, Voronoi diagrams) are harder to find in other freely available course materials and represent genuinely advanced content.

What distinguishes Erickson's notes: he uses pseudocode that's close to mathematical specification rather than any particular language, which forces students to think about the algorithm rather than the implementation. The problem sets are notoriously difficult and well-designed — the kind of problems where the solution is surprising in retrospect and teaches something structural about the problem class.

## Key points

- Free online: both the lecture notes and the final textbook are available as PDFs from Erickson's website under a Creative Commons license.
- Dynamic programming coverage: particularly thorough, working through recurrence formulation and the trade-offs between memoization and bottom-up tabulation.
- Computational geometry chapters: convex hull, line sweep algorithms, triangulation — relatively rare in freely available undergraduate algorithms materials.
- Problem set quality: the exercises are research-adjacent in difficulty and require structural insight, not just algorithmic pattern matching.
- UIUC algorithms tradition: Illinois has strong theory faculty; Erickson's notes reflect a rigorous theoretical grounding alongside practical algorithm design.
