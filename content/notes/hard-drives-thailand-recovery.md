## Summary

This Engadget report covers the recovery of hard disk drive (HDD) shipments following the catastrophic 2011 Thailand floods that devastated the HDD manufacturing industry. The floods in late 2011 submerged key industrial zones in Thailand that housed factories for Western Digital, Seagate, and their component suppliers — Thailand accounted for roughly 45% of global HDD production at the time. The resulting supply shock sent drive prices tripling overnight and persisted for most of 2012.

The recovery to record-high shipment levels by late 2012 marked the end of one of the most severe supply chain disruptions in consumer electronics history. The floods exposed a structural risk that had built up quietly over two decades: global disk manufacturing had become highly concentrated in a single geographic region due to lower labor costs and accumulated manufacturing expertise. When flooding took out that region, there were no backup suppliers at scale — the supply chain had been optimized for cost at the expense of resilience.

The episode had lasting effects on the storage industry. Short-term, it accelerated SSD adoption: with HDD prices tripling, the cost premium for SSDs narrowed significantly, bringing forward purchasing decisions that might have waited another year or two. Longer-term, it became a case study in supply chain concentration risk that manufacturers across industries cited when making sourcing and geographic diversification decisions. The COVID-19 semiconductor shortages of 2020-2021 were a much larger-scale replay of the same pattern.

## Key points

- The 2011 Thailand floods destroyed ~45% of global HDD production capacity in a single event — a textbook supply chain concentration failure.
- HDD prices tripled within weeks; shortages persisted through most of 2012 before manufacturing capacity was rebuilt.
- Accelerated SSD adoption: when HDD prices tripled, SSDs became relatively cheaper, pulling SSD purchases forward.
- No backup suppliers at scale: just-in-time manufacturing and geographic concentration had eliminated supply chain resilience.
- Precursor to COVID-19 chip shortage: same pattern of concentrated manufacturing, single-event disruption, extended recovery.
