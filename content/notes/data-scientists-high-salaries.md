## Summary

This KDnuggets article from April 2014 captures the early peak of data science hiring hype. The core claim: data scientists are hard to find, and this scarcity drives compensation well above comparable software engineering roles. The framing (elusive) reflects the genuine talent gap — demand had surged following the HBR sexiest job article but the supply of people with the right combination of skills (statistics, programming, domain knowledge) was still small.

The timing is significant context for Zipfian Academy: data science bootcamps like Zipfian, Galvanize, and General Assembly were racing to fill this supply gap by training people quickly. The article reflects the market conditions that made short, intensive bootcamps viable — if salaries are high enough, a 12-week course with a placement record can justify significant tuition.

In 2014, "data scientist" was still loosely defined. The role conflated statistics expertise, software engineering, machine learning research, and business analytics in ways that later fragmented into more specialized titles. The scarcity was partly definitional: if data scientist means someone who does all of these things well, of course they're rare.

## Key points

- 2014 data scientist salary premium reflected genuine scarcity of people combining statistics, ML, and programming.
- KDnuggets was (and remains) a leading source of salary surveys and industry trends in data science.
- Created market conditions that made data science bootcamps like Zipfian Academy viable businesses.
- "Elusive" framing: the role combined skills rarely found together — statistician + engineer + domain expert.
- The broad "data scientist" definition later fragmented into ML engineer, data analyst, AI researcher, etc.
