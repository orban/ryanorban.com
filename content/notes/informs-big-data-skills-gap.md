## Summary

In September 2013, INFORMS — the Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences — announced continuing education courses aimed at the big data skills gap for analytics professionals. This was an institutional response to the well-documented shortage of people who could extract value from large datasets: the McKinsey 2011 report had estimated a shortfall of 140,000–190,000 people with deep analytical skills in the US alone by 2018.

INFORMS occupying this space made sense given its membership: operations researchers and management scientists had been doing quantitative analysis for decades. The big data wave was partly a rebrand of skills they already had (optimization, statistical modeling, simulation) applied to larger datasets and faster pipelines. Their courses aimed to bridge the gap between traditional analytics and the new data engineering stack (Hadoop, SQL, Python).

The irony is that by 2013, the market had already largely bypassed traditional professional societies. Zipfian Academy, General Assembly, and other data science bootcamps were moving faster — 12-week programs producing job-ready practitioners vs. the slower continuing education model. The skills gap spawned an entire alternative education ecosystem that INFORMS and universities couldn't match for speed.

## Key points

- The big data skills gap was quantified and widely cited — institutional players like INFORMS, universities, and professional societies all mobilized around 2013.
- Operations research was a natural antecedent to data science — same mathematical toolkit, different scale and tooling.
- Data science bootcamps (Zipfian Academy, General Assembly) were the faster market response — weeks vs. semesters.
- Continuing education model: expensive and slow vs. the bootcamp model — but also more rigorous on statistical foundations.
- The skills gap framing drove enormous investment in data science education that ultimately produced both a supply glut and significant quality variance.
- INFORMS represented the old guard analytics community being forced to adapt as Python and Hadoop supplanted SAS and traditional BI tools.
