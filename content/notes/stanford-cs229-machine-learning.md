## Summary

Stanford CS229 is Andrew Ng's graduate machine learning course, and its materials page was a canonical bookmark for anyone self-studying ML in 2013. The lecture notes — hand-typeset in LaTeX with unusually clear explanations — covered the full traditional ML curriculum: supervised learning (linear regression, logistic regression, generalized linear models, SVMs, kernel methods), unsupervised learning (k-means, EM algorithm, PCA, ICA), reinforcement learning, and regularization.

The CS229 notes were widely used outside Stanford as primary learning materials — more readable than most textbooks, freely available, and written by someone who clearly understood the intuitions behind the mathematics. The notes on support vector machines (the margin derivation, Lagrangian duality, the kernel trick) and the EM algorithm (with detailed derivations of the E-step and M-step) are particularly well-regarded and circulated independently for years.

Andrew Ng went on to teach Machine Learning on Coursera (launched 2012) — effectively a more accessible version of CS229 that brought these materials to millions of learners. CS229 was the source material; the Coursera course was the mass-market adaptation. Both were influential in building the generation of ML practitioners that entered the field between 2012 and 2017.

## Key points

- CS229 lecture notes: free, rigorous, and clear — the Platonic ideal of ML course notes; still useful for understanding derivations that online courses skip.
- Andrew Ng's pedagogical approach: intuition first, then mathematics — explains what an algorithm does conceptually before deriving why it works.
- Coverage: supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning + optimization theory — more comprehensive than most single courses.
- Coursera ML course: the mass-market adaptation of CS229 — by 2013, over a million people had enrolled, making it the most influential ML course in history.
- Pre-deep learning curriculum: CS229 in 2013 covered neural networks but not as the dominant paradigm — that shift came with CS231n and CS224n after 2015.
- Still maintained: the CS229 materials have been updated over the years but the core lecture notes remain broadly relevant.
