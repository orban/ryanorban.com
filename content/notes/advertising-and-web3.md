## Summary

Published in The Pull Request, this essay takes a contrarian position: advertising as a business model is worth defending and preserving in whatever web3 architecture comes next. The dominant tone in crypto and web3 circles circa 2022 was that ads were surveillance capitalism at its worst and the token economy would replace them entirely. This piece pushes back on that.

The core argument is that advertising, despite its problems, has funded enormous amounts of free content and open access to information. The alternative — everything behind paywalls or token gates — creates different access problems. Web3 doesn't automatically solve the problem of who pays for content; it just changes the payment mechanism. Token-gated content is still gated content.

The attention economy critique of advertising is real — algorithmic feeds optimized for engagement created genuine harms. But the author separates the business model (selling ads) from the distribution mechanism (algorithmic recommendation) that made those ads so problematic. A web3 world that replaces ads with micropayments or NFT memberships still needs discovery mechanisms, and those mechanisms will still face the same incentive pressures.

## Key points

- The attention economy problem was amplified by algorithmic feeds, not inherent to advertising itself
- Crypto and web3 proponents often conflate surveillance-based ad targeting with advertising as a funding model
- Token-gated content solves publisher monetization but not public access — paywalls by another name
- Micropayments and NFT memberships have different UX problems: friction, speculation, complexity
- Whatever comes after the current web still needs a way to fund content — probably some form of advertising survives
