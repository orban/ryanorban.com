## Summary

This Shivon Zilis piece (then at Bloomberg Beta, later at OpenAI and Neuralink) is an early attempt to map the machine intelligence startup ecosystem — categorizing companies by application domain and underlying technique. The 2014 edition predates the deep learning takeover; the landscape included traditional ML, NLP, computer vision, and vertical applications (enterprise intelligence, personal analytics, etc.).

The piece represents a moment when the AI industry was still fragmented across many approaches — before deep learning had comprehensively displaced other methods for most perception tasks, and before the transformer architecture had unified NLP. Companies were building on a mix of SVMs, gradient boosting, shallow neural nets, and early deep learning applications. The categorical distinctions that felt meaningful in 2014 (application ML vs. infrastructure ML, horizontal vs. vertical) have since collapsed as infrastructure became commoditized and applications became defined more by data access than technique.

The Zilis machine intelligence landscape became an annual publication (produced with Jim Kurtz at Bloomberg Beta), growing from ~200 companies in 2014 to thousands in later editions. As a historical artifact, the 2014 edition shows what serious practitioners thought was technically interesting and commercially viable just before the deep learning wave made many of those bets obsolete — and created new categories that didn't yet exist.

## Key points

- 2014 snapshot of ML startup landscape — before deep learning dominance and before transformer era.
- Organized by application domain and technique — categories that have since been restructured by technical consolidation.
- By Shivon Zilis (Bloomberg Beta) — became an annual publication tracking the AI industry.
- The 200-company 2014 map grew to thousands by 2020 as the category expanded.
- Historical value: shows which problems were considered hard and which approaches were being tried before LLMs.
- Useful comparison to current landscape: what's commoditized now was differentiated then.
