## Summary

Botto is an autonomous AI art system created by German artist Mario Klingemann (a pioneer of neural network art). Each week, Botto generates 350 candidate artworks using a combination of text-to-image neural networks — the system generates a descriptive sentence, feeds it to an image model, and produces a candidate. These 350 images are then presented to a community of BOTTO token holders who vote on their preferences. The single work that wins the community vote gets minted as an NFT and auctioned.

The voting feedback loop is the core mechanism. As the community votes, Botto's model updates its understanding of what the audience responds to — building what Klingemann calls a sense of taste. Over time, the model steers its generation toward images the community has historically valued. This is RLHF applied to aesthetic preference rather than instruction following: human feedback shapes model behavior continuously.

The financial result: Botto made $1 million in NFT sales within its first five weeks, and exhibited at Art Basel Miami — bridging the gap between the traditional art world and generative AI. The governance model (token holders vote, one NFT per week) is a DAO-style structure applied to art production. It's one of the most coherent real-world experiments in human-AI collaborative creativity: not a tool that humans use, but an agent with its own evolving aesthetic identity shaped by its audience.

## Key points

- Weekly cycle: 350 AI-generated candidates → BOTTO token holder voting → one NFT minted and auctioned
- Community feedback continuously trains Botto's taste — a real implementation of RLHF for aesthetics
- By Mario Klingemann — one of the founding figures of neural network art
- $1M in sales within 5 weeks; exhibited at Art Basel Miami
- Governance: DAO-style token holder participation in creative decisions
- Distinct from AI-as-tool: Botto is framed as an autonomous artist with an evolving identity
