## Summary

This r/MachineLearning post links to metacurate.io's ranking of the most-cited and most-discussed arXiv machine learning papers from 2021. The list captures the field at a genuinely pivotal moment — the year diffusion models broke through (DDPM, DALL-E 1), vision transformers (ViT) proved that transformer architectures generalized beyond NLP, and scaling laws for language models were formalized.

Key papers that would dominate these rankings: DALL-E (multimodal generation), CLIP (contrastive image-text learning from OpenAI), ViT (Vision Transformer from Google), Codex (code generation from GPT-3 family), and DDPM (Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models) from Jonathan Ho et al. at Google Brain. The year also saw AlphaFold 2's impact propagate through the community even though the paper was late 2020.

Looking at 2021 from 2022 (when this was bookmarked), the trajectory was clear: transformers had taken over both vision and NLP, scaling was the dominant paradigm, and generative models were rapidly improving. What wasn't yet visible was how quickly Stable Diffusion's open release (August 2022) would change the accessibility of these techniques or how RLHF would transform language model usability (ChatGPT, November 2022).

## Key points

- 2021 was the year of vision transformers — ViT proved transformers generalize beyond NLP
- CLIP and DALL-E 1 established the multimodal direction that DALL-E 2, Stable Diffusion, and Midjourney would exploit
- DDPM (diffusion probabilistic models) laid the groundwork for the 2022 image generation explosion
- Codex demonstrated LLM code generation capability that would become GitHub Copilot
- Metacurate.io ranked by community engagement signals — a reasonable proxy for "papers people actually read and discussed"
