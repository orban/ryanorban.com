## Summary

Framework Ventures' compensation report addresses how Web3 organizations — crypto protocols, DAOs, and crypto-native companies — structure pay differently from traditional technology companies. The key difference: token compensation replaces or supplements equity, creating different vesting mechanics, tax implications, and risk profiles. A protocol engineer receiving tokens in a liquid token rather than equity in a private company has immediate liquidity but also immediate mark-to-market exposure.

The report covers salary benchmarks across Web3 roles (protocol engineers, smart contract auditors, product, growth, community) during a period (2022) when crypto companies were competing aggressively for engineering talent from traditional tech. Base salaries in Web3 had converged toward FAANG levels for technical roles, with token grants on top. The structure of token grants varied widely: some projects gave unvested tokens at launch, others used 4-year vesting like traditional equity, and DAOs often used streaming payments via Sablier or Superfluid.

The DAO compensation case is particularly unusual: members may be compensated in protocol tokens for contributor work, with no formal employment relationship. Valuing this compensation requires making assumptions about token price, vesting schedules, and lockup periods. The report noted that during the 2021-2022 bull market, many Web3 contributors were effectively very well-compensated in retrospect; the 2022 crypto winter reversed this for new grants at depressed prices.

## Key points

- Web3 compensation mixes base salary (often at FAANG-comparable levels) with token compensation instead of equity.
- Token grants have immediate liquidity but mark-to-market exposure — different risk profile than private equity.
- DAO contributor compensation is structurally unusual: streaming payments, no formal employment, volatile token value.
- Engineering talent was competitive in 2021-2022; Web3 salaries tracked traditional tech with tokens on top.
- Tax treatment of token compensation is complex: tokens may be ordinary income at receipt, not capital gains at liquidity.
- Superfluid and Sablier enable streaming payment mechanics unique to crypto-native organizations.
