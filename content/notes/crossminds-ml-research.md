## Summary

Crossminds was a platform for discovering and watching technical machine learning research videos — primarily conference talk recordings from NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR, and similar venues, plus invited lectures and tutorials. The goal was to surface high-quality ML content with better organization than YouTube, where relevant research talks compete with tutorial videos and course content.

The platform launched around 2020 when the explosion of virtual conferences (accelerated by COVID-19) meant that most major ML conference talks were suddenly being recorded and posted online. Crossminds tried to be the curated index for this content — tagging videos by topic, institution, and paper, so researchers could find talks related to specific papers or areas.

The broader problem Crossminds was solving is real: navigating ML research video content is genuinely hard. Papers With Code organizes papers and implementations; Semantic Scholar organizes citations and abstracts; but finding the actual conference presentation for a paper you're reading often requires manual searching. By 2022–2023, most major conferences began hosting recordings themselves, and YouTube channels became the norm, which reduced Crossminds' differentiation. The site appears to have wound down.

## Key points

- Curated index of ML research conference talks — NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR — with topic-based organization.
- Launched during the virtual conference era (2020) when most talks suddenly became available as recordings.
- Alternative to searching YouTube directly for ML research content; aimed at research-quality signal filtering.
- Potentially defunct by 2023 as conferences adopted self-hosted recordings and YouTube channels.
- Alternatives: Papers With Code for implementations, Yannic Kilcher's YouTube channel for paper walkthroughs.
