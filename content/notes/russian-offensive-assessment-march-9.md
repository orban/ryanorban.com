## Summary

The Institute for the Study of War's assessment from March 9, 2022 — two weeks into Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine that began on February 24. At this point in the war, Russia's lightning advance on Kyiv had stalled. The expected rapid fall of the capital hadn't materialized; Ukrainian resistance had been far more effective than Russian planning assumed.

The March 9 assessment would have analyzed: the stalled Russian column north of Kyiv (which satellite imagery showed stretching 40+ miles but making little progress), the developing siege of Mariupol in the south, the Russian encirclement attempt around Kherson and the southern axis, and the ongoing pressure in the Donbas.

The operational picture at this point was revealing: Russia had launched a major conventional offensive with expectations of rapid Ukrainian collapse (likely within 72-96 hours based on their planning assumptions), but encountered effective resistance, supply chain problems (fuel, ammunition, maintenance), and poor combined-arms coordination. The initial Russian failure to achieve air superiority was a key factor — unlike their doctrine assumed, Ukrainian air defenses and aircraft remained functional into the second week.

This document was bookmarked during a period when the question of whether Kyiv would hold was still live and the conflict's ultimate trajectory was genuinely uncertain. ISW's detailed operational analysis was one of the few reliable sources cutting through the information fog.

## Key points

- March 9: two weeks in, Russia's advance on Kyiv had stalled — the expected rapid capitulation hadn't happened.
- Russian operational failures: supply chain problems, poor combined arms coordination, failure to achieve air superiority.
- Mariupol siege beginning — the city's eventual 80-day defense was already being set up.
- ISW assessments provided operational specificity absent from mainstream media coverage.
- Historical context: these early assessments documented the first major reversal of expectations in the conflict.
