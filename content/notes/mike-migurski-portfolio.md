## Summary

Mike Migurski was the technical director at Stamen Design, the San Francisco data visualization and mapping studio, from the mid-2000s through 2012. His personal site (mike.teczno.com) was a technical blog covering visualization projects, mapping work, and open data. He left Stamen in 2012 to become the CTO of Code for America, where he worked on civic technology projects including work that eventually became the basis for California's new unemployment benefits portal.

At Stamen, Migurski was central to several influential projects: Prettymaps (a stylized OpenStreetMap rendering), early tile-based map rendering infrastructure, and various client projects combining data and cartography. His technical writing was well-regarded in the visualization community for being specific about implementation details rather than staying at the level of high-concept.

The save context (January 2013, same cluster as Sha Hwang and data visualization links) reflects the tight-knit SF data visualization community at the time — a group of practitioners at Stamen Design, IDEO, and independent studios who were pushing the frontier of web-based mapping and interactive data. Migurski's site was a reference point for anyone working in that space.

## Key points

- Stamen Design: SF studio known for Toner, Watercolor, and Terrain map tiles (built on OpenStreetMap) — widely used as basemaps in data visualization projects
- Code for America: civic technology fellowship and advocacy organization; Migurski joined as CTO in 2013
- Technical focus: map rendering, tile infrastructure, OpenStreetMap ecosystem — contributed to the open mapping stack
- Context: the SF data visualization community in 2013 was small and interconnected; Stamen, Mapbox, and independent practitioners formed the core of the ecosystem
- Prettymaps: a Stamen experiment using OpenStreetMap data rendered with pure geometric abstraction; influential for its aesthetics
