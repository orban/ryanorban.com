## Summary

This Hacker News thread is a useful artifact of the community's financial consensus in 2020. Someone with $450K in cash asks what to do with it. The top-voted responses cluster predictably around the Bogleheads philosophy: invest in low-cost index funds (Vanguard VTSAX or equivalent), don't try to time the market, use dollar-cost averaging if you're psychologically unable to invest a lump sum, maximize tax-advantaged accounts (401k, IRA, HSA) first.

The thread also surfaces the tensions in personal finance advice: lump-sum investing vs. dollar-cost averaging (the data consistently shows lump sum wins on average, but DCA reduces psychological regret), the role of an emergency fund (have 3-6 months expenses in cash before investing), real estate vs. equities for building wealth, and the question of international diversification.

Hacker News demographics — primarily tech workers with above-average incomes — shape the advice: several comments address concentrated employer stock risk, RSU vesting strategies, and the specific tax situation of someone with sudden liquidity (a common tech worker scenario from a company sale or IPO). The advice is generally sound but skewed toward the tech-worker situation.

## Key points

- Consensus HN advice for a large cash position: index funds, don't time the market, max tax-advantaged accounts first.
- Lump sum vs. DCA: data favors lump sum; DCA reduces psychological regret for volatile markets.
- Tax-advantaged priority: 401k (employer match) → HSA → Roth IRA → taxable brokerage.
- Emergency fund (3-6 months expenses) before investing large sums.
- HN-specific advice: handling concentrated stock positions, RSU tax strategies, sudden liquidity from tech events.
