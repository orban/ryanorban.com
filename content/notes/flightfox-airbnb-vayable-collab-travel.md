## Summary

In 2012, Flightfox, Airbnb, and Vayable combined their services for an experiment: a fully crowd-sourced vacation where the flights were optimized by Flightfox travel hackers, accommodation was booked via Airbnb, and the activities and tours were arranged through Vayable's marketplace of local experiences. The combination was an early attempt to bundle the emerging sharing economy stack into a single coherent travel product.

Each company occupied a distinct niche that had no traditional equivalent. Flightfox was a marketplace where expert travel hackers competed to find the best fares for a given itinerary — more like crowdsourced flight search than a traditional booking agent. Airbnb had just raised its Series B and was becoming mainstream for non-hotel accommodation. Vayable (later acquired and shut down) let locals sell curated experiences — tours, cooking classes, neighborhood walks — direct to travelers.

The bundle pointed at a plausible future: travel entirely intermediated by peer-to-peer platforms, with no traditional airline, hotel, or tour operator in the chain. The missing piece that the tweet author noticed — a "Buy This Trip Now" button — was the coordination layer that would have made this a real product rather than a demonstration. That coordination problem (aggregating and transacting across multiple sharing-economy platforms) is essentially what companies like Airbnb's "Experiences" feature and eventually bundled travel aggregators tried to solve in the following years.

## Key points

- Three sharing-economy companies (flight optimization + home-sharing + local experiences) bundled into one travel package — a full peer-to-peer travel stack.
- 2012 timing: Airbnb post-Series B, sharing economy terminology just emerging — this was experimentation before the category had its name.
- The missing "Buy Now" coordination layer was the real product gap — aggregating cross-platform bookings into one transaction.
- Vayable (local experience marketplace) was acquired and shut down; Airbnb's Experiences later absorbed the use case.
- Presaged the platform bundling question: will sharing-economy verticals integrate or stay siloed? Largely stayed siloed, with Airbnb winning breadth in accommodation and experiences.
