## Summary

Square was founded in 2009 by Jack Dorsey and Jim McKelvey around a simple insight: small merchants and individuals couldn't accept credit card payments because merchant accounts were too expensive and complicated to set up. The Square Reader — a tiny dongle that plugged into an iPhone headphone jack — turned any smartphone into a point-of-sale terminal. By 2012, Square was processing billions in payments annually and had grown from street vendors to small businesses.

AllThingsD reported in April 2012 that Square's next funding round would value the company at approximately $4 billion — making it one of the most valuable private fintech companies in the world at the time. The payments industry was ripe for disruption: dominated by legacy processors and banks with high fees, complex contracts, and technology that hadn't meaningfully evolved in decades. Square was winning by being dramatically simpler and more accessible.

The $4B valuation was notable for its speed: 3 years from founding to multi-billion-dollar valuation, in a category (financial services) that was traditionally considered too regulated and capital-intensive for rapid startup growth. Square was demonstrating that the payments stack could be abstracted and simplified the same way AWS had abstracted server infrastructure. The momentum was real — Starbucks would later announce a Square partnership in mid-2012. Square went public in 2015 at a market cap of ~$3B, below this private valuation, before later growing substantially.

## Key points

- Square founded 2009, raised at ~$4B valuation by early 2012 — one of the fastest paths to multi-billion valuation in fintech.
- The Square Reader reduced merchant account complexity from weeks of paperwork to plug in, swipe — transforming who could accept payments.
- Jack Dorsey was simultaneously CEO of Twitter and CEO of Square in 2012, which was itself notable.
- Payments was considered too regulated for rapid disruption — Square proved otherwise by staying above the bank licensing layer.
- Starbucks partnership (announced 2012) validated Square as enterprise-capable, not just a small-merchant tool.
- IPO in 2015 at a lower-than-private-round valuation was a cautionary note about late-stage private valuations.
