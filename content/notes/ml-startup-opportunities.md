## Summary

This guest post on VC Cafe from late 2015 attempts to map the opportunity landscape for machine learning startups — written at a moment when deep learning had proven itself on ImageNet and speech recognition but before the current wave of consumer AI products. The analysis distinguishes between ML as a feature (adding a recommendation system to an existing product) versus ML as a fundamental defensible moat.

The piece identifies verticals where machine learning provides structural advantage: healthcare diagnostics (where labeled training data is scarce and hard to acquire, creating a moat), legal and document processing (high-value repetitive tasks with clear error costs), financial fraud detection (adversarial setting requiring continuous learning), and industrial predictive maintenance (proprietary sensor data as an asset). In each case, the moat is the data and the domain expertise, not the algorithms themselves, which were increasingly commoditized through open-source frameworks like TensorFlow and scikit-learn.

One notable mention is ML education — the post explicitly calls out the shortage of practitioners as an opportunity, which is directly relevant to Galvanize and Zipfian Academy's business at the time. This was one of the cleaner early articulations that the bottleneck in ML deployment wasn't compute or algorithms but human capital.

## Key points

- ML as moat requires proprietary data + domain expertise — algorithms alone are increasingly commoditized.
- Strong verticals: healthcare diagnostics, legal/document processing, fraud detection, industrial IoT.
- Data flywheel matters: the more users, the more training data, the better the model, the more users.
- ML education cited as an underrated opportunity — the bottleneck was practitioner supply, not algorithms.
- Written in Dec 2015, just after Google open-sourced TensorFlow — captures the moment of the shift to accessible deep learning.
