## Summary

This guide by Marc Nitzsche covers vocabulary acquisition through the lens of cognitive science rather than traditional language learning advice. The core findings from memory research align on a few key principles: spaced repetition dramatically outperforms massed practice (studying the same words over spaced intervals beats cramming), retrieval practice (testing yourself on words) is more effective than re-reading, and contextual learning (encountering words in sentences or stories) builds deeper representations than isolated word-definition pairs.

The practical recommendation is spaced repetition software (SRS) — Anki being the canonical tool — which algorithmically schedules reviews at optimal intervals based on your performance. The forgetting curve (Ebbinghaus, 1885) establishes that memory decays predictably without review; SRS works by scheduling reviews at the point just before forgetting, which forces retrieval at the optimal moment for consolidation.

Comprehensible input (from Krashen's acquisition-learning distinction) is the complementary approach: exposure to language at a level just slightly above your current understanding, through reading or listening, naturally grows vocabulary through context. The evidence suggests both approaches work, and combining SRS for high-frequency vocabulary and comprehensible input for natural acquisition is close to optimal.

## Key points

- Spaced repetition beats massed practice — the forgetting curve is real; review before forgetting.
- Retrieval practice outperforms re-reading — testing yourself is more effective than passive exposure.
- Contextual learning (words in sentences) beats isolated word-definition pairs.
- Anki and spaced repetition software implement optimal spacing algorithmically.
- Comprehensible input (Krashen) complements SRS — natural acquisition through just-above-level exposure.
- Vocabulary learning research is well-established; the hard part is consistent implementation.
