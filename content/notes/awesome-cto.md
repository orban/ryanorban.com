## Summary

[Awesome CTO](/notes/awesome-cto/) is a curated resource list by Kuchin targeting Chief Technology Officers and senior engineering leaders, especially in startup contexts. Unlike generic engineering reading lists, it's organized around the actual problems a CTO faces: building and scaling engineering teams, navigating technical debt, setting technical vision, communicating with non-technical executives, hiring senior engineers, and making build vs buy decisions.

The resource list draws from a mix of blog posts, books, and talks. It covers topics like Conway's Law (system structure follows org structure), engineering culture, technical roadmap management, and the psychology of leading engineers. The startup emphasis is useful: a CTO at a 20-person company has fundamentally different concerns than a VP Engineering at a 2,000-person company, and most leadership resources don't distinguish between them.

For practitioners transitioning from individual contributor to engineering leadership, the list is a good map of what's worth reading. It includes classic management texts alongside practitioner blog posts from people who've built teams at notable companies. The GitHub format means it's updated occasionally with new additions.

## Key points

- Organized around CTO-specific concerns: hiring, technical vision, engineering culture, system design at org scale, exec communication.
- Startup-weighted: most useful for leaders at companies where the CTO is still hands-on in architecture and hiring decisions.
- Covers the hard problems: technical debt management, transitioning from IC to manager, when to rewrite vs refactor.
- References Conway's Law and org design as architecture concerns — the insight that org structure and system structure co-evolve.
- Complement to more general leadership lists like [professional-programming](/notes/professional-programming/) and the Manager's Path by Camille Fournier.
