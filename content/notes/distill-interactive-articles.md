## Summary

This Distill.pub piece is self-referential in the best way: it's an interactive article *about* interactive articles, demonstrating the medium it describes. The core argument is that interactive articles enable a qualitatively different kind of learning than static papers — they support active reading (readers can manipulate parameters and see effects), reduce cognitive load through on-demand detail, and build genuine understanding rather than the passive recognition that reading prose often produces.

The piece formalizes several design patterns for effective interactive articles: explorable explanations (readers control variables, see outcomes), guided exploration (interactive elements with constraints that prevent confusion), linked views (multiple coordinated visualizations that respond to the same interaction), and branching narratives (readers follow different paths based on their background or interest). Each pattern is demonstrated inline, so the article is both an argument and a tutorial.

Distill.pub was founded on the premise that machine learning research deserved better communication tools than LaTeX PDFs. The journal published interactive articles as a first-class format alongside (or instead of) traditional papers. Communicating with Interactive Articles is the theoretical foundation for that editorial stance — articulating why the extra engineering effort to make articles interactive produces genuine epistemic value, not just visual appeal.

## Key points

- Active reading > passive reading: interactive articles require engagement, which improves retention and understanding
- Design patterns: explorable explanations, linked views, branching narratives, guided exploration
- Explorable explanations (term from Bret Victor): articles where parameters are dials — readers discover effects by adjusting
- The medium argument: certain concepts (probability distributions, attention patterns, optimization landscapes) are genuinely easier to understand interactively than through prose and static figures
- Distill.pub as publisher: ML research in interactive web format; closed in 2021 but articles remain canonical
