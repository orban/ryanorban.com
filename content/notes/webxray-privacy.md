## Summary

webXray is a forensic web analysis platform developed by Dr. Timothy Libert, originating from 2012 academic research and commercialized in 2023. The core function: scanning websites to detect actual tracking and privacy violations — what the code does versus what the privacy policy claims. It's positioned against the compliance theater of cookie banners and checkboxes that dominate current privacy tooling.

The database covers 2.4M+ scanned pages across 1.2M+ websites. Users can search for specific trackers or scan full sites from real geographic locations (important because some tracking behavior is geofenced). The platform captures forensic evidence suitable for litigation: preserved website states, documented consent bypass mechanisms, and audit trails. This is the tool for lawyers, not just developers.

The academic lineage matters. Libert's research has influenced 900+ peer-reviewed citations and established webXray as a serious empirical tool for web privacy research before it became a commercial product. The underlying methodology — analyzing third-party requests, cookies, and fingerprinting signals — reflects what's needed to actually audit compliance rather than assume it.

## Key points

- Forensic web tracking analysis — detects real violations, not just cookie banner compliance.
- 2.4M+ page scans, 1.2M+ website database, scanning from real geographic locations.
- Evidence documentation for litigation: preserved sites, consent bypass analysis.
- Customers: class action law firms, Fortune 100 compliance teams, privacy litigators.
- Founded on 12+ years of academic research (2012) by Dr. Timothy Libert.
- Addresses the gap between privacy policy claims and actual tracking behavior.
