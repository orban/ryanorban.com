## Summary

This Twitter thread by Julian Shapiro covers documented decision-making techniques that both Elon Musk and Jeff Bezos have described in interviews and writing. The most well-known is Bezos's regret minimization framework: when facing a major decision, project yourself to age 80 and ask which choice you'd regret more — doing it and failing, or not doing it at all. This reframes risk as regret rather than probability, which tends to push toward action for asymmetric opportunities.

Musk's documented approach is first-principles thinking: rather than reasoning by analogy ("this is how it's always been done"), break problems down to fundamental physical or logical truths and reason up from there. Applied to battery costs, the question isn't how much do batteries cost? but "what are the raw material costs of the chemical components?" — which revealed that batteries were much cheaper to manufacture than their market price suggested.

The thread also covers the two-pizza rule (Bezos: meeting rooms should be small enough that two pizzas feed everyone — signals against large committee decisions) and Type 1 vs Type 2 decisions (Type 1 = irreversible, consequential, decide slowly; Type 2 = reversible, decide quickly — most organizational slowness comes from applying Type 1 caution to Type 2 decisions).

## Key points

- Regret minimization framework (Bezos): decide from the perspective of your 80-year-old self — minimize future regret.
- First-principles thinking (Musk): question assumptions, reason from fundamentals, don't accept inherited constraints.
- Type 1 vs Type 2 decisions (Bezos): irreversible vs reversible — most orgs over-apply caution to reversible decisions.
- Two-pizza rule: signals preference for small decision-making groups.
