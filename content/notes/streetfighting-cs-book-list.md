## Summary

Nick Black (author of Notcurses and contributor to low-level systems work) maintains a curated reading list for developing genuine, deep computer science competence — what he calls streetfighting. The metaphor is explicit: these are books to read so you can cut through problems with precision, not collect credentials.

The list is organized by depth level. The introductory tier focuses on systems-level languages: C (Gustedt's *Modern C*, Stepanov's work), C++ (Stroustrup), and Rust (Klabnik's *The Rust Programming Language*). The intermediate tier is algorithms and systems programming: Knuth's *The Art of Computer Programming*, CLRS (*Introduction to Algorithms*), Stevens' *Advanced Programming in the UNIX Environment*, McKusick on BSD, and Kerrisk's *The Linux Programming Interface*. Architecture and performance round out this tier: Hennessy & Patterson's *Computer Organization and Design*.

The advanced tier pushes into computational complexity, type theory, functional programming, and mathematics — linear algebra, combinatorics. The explicit exclusions are notable: no machine learning, no quantum computing, no infosec. The author sees these as separate specializations rather than core streetfighter knowledge. The list assumes someone who wants to understand computers at the systems level, not someone chasing applied domains.

## Key points

- Structured by depth: introductory (C, C++, Rust) → intermediate (algorithms, systems) → advanced (theory, math).
- Core texts: CLRS, Knuth, Stevens, Kerrisk, Hennessy & Patterson.
- Explicitly excludes ML, quantum, infosec — treats these as separate specializations.
- From Nick Black — a practitioner's view of foundational CS, not an academic syllabus.
- The streetfighting framing emphasizes practical mastery over credentialing.
