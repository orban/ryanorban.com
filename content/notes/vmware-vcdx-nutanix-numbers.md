## Summary

This piece analyzed where VMware VCDX (VMware Certified Design Expert) holders worked — the elite tier of VMware certification — and found Nutanix ranking third alongside much larger established players like VCE (Vblock), EMC, and Dell. VCDX is the hardest VMware certification to earn and held primarily by senior data center architects at major infrastructure vendors and customers.

Nutanix achieving top-3 placement for VCDX holders in 2013 was a signal of serious enterprise credibility for what was then a 4-year-old startup. Nutanix was commercializing hyper-converged infrastructure (HCI) — collapsing storage, compute, and networking into a single software-defined appliance that could replace traditional SAN/NAS data center architectures.

Ryan saved this from a tweet — likely context-setting about Nutanix during a period when the company was a prominent SF startup (founded 2009, IPO would come in 2016). The technical community was closely watching whether HCI could genuinely displace traditional data center stacks.

## Key points

- Nutanix ranking #3 for VMware VCDX holders in 2013 signaled enterprise data center credibility despite being a relatively young startup.
- VCDX is the highest VMware certification — held by senior architects; distribution tracks where serious data center talent concentrates.
- Nutanix was building hyper-converged infrastructure: software-defined, collapse storage+compute+networking into commodity hardware appliances.
- The competitive set — VCE, EMC, Dell — represented the incumbent data center giants HCI was disrupting.
- Nutanix IPO'd in 2016; by 2020 it had become one of the dominant enterprise cloud infrastructure vendors.
