## Summary

LogoArchive is a purpose-built archive and research tool for logo design history, founded by Richard Baird and developed with Christian Laufenböck. Unlike image search engines or general design inspiration sites, LogoArchive is specifically focused on historical logos as research artifacts — with a team of over 40 international researchers curating entries and documenting provenance.

The use case is primarily historical and analytical research rather than just inspiration browsing. Designers and design historians use it to study how visual identity conventions evolved, how specific categories (financial services, pharmaceutical companies, automotive brands) developed distinctive design vocabularies, and how individual designers' influence spread across the field. The archival approach — with proper attribution and historical context — distinguishes it from Pinterest boards or design blogs that aggregate logos without documentation.

LogoArchive occupies an interesting niche in design resources: detailed enough to be useful for serious research, accessible enough for practitioners looking for historical precedents. The growth of community researchers beyond the founding team reflects the appetite for well-documented design history, which is underserved relative to other disciplines where historical documentation is considered important scholarly work.

## Key points

- Historical logo archive with documented provenance — not just inspiration but research-quality records.
- 40+ international researchers curate and document entries.
- Founded by Richard Baird; developed by Christian Laufenböck.
- Primary use: studying visual identity evolution, design convention development, designer influence tracing.
- Fills a real gap — design history documentation is underserved relative to other creative disciplines.
