## Summary

A Speedtest.net result from Gogo inflight wifi at 10,000 feet — shared with Not bad for 10,000 ft. The speedtest captured Gogo's air-to-ground network performance in 2012, when inflight internet was still relatively novel and the fact that it worked at all felt impressive.

Gogo (formerly Aircell) launched commercial inflight internet in 2008. The technology used air-to-ground connectivity: antennas on the plane connected to cell towers on the ground, with the plane's antenna tracking the towers as it flew. This worked well over land in the continental US but failed over oceans (where Gogo's satellite network was needed). Typical 2012 speeds: 1-3 Mbps download, shared across all passengers on the plane, with high latency (~600ms round trip due to the ground-to-tower-to-plane path).

The not bad framing reflected the 2012 standard: if you could load a webpage and check email, inflight wifi was functional. Streaming video was marginal; large downloads were effectively impossible. Gogo eventually switched to satellite connectivity for better speeds and ocean coverage, before facing competition from SpaceX Starlink's aviation service in the 2020s.

## Key points

- Gogo air-to-ground wifi: plane antenna tracks ground cell towers; works over continental US, fails over oceans.
- Typical 2012 speeds: 1-3 Mbps shared across all passengers, ~600ms latency.
- The 10,000 ft detail: altitude limits (FCC regulations required 10,000+ ft for inflight wifi use).
- Gogo eventually transitioned to satellite for better speeds; faces competition from Starlink aviation.
