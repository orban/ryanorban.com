## Summary

Sourcemap was a platform for visualizing and publishing supply chain data. Founded at MIT Media Lab by Leonardo Bonanni, it let companies and researchers map the origins of products — tracing raw materials from extraction through manufacturing, assembly, and distribution. The maps were interactive, showing geographic flows and associated data like carbon footprint and transport distances.

The content model was open: anyone could create and publish a supply chain map. Sourcemap built a library of maps for products ranging from coffee to smartphones to lipstick. The bookmark's comment ("where things come from — Tom's, lipstick, ham, etc.") captures the consumer-facing angle: you could look up supply chains for familiar brand-name products and see the global production network behind them.

The transparency angle was increasingly relevant in 2012. The Fair Trade movement, concern about conflict minerals (the Dodd-Frank Act Section 1502 requiring minerals conflict reporting had passed in 2010), and supply chain disruptions from the Japan earthquake (2011) had all raised awareness that global supply chains were complex and opaque. Sourcemap proposed making them legible.

The tool later pivoted to enterprise supply chain risk management — the B2B SaaS model proved more viable than the consumer-facing open platform.

## Key points

- Sourcemap (MIT Media Lab, Leonardo Bonanni): interactive supply chain visualization from raw material to consumer.
- Open content model: companies and researchers could publish their supply chains publicly.
- Maps included carbon footprint and distance data for each supply chain leg.
- Context: Dodd-Frank conflict minerals disclosure (2010), Fair Trade growth, supply chain disruptions creating demand for visibility.
- Later pivoted to enterprise supply chain risk management software — the open platform model gave way to B2B SaaS.
