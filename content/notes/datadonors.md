## Summary

[DataDonors](/notes/datadonors/) was a platform designed to match data science talent with nonprofits that needed analytical help but couldn't afford full-time data staff. The model is volunteer or pro-bono: data scientists donate their skills to organizations working on social problems. This is the data for good equivalent of pro-bono legal work or open source software contribution.

The platform launched alongside a cluster of similar initiatives in 2013-2014: Bayes Impact (same period, similar goal), DataKind (slightly earlier, 2012), and DrivenData (which ran hackathons for social good causes). All of these responded to the same observation: the data science talent pool was growing rapidly and concentrating in tech companies, while nonprofits and government agencies were data-rich but analytically underserved.

The challenge with these platforms is project scope. Nonprofits often need help with messy, poorly documented data, unclear objectives, and limited technical infrastructure — exactly the conditions where short volunteer engagements struggle to deliver value. DataKind developed a model with DataDives (intensive weekend events) and longer-term partnerships; [DataDonors](/notes/datadonors/) appears to have been a lighter-weight matching platform.

## Key points

- Volunteer talent-matching platform: data scientists donate skills to nonprofits that can't afford them.
- Part of 2013-2014 cluster of data for good organizations: DataKind, Bayes Impact, DrivenData.
- Challenge: nonprofit data projects often require more sustained engagement than volunteer models provide.
- DataKind emerged as the most durable of the cohort, with structured DataDive events and long-term partnerships.
- Reflects the same ethos driving Bayes Impact: redirecting growing data science talent toward social benefit.
