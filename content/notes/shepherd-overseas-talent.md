## Summary

Shepherd is a headhunting agency that finds employees in Latin America and Southeast Asia for US companies, at significantly lower cost than equivalent domestic hires. The pitch is explicit: 80% cost savings vs US equivalents, with Shepherd doing the sourcing and vetting. The model is targeted at early-stage startups and small businesses that need to hire for operations, customer support, administrative, or other roles without the fully-loaded cost of a US salary.

The category is EOR (Employer of Record) adjacent — services like Deel, Remote.com, and Oyster provide the compliance infrastructure to hire internationally, while agencies like Shepherd focus on the sourcing side. For a founder who doesn't want to learn recruiting in unfamiliar markets, paying an agency a placement fee to find candidates who have already been vetted is a reasonable tradeoff.

The model raises real questions about labor arbitrage and the long-term sustainability of significant pay gaps between equivalent roles in different geographies. The overseas talent framing papers over a relationship that's fundamentally about cost reduction. That said, it creates real employment opportunities in markets where the absolute salary — while far below US levels — can represent good compensation locally. The remote work infrastructure built during COVID made this model more viable at scale.

## Key points

- Headhunting agency placing overseas talent (Latin America, Southeast Asia) for US companies at ~80% cost savings.
- Focuses on sourcing and vetting; complements EOR services like Deel or Remote.com for compliance.
- Primary market: early-stage startups needing ops, support, and administrative hires to stretch runway.
- The model is fundamentally labor arbitrage — pay gap between markets is the value proposition.
- Remote work infrastructure from COVID made international sourcing more operationally practical.
