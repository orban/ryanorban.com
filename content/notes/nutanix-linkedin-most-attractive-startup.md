## Summary

LinkedIn released a ranking of the most attractive Bay Area startups for engineers in July 2013, based on engagement metrics from LinkedIn profiles — job transitions, follower counts, and employee career trajectories. Nutanix made the list, which was notable for a company that at the time was best known in enterprise data center circles and less visible to the broader software engineering community.

The ranking mattered as a recruiting signal. In 2013, Nutanix was in a high-growth phase: the company had been founded in 2009, raised significant venture funding, and was selling hyper-converged infrastructure into enterprise data centers. Being on a LinkedIn most attractive startup list helped surface the company to engineers who might not encounter it through traditional channels — Nutanix was competing for talent against consumer-facing companies with higher brand recognition.

Ryan bookmarked this while working at Nutanix, so this is also a personal artifact from that period.

## Key points

- LinkedIn's startup attractiveness rankings (2013 era) used engagement signals from profile data, not self-reported surveys — a data-driven approach to employer branding.
- Nutanix competed for engineering talent in an era when consumer tech (Google, Facebook, Twitter) dominated hiring pipelines; being on this list helped enterprise infrastructure companies get visibility.
- The timing: ~3 years before Nutanix's 2016 IPO, still in high-growth startup mode.
