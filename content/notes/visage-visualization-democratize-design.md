## Summary

Visage was a data visualization tool from Prismatic's 2014 coverage that positioned itself as making professional chart design accessible to non-designers. The premise: most charts are ugly because the people making them (analysts, journalists, researchers) aren't designers, and design tools like Illustrator are too complex. Visage aimed to sit between Excel charts and fully custom D3.js — give non-designers good defaults and a constrained design system that makes it hard to produce bad output.

The democratize design framing was common in 2014 tools. Canva (launched 2013) had similar positioning for general graphic design. Tableau took a similar approach for interactive data visualization. The underlying insight is correct: data visualization quality in organizations is bottlenecked by the distribution of design SKILL, not the distribution of data.

Visage's specific angle was editorial: the tool was oriented toward shareable graphics for marketing and communications rather than analytical exploration. This distinguished it from Tableau and D3.js-based tools, which prioritized analytical power over visual polish.

## Key points

- Targeted non-designers who need professional-looking charts and infographics without design expertise.
- Positioned between Excel (ugly, limited) and D3.js/Illustrator (powerful, complex).
- "Democratize design" framing was common in 2014: Canva, Tableau, and others took similar angles.
- Editorial orientation: shareable, publication-ready graphics rather than analytical dashboards.
- Constrained design system — good defaults are a form of UX that prevents bad choices.
