## Summary

This post from the Oscillatory Thoughts neuroscience/philosophy blog engaged with a paradox emerging from big data: as datasets grow large enough to be statistically powered for detecting any effect, and as machine learning can extract patterns from those datasets without human-specified hypotheses, we produce empirical findings that are technically valid but epistemically hollow. The pattern is real; the explanation is absent; the mechanism is unknown; the finding may not generalize.

The paradox of information framing: more data should mean more knowledge, but beyond a certain scale, data without theory produces correlation-mining that feels like discovery but isn't. Peter Norvig's famous line — "more data beats a better algorithm" — was being taken beyond its original scope to argue that data could replace mechanistic understanding entirely. The blog post pushed back: empiricism without mechanism produces a catalog, not science. You can predict flu outbreaks from search queries (as Google Flu Trends claimed in 2009), but without understanding *why* the correlation holds, you can't know when it breaks — and it does break (Google Flu Trends famously failed by 2013-2014).

The automated science concern: if machine learning finds patterns autonomously in large datasets, who is responsible for the hypothesis? Traditional scientific method requires a falsifiable prediction before an experiment. Data dredging produces observations, not tests. This was a prescient critique in 2013 — the replication crisis in psychology and social science was about to hit its peak, and the failure modes were exactly what this post described.

## Key points

- Paradox of deep data: sufficiently large datasets + automated pattern detection produces discoveries that are statistically valid but not scientifically meaningful.
- Mechanism vs. correlation: without understanding why a pattern holds, you can't predict when it breaks — the Google Flu Trends failure is the canonical example.
- Hypothesis-free science concern: machine learning extracts patterns without requiring a hypothesis — this violates the logical structure of falsifiable science.
- The replication crisis connection: data dredging and underpowered studies with flexible analysis choices produce unreliable published findings at scale.
- Norvig's "more data beats better algorithms" was sound engineering advice, not epistemology — the post argued for keeping the distinction clear.
