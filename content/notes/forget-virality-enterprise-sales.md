## Summary

Roman Stanek (then CEO of GoodData, a BI company) wrote this TechCrunch piece as a corrective to the prevailing startup wisdom of 2013 that product-led growth and virality were replacing traditional sales. His argument: for enterprise software — actual deals with procurement, IT security review, legal, and CFO sign-off — the fundamental dynamics of trust-based sales had not changed. You still needed relationships, an executive champion inside the customer, and the patience to navigate institutional buying processes.

The broader context was a tension in the enterprise software world between the SaaS model (which did change distribution and pricing) and the underlying sales motion. Salesforce was often held up as the model: it democratized access to CRM with a subscription model, but it built a massive direct sales force and partner ecosystem to close enterprise deals. The viral elements (low-cost entry, in-product sharing) drove top-of-funnel, but the revenue came from sales-driven expansion in large accounts.

Stanek's point survives in the distinction between PLG (product-led growth) and enterprise sales: PLG works well for mid-market with technical buyers who can self-serve, but complex enterprise sales — security reviews, procurement, multi-stakeholder consensus — still require dedicated account executives and long sales cycles. This distinction is standard knowledge in B2B SaaS today, but in 2013 it was a useful corrective to hype.

## Key points

- Enterprise buying has institutional friction that viral loops can't overcome: procurement, security review, legal, budget cycles
- Executive champion: the internal buyer advocate who makes enterprise deals happen — relationship-driven, can't be replaced by product virality
- PLG vs enterprise sales distinction: low-friction entry works for acquiring users and mid-market; enterprise revenue still requires sales motion
- Salesforce is the canonical example: SaaS model + massive sales force + partner ecosystem; the model was never purely self-serve at the enterprise tier
- This dynamic has been confirmed by every major enterprise SaaS company since: even the most product-led companies (Slack, Figma) built significant sales teams for large accounts
