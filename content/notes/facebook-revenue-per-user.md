## Summary

In April 2012, just weeks before Facebook's IPO, TechCrunch published an analysis of Facebook's revenue per user: roughly $1.21 per quarter, or about $4.84 per year. The number came from Facebook's own S-1 filing in preparation for the IPO. The piece was important because it made concrete a relationship that users had been treating as abstract — Facebook was free, but that meant Facebook was selling advertising against user attention and data, generating real revenue per person.

The framing "you earn Facebook $1.21" (putting the user in the role of generator rather than recipient) was deliberately provocative. At the time, the critique "you are the product" was gaining traction in tech discourse — the idea that when a service is free, the user isn't the customer but the product being sold. The $1.21 number made that abstract claim financially precise. For users, it raised the question of whether the product they were getting was worth the value they were generating.

The advertising model that drove this revenue was primarily display advertising and social ads, not yet the performance advertising and targeting infrastructure that would make Facebook a dominant advertising platform in subsequent years. In 2012, Facebook's revenue per user was significantly lower than Google's — a fact that shaped the skepticism around Facebook's IPO valuation and explained why the stock dropped substantially after listing.

## Key points

- Facebook earned ~$1.21/user/quarter ($4.84/year) in early 2012, from the S-1 filing.
- Made concrete the "you are the product" critique — giving the user relationship a dollar figure.
- Facebook's revenue per user was far below Google's, which reflected weaker ad targeting and lower commercial intent.
- Published weeks before the Facebook IPO — shaped investor perception of the revenue model's limitations.
- Mobile advertising was near-zero for Facebook in 2012, which was the key risk the IPO underwriters flagged.
- Advertising revenue per user has since grown dramatically — by 2021 Facebook earned ~$11/user/quarter in North America.
