## Summary

A curated list of films recommended for physics students, covering both Hollywood dramatizations and documentaries. The list spans several categories: biographical films about physicists (The Theory of Everything about Stephen Hawking, Oppenheimer, A Beautiful Mind about John Nash), science fiction that engages seriously with physics concepts (Interstellar for general relativity and black holes, Contact for SETI), and documentaries covering particle physics and cosmology.

The list reflects a common recommendation practice in physics education — using film to make abstract ideas emotionally tangible. Interstellar is notable for having Kip Thorne as a scientific consultant, making its depictions of gravitational time dilation and black hole accretion discs more accurate than typical sci-fi. Oppenheimer's historical treatment of the Manhattan Project and the ethical dimensions of physics research is frequently cited in courses on science and society.

The utility of such a list is mostly motivational rather than educational — films simplify and dramatize, but they can inspire students to go deeper into the underlying physics. The biopic genre in particular has popularized physicists who were previously known mainly within the field.

## Key points

- Biographical films: The Theory of Everything (Hawking), Oppenheimer, A Beautiful Mind (Nash).
- Hard sci-fi picks: Interstellar (relativity, black holes — Kip Thorne as consultant), Contact (SETI).
- Value is motivational: films make physics emotionally engaging, not technically precise.
- Oppenheimer serves double duty as physics history and ethics of science and society.
- Lightweight reference list — primarily for inspiration and cultural grounding.
