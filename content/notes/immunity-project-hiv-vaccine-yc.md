## Summary

Immunity Project was a nonprofit startup that used machine learning and data science on HIV genomic data to design a free HIV vaccine. The approach targeted the virus's conserved regions — parts of the HIV genome that can't mutate without losing function — rather than the highly variable surface proteins that conventional vaccine attempts had targeted. They were accepted into Y Combinator as only the second nonprofit in its history.

The story captured attention in early 2014 as an example of data science applied to one of medicine's hard problems. The key insight was that ML on large genetic datasets could identify immune responses that consistently appeared in elite controllers — people who are HIV-positive but never progress to AIDS — and then reverse-engineer what about their immune response was protective.

## Key points

- Applied machine learning to genomic data from elite controllers (HIV+ individuals who never develop AIDS) to identify protective immune responses
- Targeted conserved viral regions rather than surface proteins — the HIV surface mutates too fast for conventional vaccines to track
- Y Combinator backing was significant: the second nonprofit ever accepted, signaling that mission-driven science startups could fit the accelerator model
- Planned to distribute the vaccine for free — nonprofit model from the start
- An early high-profile case of machine learning in computational biology and vaccine design, before the field became standard practice
