## Summary

Derek Sivers summarizes Peter Attia's 2023 book *Outlive: The Science and Art of Longevity*. Attia is a physician specializing in longevity medicine whose core argument is that conventional medicine optimizes for lifespan (not dying) rather than healthspan (staying functional and healthy). The book introduces Medicine 3.0: a proactive, personalized, data-driven approach to health that starts preventive interventions decades earlier than standard care.

Attia's framework centers on what he calls the four horsemen of longevity-limiting disease: atherosclerosis (cardiovascular disease), cancer, Alzheimer's, and metabolic syndrome. He argues that each of these develops over decades, but conventional medicine only intervenes when symptoms appear — by which point significant damage has occurred. The solution is early measurement, lifestyle modification (exercise, sleep, nutrition), and where appropriate, pharmacological intervention years before standard guidelines recommend.

The most actionable claims: VO2 max is one of the strongest predictors of longevity; zone 2 training (low-intensity aerobic exercise) is undervalued relative to high-intensity training; muscle mass matters more for longevity than most people assume; and sleep quality is underinvested in relative to its impact on almost every health marker.

Sivers' notes capture the book's practical framework and specific recommendations, filtering through his characteristic lens of what would I actually change?

## Key points

- Peter Attia's Medicine 3.0: proactive, personalized intervention decades before symptoms appear.
- The "four horsemen": atherosclerosis, cancer, Alzheimer's, metabolic syndrome — all preventable decades earlier.
- VO2 max is one of the best longevity predictors; zone 2 training significantly improves it.
- Muscle mass matters for longevity: sarcopenia (muscle loss with age) is a primary aging driver.
- Sleep quality underinvested in relative to its outsized impact on cognitive and physical health.
