## Summary

The Lowline project, conceived by James Ramsey and Dan Barasch, proposed transforming the disused Williamsburg Bridge Trolley Terminal on Manhattan's Lower East Side into an underground public park. The terminal — a 1908 structure that was last used in 1948 — sits beneath Delancey Street, spanning roughly 60,000 square feet. The key technical challenge: how to get sunlight into a space with no natural light.

Ramsey's solution used remote skylight technology: rooftop collectors that capture and concentrate sunlight, then transmit it via fiber optic cables or reflective light channels to an underground distribution system that spreads diffuse natural light across the space. The same technology that enables fiber optic lighting in decorative contexts gets scaled to something capable of sustaining plant growth. Ramsey had worked on this technology at NASA before moving into architecture.

The Lowline was explicitly positioned as the inverse of the High Line — the elevated freight rail line converted to a linear park by the Hudson River, which had become one of the most celebrated urban design projects of the 2000s. Where High Line repurposed elevated infrastructure, Lowline proposed going underground, reclaiming the vast network of abandoned tunnels, terminals, and utility spaces beneath Manhattan. The project ran a successful lab demonstration (the "Lowline Lab") in 2012-2016, proving the light technology worked, before running into city approval complications.

## Key points

- Williamsburg Bridge Trolley Terminal: 60,000 sq ft underground space, last used 1948, beneath Delancey St on the Lower East Side.
- Remote skylight technology: rooftop collectors + light distribution channels deliver natural sunlight underground.
- James Ramsey developed the light tech at NASA before applying it to architecture.
- Positioned as the inverse of High Line — same reclaimed-infrastructure logic, applied below street level.
- Lowline Lab (2012-2016) proved the technology; city approvals and COVID ultimately killed the project.
