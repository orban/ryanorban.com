## Summary

A Twitter photo showing a Nutanix node arriving and being immediately put into production — the tweet context noting it arrived and was already running a local cluster within hours. This kind of grassroots social media signal was significant for Nutanix's go-to-market in 2013: practitioners sharing unboxing-to-production timelines contrasted sharply with traditional SAN deployments that required professional services, multi-day cabling sessions, and complex zoning configuration.

The Nutanix value proposition in the field was specifically about this: a node arriving, being racked, cabled, and added to a cluster in hours rather than days. The Nutanix Foundation imaging process and the Prism management UI were designed to make this feasible for operators without specialized storage expertise. The deployment simplicity story was a competitive differentiator against EMC and NetApp which required certification and professional services.

## Key points

- Nutanix deployment model: arrive, rack, cable, run Foundation imaging, add to cluster — measured in hours
- Contrasts with SAN deployment: LUN provisioning, zoning, multipath configuration, professional services — days or weeks
- Social proof via practitioner tweets was part of Nutanix's early marketing strategy
- Nutanix Foundation: the deployment tool that images all nodes in a new cluster from a single laptop
- Prism: the management UI that replaced the traditional CLI-heavy storage management workflow
