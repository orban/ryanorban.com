## Summary

This is the free PDF of *Algorithms* by Sanjoy Dasgupta, Christos Papadimitriou, and Umesh Vazirani — the undergraduate algorithms textbook used in Berkeley's CS170 course. The authors made the full PDF freely available from the UC Berkeley CS department, which was unusual for a major textbook publisher in 2012.

The book covers the standard undergraduate algorithms curriculum: divide and conquer, dynamic programming, greedy algorithms, graph algorithms (BFS, DFS, Dijkstra's algorithm, Bellman-Ford, minimum spanning tree), network flow, linear programming, NP-completeness, and randomized algorithms. What distinguishes it is the writing quality — Papadimitriou in particular writes with clarity and even wit, making proofs and complexity arguments more readable than most textbooks.

The free availability made it a standard reference link in the early 2010s Reddit and Hacker News circles discussing self-directed CS education. Alongside CLRS (Introduction to Algorithms by Cormen et al.) and Skiena's Algorithm Design Manual, DPV (as it's often called) occupies a distinct niche: more approachable than CLRS, more theory-focused than Skiena's practice-oriented approach.

## Key points

- Authors: Sanjoy Dasgupta, Christos Papadimitriou (complexity theory), Umesh Vazirani (quantum computing) — all Berkeley faculty
- Covers: divide and conquer, dynamic programming, greedy algorithms, graph algorithms, NP-completeness, linear programming
- Free PDF hosted by Berkeley — unusual openness for a commercial textbook
- Used in Berkeley's CS170 (Efficient Algorithms and Intractable Problems)
- Papadimitriou is also author of *Turing* (novel) and the complexity theory classic *Computational Complexity*
