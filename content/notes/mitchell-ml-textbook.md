## Summary

Tom Mitchell's *Machine Learning* (McGraw-Hill, 1997) is one of the foundational textbooks of the field — written when machine learning was primarily about decision trees, Bayesian networks, genetic algorithms, and shallow neural networks. Mitchell wrote it at Carnegie Mellon University, where he founded and led the Machine Learning Department.

The book's most lasting contribution is its opening definition: "A computer program is said to learn from experience E with respect to some class of tasks T and performance measure P, if its performance at tasks in T, as measured by P, improves with experience E." This became the canonical formal definition quoted in virtually every ML course for two decades.

By 2022 when this was bookmarked, deep learning had long superseded many of Mitchell's classical approaches. But the book remains in use for its clear conceptual foundations — the problems it poses (generalization, overfitting, hypothesis spaces) are the same problems deep learning grapples with, even if the solutions look different. It functions as theoretical grounding that complements practical PyTorch and TensorFlow resources.

## Key points

- 1997 foundational machine learning textbook by Tom Mitchell at CMU.
- Canonical definition of ML: "learn from experience E, task T, performance measure P."
- Covers decision trees, Bayesian learning, shallow neural networks, reinforcement learning.
- Available free online from CMU — open access decades before open source ML was common.
- Foundation still taught in graduate ML courses; complements modern deep learning resources.
- Tom Mitchell also founded CMU's Machine Learning Department — the field's organizational center in academia.
