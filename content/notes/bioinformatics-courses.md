## Summary

This page was maintained by C. Titus Brown at Michigan State University (MSU) as part of the ANGUS 2.0 documentation — a community resource documenting the bioinformatics training landscape. Titus Brown ran workshops on next-generation sequencing analysis and was a prominent voice in the open science and open training movement in computational biology.

In early 2013, bioinformatics education was fragmented. Universities were only beginning to formalize graduate programs; most practitioners were self-taught biologists who had learned computational methods ad hoc, or computer scientists who had pivoted to biological data. The gap was real and consequential: sequencing costs were dropping fast, data was piling up, and very few people could actually analyze it. Resources like this course list — community-maintained, freely available — were the primary on-ramps.

The courses listed would have included a mix of academic semester courses (computational biology at major universities), summer intensive workshops (Cold Spring Harbor Lab bioinformatics, Titus Brown's own ANGUS workshop at MSU), and online resources. Topics: sequence alignment, genome assembly, RNA-seq analysis, ChIP-seq, variant calling, and the statistical foundations underlying these methods.

## Key points

- C. Titus Brown was a leading figure in open computational biology education — his workshops trained hundreds of researchers
- The ANGUS workshop (Analysis of Next-Generation Sequencing) was a prestigious intensive course for wet-lab biologists entering NGS analysis
- Bioinformatics training in 2013 was mostly informal and community-organized — formal degree programs in computational biology were rare outside elite institutions
- Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory courses were (and remain) the gold standard intensive format for bioinformatics training
- The skills gap between sequencing throughput and bioinformatics capacity was one of the motivating forces behind the NIH Big Data to Knowledge initiative
