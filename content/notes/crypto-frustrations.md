## Summary

Amir Bolous published this essay as an honest internal critique of the crypto space circa mid-2022. The core frustration: despite nearly 14 years since Bitcoin, most on-chain activity is speculative rather than genuinely solving user problems. When he asks crypto builders what their application solves for real users, he rarely gets a satisfying answer. The space has largely abandoned the startup discipline that would normally be applied — user research, measuring churn, finding organic demand, validating that the problem exists before building the solution.

The domain-by-domain breakdown is sharp. DeFi primarily serves leverage and speculation, not the banking the unbanked promise. NFTs devolved into collectible trading. Play-to-earn gaming was mostly gambling with extra steps; he carves out Dark Forest as the rare exception where the game was actually fun. DAOs have centralized governance in practice. ZK and infrastructure are technically interesting but dependent on application demand that doesn't yet exist. The through-line is that narrative runs far ahead of product reality.

The overall stance is cautiously optimistic but intellectually honest. The author explicitly says: "We will onboard the next billion people to blockchains when there are applications built on top of them that actually solve problems for people." This is a useful artifact of 2022 thinking — written before the FTX collapse and crypto winter, by someone inside the ecosystem calling for higher standards. It reads well against subsequent events.

## Key points

- Core critique: crypto has product-market fit for speculation, not for solving actual user problems
- Has abandoned startup discipline: no user research, no churn measurement, no organic demand validation
- DeFi: banking the unbanked rhetoric doesn't match the reality of leverage and yield farming
- NFTs: collectibles and trading, not meaningful utility; Play-to-earn was gambling
- DAOs: governance is still centralized in practice; unclear value for average participants
- Stance: cautiously optimistic — calling for higher standards, not pessimism about crypto broadly
