## Summary

This GeekWire piece covers Galvanize's announcement of a massive 71,000-square-foot Seattle campus — at the time its largest, combining coworking space for startups with coding school facilities for gSchool web development and data science programs. The Seattle expansion reflected Galvanize's model at peak: the physical campus as the differentiator, creating a community of students, alumni, and companies in the same building.

The hybrid coworking/education model was Galvanize's core thesis: students learn in the same environment where startups work, creating natural hiring pipelines, mentorship networks, and cultural osmosis. Graduates stayed as tenants or employees; companies hired from the student cohorts. The physical space served as infrastructure for both the education and startup community sides of the business.

The timing (early 2015) was near the top of the bootcamp boom and the coworking market expansion (WeWork was raising at enormous valuations). Both markets subsequently contracted: bootcamps faced increasing skepticism about outcomes, and coworking faced consolidation. Galvanize was acquired by Stride in 2021 after the pandemic eliminated the physical campus advantage entirely.

## Key points

- 71,000 sq ft Seattle campus — Galvanize's largest at the time.
- Coworking + coding bootcamp hybrid: startups and students sharing the same physical space.
- Core thesis: proximity creates hiring pipelines, mentorship, and community that pure online education can't replicate.
- Peak timing: 2015 was near the top of both bootcamp and coworking market expansions.
- Both markets contracted; Galvanize acquired by Stride 2021 after pandemic undermined the physical model.
- Ryan Orban was involved in the Seattle expansion as part of his role building the data science program.
