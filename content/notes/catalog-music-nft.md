## Summary

Catalog is a music NFT platform on Ethereum where musicians sell single-edition (1/1) recordings directly to collectors. The model is simple: an artist mints a song as an NFT, one person owns it, and they can resell it with the artist receiving royalties on secondary sales. The pitch is a new direct patronage model — skip streaming services that pay fractions of a cent per play and instead sell directly to listeners who want to be patrons.

The platform launched in beta in 2021 and was one of several music NFT experiments in this period, alongside Sound.xyz (which allowed artists to release editions of songs to multiple collectors simultaneously) and general-purpose NFT platforms like Zora and OpenSea where artists could self-list. The 1/1 model for music differs from the edition model: one collector owns the NFT, but the music itself is still freely available to stream — you're buying a unique artifact and the patronage relationship, not exclusive listening access.

The underlying tension in music NFTs was never fully resolved: the value of ownership isn't clear when the music is freely available everywhere. The answer the community gave was a combination of speculative value (someone will pay more later), cultural/social capital (bragging rights of being the first owner), and genuine artist support (wanting to give money to musicians you care about without platform intermediaries). Catalog in particular attracted a serious music community — artists and collectors who cared about the experiment as a mechanism design problem for musician compensation.

## Key points

- 1/1 music NFT marketplace on Ethereum: one collector owns each song NFT, with royalties on secondary sales.
- Patronage model: buy a unique claim to a recording without exclusive listening rights — the music is still freely available.
- 2022 music NFT context: Catalog, Sound.xyz, and Zora were the main experiments in direct musician monetization.
- Value question: what does NFT ownership mean when the audio is freely accessible? Social capital + speculation + patronage.
- Competition: streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music) pay fractions of cents per stream — direct patronage offers orders of magnitude more per sale.
- Still unresolved: whether music NFT ownership has lasting cultural meaning or was a 2021-2022 crypto-fueled experiment.
