## Summary

Kirk Goldsberry is one of the pioneers of sports analytics visualization, combining academic geography training with basketball analysis to produce shot charts that were genuinely revelatory. This tweet showing every made shot by the Golden State Warriors in their 2014-15 championship season came at the apex of their shooting prowess — the season in which Stephen Curry changed the acceptable range for a catch-and-shoot three-pointer.

The visualization uses court mapping to show spatial distribution of made shots. What made the Warriors extraordinary wasn't just their shooting percentage but their shot selection — they had systematically eliminated low-value mid-range shots and replaced them with corner threes and layups, the two highest-value shot locations in the game. A shot chart of their made baskets looked structurally different from other teams.

Goldsberry's work was part of a broader basketball analytics revolution — the adoption of tracking data (SportVU cameras installed in all arenas by 2013-14) made this kind of spatial analysis possible. This connects to the data journalism pattern of the era: newly available granular data → novel visualization → insight that changes how practitioners think about the domain.

## Key points

- Kirk Goldsberry using court geography to visualize shot selection patterns.
- 2014-15 Warriors: the season that definitively demonstrated the value of three-point shooting over mid-range.
- Shot selection > shooting SKILL: the Warriors' advantage was what shots they took, not just how well they shot.
- SportVU tracking data made spatial basketball analysis possible at scale from 2013 onward.
- Pattern: new data source → spatial visualization → domain insight — applies across sports, urban planning, transportation.
