## Summary

The CTO Field Guide is a structured reference for technical leaders — people in CTO, VP of Engineering, or senior engineering leader roles navigating the operational challenges of the job. The focus is practical: how to hire senior engineers, when to rewrite vs. refactor, how to structure engineering teams, when to intervene in technical decisions, how to interface with the board and investors. Unlike the many how to be a leader books that stay at the inspirational level, field guide formats address what actually happens in the work.

The CTO role is one of the most context-dependent in tech: a CTO at a 5-person seed-stage startup has almost nothing in common with a CTO at a 500-person growth company or a public company. The job involves a permanent balancing act — writing code vs. managing people, technical debt vs. speed, hiring generalists vs. specialists, autonomy for teams vs. architectural coherence. Guides like this try to provide decision frameworks that survive across these contexts.

This resource appeared in early 2022 as interest in engineering leadership content was growing — Stripe Press published An Elegant Puzzle, Will Larson's Staff Engineer was circulating, and Camille Fournier's The Manager's Path had become standard reading. The [CTO Field Guide](/notes/cto-field-guide/) occupies the practical end of that spectrum, less narrative and more reference.

## Key points

- Practical reference for CTOs and VP of Engineerings on hiring, architecture, team structure, and culture.
- Addresses the fundamental context-dependency of the CTO role — the job changes completely with company stage.
- Covers the code vs. management tradeoff, technical debt decisions, and engineering org design.
- Sits in the engineering leadership canon alongside An Elegant Puzzle, The Manager's Path, Staff Engineer.
- Field guide format: decision frameworks over inspiration — what to do when things happen, not just vision.
