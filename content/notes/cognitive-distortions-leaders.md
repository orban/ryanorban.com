## Summary

This Leading Sapiens post applies the CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy) and REBT (Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy) catalog of cognitive distortions to leadership contexts. The core claim: high-performers aren't typically pathological, but under stress or upset they fall into the same thinking traps as everyone else — all-or-nothing thinking, catastrophizing, mind reading, personalization, emotional reasoning — and these distortions directly impair the quality of decisions, communication, and strategy that leaders are paid to produce.

The list of distortions covered includes the CBT standards: overgeneralization ("this always happens"), mental filtering (fixating on the one negative among many positives), jumping to conclusions (assuming intent without evidence), magnification/minimization, and should statements (rigid rules that generate unnecessary guilt and frustration). What makes this leadership-specific is the context: a CEO catastrophizing about a failed product launch, a manager mind-reading team sentiment in a difficult conversation, or an executive personalizing a competitive loss all exhibit these patterns in high-stakes situations.

Cognitive distortions are worth knowing because naming them is the first step to catching yourself. The CBT intervention is cognitive restructuring — examining the evidence for and against a distorted belief, finding a more accurate framing. Leaders with emotional intelligence who know these patterns can apply that intervention in the moment, rather than letting distorted thinking shape consequential decisions.

## Key points

- CBT/REBT cognitive distortion catalog applied to leadership and high-performers under stress.
- Key distortions: all-or-nothing thinking, catastrophizing, mind reading, personalization, emotional reasoning.
- High-performers aren't immune — distortions appear under pressure, not just in clinical contexts.
- Naming the distortion is the intervention: awareness enables cognitive restructuring in the moment.
- Connects to emotional intelligence and the broader literature on leader decision quality under stress.
