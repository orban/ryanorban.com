## Summary

Written by Zoltan Pozsar at Credit Suisse in August 2022, this note argues that the simultaneous collapse of two geo-economic partnerships — Chimerica (US-China) and Eurussia (Europe-Russia) — marks the end of a three-decade lowflation regime. Pozsar draws on Dale Copeland's theory of trade expectations: when great powers lose positive expectations about future trade, they initiate crises to protect commercial interests. With trust gone on both fronts, de-globalization is structurally locked in.

The paper introduces the concept of a "Real Minsky Moment" for global supply chains: just as banks ran on just-in-time liquidity before 2008, corporations run just-in-time supply chains assuming they can always source inputs without disruption. Russia's invasion of Ukraine and China's blockade of Taiwan exposed infinite leverage in those assumptions. Pozsar compares Germany's $2 trillion industrial economy depending on $20 billion of Russian gas to 100x leverage — worse than Lehman's. He frames the US's CHIPS Act as a Hank Paulson moment for semiconductor sovereignty.

The prescriptive conclusion is that Western governments will have to pour trillions into four industrial policy priorities: re-arm (defense production), re-shore (move supply chains home), re-stock (commodity inventories), and re-wire (energy transition grid). This agenda is commodity-intensive, capital-intensive, interest-rate-insensitive, and uninvestable for Eastern sovereign wealth funds — implying a structural commodity supercycle and the end of the private equity era funded by cheap money and cheap imports.

## Key points

- Chimerica (US-China) and Eurussia (EU-Russia) were the twin pillars of cheap goods, cheap energy, and recycled dollar/euro reserves that enabled quantitative easing and low inflation for three decades
- The TRICKs (Turkey, Russia, Iran, China, North Korea) alliance of sanctioned economies is forming a counter-bloc on the Eurasian chessboard, creating persistent supply uncertainty
- Industrial warfare demands stockpiles, not just high-tech weapons; annual US production of 155mm shells would last two weeks in Ukraine — exposing catastrophic underinvestment in basic military manufacturing
- ASML CEO noted executives buying washing machines to extract chips for industrial modules — a vivid sign of semiconductor supply fragility
- Bretton Woods III is framed as the inevitable outcome: a new monetary order tied to commodity-backed currencies rather than G7 debt claims, as Eastern nations stop recycling trade surpluses into US Treasuries
