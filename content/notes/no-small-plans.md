## Summary

[No Small Plans](/notes/no-small-plans/) is an executive coaching practice by Casey Rosengren that uses Acceptance and Commitment Training (ACT) — a psychology framework backed by 1,300+ randomized controlled trials — as its primary methodology. The practice targets founders scaling companies, post-exit founders navigating high optionality, and executives who feel misaligned between their work and what actually matters to them.

ACT as a framework is interesting for coaching applications: unlike cognitive-behavioral approaches that try to change thinking patterns, ACT focuses on clarifying values and committing to action aligned with those values — hence Acceptance and Commitment. The evidence-based framing distinguishes [No Small Plans](/notes/no-small-plans/) from purely intuitive coaching; citing 1,300+ RCTs gives the methodology more empirical grounding than most coaching approaches, where efficacy is rarely measured.

The six-month sprint model (assessment → outcome definition → regular 1:1 sessions, curated readings, practices) is more intensive and longitudinal than typical coaching engagements. The philosophy — "the higher the stakes, the more your psychology matters" — positions psychology not as remedial but as a performance edge for leaders operating at high complexity.

## Key points

- Executive coaching using Acceptance and Commitment Training (ACT) methodology.
- Targets: founders scaling companies, post-exit founders, executives misaligned with their values.
- Six-month sprint: assessment, outcome definition, regular sessions + readings + practices.
- ACT focuses on values clarification and committed action — distinct from CBT thought-pattern approaches.
- "Evidence-based" positioning: 1,300+ RCTs support ACT methodology — rare rigor in coaching.
- Founded by Casey Rosengren, a bootstrapped founder turned ACT-trained coach.
