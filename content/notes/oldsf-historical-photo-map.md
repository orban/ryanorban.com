## Summary

OldSF is a map-based archive of historical photographs of San Francisco spanning 1850 to 2000. It plots photos geographically so you can navigate to any point in the city and see what it looked like at different points in history. The note when saving expressed the obvious next step: a mashup with Google Street View to allow direct temporal comparison of the same vantage point.

The project is a good example of geographic data journalism and archival visualization — taking a collection that existed in flat databases (the San Francisco History Center at the public library, Online Archive of California, etc.) and adding spatial interface so you can explore it as a place rather than a catalog. The SF photograph archive is particularly rich because the city was heavily documented from its earliest years as a boomtown, survived the 1906 earthquake to be rebuilt and re-documented, and is in a region with strong photographic traditions.

The Street View comparison idea was eventually realized by others in the then and now genre: Google Street View itself has some historical layers, and projects like Historypin and independent apps for various cities have built this interface. The underlying appeal is time travel via photography — standing at an intersection and seeing it as it appeared in 1906, 1945, 1970, and today.

## Key points

- Interface: geographic map of SF; click any area to see historical photos taken near that location from 1850-2000
- Photo sources: San Francisco History Center, California Historical Society, UC Berkeley Bancroft Library, and other archives
- Stamen Design (mentioned in the save tweet) was a San Francisco data visualization studio active in this kind of civic data work
- The obvious mashup (Street View + historical photos) was realized by Historypin and similar services
- Historical coverage is asymmetric: more photos from downtown and notable neighborhoods, less from residential areas
