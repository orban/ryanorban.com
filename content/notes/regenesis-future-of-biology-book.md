## Summary

This io9 review recommends Regenesis: How Synthetic Biology Will Reinvent Nature and Ourselves by George Church and Ed Regis (2012) as the essential book on where biology was heading. The save note In for one indicates it was bookmarked with intent to read.

George Church is a central figure in synthetic biology and genomics — professor at Harvard Medical School, founder of the Personal Genome Project, and a prolific inventor of genomics technologies including contributions to early next-generation sequencing methods and, later, significant work on CRISPR applications. Writing Regenesis in 2012, he articulated a vision where the ability to read and write DNA at scale would eventually allow redesigning organisms from scratch.

The book covers the arc from the history of genetics to Church's work in the lab to speculative projections: reviving extinct species (de-extinction), engineering microbes to produce fuels and medicines, designing cells as programmable machines, and ultimately the possibility of synthesizing an entire genome from scratch. The "regenesis" of the title refers both to re-creating extinct life and to the broader project of becoming capable of designing life intentionally.

## Key points

- George Church: Harvard genomics professor; Personal Genome Project founder; key figure in sequencing technology development and CRISPR applications
- Regenesis (2012): covers synthetic biology's history, Church's lab work, and speculative projections for genome engineering
- De-extinction: the project of reviving extinct species from preserved DNA — Church's lab later worked on the woolly mammoth revival project
- Personal Genome Project: Church's effort to sequence and publicly release full genomes of volunteers — democratizing personal genomics data
- 2012 publication timing: before CRISPR became widely known — Church was already working on it but the Doudna/Charpentier paper had just appeared months earlier
