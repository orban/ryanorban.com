## Summary

Typesafe raised $14 million from Shasta Ventures, Greylock Partners, and Juniper Networks in August 2012 to build a commercial business around Scala and the Reactive programming stack. Typesafe was founded by Martin Odersky (creator of Scala) and Jonas Bonér (creator of Akka), combining the language with the two major frameworks that had grown up around it: Akka for concurrent/distributed systems and the Play Framework for web applications.

The bet Typesafe was making was that Scala would become the dominant language for the JVM in enterprise and high-throughput systems — a statically-typed, functional-leaning alternative to Java that could handle the concurrency demands of distributed systems. Akka's actor model was particularly attractive for building systems that needed to handle millions of concurrent connections, the kind of scale that Java's thread-per-request model struggled with.

The $14M was used to build out enterprise support, tooling, and the Typesafe Activator (a starter environment for Scala projects). The thesis proved partially right: Scala gained significant adoption at Twitter, LinkedIn, Foursquare, and others, but mass enterprise adoption never materialized. Typesafe eventually rebranded as Lightbend in 2016, broadening beyond Scala to include reactive systems more generally.

## Key points

- $14M raised from Shasta Ventures, Greylock Partners, and Juniper Networks to commercialize Scala.
- Typesafe founded by Martin Odersky (Scala creator) and Jonas Bonér (Akka creator).
- Core stack: Scala language + Akka actor model + Play Framework for web.
- Akka's actor model addressed concurrency at scale that Java's thread model struggled with.
- Big tech adoption (Twitter, LinkedIn) but mass enterprise adoption never happened.
- Company rebranded as Lightbend in 2016, broadening beyond Scala.
