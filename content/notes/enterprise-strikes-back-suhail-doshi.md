## Summary

Suhail Doshi (founder of Mixpanel) wrote this essay as the venture capital world was rediscovering enterprise software as a venture-backable category. The 2009–2012 consumer app wave — driven by iOS, Android, and social networks — had produced enormous valuations but also crowded, hit-driven markets. Enterprise, by contrast, was systematically underserved by well-designed software.

The consumerization of enterprise thesis, popular at the time, was that consumer-grade UX was invading workplace tools. Dropbox, Box, Yammer, Asana, and Slack (not yet launched in 2012 — that would come in 2013) were all attacking enterprise from the bottom up: individuals adopted them, departments followed, IT eventually ratified. This bottom-up adoption bypassed the traditional enterprise sales cycle.

The essay reflects a turning point in how Silicon Valley thought about enterprise. The SaaS model had made enterprise economics more legible: recurring revenue, churn rates, LTV/CAC ratios — metrics that fit the venture model. The enterprise is boring stigma was fading as investors realized that Salesforce's 1999 success was repeating at scale across many categories. Doshi's Mixpanel was itself a B2B analytics product winning by being better-designed than legacy alternatives.

## Key points

- 2012 investor shift: consumer app crowding drove renewed interest in enterprise as a startup target
- "Consumerization of enterprise": Dropbox, Box, Yammer, early Asana — bottom-up adoption bypassing IT
- SaaS metrics (ARR, churn, LTV/CAC) made enterprise venture economics legible and attractive
- Suhail Doshi's Mixpanel was itself an example: B2B analytics winning on design vs. legacy tools like Omniture
- The Slack era (post-2013) validated the thesis more completely than any prior product
