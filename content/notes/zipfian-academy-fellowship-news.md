## Summary

This Data Science 101 post covered Zipfian Academy's expansion into two new programs: the Data Fellowship (preparatory/part-time track) and the Data Engineering Immersive. It's secondary-source coverage of the Zipfian Academy blog announcement, adding context from the broader data science education community.

By mid-2014, the bootcamp ecosystem was growing rapidly enough that industry blogs were covering new program launches as news. Zipfian Academy's expansion into data engineering was a signal: the market was demanding engineers who could build data pipelines, not just analysts who could run queries. The gap between data available for analysis and data accessible at all had become painfully visible — data scientists at many companies spent 80% of their time doing what would later be called data engineering.

The Data Engineering Immersive covered Hadoop, Spark, Kafka, stream processing, and distributed systems design — the toolchain for building production data infrastructure. This was a distinct SKILL set from data science (Python, statistics, ML), and the market rewarded it differently: data engineers commanded strong compensation even at companies that weren't yet doing sophisticated ML.

## Key points

- Data engineering as a distinct discipline from data science was solidifying in 2014: building reliable pipelines vs. building models that consume them.
- The Zipfian Academy Data Engineering program covered Hadoop, Spark, Kafka, and distributed systems — the 2014 production data stack.
- External coverage in Data Science 101 signals bootcamp programs were becoming a mainstream career path, not an experiment.
- The "fellowship" framing (part-time, preparatory) reflected demand from people who wanted the full immersive but lacked the quantitative prerequisites.
- Galvanize later acquired Zipfian Academy in 2015, folding the data engineering curriculum into a broader technical education portfolio.
