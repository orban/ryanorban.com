## Summary

Gil Press's Forbes piece traces the intellectual lineage of data science from John Tukey's prescient 1962 paper "The Future of Data Analysis" through to 2013. It's a useful corrective to the narrative that data science was invented when McKinsey declared it the "sexiest job of the 21st century" — the intellectual foundation is decades older.

The key milestones: John Tukey (1962) argued that data analysis should be recognized as a distinct statistical science, not just applied mathematics. Peter Naur (1974) coined datalogy and wrote about data science as the science of dealing with data. The Journal of Data Science launched in 2002. DJ Patil and Jeff Hammerbacher (2008) popularized the title "data scientist" at LinkedIn and Facebook respectively. Harvard Business Review's "Data Scientist: Sexiest Job of the 21st Century" (2012) set off the hiring wave.

The historical framing matters: statistics, machine learning, data mining, and data science are overlapping but distinct communities with different cultures, tools, and epistemologies. Understanding the lineage helps explain why, for example, statisticians were initially skeptical of the data science framing — from their view, it was just statistics with better marketing.

## Key points

- John Tukey 1962: "The Future of Data Analysis" — the intellectual origin of treating data analysis as its own science
- Peter Naur 1974: coined "datalogy" and early definition of data science
- KDD (Knowledge Discovery in Databases) community 1989–2000s: the data mining tradition
- DJ Patil + Jeff Hammerbacher 2008: the "data scientist" job title at LinkedIn and Facebook
- HBR 2012 "sexiest job" article: the hiring boom trigger
- The term's contested origin reflects genuine disciplinary tensions between statistics, CS, and domain sciences
