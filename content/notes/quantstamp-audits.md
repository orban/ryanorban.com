## Summary

Quantstamp is one of the earliest and most prominent professional smart contract security firms, with an audit portfolio spanning Ethereum 2.0, OpenSea, MakerDAO, and hundreds of DeFi protocols. By their claim in 2022, they had audited over $200 billion in digital assets — the scale reflects how dominant a handful of firms became in the blockchain security landscape once institutional capital started deploying into DeFi.

The audit business model is straightforward: teams of security researchers manually review Solidity (or Rust, or Vyper) code, produce a report listing vulnerabilities by severity (Critical, High, Medium, Low, Informational), and work with the client team to remediate. Quantstamp sits alongside Trail of Bits, OpenZeppelin, Consensys Diligence, Certora, and Halborn as the recognized names that institutional protocols use to demonstrate security diligence before launch.

What makes Quantstamp's portfolio page useful is the public track record: which protocols they've audited, when, and whether the audits were pre-launch or post-exploit. The presence of ETH2.0 (the Beacon Chain and deposit contract) in their portfolio is notable — that code secures billions in staked ETH, and having it audited by multiple independent firms was part of Ethereum Foundation's launch risk management. The OpenSea inclusion shows that Web3 infrastructure beyond pure DeFi (NFT marketplaces, bridges) also requires this level of security scrutiny.

## Key points

- Quantstamp is an early institutional smart contract audit firm — >$200B in assets audited by 2022
- Major clients: ETH2.0 deposit contract, OpenSea, MakerDAO — flagship audits that established credibility
- Audit reports are public: findings by severity with client remediation responses
- Competitor landscape: Trail of Bits, OpenZeppelin, Consensys Diligence, Certora (formal verification), Halborn
- Automated tooling (like Slither-simil, Sec3, Mythril) supplements manual audits but doesn't replace them for high-value protocols
- Security theater risk: projects that ship quickly get audited after exploits; audit != secure
