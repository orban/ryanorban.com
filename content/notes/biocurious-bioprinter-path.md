## Summary

A personal photo from BioCurious in Sunnyvale, California, documenting the early build phase of the community bioprinter project. This was the project that later became the Wired-covered DIY bioprinter — built from salvaged CD drive mechanisms, an Arduino, and an Inkshield (an Arduino shield for driving inkjet printer heads to deposit liquid).

BioCurious was the community biology lab that embodied maker culture applied to biology: a membership-based hackerspace where people with biology backgrounds (and curious non-biologists) could work on projects that required wet lab equipment. The bioprinter project was one of its flagship efforts — building laboratory-grade equipment from cheap hardware as a demonstration that citizen scientists could replicate the function of expensive commercial biotech equipment.

The May 4, 2012 date places this at the beginning of the build — the Wired article covering the completed bioprinter came out in January 2013. See also: 2012-06-09-bioprinter-arduino-biocurious for the photo of the completed build.

## Key points

- BioCurious (Sunnyvale, CA): community biology lab, membership-based hackerspace for citizen science.
- Bioprinter project used: two salvaged CD drive mechanisms (linear motion), Arduino (control), Inkshield (fluid deposition).
- This photo (May 2012) predates the completed build covered by Wired in January 2013.
- Project demonstrated that lab-grade biotech equipment could be replicated at hobby cost (~$100).
