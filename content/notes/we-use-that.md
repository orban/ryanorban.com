## Summary

[We Use That](/notes/we-use-that/) was a community website where tech companies and developers shared what tools, services, and products they used in their stacks. The concept: instead of finding tools through marketing or reviews, see what actual engineering teams at respected companies have adopted. The premise was that tool selection by trusted peers carries more signal than vendor claims.

The model was straightforward — companies would list the tools they used across categories: hosting, databases, frameworks, monitoring, analytics, payment processing, etc. If you knew a company had an engineering culture you respected, you could look at their stack for inspiration. This is how tool discovery actually happens in engineering teams: not from vendor marketing but from "what does [company we admire] use?"

[We Use That](/notes/we-use-that/) was a precursor to what StackShare later systematized with more structured data and social features. StackShare launched around 2013-2014 and became the canonical version of this idea, with aggregated data on which tools were used together and ability to see trends across many companies. The underlying insight both sites shared: tech stack decisions are high-signal social proof for tool adoption, and that signal was not being aggregated anywhere accessible before these products.

## Key points

- Community site for tech teams to share their tool and service stacks.
- Signal: peer adoption by respected engineering teams is more reliable than vendor marketing.
- Covered categories: hosting, databases, frameworks, monitoring, analytics, communication tools.
- Preceded StackShare which later systematized the concept with more structure and scale.
- The "what does [company I respect] use?" discovery pattern is how engineering tool adoption actually spreads.
- Reflects 2012 era when SaaS tool explosion was beginning and stack composition was becoming a differentiator.
