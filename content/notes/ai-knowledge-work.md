## Summary

This newsletter synthesizes early research on how AI tools are affecting knowledge workers across three sectors. The productivity numbers are real: developers using GitHub Copilot complete tasks 55% faster; BCG consultants using GPT-4 completed 12.2% more tasks, 25% faster, with quality improvements up to 40%; customer support agents using AI suggestions improved productivity by 14% per hour.

The more interesting finding is the distribution of those gains. Across all three sectors, lower-performing workers benefit significantly more than high performers. In consulting, lower performers improved 43% vs. 17% for high performers. In customer support, less experienced agents improved 34%; top performers barely moved. The implication is that AI tools act as a floor-raiser rather than a ceiling-raiser — they compress the performance distribution toward the top.

The critical caveat: BCG consultants performed **19% worse** on tasks outside AI's current capabilities when using AI versus not using it. The productivity gains evaporate and reverse when AI is applied indiscriminately. The SKILL required isn't just using AI tools — it's accurately knowing when to use them.

## Key points

- GitHub Copilot → 55% faster task completion; GPT-4 at BCG → 12.2% more tasks, 25% faster.
- Lower performers gain more: lower-skill customer support agents improved 34%; top performers barely moved.
- BCG study: low performers improved 43% with AI; high performers improved 17%.
- **Negative effect**: 19% performance drop when AI applied to tasks outside its capability range.
- AI compresses the performance distribution — it's a floor-raiser, not a ceiling-raiser.
- The key skill is knowing when to use AI, not just how.
