## Summary

Danielle Fong is a physicist and entrepreneur who co-founded LightSail Energy, a compressed air energy storage company. This talk at the Nantucket Project — shared widely by Bret Victor with the quote "You'll notice it's gone basically to zero" — makes the case for urgency in clean energy development.

The quote refers to a cost curve: likely the dramatic decline in solar panel costs (which fell roughly 99% from 1976 to 2014), or perhaps battery storage costs, or carbon capture costs. The rhetorical power is in presenting a trend that looks like an exponential decay approaching zero — the visual evidence that something expensive is becoming essentially free, which changes the calculus entirely on what's economically viable.

Danielle Fong represents a specific type of scientific entrepreneur: a physicist who left academia (she was a PhD student at Princeton) to work on energy storage because she believed the technology was close enough to viable that a startup could close the gap. LightSail Energy used diabatic compressed air energy storage with water vapor injection to recover heat losses — a clever thermodynamic trick. The company ultimately closed in 2018, but the underlying physics remained sound.

Bret Victor's amplification of the talk is itself notable — Victor (creator of influential essays like Inventing on Principle) was focused in this period on how scientists and engineers can address civilizational-scale problems, and Fong's talk aligned with that.

## Key points

- Danielle Fong: physicist, co-founder of LightSail Energy (compressed air energy storage) — left Princeton PhD program to start the company.
- Talk at Nantucket Project arguing for urgency on clean energy, citing dramatic cost curve declines.
- Gone basically to zero = exponential cost decline in solar or energy storage — changing economic viability fundamentally.
- Shared by Bret Victor (creator of "Inventing on Principle") who was focused on scientists addressing civilizational problems.
- LightSail Energy closed in 2018 after failing to scale the compressed air storage technology to commercial viability.
- Context: October 2014 — solar had crossed grid parity in some markets; the energy transition acceleration was becoming visible.
