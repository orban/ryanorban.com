## Summary

Shadow and Substance was a web-based eclipse visualization tool that provided animated views of solar eclipse shadow paths across Earth's surface. Shared during the May 20, 2012 annular solar eclipse, it showed the progression of the umbra (full shadow, where annular/total coverage occurs) and penumbra (partial shadow) across a map, allowing viewers to understand both what to expect in their location and the overall geometry of how the Moon's shadow sweeps across the planet.

The tool addressed a common mental model gap: people know an eclipse is happening but have trouble visualizing the scale and geometry. The Moon's full shadow (umbra) during an annular eclipse traces a narrow path — typically 100-200 km wide — across thousands of miles of Earth's surface. Outside that path, you get partial coverage that diminishes with distance from the center line. Seeing this animated on a map connects the abstract geometry to the observed experience.

Tools like this were valuable in 2012 because high-quality eclipse visualization required either professional astronomical software or well-designed web tools. NASA's eclipse pages had static maps; tools like Shadow and Substance and the later work of Xavier Jubier (Google Maps eclipse visualizations) provided interactive alternatives that made eclipse geometry genuinely understandable.

## Key points

- Animated visualization of umbra and penumbra sweep paths for solar eclipses.
- May 2012 annular eclipse path: Pacific Coast through Nevada/Utah/Colorado/Texas → Japan/China.
- Eclipse geometry: umbra (full annular/total shadow) ~100-200km wide; penumbra (partial) thousands of km wider.
- Complements NASA static eclipse maps with interactive animation.
- Related tools: Xavier Jubier's Google Maps eclipse path tools, NASA's Eclipse Web Site (Fred Espenak).
