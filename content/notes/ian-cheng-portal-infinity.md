## Summary

Ian Cheng is a New York-based artist known for creating lifeform simulations — open-ended digital worlds that run indefinitely without predetermined outcomes. His work sits at the intersection of art, game design, and cognitive science: he was trained as a cognitive scientist before becoming an artist, and this background deeply informs his practice of creating environments that evolve through their own logic rather than following a scripted narrative.

His most well-known works include Emissaries (a trilogy of simulation-based videos, 2015–2017) and BOB (Bag of Beliefs, 2018–2021), an AI-driven lifeform that has its own persistent beliefs, needs, and behaviors accessible via a companion app. BOB represents a shift from passive observation of simulation to active relationship with a simulated entity — users can interact with BOB over time and watch it evolve.

Cheng's interest is in the nature of emergence — how complex behavior arises from simple rules — and in infinite games (in the sense of James Carse's framework: games played for the purpose of continuing play, not ending it). His simulations don't have win states or defined conclusions. This is philosophically deliberate: he's exploring what it means to create worlds that can surprise their creator.

## Key points

- Ian Cheng makes simulation-based art: open-ended worlds running indefinitely without scripted outcomes.
- Emissaries trilogy and BOB (Bag of Beliefs) are his major works — both running simulations as art objects.
- Cognitive science training shapes his interest in emergence and behavior without narrative resolution.
- Infinite games framing (James Carse): simulations designed to perpetuate themselves rather than conclude.
- Relevant context for anyone working on AI agents, generative worlds, or the philosophy of simulation.
