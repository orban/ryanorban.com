## Summary

This TechCrunch post surfaced a Quora answer that went viral shortly after Facebook's $1 billion acquisition of Instagram in April 2012. The person writing had declined a job offer from Instagram before the acquisition — and was reflecting on whether that was the right call. Their stated reason: they wanted to work on something with larger intellectual scope, specifically a "platform for all human knowledge" type mission rather than photo filtering.

The answer resonated because it touched a real tension in the 2012 tech ecosystem. Instagram's acquisition meant that anyone who had joined before the acquisition (and had vested equity) was suddenly wealthy. The question "was declining Instagram a mistake" was being asked by many people in the Bay Area who had passed on early opportunities at companies that subsequently exploded. The Quora format — first-person, thoughtful, with detail — made it an unusually honest window into how people in tech actually reason about career decisions.

The deeper question the post raised: what's the right framework for career decisions in early-stage companies? Join companies for their potential outcome (financial) or for the work (mission/craft)? The people who got wealthy at Instagram weren't necessarily those who chose best — many joined for reasons that had nothing to do with predicting the $1B exit. The hindsight bias in I should have joined Instagram arguments misses this. The person in the Quora post had decent reasons for their choice; they just couldn't know that Instagram would get acquired for 10x what anyone expected.

## Key points

- Written after Facebook acquired Instagram for $1B in April 2012 — one of the surprising early tech mega-acquisitions.
- The "platform for all human knowledge" ambition likely referred to Wikipedia, Quora, or related information infrastructure projects.
- Classic career decisions dilemma: mission/craft alignment vs. financial upside, made impossible by information asymmetry.
- Quora in 2012 was still seen as a serious intellectual platform — answers like this are why it had cultural weight.
- Hindsight bias trap: the people who joined Instagram weren't systematically smarter; the outcome was partially unpredictable.
