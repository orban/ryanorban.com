## Summary

SpaceX won a $440 million NASA Commercial Crew Integrated Capability (CCiCap) contract in August 2012 to develop crew transportation capability to the International Space Station, targeting the first astronaut flights as early as 2015. The award was part of NASA's strategy to outsource low-Earth orbit crew transport to private companies after the Space Shuttle program ended in 2011, freeing NASA to focus on deep space exploration.

The CCiCap award went to three companies: SpaceX ($440M for the Dragon crew variant), Boeing ($460M for the CST-100 Starliner), and Sierra Nevada Corporation ($212.5M for the Dream Chaser). The competitive structure was intentional — NASA wanted multiple vendors to reduce program risk and promote innovation through competition. This was a deliberate philosophical shift from the single-vendor, cost-plus contracts of the Space Shuttle era.

SpaceX had already proven cargo delivery to the ISS with Dragon in May 2012 (the first commercial cargo mission), which made the crew contract less speculative than it might otherwise have appeared. The crew Dragon contract was the next logical step. SpaceX ultimately achieved crewed ISS transport in 2020 with Demo-2, about five years later than the 2015 target. Boeing's Starliner had significant delays and didn't achieve crewed operational status until 2024.

## Key points

- $440M NASA CCiCap award to SpaceX for crew transportation to ISS, targeting 2015.
- Part of NASA's strategy to commercialize low-Earth orbit crew transport post-Space Shuttle.
- Three-way competition: SpaceX (Dragon), Boeing (Starliner), Sierra Nevada Corporation (Dream Chaser).
- SpaceX had already demonstrated cargo Dragon capability in May 2012 — crew version was the next step.
- SpaceX achieved crewed ISS flights in 2020 (Demo-2); Boeing Starliner delayed significantly.
