## Summary

John Foreman (data scientist and author of *Data Smart*) wrote this essay at the peak of the 2015 data science hype cycle, when the term was being applied to everything from basic SQL queries to cutting-edge ML research, and every company believed it needed a data scientist immediately. His argument: the hype is survivable if you stay grounded in what data science is actually *for*.

The core reframe: data science is decision science. The value isn't in the algorithms or the models or even the insights — it's in the decisions those things enable. A model that's never used to make a decision is an academic exercise. A model that improves a single important decision by a small amount can generate enormous value. This reframing helps practitioners resist the tool-chasing behavior the hype encourages (always learning the latest framework) in favor of understanding what decisions they're actually trying to improve.

Foreman is also sharp about the hype creating unrealistic expectations that damage the field. When companies hire data scientists expecting magic and get incremental improvements from well-executed A/B tests, the backlash is predictable. The practitioners who survive the hype cycle are those who set expectations correctly from the start — and who focus on making data science useful rather than impressive.

## Key points

- Data science = decision science: value comes from improved decisions, not from models per se.
- Hype creates misaligned expectations that damage the field when reality doesn't match.
- Practitioners who survive: those who set honest expectations and focus on decisions over methods.
- Tool-chasing is a trap — the incentive structure of the hype favors learning new frameworks, not solving problems.
- By John Foreman (MailChimp Chief Data Scientist, author of *Data Smart*) — practitioner perspective, not academic.
- Still relevant: every AI hype cycle produces the same dynamic.
