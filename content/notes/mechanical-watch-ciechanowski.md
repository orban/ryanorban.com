## Summary

Bartosz Ciechanowski's interactive article on mechanical watches is one of the finest examples of interactive explanation on the web. It walks through the complete mechanism — from the mainspring that stores energy, through the gear train that transmits it, to the escapement that regulates its release — using 3D WebGL animations where every gear turns, every spring compresses, and every component responds to your interaction. You can drag to rotate the mechanism, adjust parameters, and watch the physics change in real time.

The escapement is the heart of the explanation. This is the mechanism that converts the continuous unwinding of the mainspring into discrete ticks — the tick-tock of a watch. Ciechanowski shows how the escape wheel, pallet fork, and balance wheel interact in a feedback loop: the balance wheel oscillates at a fixed frequency, the pallet fork alternately locks and unlocks the escape wheel, and the escape wheel's impulse pins nudge the balance wheel to keep it oscillating. Understanding this through animation makes clear in minutes what static diagrams struggle to convey in pages.

Ciechanowski's other explainers cover GPS, camera lenses, Fourier transforms, naval vessels, light and shadow, and color spaces. The common thread is that he picks mechanisms where the mental model is genuinely hard to build from text or static images, and where interactive simulation closes the gap. These articles are often cited as benchmarks for what educational content on the web can achieve — a few researchers have studied them as models for science communication and technical writing.

## Key points

- Full mechanical watch mechanism in interactive 3D: mainspring → gear train → escapement → display.
- The escapement mechanism (escape wheel + pallet fork + balance wheel) is the conceptual crux — regulation via feedback.
- WebGL-based animations: every component moves, responds to interaction, illustrates cause and effect.
