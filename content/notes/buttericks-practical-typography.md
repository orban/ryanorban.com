## Summary

Butterick's Practical Typography is an online book by Matthew Butterick that teaches typography with a practical, opinionated tone aimed at people who write but aren't professional designers — lawyers, academics, programmers, anyone who creates documents that people have to read. The book is available for free online (with a payment option) and covers the full stack of typographic decisions: font selection, point size, line spacing, line length, margins, emphasis techniques, and common mistakes to avoid.

Butterick's approach is refreshingly direct: he tells you what to do and what not to do, rather than surveying the history of type or hedging everything with it depends. His main targets are the default Microsoft Word aesthetic (Times New Roman at 12pt with 1-inch margins and double-spacing) and the habits that come from typing on typewriters (double spaces after periods, underlining for emphasis). The book is organized around the claim that typography is a form of visual rhetoric — the way text is set affects how it's read and how credible it appears, independent of the content.

Butterick also made Pollen, a markup language for digital books designed around typographic quality. Both projects share the same conviction: the appearance of written text matters, good defaults are within reach, and most documents are set in ways that actively harm legibility and credibility. The book is a fast read and immediately applicable — anyone who produces reports, papers, or web content and wants them to look professional can implement the recommendations in an afternoon.

## Key points

- Practical, opinionated rules: specific font recommendations, line spacing (120-145% of point size), line length (45-90 characters), margin width.
- Targets the main failure modes: Times New Roman as default, double-spacing after periods, underline for emphasis, too-large body text.
- Typography as rhetoric: visual presentation signals credibility and professionalism independent of content quality.
- By Matthew Butterick — also creator of Pollen markup language and Equity, Concourse, and Advocate fonts.
- Free to read online; pay-what-you-want model. One of the highest signal-to-noise typography resources available.
- Short: a few hours to read, immediately actionable for anyone who produces written documents.
