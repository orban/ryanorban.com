## Summary

The AutonLab at Carnegie Mellon University published this tutorial series on statistical data mining and machine learning as freely accessible PDFs — a significant resource in the early 2010s when most rigorous treatments were locked behind textbooks or paywalls. The lab, founded by Andrew Moore, focused on scalable algorithms for very large datasets, so the tutorials reflect both theoretical depth and computational practicality.

The coverage spans the core algorithmic toolkit of a 2014 data scientist: density estimation, Bayesian networks, Gaussian mixture models, k-means clustering, EM algorithm (Expectation-Maximization), principal component analysis (PCA), kernel methods, and anomaly/outlier detection. Each tutorial is a self-contained PDF with enough mathematical detail to understand what the algorithm is doing, but written for practitioners rather than theoreticians.

The AutonLab tutorials occupy an interesting position in the data science education stack: more rigorous than blog posts but more accessible than textbooks like PRML (Bishop). They served as a bridge for engineers making the transition into data science who needed the conceptual scaffolding without a graduate-level commitment.

## Key points

- Bayesian networks: probabilistic graphical models for representing conditional dependencies — structure learning and inference covered.
- Gaussian mixture models and EM algorithm: the standard approach to soft clustering with probabilistic assignments.
- Principal component analysis (PCA): linear dimensionality reduction via eigendecomposition — foundational for feature engineering and visualization.
- Anomaly detection: statistical approaches for flagging outliers — density-based, distance-based, and model-based methods.
- The AutonLab moved to Google (Andrew Moore became VP of engineering) — the tutorials remain as legacy CMU resources.
