## Summary

Written in May 2022 as interest rates were rising and venture capital sentiment was shifting, this post from Canvas App addresses the question every startup CEO was suddenly asking: how do you survive when easy fundraising dries up? The 2021 environment of frothy valuations and cheap capital had ended, and startups that had built burn models around the assumption of easy follow-on rounds were suddenly exposed.

The core advice is straightforward: cut expenses ruthlessly, focus on revenue over growth metrics, freeze non-essential hiring, renegotiate contracts, and internalize the "default alive" vs. "default dead" framing from Paul Graham's essay. "Default alive" means your current revenue trajectory, combined with planned cuts, gets you to breakeven before you run out of money — without requiring additional capital. Most 2021-vintage startups that hadn't reached this point were default dead and needed to either raise quickly at worse terms or cut aggressively.

The broader context matters: 2022 was the first time in over a decade that many startup founders had to think seriously about burn rates and runway. The ZIRP era had created a generation of founders and investors who had only experienced one mode — raise-and-grow. The funding winter forced a reversion to the older discipline of capital efficiency and unit economics, which the essay captures as the operative shift. Y Combinator's famous "default alive" advice suddenly felt newly urgent.

## Key points

- Default alive test: does your current revenue trajectory + cost cuts reach profitability before cash-out? If not, you're default dead
- Immediate lever: expense audit — fixed costs (office, SaaS subscriptions, headcount) are the fastest path to extending runway
- Revenue focus: prioritize ARR over usage/engagement metrics when investors are tightening; revenue buys time
- Hiring freeze: easiest lever; cost of not hiring is almost always lower than the cost of hiring then laying off
- The 2022 market shift: ZIRP era ended, venture capital discipline returned after a decade of cheap capital
- Related: Paul Graham's Default Alive or Default Dead essay; Sequoia's RIP Good Times and 2022 equivalent
