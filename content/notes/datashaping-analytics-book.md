## Summary

Vincent Granville of datashaping.com and later Data Science Central self-published a series of analytics and data science PDFs, distributed free from his website. This bookmark points to ABbook5.pdf — one chapter or volume of his analytics book series covering applied statistical analysis and data science methods.

Granville was an early practitioner who had worked in statistical modeling and machine learning before the data science label existed, and he became one of the early community builders — writing extensively on datashaping.com before founding Data Science Central as a broader community platform. His self-published materials filled a gap: free, applied-practitioner guides to analytics methods that sat between textbook rigor and blog-post brevity.

The "AB prefix likely refers to Analytics Book" — a series of practical guides covering topics like regression, clustering, A/B testing, or time series analysis. These circulated in the early data science community as accessible self-education resources.

## Key points

- Vincent Granville: one of the first data science community organizers, pre-dating the data scientist job title and building resources for the community that would adopt it.
- Self-published analytics PDFs: a common format in 2013 for practitioners who wanted to share practical knowledge without the 2-year academic publishing cycle.
- datashaping.com → Data Science Central: Granville's platform evolution mirrored the field's professionalization — from personal site to community hub.
- Free distribution model: making technical books openly downloadable was still notable in 2013 — before the O'Reilly Atlas and similar open publishing models normalized it.
