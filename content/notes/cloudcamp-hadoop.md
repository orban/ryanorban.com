## Summary

CloudCamp was a series of unconference events focused on cloud computing and emerging infrastructure. This PBworks wiki page served as a community knowledge base for attendees, and this particular page covered Hadoop — the dominant big data framework of the era.

In early 2013, Hadoop was at peak hype. It had moved from a Yahoo/Google curiosity to an enterprise requirement, and community resources like CloudCamp wiki pages filled the gap between sparse official documentation and the demand for accessible introductions. The page likely covered HDFS for distributed storage and MapReduce for parallel batch processing — the two-component architecture that defined Apache Hadoop 1.x.

The CloudCamp community represented the practitioner layer of the cloud/big data adoption curve: people deploying these systems in the real world before vendors had packaged them cleanly, sharing hard-won operational knowledge informally. At this moment, Cloudera, Hortonworks, and MapR were just starting to commercialize the ecosystem.

## Key points

- Hadoop in early 2013 meant HDFS + MapReduce: distributed filesystem paired with batch compute
- Community wikis were a primary knowledge-sharing mechanism before vendor documentation matured
- CloudCamp unconferences seeded the practitioner community that would later staff big data teams
- The Apache Hadoop ecosystem was still expanding rapidly — Hive, Pig, HBase, and YARN were in various stages of maturity
- Cloudera and Hortonworks were competing to become the Red Hat of Hadoop
