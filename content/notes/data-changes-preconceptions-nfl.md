## Summary

This TechCrunch piece explored how data analysis challenges conventional wisdom in NFL football — the sports-analytics wave applied to the most statistically documented team sport in America. Common football beliefs (bad weather benefits the home team, star quarterbacks win in clutch situations, defense wins championships) could be tested against decades of play-by-play data, and often the data contradicted the received wisdom.

Sports analytics had been mainstreamed by Moneyball (Michael Lewis, 2003; film 2011) applied to baseball, and by 2013 the same methodology was spreading to football (Football Outsiders, Pro Football Focus, ESPN Statistics and Information Group). The NFL provided unusually rich data: every play is documented with down, distance, field position, score, and time — enabling detailed analysis of decision-making quality independent of outcomes.

The parallel universe framing in the title referenced counterfactual analysis: what would have happened if a team had made different decisions? Expected Points Added (EPA) and Win Probability Added (WPA) were metrics that tried to answer this — measuring whether a play improved the team's expected outcome rather than just whether it produced a first down. This kind of process vs. outcome thinking — evaluating decisions by their expected value rather than their results — is directly applicable to data-driven decision-making in business contexts.

## Key points

- Expected Points Added (EPA): measures the change in expected points resulting from a play — the analytical alternative to raw yardage or touchdowns.
- Win Probability Added (WPA): the change in win probability from a play — captures high-leverage decisions differently than EPA.
- Weather myth: data showed that bad weather affected both teams equally — home-field advantage in bad weather exists mainly because the home team's fans are more comfortable, not tactical.
- Football Outsiders: the primary independent football analytics site — created DVOA (Defense-adjusted Value Over Average) as the standard team efficiency metric.
- Process vs. outcome: Moneyball's core lesson applied to football — evaluating decisions by expected value, not realized outcomes.
- Connected to broader data journalism wave: FiveThirtyEight, The Upshot, and ESPN's analytics groups were all building at this intersection in 2013.
