## Summary

[Cargo cult analytics](/notes/cargo-cult-analytics/) is the practice of running dashboards, models, and analyses without ever questioning whether you're asking the right question or using the right method — following the rituals of data science without understanding them. The term borrows Richard Feynman's cargo cult science framing: the superficial appearance of scientific practice without the underlying epistemics.

The pattern is common in organizations that have adopted data science tooling (dashboards, A/B testing, machine learning pipelines) without investing in the statistical literacy to use them well. Teams track metrics because everyone tracks metrics, run statistical tests because tests are expected, and make decisions data-driven by citing numbers that don't actually answer the question at hand.

The four-step program proposed in the article mirrors good scientific methodology: (1) ask whether the question is well-defined, (2) ask whether the data you have can answer it, (3) ask whether your method is appropriate for the data and question, and (4) ask whether the answer actually changes what you would do. Step 4 is the most skipped — much analysis is post-hoc rationalization of decisions already made.

## Key points

- [Cargo cult analytics](/notes/cargo-cult-analytics/): following data science rituals (dashboards, models, tests) without epistemic grounding — the appearance of rigor without the substance.
- Richard Feynman's "cargo cult science" concept applied to data analysis: surface mimicry of scientific process without the underlying reasoning.
- Common failure mode: running A/B tests whose outcome won't change the decision, or tracking metrics that don't align with actual goals.
- The four-step corrective: Is the question well-defined? Can this data answer it? Is the method appropriate? Will the answer change behavior?
- Precursor to the broader "data-driven" skepticism that emerged as organizations realized data without judgment is noise.
