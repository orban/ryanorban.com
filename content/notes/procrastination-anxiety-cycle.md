## Summary

The relationship between anxiety and procrastination is bidirectional and self-reinforcing. Anxiety causes procrastination through three mechanisms: it heightens task aversion (making avoiding the task feel more relieving than starting it), intensifies worry and overthinking about the task, and amplifies rumination about past failures or consequences. But procrastination then makes anxiety worse — avoiding the task doesn't resolve the underlying concern, it just adds the stress of having avoided it.

This creates a cycle that's hard to break from inside: you avoid because you're anxious, the avoidance temporarily reduces anxiety (negative reinforcement), but the underlying task and its emotional weight remain. Over time, the tasks pile up and the anxiety grows. The cycle can coexist with or be driven by other factors — perfectionism, depression, ADHD — making it important not to assume anxiety is always the root cause.

Practical interventions the article recommends: inquiry-based stress reduction (examining the actual evidence for feared outcomes), confronting fears directly (exposure to the avoided task), task decomposition (breaking into smaller steps that feel less aversive), and self-compassion practices. Professional help is flagged for severe anxiety cases. The key leverage point is recognizing that the avoidance itself maintains the cycle.

## Key points

- Anxiety drives procrastination via task aversion, overthinking, and rumination about past/future failures.
- Procrastination exacerbates anxiety — the avoided task remains, plus there's now guilt about the avoidance.
- Avoidance works as negative reinforcement: short-term anxiety reduction → long-term anxiety amplification.
- Other causes (perfectionism, depression, ADHD) can produce the same pattern without anxiety being primary.
- Interventions: examine feared outcomes, break tasks down, exposure to the avoided task, self-compassion.
