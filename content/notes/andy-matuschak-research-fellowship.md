## Summary

Andy Matuschak runs a six-month research fellowship for emerging designers and technologists who want to work on tools for thought — systems that expand human cognitive capabilities. Fellows receive a $25,000 stipend ($30,000 if relocating to San Francisco) to work full-time on a research project developed collaboratively with Matuschak. He describes it as a miniature graduate program involving bi-weekly mentorship and public dissemination of results.

What distinguishes this fellowship from typical academic or industry programs is its honesty about uncertainty. Matuschak explicitly notes there's no clear path to post-fellowship employment, the field lacks established methodologies, and "there's a pretty good chance that nothing meaningful comes of this fellowship." No health insurance, equipment costs, or visa sponsorship are provided. The framing is a bet — both Matuschak's and the fellow's — on the idea that deeply personal, sustained exploration of tools for thought is worth doing even when the outcome is unclear.

The ideal candidate is someone "at the cusp of independent work" who can both conceive novel interfaces and execute them end-to-end. The output: a working prototype tested with real users, plus an informal essay documenting theory and findings. This is adjacent to the research model Ink & Switch pioneered — small teams doing speculative interface research outside academia with a bias toward working artifacts. Matuschak himself worked with Michael Nielsen on their Quantum Country spaced repetition project, and his personal notes site (notes.andymatuschak.org) is a well-known example of evergreen notes practice.

## Key points

- Six-month fellowship program for tools for thought research — $25k stipend, mentorship, working prototype output.
- Honest about the bet: no guaranteed career path, no institutional backing, uncertain field.
- Targets people at the cusp of independent work who can design and build end-to-end.
- Outputs: working prototype tested with users + informal essay on theory and findings.
- Reflects a model of independent speculative research outside academia — similar to Ink & Switch.
- Andy Matuschak co-created Quantum Country with Michael Nielsen and maintains a widely-followed evergreen notes system.
