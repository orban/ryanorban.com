## Summary

Adam Nash (former CEO of Wealthfront, later VP at Dropbox) teaches Stanford CS 007, a practical personal finance course specifically aimed at engineers and tech workers. The slides from the 2019 additional topics session cover areas especially relevant to the tech worker financial situation.

The course stands out because it assumes a technically sophisticated audience and doesn't oversimplify. Topics are treated quantitatively: compound interest and time value of money are done with actual math, tax implications are calculated with real marginal rates, and expected value reasoning is applied to insurance and risk decisions. The framing is engineer-appropriate — "here's the model, here's the parameter, here are the edge cases."

Key topics in the CS 007 curriculum: the mechanics and tax treatment of 401k, Roth IRA, HSA, and 529 accounts; equity compensation (RSUs vs. ISOs vs. NQSOs, with the specific tax landmines for each); the basics of asset allocation and why the Bogleheads approach makes sense mathematically; home ownership vs. renting analyzed as a financial decision; and insurance as risk transfer (not investment). The additional topics session covers areas like real estate investment, angel investing, and estate planning.

## Key points

- Course by Adam Nash (ex-Wealthfront CEO) tailored to tech worker financial situations.
- Tax-advantaged accounts: 401k, Roth IRA, HSA mechanics and optimization order.
- Equity compensation: RSU vesting, ISO vs. NQSO exercise strategies, AMT for ISOs — key for SV employees.
- Bogleheads investment approach explained with quantitative backing (not just buy index funds).
- Home ownership modeled as a financial decision, not a lifestyle assumption.
- Related vault notes: fatFIRE, Ask HN $450K investment, [Bogleheads passive investing forum](/notes/bogleheads-passive-investing-forum/).
