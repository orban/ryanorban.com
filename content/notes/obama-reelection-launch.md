## Summary

Barack Obama formally launched his 2012 re-election campaign in early May 2012 with a 17-minute video addressed directly to supporters — bypassing traditional media and delivering the launch message through email lists and social networks built during the 2008 campaign. The BBC covered it as a news event; the campaign framing emphasized grassroots organizing and the stakes of the election.

The 2012 Obama campaign is notable from a technical and organizational standpoint as much as a political one. The Obama 2008 campaign had pioneered digital organizing at scale — MyBarackObama.com, email fundraising, and social media use were ahead of any prior campaign. The 2012 version refined this: the Obama for America data team (led by Harper Reed) built sophisticated A/B testing infrastructure for email fundraising, modeled individual voter likelihood scores using machine learning, and integrated voter contact with real-time data analysis. The campaign's data operation was described afterward as a significant advancement over what campaigns had done before.

The content of this saved item — "So glad to have campaign Obama back!" — captures the energy around seeing the 2008 enthusiasm rekindled. The 2012 campaign ultimately succeeded, with Obama winning 332 electoral votes against Mitt Romney.

## Key points

- Launch via 17-minute supporter video, distributed through email list and social channels — not traditional press conference.
- Obama for America 2012 had a sophisticated data operation: Harper Reed CTO, ML-based voter modeling, A/B email testing.
- The 2012 digital campaign infrastructure became a template for subsequent campaigns and political tech startups.
- Mitt Romney was the Republican nominee; Obama won 332 electoral votes in November 2012.
- Campaign launched amid a recovering (but sluggish) post-recession economy — the core political vulnerability.
