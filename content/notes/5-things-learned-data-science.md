## Summary

Nicholas Arcolano's post reflects on practical lessons from applied data science work. The core themes are: most of the work is data cleaning and problem framing, not modeling; communicating results matters as much as producing them; and domain knowledge often matters more than algorithmic sophistication.

These lessons were common in early data science writing and have only become more widely accepted over time. The gap between "apply a classifier to clean data (what ML courses teach) and figure out what question to answer, get the data, clean it, build something interpretable enough that stakeholders trust it" (what the job actually involves) was frequently underestimated by new practitioners.

## Key points

- Problem framing is the most leveraged SKILL: a well-specified question with simple analysis often beats a sophisticated model on a poorly-specified one
- Data cleaning and preparation typically takes 80%+ of the time — mastering pandas and data pipelines is more important than mastering the latest algorithm
- Communicating uncertainty and limitations to non-technical stakeholders is a separate skill from doing the analysis
- Domain knowledge closes gaps that models can't — knowing which features matter before you fit anything is a real advantage
- Iteration speed matters: a simpler model you can explain and iterate on is more valuable than a black box that slightly outperforms it
