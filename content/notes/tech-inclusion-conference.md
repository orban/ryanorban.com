## Summary

Tech Inclusion 2015 was a San Francisco conference explicitly focused on diversity and inclusion in the tech industry — solutions-oriented, not just diagnostic. It was held at Galvanize, which positioned itself as a more accessible on-ramp to tech careers. The conference drew practitioners, researchers, and advocates working on pipeline problems, workplace culture, and structural barriers to representation in software engineering and data science.

The 2015 moment was charged: the major tech companies had just begun releasing workforce diversity data (under pressure), and the numbers were uniformly poor. The dominant conversation was shifting from do we have a problem? to what do we do about it? — with real disagreement about whether the issue was the pipeline (supply of diverse candidates) or the culture (retention and advancement).

Galvanize hosting this made sense given its positioning as an alternative credential pathway — coding bootcamps were being discussed as a potential diversity pipeline, though later evidence on this was mixed.

## Key points

- 2015 conference at Galvanize, SF — solutions-focused on tech diversity.
- Context: major tech companies had just begun releasing diversity data showing systemic gaps.
- Pipeline vs. culture debate: is the problem supply of diverse candidates, or retention/advancement?
- Bootcamps like Galvanize were positioned as alternative pathways — with mixed later evidence.
- Saved via a tweet noting that representation from "the entire valley" was concentrated in one building.
