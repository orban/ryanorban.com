## Summary

[Digital Materiality of GIFs](/notes/digital-materiality-of-gifs/) examines the GIF not as a transparent container for moving images but as a medium with its own physical and aesthetic properties — properties that shape what gets made with it. This is a media theory perspective: the format's constraints (256-color palette, no audio, infinite looping, lossless-ish compression with visible artifacts) aren't limitations to work around but defining features that produce a specific aesthetic grammar.

The 256-color palette dithering is the most visible materiality marker — the way GIFs quantize color produces distinctive gradient patterns that became an aesthetic of their own, associated with early web graphics and now with nostalgia. The mandatory loop creates a different temporal experience than video: no beginning or end, repetition as the primary mode. This is why GIFs excel at capturing gestures, reactions, and short rhythmic events — the loop makes them feel like living images rather than recorded moments.

GIF as a cultural artifact is interesting because it's a format that survived its own obsolescence. JPEG and PNG replaced it for static images; video formats replaced it for motion — yet GIF persisted because the loop and the instant playback (no buffering required in the HTML img tag) gave it functional advantages that technically superior formats didn't replicate until much later. The project explores how these technical accidents became expressive tools.

## Key points

- GIF constraints — 256 colors, looping, no audio — aren't bugs; they define the format's aesthetic possibilities.
- Palette dithering artifacts are materially visible in GIFs and became an aesthetic signature of early web culture.
- Looping creates a different temporal mode: no narrative arc, repetition as default — ideal for reactions and gestures.
- GIF survived format obsolescence because its loop + instant playback combo had no easy replacement.
- Media theory framing: the format shapes what gets made, not just how it's displayed.
