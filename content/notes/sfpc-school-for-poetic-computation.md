## Summary

The School for Poetic Computation (SFPC) opened in New York in 2013, founded by Zach Lieberman, Taeyoon Choi, Amit Pitaru, and Jen Lowe. It's a small, artist-run school — ten students per session, ten weeks, tuition-supported by scholarships — focused on the intersection of creative coding, critical theory, hardware, and art. The name is intentional: poetic signals that code here is a medium for expression and inquiry, not just a professional SKILL.

Where traditional CS programs teach computation as a tool for building products, SFPC asks what computation *means* — culturally, politically, aesthetically. Students build interactive installations, generative art, physical computing projects, and experimental interfaces. The pedagogy draws from Processing, openFrameworks, and Arduino but also from art history, feminist theory, and media studies. Zach Lieberman's own work — live-coded performances, gesture-tracking installations — embodies the school's aesthetic.

SFPC represents a recurring tension in the tech world: whether coding is fundamentally a craft (optimization, correctness, efficiency) or an art (expression, surprise, ambiguity). The school sits firmly on the art side and has influenced a generation of artists who work with code — including many who later showed at major art institutions and contributed to open-source creative tools.

## Key points

- Founded 2013 in New York by Zach Lieberman, Taeyoon Choi, Amit Pitaru, Jen Lowe — all practitioners in creative coding and generative art.
- Small-cohort model: ~10 students per session, intensive 10-week programs, emphasis on community over credentials.
- Tools: openFrameworks, Processing, Arduino, custom hardware — combined with theory seminars on media, technology, and society.
- Positioned as an alternative to both traditional CS education and commercial bootcamps — the question is always why? not just how?
- Part of the broader creative technology ecosystem that includes ITP at NYU, Recurse Center, and Eyebeam.
