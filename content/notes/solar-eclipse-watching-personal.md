## Summary

A personal photo shared on Instagram during the May 20, 2012 annular solar eclipse, showing solar eclipse viewing using shade 14 welding glass. Shade 14 welding glass is one of the recommended improvised solar filters — it blocks enough light and UV to safely view the partial and annular phases of a solar eclipse without dedicated eclipse glasses.

The eclipse was visible from the Bay Area at around 5:30 PM, reaching ~94% partial coverage. The annular solar eclipse path — which produced the ring of fire — passed north of the Bay Area through Redding and into Nevada, but the deep partial phase was clearly visible from San Francisco and surroundings.

Instagram in May 2012 was in its last month as an independent company — Facebook had acquired it for $1 billion in April and the integration was beginning. The platform was primarily photo-sharing with no video, still filtered-square-photos only, and far smaller than it became.

## Key points

- Shade 14 welding glass: correct filter density for safe solar observation during partial/annular phases.
- Bay Area eclipse May 20, 2012: ~94% coverage around 5:30 PM local time.
- Instagram in 2012: recently acquired by Facebook, still square-only photo platform.
- Safe eclipse viewing options: shade 14 welding glass, ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses, or Baader AstroSolar film.
