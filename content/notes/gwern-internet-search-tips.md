## Summary

Gwern Branwen's search guide is one of the most thorough practical treatments of how to actually find things on the internet — going well beyond use Google operators to cover the epistemics of research: how do you know when you've found what you're looking for, when to trust a source, and when to give up on web search and go to primary sources.

The guide covers advanced Google operators (site:, filetype:, date ranges, intext:), specialized databases that general search engines miss (Google Scholar, JSTOR, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, Wayback Machine for deleted content), and strategies for specific research types. For academic topics, the chain is typically: find a recent review paper → mine its references → find the primary sources. For news/current events, date-restricted searches find earlier coverage. For obscure technical topics, GitHub search and StackOverflow often outperform Google.

The Gwern framing is characteristic: systematic, obsessive, and grounded in the view that most people dramatically underinvest in search quality. The hours spent with bad sources after a failed search are worse than the minutes spent learning to search better. The archived version (Wayback Machine URL) is saved because gwern.net content occasionally changes or disappears.

## Key points

- Covers advanced Google operators, specialized databases, and domain-specific search strategies.
- Google Scholar → reference mining → primary sources: the academic research chain.
- Wayback Machine for finding deleted/changed content; critical for web research.
- Epistemics of search: know when you've actually found the answer vs. when you're pattern-matching to a plausible-sounding result.
- By Gwern Branwen — long-form, systematic, reflects an unusual level of investment in research craft.
