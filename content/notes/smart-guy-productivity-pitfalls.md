## Summary

Tom Forsyth (writing as Book of Hook, a game engine developer and veteran programmer) published this essay observing that intelligent people fall into specific, predictable productivity traps that less clever people avoid. The central insight: raw intelligence doesn't map directly to output, and in some ways it actively undermines it.

The pitfalls Forsyth identifies cluster around a few themes. **Analysis paralysis**: smart people can see more failure modes, edge cases, and second-order consequences than average, which makes it harder to commit to action. **Over-engineering**: the ability to design a general solution for a class of problems makes it tempting to build the general solution when you only need to solve one instance. **Perfectionism**: high standards combined with the ability to envision better solutions means smart people often delay shipping because they can see how it could be better. **Novelty bias**: the tendency to build new things rather than use existing solutions because the new thing is more intellectually interesting.

The irony is that productivity often comes from constraints and simplicity, which intelligence can subvert. A clear problem definition with limited options forces output; unlimited option space invites endless exploration.

## Key points

- Analysis paralysis is more common in high-intelligence engineers because they can enumerate more failure paths — the solution is committing to action with known risks, not eliminating all risk
- Over-engineering is the failure mode of confusing design SKILL with product skill: building an elegant general solution to a problem that only needed a specific one
- Perfectionism in engineering often delays shipping without proportionate quality improvement — the 80/20 rule applies to quality as well as features
- The novelty bias: intelligent people prefer solving new problems over repeating solved patterns, which conflicts with productive software development
- Counterintuitively, constraints often improve productivity for smart people by removing the infinite option space
