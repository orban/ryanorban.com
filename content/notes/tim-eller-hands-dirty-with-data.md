## Summary

Tim Eller (later co-founder of Kite, a developer tools company) made this point at the SF Data Science meetup: no amount of theoretical knowledge about algorithms and statistics substitutes for spending serious time working directly with your specific dataset.

The hands dirty framing became a recurring theme in applied data science circles in 2013. The argument: practitioners who jump straight to sophisticated models without first deeply understanding their data — its distributions, noise structure, missing data patterns, and domain-specific quirks — routinely build models that fail in production or are misinterpreted. Data exploration and sanity checking were presented as professional obligations, not optional steps.

Eller represented a class of SF-area practitioners building data products at scale (before Kite he was at Chegg) who valued craft and depth over breadth — the opposite of the "just apply a random forest to everything" heuristic that was also popular at the time.

## Key points

- Core principle: direct, intimate familiarity with a specific dataset is irreplaceable — no algorithm knowledge substitutes.
- Applied practice: distributions, noise, missingness, and domain semantics need to be understood before modeling begins.
- Tim Eller context: built data products in production (Chegg, later Kite) — spoke from experience, not theory.
- This ethos contrasts with the big data era hype of applying black-box models at scale without understanding the data.
- Captured at the Zipfian Academy graduation / SF Data Science meetup, December 2013.
