## Summary

This list from The Remote Company catalogs companies that are remote-first by design — meaning remote work is the default mode, not an accommodation. The distinction matters: a company that allows remote work may still center its culture on an office, while a remote-first company builds its communication, documentation, and process around the assumption that people aren't colocated.

Saved in May 2021, this list reflects the post-COVID moment when the conversation about remote work shifted from is this even viable? to "which companies are committing to it long-term?" Notable companies on lists like this typically include GitLab (the canonical all-remote example, with a public handbook covering how they operate), Basecamp, Automattic, Buffer, and Zapier — companies that had been remote-first before it was common.

The list was saved as a job search or research resource, reflecting interest in remote work as a sustained career mode rather than a pandemic accommodation. By 2026, the landscape has shifted considerably — many companies that went remote-first in 2020-2021 have since instituted return-to-office policies.

## Key points

- Remote-first (by design) vs. remote-tolerant (by accommodation) — the distinction that matters for long-term culture.
- GitLab, Automattic, Buffer, Zapier, Basecamp are canonical examples of genuine remote-first cultures.
- GitLab's public handbook is the most detailed documentation of how an all-remote company operates.
- Saved in 2021 at peak remote-work enthusiasm — landscape has since evolved with return-to-office pressure.
