## Summary

Sequoia Capital published this analysis in late 2023 arguing that the AI infrastructure buildout — dominated by GPU procurement from NVIDIA — had dramatically outpaced the revenue being generated by AI applications. The "$200B question" was: who generates the $200B in revenue needed to justify the GPU capital being deployed? At the time, the gap between GPU spending and AI application revenue was stark.

The argument: NVIDIA was selling H100 GPUs at enormous scale. The hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon) were buying them and passing costs to enterprise customers. OpenAI and AI startups were buying them to train and serve models. But the actual AI application revenue — what businesses were paying for AI services and generating in AI-adjacent product revenue — was a fraction of the capital being deployed. Either revenue would have to scale dramatically (which it has, somewhat) or capacity would contract.

Sequoia positioned this as a structural concern, not a bearish call on AI overall. The framing was: this is good for startups who build applications (GPU costs will fall as supply outpaces demand) and bad for companies betting on indefinite GPU scarcity. In hindsight, the analysis was partially correct — GPU costs have moderated, and the infrastructure overhang concern became a real 2024 discussion — but the AI revenue numbers also grew much faster than most 2023 projections.

## Key points

- $200B in GPU investment with far less in monetizable AI revenue in late 2023.
- Sequoia thesis: either revenue catches up or capacity contracts — a structural supply/demand imbalance.
- Good for startups: GPU cost deflation coming; bad for those betting on hardware scarcity premium.
- NVIDIA H100 procurement outpacing application-layer revenue generation.
- Partially prescient: GPU costs have moderated; the infrastructure overhang became a real 2024 discussion.
- Published at peak generative AI hype — a rare contrarian note from a major venture capital firm.
