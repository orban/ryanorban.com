## Summary

[Biological Circuit Design](/notes/biological-circuit-design/) is a web-based textbook by Michael Elowitz and Justin Bois at Caltech, covering the quantitative analysis of genetic circuits — how cells use networks of genes and proteins to perform computations, maintain homeostasis, respond to signals, and coordinate development. It treats biology through the lens of circuit theory: feedback, signal amplification, oscillators, logic gates, implemented in biochemistry rather than silicon.

The content spans gene expression regulation, feedback mechanisms, signal amplification, oscillatory dynamics (like circadian rhythms), cellular communication via signaling cascades, biological noise, and developmental patterning in multicellular organisms. Python exercises accompany the theory, making it a hands-on resource rather than purely conceptual. Michael Elowitz is notable for his work creating the repressilator — one of the first synthetic genetic oscillators — which features directly in this curriculum.

Synthetic biology and systems biology share this mathematical foundation. Understanding how genetic circuits can be designed and analyzed the same way engineers design electronic circuits is what makes synthetic biology tractable as a discipline. This textbook provides the conceptual tools for moving from biology is complicated to "these are the design principles that make biological systems legible."

## Key points

- Web textbook by Michael Elowitz and Justin Bois (Caltech); CC BY-NC-SA licensed, MIT license for code.
- Covers genetic circuits: feedback, signal amplification, oscillators, noise, developmental patterning.
- Python computational exercises throughout — not just theory.
- Michael Elowitz: creator of the repressilator (synthetic genetic oscillator), pioneer of synthetic biology.
- Treats cells as circuit designers — quantitative framework for systems biology.
- Under active development; some sections may be incomplete.
