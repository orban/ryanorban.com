## Summary

The Turntable Rider is a bicycle handlebars design that functions simultaneously as a DJ mixing controller — the rider's posture and handlebar movements (leaning, turning) map to DJ functions like pitch control and crossfader manipulation. The bookmarker noted "two of my favorite things collide" — a personal resonance that makes it a good example of what gets bookmarked at the intersection of interests.

The design represents a 2012 trend in physical computing and tangible interfaces: using the body's natural movements as input to digital systems, rather than mapping digital controls to screens. MIDI controllers were evolving beyond button grids and knobs toward objects that imposed physical metaphors on music control — Tyvek, Reactable, and various bespoke controller designs were in this space.

The project also fits within the maker movement's aesthetic of the time: combining domains that don't typically intersect (cycling + DJing), using off-the-shelf electronics integrated into physical objects, and favoring experience and expression over commercial viability. Whether the Turntable Rider is practical as either a bicycle or a DJ controller is beside the point — it's interesting as a design provocation.

## Key points

- Physical computing movement: using embodied movement and physical metaphors as input to digital systems, not just screens and buttons.
- Intersection of cycling culture and DJ culture — two domains with their own communities and aesthetics, both hardware-centric.
- The maker era (2010-2015) produced many two things I love combined projects; most were provocations more than products.
- Relates to broader interest in gestural interfaces and embodied interaction — using the whole body rather than just hands.
- Wired Underwire (culture/entertainment section) covered tech-art intersections like this; it was distinct from Wired's product and business coverage.
