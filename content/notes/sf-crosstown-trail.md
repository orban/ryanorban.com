## Summary

The [SF Crosstown Trail](/notes/sf-crosstown-trail/) is a 17-mile route through San Francisco connecting Candlestick Point in the southeast to Lands End in the northwest. Designed as a continuous urban trail, it stitches together existing parks, neighborhood streets, and green corridors that aren't usually thought of as connected — passing through McLaren Park, the Excelsior, Glen Canyon, Twin Peaks, the Panhandle, and Golden Gate Park before reaching the Sutro Baths.

The trail was formalized as a route in 2019 by a coalition including the San Francisco Parks Alliance, making explicit a connection that existed in fragments but had never been documented as a continuous path. It's designed to be done in pieces — a few miles at a time — or as a full-day challenge hike for those with the stamina for 17 miles.

The appeal is the perspective: hiking the Crosstown Trail reveals parts of San Francisco that most residents never visit, including its underrated park system. McLaren Park alone is 317 acres — larger than Dolores Park and Alamo Square combined — but few SF residents have ever been there. The trail is a way into the city's less-touristed geography.

## Key points

- 17-mile urban hiking route from Candlestick Point (SE) to Lands End (NW).
- Connects McLaren Park, Glen Canyon, Twin Peaks, Panhandle, Golden Gate Park.
- Formalized as a continuous route in 2019 by San Francisco Parks Alliance.
- Designed to be done in segments or as a full day challenge.
- Passes through San Francisco neighborhoods rarely visited by most residents.
- Free, public — no permits or fees required.
