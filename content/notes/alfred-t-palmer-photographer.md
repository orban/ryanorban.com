## Summary

Alfred T. Palmer was an American photographer who worked for the Farm Security Administration (FSA) and Office of War Information (OWI) in the early 1940s, producing Kodachrome color photographs of wartime industrial America. His photographs are among the most visually striking documentary images of World War II-era American manufacturing and labor.

The FSA/OWI photography program was one of the most significant documentary photography efforts in American history — employing photographers like Dorothea Lange, Walker Evans, and Gordon Parks to document Depression and wartime America. Palmer's contribution was working specifically in color (Kodachrome was relatively new at the time), which gives his images of factory floors, shipyards, and military hardware a vividness that black-and-white contemporaries lack.

The Wikimedia Commons collection linked here is a good entry point to his work — the photographs are in the public domain as works of the U.S. government, making them freely usable. The images of women working in aircraft factories and men in steel mills have the quality of color paintings from the era, combining documentary intent with striking compositional SKILL.

## Key points

- Photographer for the Farm Security Administration and Office of War Information (1940s).
- Worked in Kodachrome color — rare for documentary photography at the time, giving his images unusual vibrancy.
- Subject matter: wartime industrial production — aircraft factories, shipyards, military hardware manufacturing.
- Part of the famous FSA/OWI photography program alongside Dorothea Lange, Walker Evans, and others.
- Images are in the public domain (U.S. government works) — available on Wikimedia Commons.
