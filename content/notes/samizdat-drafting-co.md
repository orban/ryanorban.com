## Summary

Samizdat Drafting Co. (samizdat.cc) takes its name from samizdat — the Soviet-era practice of underground self-publishing, where citizens would type and distribute banned literature by hand, passing physical manuscripts person-to-person to circumvent censorship. The word literally means self-publishing in Russian and became a cultural symbol for independent, uncensored expression.

The site was saved in January 2013 alongside a cluster of design tools, typography references, and visualization resources — suggesting it was a small design studio, type foundry, or tools project rather than a pure news or commentary site. The .cc domain and the invocation of samizdat aesthetics point toward something in the independent publishing, letterpress, or design tools space.

The name choice in 2013 was part of a broader aesthetic in design culture that drew on Soviet constructivism, underground publishing aesthetics, and the zine tradition — a reaction against polished corporate design that valued roughness, directness, and ideological independence. This sensibility appeared in letterpress typography, risograph printing, and small-run publication work.

## Key points

- Samizdat: Soviet-era underground self-publishing; the name signals independence, anti-corporate aesthetics, and commitment to self-distribution
- The 2013 design culture context: letterpress, risograph, zines, and independent publishing were experiencing a revival as counterpoint to digital design polish
- Saved in a cluster of design/visualization/tools links — likely a studio, type foundry, or small tools project
- The ".cc" domain convention was common for creative collectives in this era (Country Code: Cocos Islands, used as "CC for creative collective")
- Related aesthetic: Soviet constructivism, Bauhaus influence in contemporary graphic design
