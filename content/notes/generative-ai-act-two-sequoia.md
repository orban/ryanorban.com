## Summary

Sequoia Capital published this essay one year after their original "generative AI" platform shift piece — reflecting on what had actually happened since the ChatGPT launch and where the next phase of value creation lies. The "Act Two" framing: Act One was the emergence and experimentation phase (foundation models, API wrappers, demo products). Act Two is the phase where application layer companies figure out where generative AI delivers durable business value versus hype.

The core argument: most of the obvious surfaces — search, content generation, coding tools — had been disrupted or were already being claimed by incumbents (Google, Microsoft, GitHub). Act Two belongs to applications that go deeper into specific workflows, accumulate proprietary data through use, and develop defensible moats that generic foundation models can't easily replicate. The killer apps are in vertical applications, workflow integration, and places where AI augments human judgment rather than replacing it.

Sequoia also introduced the value stack concept: foundation models (infrastructure), LLM infrastructure (vector databases, LangChain, etc.), and applications. Their 2023 concern was that the infrastructure layer was over-funded relative to its defensibility, while the application layer had more durable opportunity for companies that could earn trust and accumulate data.

## Key points

- Act Two thesis: move from experimentation to real business value — the easy surfaces are claimed.
- Durable opportunity: vertical applications with proprietary data loops, not generic wrappers.
- Value stack: foundation models → LLM infrastructure → applications (most durable value at edges).
- Incumbent risk: Google, Microsoft, GitHub absorb obvious surfaces; apps must go deeper into workflows.
- From Sequoia — influential framing for how the generative AI investment landscape was shifting.
- September 2023 context: follows the initial ChatGPT hype cycle and precedes the agent wave.
