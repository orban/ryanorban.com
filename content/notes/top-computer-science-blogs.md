## Summary

Dr. Tom Crick's list of top computer science blogs from 2012 captured the state of technical discourse before it migrated to Twitter threads, Substack newsletters, and YouTube explainers. In 2012, the blog was still the primary medium for thoughtful long-form technical writing outside of academic papers — people like Jeff Atwood (Coding Horror), Joel Spolsky (Joel on Software), Scott Aaronson (Shtetl-Optimized), and Terence Tao maintained blogs as primary publishing venues.

The list spanned several categories: programming practice blogs (Coding Horror, Joel on Software), theoretical computer science (Shtetl-Optimized for complexity/quantum), programming languages and type theory (Lambda the Ultimate), systems and architecture, and math-adjacent CS (Terence Tao's blog which covers everything from combinatorics to compressed sensing). The academic blogs were particularly valuable — researchers posting informal discussions of results, proof sketches, and research directions months or years before formal publication.

The medium has since fragmented significantly. Hacker News handles aggregation, Twitter handles real-time discussion, Substack handles paid long-form. The technical blog still exists but the ecosystem of regularly updated, heavily read CS blogs is smaller than in 2012. What was lost: the modestly gated discussion (you had to be motivated to find and read a blog) that tended to elevate signal-to-noise ratio compared to social media.

## Key points

- Jeff Atwood / Coding Horror: practical programming craft, developer culture (co-founded Stack Overflow).
- Joel Spolsky / Joel on Software: software management, product thinking, hiring — still widely cited.
- Scott Aaronson / Shtetl-Optimized: complexity theory, quantum computing, accessible to non-specialists.
- Terence Tao's blog: mathematics from a Fields Medalist — from research updates to expository writing.
- Lambda the Ultimate: programming languages, type theory, PLT research — community aggregator.
