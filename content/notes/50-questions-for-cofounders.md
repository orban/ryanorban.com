## Summary

This document provides a curated set of questions that prospective co-founders should work through together before formally committing to building a company. The questions cover the major axes of co-founder alignment that most frequently surface as sources of conflict or failure: equity split rationale, decision-making authority, salary expectations, outside commitments, personal financial runway, risk tolerance, and the circumstances under which someone would quit or be asked to leave.

The framework draws on the reality that most startup failures attributed to "market" or "product" problems have a co-founder dynamic component — either founders didn't discuss important topics at all, or they discovered incompatibility after it was costly to unwind. Working through these questions functions as a structured founder due diligence process, similar to how investors screen companies but applied by founders screening each other. The questions span practical logistics (what happens if one founder's spouse wants to move cities?), values alignment (how do you feel about acquiring a competitor?), and exit philosophy (what is your minimum acceptable acquisition price?).

The document is particularly useful because it makes implicit assumptions explicit before they calcify into unstated expectations. Topics like equity vesting, the meaning of full-time commitment, and how to handle a founder who underperforms are easier to negotiate in the abstract than once a company has real assets, employees, and investor relationships at stake.

## Key points

- Equity split conversations should include rationale, not just percentages — founders should be able to explain why the split is fair as circumstances change
- Questions about founder vesting and cliff periods should be settled before incorporation, not during first term sheet negotiation when outside pressure distorts judgment
- Personal financial runway matters: a founder who needs salary from month one and one who can work unpaid for two years have genuinely different risk tolerances that affect every product and hiring decision
- Decision-making questions (who has final say on hiring, on pivots, on fundraising) reveal whether the partnership has clear accountability or will collapse into consensus paralysis
- Exit philosophy divergence — one founder wants a $50M acquisition, another wants to build a generational company — is a common and late-discovered source of co-founder conflict
