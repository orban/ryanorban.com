## Summary

Bartosz Ciechanowski writes some of the best technical explainers on the internet. His posts cover physics and engineering concepts — GPS, clocks, mechanical watches, color spaces, cameras, light and shadows — through deeply interactive WebGL simulations that let you manipulate the underlying variables and watch the physics respond in real time. Each post takes months to write and can be thousands of words with dozens of interactive models embedded throughout.

The pedagogical approach is pure learning by doing: rather than showing a diagram and describing it, Ciechanowski builds a simulation where you can see the concept operating. The GPS post, for example, lets you watch satellite signals triangulate your position as you adjust satellite orbits. The mechanical watch post shows the escapement mechanism, barrel spring, and gear train all in motion, with interactive sliders. This makes abstract mechanisms viscerally comprehensible in a way that static diagrams can't achieve.

The archives link sits as a page listing all his posts. It's a finite resource — these posts are so labor-intensive that there are only ~15-20 of them — but each is extraordinary. They're frequently cited as examples of what interactive journalism and computational essays can be. Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown) and Ciechanowski represent the high end of technical visual explanation, though their formats differ: 3Blue1Brown is video, Ciechanowski is interactive web.

## Key points

- Deep interactive WebGL simulations for physics and engineering concepts — GPS, clocks, optics, mechanics.
- Posts take months to produce; ~15-20 exist, each extraordinary.
- Learning by doing approach: you manipulate the simulation, not just observe diagrams.
- Among the best examples of interactive journalism and computational essays on the web.
- Comparable to 3Blue1Brown in quality of technical explanation; interactive web vs. video format.
- Rare: resource-intensive enough that there will never be many posts, but each is a reference.
