## Summary

[Scrapism](/notes/scrapism/) is a guide by Sam Lavigne on web scraping framed as artistic and activist practice rather than data engineering. Sam Lavigne is a digital artist and educator (NYU ITP faculty) who has built projects that scrape corporate websites, government databases, and social media to expose patterns, generate art, and enable political commentary that would otherwise require enormous manual effort.

The guide teaches Python scraping (Requests, BeautifulSoup, Selenium) but in service of creative and critical goals: collecting every tweet from a politician, scraping corporate job listings to analyze labor patterns, downloading all of ICE's detention records, remixing public data into new forms. This "critical data practice" treats the web as a public archive to be read against the grain — using the same tools as commercial scrapers but toward radically different ends.

The technical instruction is competent but the conceptual framing is the distinctive contribution. Most scraping tutorials ask how do I get product prices? [Scrapism](/notes/scrapism/) asks "what does it mean to collect this data, who benefits, and what can you make with it?" This situates scraping in a broader tradition of media art, investigative journalism, and digital humanities — alongside tools like [OpenRefine](/notes/openrefine/), OSINT frameworks, and the Freedom of Information Act as methods for making institutions legible.

## Key points

- [Scrapism](/notes/scrapism/) frames web scraping as artistic/activist practice, not just data engineering
- Sam Lavigne projects: scraping ICE detention records, corporate data, political statements for critical analysis and art
- Technical stack: Requests, BeautifulSoup, Selenium — same as any Python scraper
- Critical data practice: who owns the data, what can be exposed, who benefits from collection?
- Pairs with Web Scraping Open Project for technical depth; diverges in purpose and framing
- Related: Sam Lavigne's Videogrep (scraping for video remix), data journalism tools, OSINT communities
