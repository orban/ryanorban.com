## Summary

Gifpop was a Kickstarter project by Sha Hwang and Rachel Binx that turned animated GIFs into physical lenticular prints — cards that appear to animate as you tilt them. The technique is old (lenticular printing has been used for novelty items since the 1940s), but applying it to user-submitted internet GIFs was a clever bridging of digital and physical media.

Sha Hwang and Rachel Binx were both data visualization designers with backgrounds at Stamen Design and other visualization shops. The project reflected a playful side of the data viz community that overlapped with generative art and creative coding. Their collaboration represented the intersection of technical SKILL (D3.js, data processing) and aesthetic sensibility that characterized the best data visualization work of the period.

The project funded successfully on Kickstarter and became a small consumer business. It's a good example of how creative technologists in 2013 were exploring the physical/digital boundary — making the ephemeral permanent, the screen-based tactile.

## Key points

- Lenticular printing technology applied to user-submitted GIFs — physical objects that animate through tilt.
- By Sha Hwang and Rachel Binx, both prominent in the data visualization and creative coding community.
- Funded on Kickstarter in late 2013; represents the playful intersection of internet culture and physical craft.
- Part of a broader creative technologist aesthetic that saw screen-based work as a starting point rather than an endpoint.
- Rachel Binx later cofounded Monochrome, continuing work at the intersection of data visualization and design.
