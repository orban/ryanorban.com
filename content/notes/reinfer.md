## Summary

[Re:infer](/notes/reinfer/) (reinfer.io) is an enterprise NLP platform that processes conversational data — customer emails, support tickets, live chat, internal communications — to extract intent, classify messages, measure sentiment, and trigger automations. The pitch is that most organizations have enormous amounts of unstructured text data flowing through their customer-facing and internal channels that is currently invisible to analytics: it's not in a database, it's not tagged, and it requires human reading to understand what's in it. Re:infer makes this data legible at scale.

The product sits in a specific niche: not general-purpose text analytics but specifically conversational intelligence at enterprise scale, with a focus on CX (customer experience) and operational efficiency. The model trained per-customer on their own communication patterns, which matters because domain-specific language (product names, jargon, internal process vocabulary) is crucial for accuracy in classification tasks. This was a pre-LLM approach — fine-tuned BERT-style models rather than zero-shot generalization.

[Re:infer](/notes/reinfer/) was acquired by UiPath in 2022 as part of UiPath's strategy to add natural language processing capabilities to their RPA (robotic process automation) platform. The acquisition reflects the broader pattern: RPA vendors recognized that structured automation (form filling, button clicking) needs to be augmented with unstructured data understanding to handle real-world business processes.

## Key points

- Enterprise conversational analytics: classify, route, and automate based on message content
- Focus on CX and operational efficiency — visibility into what customers and employees are actually saying
- Customer-specific model training for domain-adapted accuracy — not zero-shot
- Acquired by UiPath in 2022 to add NLP to their RPA automation platform
- Category: conversational intelligence — peers included Observe.AI, MaestroQA, Verint
- Pre-LLM architecture: fine-tuned classification models, not generative
