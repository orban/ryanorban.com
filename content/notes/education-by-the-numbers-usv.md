## Summary

Union Square Ventures examines US education through enrollment and revenue data to identify where structural change is happening and why. The core observation: university revenues are growing significantly faster than student enrollment, creating a financial pressure that's generating demand for alternatives like Income Share Agreements (ISAs) and driving EdTech investment. K-12 education by contrast has seen only modest change in structure over 20 years, with small but growing alternatives in homeschooling and charter schools.

The most interesting framing in the post is around schools' changing function: if digital content and online learning platforms make instruction itself a commodity, schools may shift "from a place of instruction to a safe space where learning is encouraged." The credentialing function, socialization function, and physical safety function remain hard to replace — but the instruction function is increasingly contestable.

USV is a venture capital firm (known for Twitter, Tumblr, Coinbase early investments), so this analysis is part investor thesis, part market map. The data-driven approach reflects their networks of engaged users investment framework applied to education.

## Key points

- University revenues growing faster than enrollment = pricing power being extracted from students, not driven by demand growth — a sign of structural market distortion.
- Income Share Agreements (ISAs) as an alternative financing model address the value-alignment problem: schools get paid when graduates earn, aligning incentives.
- K-12 education has been relatively stable structurally despite decades of reform attempts — harder to disrupt than higher education.
- The shift from instruction to learning environment is the key transformation hypothesis: content becomes free, curation and environment become the scarce good.
- Online learning growth creates pressure from both directions: cheaper alternatives for some, and employer acceptance of non-traditional credentials expanding.
