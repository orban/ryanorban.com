## Summary

[South Park Commons](/notes/south-park-commons/) (SPC) is a San Francisco-based member community for technologists — specifically people who are figuring out what's next rather than grinding at an established company or deep in execution mode at a startup. The Wired piece frames it as a modern Junto Club (referencing Benjamin Franklin's 18th-century Philadelphia intellectual society), a place where learning and intellectual exchange precede entrepreneurship rather than following from it.

The membership profile is distinctive: ex-FAANG engineers who left to explore, founders between companies, researchers stepping out of academia, investors who want to understand new technical areas. What makes SPC different from a coworking space or incubator is the explicit anti-hustle culture: the point is to learn, not to close your seed round. Members give talks, run workshops, write essays, and explore ideas without the pressure of a launch timeline or investor pressure.

Felicis Ventures' Niki Bhatt and former Samsara employee Ruchi Sanghvi are among the notable alumni (the Wired piece profiles some of them). The community sits in South Park, the actual park in SoMa that gave Silicon Valley's early startup scene its name. The 2022 context matters: crypto was at peak, remote work had fragmented Bay Area community, and SPC offered a specific answer to "where do technical people congregate to think in person?"

## Key points

- [South Park Commons](/notes/south-park-commons/): curated SF community for technologists between projects — learning-first, not launch-first
- Modern Junto Club: intellectual community as a precondition for building rather than a byproduct
- Membership profile: post-FAANG, inter-startup, post-academia — people with means and skills but not yet a mission
- Anti-incubator: no equity taken, no launch timeline, no pressure — the opposite of YC's 3-month sprint model
- Community signal: the applications are selective (the apply link was bookmarked alongside this article)
- Related: Pioneer (distributed intellectual community), On Deck, Recurse Center (technical community)
