## Summary

[NLP Demystified](/notes/nlp-demystified/) is a free online course covering natural language processing from fundamentals through modern transformer-based approaches. The curriculum spans the full arc: text preprocessing and tokenization, bag of words and TF-IDF representations, classical ML approaches (Naive Bayes, SVM, logistic regression for text), word embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe), RNNs and LSTMs for sequence modeling, and finally the Transformer architecture and BERT-style pre-trained language models.

The approach is practitioner-oriented — focused on building conceptual understanding of why each technique exists and what problems it solves, before going into implementation. This distinguishes it from more recipe-oriented tutorials (here's how to fine-tune BERT) by building the mental model that makes later techniques intelligible. The course fills the gap between I understand Python and "I understand how modern NLP systems actually work."

In December 2022, this kind of foundational NLP curriculum became suddenly more valuable: ChatGPT launched and millions of developers wanted to understand what was running underneath. Courses like [NLP Demystified](/notes/nlp-demystified/), Andrej Karpathy's [nanoGPT](/notes/nanogpt/) tutorial, and [fast.ai](/notes/fastai/)'s practical deep learning gave practitioners accessible paths into the field without requiring a PhD in linguistics or ML theory. The course's timing (predating the ChatGPT wave) meant it covered foundations that hadn't yet been overshadowed by the application layer.

## Key points

- Free NLP course covering text preprocessing → word embeddings → LSTMs → Transformers → BERT.
- Builds conceptual understanding first — explains why each technique exists, not just how to use it.
- Good for practitioners who want foundations before diving into fine-tuning or prompt engineering.
- Fills the gap between Python knowledge and understanding how LLMs work.
- Comparable to [fast.ai](/notes/fastai/) practical deep learning and Andrej Karpathy's tutorials.
- Foundation-building is more durable than tutorial content — explains concepts that remain relevant as models change.
