## Summary

Chris Stucchio's apology is a defense in the classical sense — a reasoned argument, not an expression of remorse. He pushes back on the popular narrative that high-frequency trading (HFT) is parasitic front-running that harms retail investors. His core claim: HFT firms function as modern market makers, and the spread compression they've achieved since the 1990s has transferred billions of dollars in value from professionals to ordinary investors.

The argument hinges on bid-ask spread economics. Before electronic trading, spreads on stocks like IBM might be $0.25 or more. By 2012, spreads had collapsed to fractions of a cent. Stucchio attributes much of this compression to HFT competition — each firm racing to offer tighter prices to capture order flow. The front-running complaints conflate two different things: illegal anticipatory trading (which was a real scandal in the floor-trading era) and legal co-location-based speed advantages that are available to anyone who pays for them.

The piece is part one of what was intended to be a series. It's written from inside the industry — Stucchio worked as a quant — which gives it credibility but also means it's a partial account. The flash crash of 2010 and the Michael Lewis critiques that came later ("Flash Boys") complicated this narrative considerably.

## Key points

- HFT firms compete aggressively on spread — their profit comes from tiny edges at high volume, not from extracting large amounts per trade.
- Bid-ask spread collapse since the 1990s has been one of the most tangible improvements for retail investors, and algorithmic trading drove most of it.
- The "apology" format (classical defense) signals Stucchio is responding to a specific accusatory narrative, not just explaining the industry.
- Co-location and speed advantages are structural features of the market, not abuses — the same logic applied to floor traders who had geographic proximity.
- Written in 2012, before Flash Boys (2014) shifted the public debate significantly toward Stucchio's critics.
