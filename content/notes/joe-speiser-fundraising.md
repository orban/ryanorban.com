## Summary

Joe Speiser (serial founder, @jspeiser) shared a Twitter thread in May 2022 outlining a systematic cold email approach to fundraising that he claims helped raise $20M across his startups. The thread is in the tradition of founder-to-founder fundraising advice that circulates heavily on Twitter during hot fundraising environments.

The core claim: fundraising is a numbers game with a SKILL component, and the skill is primarily in cold email quality and follow-up discipline. The system involves building a targeted list of relevant investors, personalizing outreach with specific reasons for reaching out (not templates), maintaining a CRM to track response rates and follow-ups, and treating investor outreach like a sales funnel rather than a hope-based activity.

The advice is directional but not novel — most of it mirrors what Y Combinator, First Round Capital's resources, and other fundraising guides recommend. What distinguishes the thread is the emphasis on volume plus personalization, and the explicit sales-process framing. Many founders resist treating investors as sales targets; Speiser's point is that resistance costs them deals.

## Key points

- Cold email fundraising: targeted outreach to relevant investors, personalized per recipient
- CRM discipline: track outreach, follow-up systematically — treat like a sales funnel
- Volume + personalization: reach enough investors while making each message feel specific
- Personalization signals: reference their portfolio, specific investment thesis, or recent public statement
- Related resources: Y Combinator's fundraising advice, First Round Capital's resources, Paul Graham's essays on raising
- Caveat: this playbook works better in hot markets; cold outreach conversion rates drop significantly in funding winters
