## Summary

Written by Jacqueline Nolis and Emily Robinson, this book from Manning Publications (2020) covers the full arc of a data science career — from deciding whether data science is the right fit, through getting hired, to becoming a senior contributor or moving into management. It's structured as a practical guide, not an inspirational one, which makes it stand out in the crowded career-advice space.

The book is organized in roughly chronological career order. Early chapters address building skills, choosing between analyst, ML engineer, and research-oriented data science roles, and constructing a portfolio that demonstrates real ability rather than just course completions. The job search section covers resume writing, take-home projects, and technical interviews specific to data science hiring, including the reality that DS interviews vary wildly across companies.

Later chapters get into the less-discussed parts of the job: managing stakeholders, dealing with data quality problems that aren't your fault, communicating uncertainty to non-technical audiences, and deciding when to leave a role. The final section addresses paths into data science management and the tradeoffs of the IC-vs-manager choice. Throughout, both authors draw on their own careers rather than purely theoretical advice.

## Key points

- Distinguishes between data analyst, machine learning engineer, and research scientist roles, which have different hiring processes and day-to-day work
- Emphasizes building a portfolio with end-to-end projects, not just Kaggle notebooks or tutorial reproductions
- Technical interviews for DS vary widely: some use whiteboard coding, others use take-home projects, others focus on SQL and statistics
- Stakeholder communication and navigating org politics gets substantial coverage — often ignored in technical career books
- Data science management chapter covers the IC-to-manager transition honestly, including the loss of hands-on technical work
