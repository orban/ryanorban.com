## Summary

This Forbes piece by Anthony Kosner covers the same Pebble Watch Kickstarter campaign as the Wired Q&A from the same week — a sign of how broadly the story was covered across tech media in April 2012. At the time of this Forbes piece, Pebble had already crossed the record for most-funded Kickstarter project, and the campaign was still running.

Kosner's Forbes take focuses on the business story rather than the product: what does it mean that a hardware startup can raise millions from consumers before the product ships? The investor model was being disrupted — you no longer needed venture capital to fund a hardware product's first run if you could pre-sell enough units through crowdfunding. Kickstarter was functioning as a demand discovery and pre-order mechanism simultaneously.

The Pebble story bookmarked twice (here and in the Wired Q&A) suggests real interest in the moment: the convergence of wearable computing, mobile connectivity, and crowdfunding as a distribution model. In retrospect, Pebble was a proving ground for ideas that Apple Watch, Fitbit, and Garmin would develop at scale.

## Key points

- Multiple mainstream publications (Wired, Forbes, TechCrunch) covering the same campaign shows Kickstarter hardware had crossed into mainstream tech news by 2012.
- The business implication: crowdfunding as pre-order demand discovery means hardware startups can validate market before building production inventory.
- Pebble's campaign redefined expectations for hardware crowdfunding — post-Pebble, six-figure Kickstarters became common; seven-figure ones less surprising.
- Wearable computing category validation: the demand signal from Pebble's campaign was read by Apple and Samsung as evidence the market existed.
