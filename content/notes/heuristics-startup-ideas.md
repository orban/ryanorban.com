## Summary

Avichal Garg (Electric Capital, former Facebook product director) offers a set of practical heuristics for generating startup ideas that have structural advantages — not just ideas in the abstract, but ideas positioned to benefit from market forces. The framing is useful: most startup failures aren't wrong answers to good questions, they're right answers to questions nobody needed solved. The heuristics are designed to help find questions worth answering.

The core heuristics: follow technology shifts (when new technology makes something possible that was impossible, look for the new business models it enables — mobile didn't just move desktop apps to phones, it created new categories); follow regulatory changes (new regulations create compliance needs and new market structures; old regulatory protections that erode create competition opportunities); find complain industries (businesses people use despite hating them — industries where the bar is low because incumbents are complacent); look for things that are expensive or rare today that could be cheap or common (this is often about technological progress making unit economics work); and build for your own acute problems (the scratching your own itch heuristic, but focused on shared professional pain rather than personal quirks).

The venture-relevant insight is market timing. Marc Andreessen has noted that of the three things that determine startup success (team, product, market), market is the most important — a great team with a bad market will lose to a mediocre team in a great market. Garg's heuristics are structurally about finding those great market moments: when external forces (technology, regulation, social change) shift the ground such that new entrants have an inherent advantage over incumbents who optimized for the old world.

## Key points

- Technology shifts are the most reliable source of startup opportunities — new tech creates new possible business models, not just better versions of old ones.
- Regulatory changes (new and old unwinding) create structured market opportunities — compliance needs, new entry opportunities.
- "Complain industries": find businesses with high usage despite deep customer dissatisfaction — indicates low incumbent investment in quality.
- Market timing trumps team and product quality — structural forces matter more than execution for determining which startups can succeed.
- Acute vs. chronic pain distinction: build for acute pain (users actively seeking a solution) rather than chronic pain they've accepted.
- By Avichal Garg (Electric Capital partner) — VC with operational background; the heuristics reflect what investors look for in pitch framing.
