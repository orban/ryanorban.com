## Summary

42Floors was a commercial real estate startup (later acquired and folded into CommercialCafe) focused on office space search. Their 2012 blog post observed a notable shift in the San Francisco office market: after 2008's downturn had created a prolonged tenant's market — plenty of space, falling rents, landlords offering free months and tenant improvement allowances — the rapid expansion of tech companies had absorbed available inventory and the dynamic had flipped.

The pendulum metaphor described the power shift from tenants to landlords. As startups scaled up and established tech companies like Twitter, Zynga, Dropbox, and Salesforce took large blocks of space, South of Market (SoMa) vacancy rates fell sharply. Landlords who had been eager to negotiate suddenly weren't. New lease terms got shorter. Prices went up. The free months and TI allowances evaporated.

This was written at the height of pre-Facebook IPO optimism in 2012 — the moment before tech's first major public offering of that cycle was supposed to validate the whole ecosystem. The post captures the real estate consequences of that froth: space had become a scarce resource, and companies that hadn't locked in long leases before 2012 were competing for a shrinking supply.

## Key points

- San Francisco office market shifted from tenant-friendly (2009–2011) to landlord-friendly (2012) as tech expansion absorbed supply.
- SoMa vacancy rates fell as Twitter, Zynga, Dropbox, and others took large blocks of space.
- Tenant concessions (free months, TI allowances) disappeared as landlords regained pricing power.
- Timing insight: companies that waited too long for the "bottom" missed the window.
- 42Floors positioned as real estate intelligence for startups navigating this market shift.
- Written at peak pre-Facebook IPO optimism — real estate as a lagging indicator of the tech bubble moment.
