## Summary

Patrick McKenzie (patio11) argues that software engineers should structure their work to produce permanent artifacts — not just ephemeral labor. The essay's core claim: each week of work should leave behind something that persists and compounds, rather than disappearing into internal systems no one will ever see. A blog post, an open-source contribution, a piece of writing, a tool — something that accumulates value over time and is visible to people who haven't met you.

The economics are stark: if you spend 40 hours solving a problem inside a company, that value mostly accrues to the company. If you spend some of those hours writing about what you learned, the insights become a permanent asset. People find your writing years later, it builds professional reputation, it attracts opportunities, and it compounds in ways internal work cannot. McKenzie cites his own trajectory as evidence — his Hacker News comments and blog posts generated more career opportunities than his day job.

This connects to his broader framework about career development: software engineers routinely underprice themselves because their value-generating work is invisible and ephemeral. Making work visible through writing and open-source contribution converts invisible labor into visible reputation. The advice also extends to building in public, contributing to external communities, and preferring jobs where external artifacts are permitted and encouraged over jobs that lock everything behind NDAs.

## Key points

- Each week should produce a permanent artifact: blog post, open-source contribution, or piece of writing.
- Visible external work compounds; invisible internal work mostly accrues to employers.
- Open-source contributions and writing create career optionality — opportunities find you without applying.
- Connects to patio11's salary negotiation advice: both argue engineers systematically underinvest in career capital.
- By Patrick McKenzie — also wrote Salary Negotiation (the canonical negotiation essay for tech workers).
