## Summary

The "consumerization of enterprise IT" was a 2012–2013 argument with real teeth: iPhone and iPad had reset user expectations for software interfaces so dramatically that employees were comparing their work tools to their personal devices — and finding the enterprise tools wanting. GigaOM's piece made the case that this pressure was reaching infrastructure software, not just end-user applications.

The argument went: consumer apps had shown that complex functionality could be packaged behind simple, discoverable interfaces. There was no reason this had to stop at enterprise SaaS — it should apply to storage, networking, compute management too. Nutanix was the canonical infrastructure example: hyperconverged storage and compute in a 2U appliance managed through a web interface that looked like a consumer app, not an IBM Storage Navigator. This was genuinely new — most enterprise infrastructure software in 2013 was configured through CLI and XML configuration files, with GUI interfaces bolted on as afterthoughts.

The broader thesis — that buyers inside enterprises were increasingly making or influencing purchasing decisions, not just IT departments — proved largely correct. Dropbox, Slack, Box, and later Zoom all entered enterprises via individual user adoption before landing enterprise contracts. Nutanix rode the same wave: DevOps teams who'd used AWS and had no patience for traditional SAN workflows made the case internally.

## Key points

- Consumerization of IT: employees exposed to polished consumer apps began demanding comparable UX from enterprise software — a genuine forcing function on enterprise product design.
- Nutanix exemplified the pattern at the infrastructure layer: hyperconverged appliances with a consumer-grade management interface, bypassing the traditional 3-tier storage architecture.
- The trend had two vectors: bottom-up (employees choose tools) and top-down (buyers expect consumer-quality UX from vendors they evaluate).
- SaaS tools like Dropbox and Box proved the bottom-up model; infrastructure vendors like Nutanix and later Pure Storage proved it could extend to hardware.
- By 2023, enterprise-grade UX has become table stakes rather than differentiation — the thesis played out fully.
