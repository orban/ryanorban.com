## Summary

Mike Tamir was head of data science at Galvanize / Zipfian Academy and later at Uber ATG (autonomous vehicles). His Quora answer on data science interview puzzle questions was widely shared in the 2014 bootcamp and hiring community. It touches on a genuine design problem: puzzle questions that test clever thinking vs. rote knowledge of statistics.

The canonical data science interview puzzles of this era tested things like: conditional probability reasoning (e.g., Monty Hall, disease test false positives), back-of-envelope estimation (fermi problems — "how many piano tuners in Chicago?"), product sense (design metrics for a feature), and A/B test design (what sample size do you need, how do you handle peeking). These categories map to the distinct skills that data science roles actually require: statistical reasoning, quantitative intuition, product judgment.

Mike Tamir's perspective would have been shaped by running hiring for data science bootcamp graduates — a population with diverse academic backgrounds but explicit training in applied statistics and Python. The challenge for bootcamp hiring was distinguishing candidates who had memorized answers from those who could reason under uncertainty, which pushed toward open-ended puzzle questions.

## Key points

- Mike Tamir: head of data science at Galvanize / Zipfian Academy, later Uber ATG.
- Good puzzle questions test probabilistic reasoning and estimation — not memorization of formulas.
- Classic categories: conditional probability, Fermi estimation, A/B test design, product metrics.
- Bayes' theorem interpretation: the canonical "90% accurate test, 1% base rate — what's the probability of actually having the disease?" test.
- A/B testing design: power calculations, sample size, handling sequential testing (peeking problem).
- Fermi problems test comfort with uncertainty and structured decomposition — skills more predictive of job performance than textbook knowledge.
