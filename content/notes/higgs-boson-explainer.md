## Summary

Saved in June 2012, just weeks before CERN announced the discovery of the Higgs boson on July 4, 2012 — one of the most significant moments in particle physics in decades. The PHD Comics video (narrated by Daniel Whiteson and animated in the RSAnimate style) explained the particle's significance in plain terms at a moment of huge public interest.

The Higgs boson is the particle associated with the Higgs field — a field that permeates all of space and gives other particles their mass. Without mass-conferring interactions with the Higgs field, particles like the W and Z bosons (which carry the weak nuclear force) would be massless and travel at the speed of light, which would make chemistry and therefore matter impossible. The Standard Model of particle physics predicted the Higgs in 1964 (by Peter Higgs, François Englert, and others) but it took until the Large Hadron Collider at CERN to reach the energies required to produce one.

CERN's LHC confirmed the Higgs at approximately 125 GeV — smashing protons at nearly the speed of light and looking for the decay signatures that indicate a Higgs was momentarily created. Peter Higgs and François Englert won the 2013 Nobel Prize in Physics for the theoretical prediction. The discovery completed the Standard Model and closed a 48-year experimental gap.

## Key points

- Higgs boson is the excitation of the Higgs field, which gives mass to W and Z bosons of the weak nuclear force.
- Without the Higgs mechanism, the fundamental forces would unify at all energies and atoms couldn't exist.
- CERN's LHC confirmed discovery July 4, 2012, at ~125 GeV — 48 years after theoretical prediction.
- Peter Higgs and François Englert won the Nobel Prize in Physics 2013 for the prediction.
- PHD Comics/RSAnimate style made complex physics accessible during the peak of public interest.
