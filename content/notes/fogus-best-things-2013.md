## Summary

Michael Fogus (author of *The Joy of Clojure* and *Functional JavaScript*) publishes an annual best things list that reads like a curated reading list from someone who follows programming languages, computer science history, and software philosophy seriously. The 2013 edition spans blog posts, technical books, papers, and miscellany.

Notable blog posts highlighted include Joe Armstrong's My Favorite Erlang Program, Martin Fowler's Internal Reprogrammability, and Bob Nystrom's JavaScript Isn't Scheme (Fogus's pick for best of the year). The list skews toward foundational computing concepts, design thinking, and historically underappreciated figures. Fogus's own most-viewed post — "10 Technical Papers Every Programmer Should Read" — had been at the top of his own traffic for three consecutive years, which says something about what programmers actually want.

The technical books section highlights "10 PRINT CHR$(205.5+RND(1)); : GOTO 10" — a whole book analyzing a single line of Commodore 64 BASIC code, examining what it reveals about randomness, aesthetics, and emergent behavior. Non-technical reading: *Earth Abides* (post-apocalyptic novel).

## Key points

- Joe Armstrong (Erlang creator)'s My Favorite Erlang Program is highlighted — usually about the elegance of small programs.
- Bob Nystrom's "JavaScript Isn't Scheme" named best post of 2013 — on the gap between aspirational and actual language design.
- Martin Fowler's "Internal Reprogrammability" — software that can modify its own behavior.
- Book: *10 PRINT* — a book-length analysis of a single line of Commodore 64 BASIC, examining randomness and emergent aesthetics.
- Fogus's most popular post: "10 Technical Papers Every Programmer Should Read" — consistently high demand for foundational CS literature recommendations.
- The list is a useful signal for what serious functional programming practitioners were reading in 2013.
