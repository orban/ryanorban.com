## Summary

This CIO.com article surveyed the landscape of data science education in late 2013, naming UC Berkeley (via BerkeleyData / the School of Information) and Zipfian Academy as leading sources of data science talent. The article appeared as the first major wave of demand for data scientists was crashing into a supply shortage — companies were hiring, universities hadn't yet spun up degree programs at scale, and bootcamps like Zipfian Academy were filling the gap.

Ryan saved this article while literally being trained at Zipfian Academy — a meta-moment. The program was positioning itself as the fastest path from technical background to employable data scientist, and coverage like this (reaching CIOs at hiring companies) was part of building the demand side for graduates.

The two paths being compared were qualitatively different. Berkeley's programs gave academic depth — statistics theory, research methodology, peer review — that the bootcamp couldn't match. Zipfian Academy gave applied SKILL in a fraction of the time: 12 weeks covering Python, SQL, machine learning, and project-based work with real datasets. Both were producing people companies wanted to hire, but for different roles and at different price points.

## Key points

- CIO.com article positioned Berkeley and Zipfian Academy as the leading sources for data science talent in 2013.
- The demand/supply mismatch for data scientists in 2013 made bootcamps viable: companies would hire graduates with demonstrated skills regardless of credential.
- Zipfian Academy offered speed (12 weeks) and application; Berkeley offered depth and credentialing — both met real hiring needs.
- Ryan saved this while attending Zipfian Academy, making it a personal touchstone for the bootcamp's external positioning.
- Precursor to the massive bootcamp expansion of 2014–2016 as dozens of programs followed the Zipfian model.
