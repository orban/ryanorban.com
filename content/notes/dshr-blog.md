## Summary

David Rosenthal is a computer scientist who co-developed the LOCKSS (Lots of Copies Keep Stuff Safe) digital preservation system at Stanford University Libraries, and DSHR's Blog is his long-running writing on digital preservation, storage economics, and internet infrastructure. It's one of the few places where the economic realities of long-term data storage are analyzed with genuine technical rigor.

A recurring theme is storage economics: the cost of keeping data for decades follows trends that most people misunderstand. Disk prices have fallen dramatically over time, but the rate of decline is slowing, and the cost of *accessing* stored data (retrieval, format migration, ongoing system maintenance) often dominates the cost of the storage media itself. This makes long-term preservation harder than naive "just store everything on cheap disks" intuitions suggest.

Rosenthal is also a consistent skeptic of blockchain-based storage and preservation proposals. He applies straightforward economics to claims like "the blockchain will preserve everything forever" and typically finds the economics don't work: the incentive structures break down over long time horizons, or the implied cost-per-byte is orders of magnitude too high. His critiques are technically grounded rather than dismissive.

## Key points

- Co-developer of LOCKSS, the Stanford digital preservation system — David Rosenthal has deep domain credibility.
- Storage economics: the cost model for long-term preservation is dominated by access and migration costs, not raw storage media.
- Recurring blockchain storage critique: applies real economics to claims about blockchain-based preservation; consistently finds the numbers don't work.
- Covers internet history, link rot, and the practical failures of digital archiving at scale.
- Rare perspective: technical rigor + long-run thinking + genuine expertise in a domain most technologists underestimate.
