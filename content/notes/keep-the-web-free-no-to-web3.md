## Summary

This manifesto from the Yesterweb community is one of the cleaner articulations of the skeptic's case against Web3 — not a technical rebuttal but a values-based critique of what the movement actually produces versus what it claims to produce.

The core argument: Web3 marketing promises decentralization and democratization, but the actual power structure concentrates control among early investors and wealthy whales who hold most tokens. The decentralization is nominal — infrastructure still runs on AWS and Cloudflare, the major DeFi protocols have upgrade keys held by small teams, and governance tokens are distributed unequally enough that large holders effectively control decisions. This isn't decentralization; it's different centralization with different beneficiaries.

The economic critique is blunt: NFTs and cryptocurrency create artificial scarcity in a medium defined by abundance, and the primary wealth transfer mechanism moves money from ordinary participants to early adopters and insiders. The energy argument is stated in stark terms — Bitcoin mining uses more electricity than many small countries, accelerating the environmental crisis. The information asymmetry argument notes that understanding what you're buying in Web3 requires enough financial and technical knowledge that most participants make uninformed decisions.

## Key points

- Decentralization rhetoric vs. reality: infrastructure still runs on AWS/Cloudflare; upgrade keys held by small teams; governance tokens distributed unequally.
- Economic structure: NFTs create artificial scarcity; wealth transfers from ordinary participants to early adopters and insiders.
- Environmental cost: Proof of Work blockchains consume electricity at nation-state scale.
- Information asymmetry: complexity creates inherent advantage for technically sophisticated early adopters over ordinary participants.
- The critique isn't blockchain is bad technology but "the implementation serves different values than advertised."
- From Yesterweb — the community focused on reclaiming the early open web aesthetic and ethos.
