## Summary

Molecular Notes is a personal knowledge management (PKM) system built around three primitives: **Sources** (information inputs — articles, books, podcasts), **Atoms** (established concepts extracted from Sources), and **Molecules** (personal insights formed by combining Atoms). The workflow: mine Atoms from Sources, synthesize Atoms into Molecules, organize everything with Topics.

The core insight is about quadratic growth in knowledge connections: every new Atom you add can potentially connect with every existing Atom, creating cross-links that generate new Molecules. This is the Zettelkasten observation applied more explicitly — a network of concepts compounds in value as it grows, because you're not accumulating notes linearly but building a graph.

"Molecules" are specifically personal insights — either genuinely original or understanding so deeply internalized it functions as original. This distinction from Atoms is meaningful: Atoms are established knowledge anyone could extract; Molecules are your synthesis. The system addresses the common PKM failure mode of accumulating notes without transforming them into internalized understanding. Related to linking your thinking in Obsidian and Roam Research.

## Key points

- Three-primitive system: **Sources** → **Atoms** (concepts) → **Molecules** (personal insights).
- Molecules = either original insights or deeply internalized understanding — not just summaries.
- Quadratic growth: each new Atom connects with all existing Atoms, multiplying connection opportunities.
- Addresses the PKM failure mode of note accumulation without genuine synthesis.
- Conceptually similar to Zettelkasten but more explicit about the synthesis step (Sources → Atoms → Molecules).
- Related to linking your thinking and networked thought as implemented in Obsidian and Roam Research.
