## Summary

SF Chronicle reporter Heather Knight published this investigative piece finding that SFPD's 45 dedicated traffic officers were collectively issuing only about 10 citations per day combined — roughly one ticket per officer every 4.5 days, in a city with significant traffic fatality problems.

The investigation raised accountability questions: what were these officers doing with their time if not enforcing traffic laws? The SFPD's own data showed citation rates had collapsed from several hundred per day in earlier years to near zero. San Francisco had simultaneously declared traffic safety emergencies and set Vision Zero goals (zero traffic fatalities) — the enforcement collapse made those goals incoherent.

The broader context: San Francisco law enforcement under-enforcement became a significant political issue in the early 2020s, with critics pointing to data like this as evidence of systemic accountability failures. The traffic enforcement data was particularly striking because it was concrete and measurable — unlike other enforcement questions, citation rates are directly countable.

## Key points

- 45 dedicated SFPD traffic officers issued ~10 citations combined per day in 2022.
- ~1 citation per officer every 4.5 days in a city with active Vision Zero traffic safety goals.
- Citation rates had collapsed from hundreds per day in earlier periods.
- Accountability gap: the enforcement data directly contradicted stated San Francisco traffic safety priorities.
- Reported by Heather Knight at the SF Chronicle.
- Part of broader data journalism on SFPD performance metrics.
