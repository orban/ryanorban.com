## Summary

Daniel Tenner's swombat.com post addresses a specific cognitive trap common in startup founders: holding unexamined beliefs about what's possible that constrain the scope of what they attempt. The framing is practical and technical — hacking beliefs means treating them as modifiable parameters in a system rather than facts about the world. A belief is only useful if it's true; if it's false, it's a bug.

The mechanism works in both directions. Useful limiting beliefs ("I can't raise $10M on my first startup") can prevent wasted effort. But false limiting beliefs ("enterprise sales requires a large team or this market is too competitive") foreclose options before they're examined. The post's argument: most people never explicitly audit their beliefs — they inherited them from environment, past experience, or social consensus, and they feel like facts rather than hypotheses. Treating them as hypotheses opens them to testing.

The practical approach is disconfirmation: find one person or company who has done the thing you believe is impossible. That single counter-example falsifies the universal claim. "Nobody's built a successful B2B product without an outbound sales team" — find the one that did, and the belief collapses. What remains might be a useful prior ("it's harder without an outbound team") but not a constraint. The growth mindset literature (Carol Dweck) makes a similar argument at the level of abilities; this applies the same logic to strategic and environmental beliefs.

## Key points

- Beliefs about the world function as constraints on strategy: unexamined false beliefs foreclose options before they're evaluated.
- The hack framing: treat beliefs as hypotheses to test rather than facts to accept — apply Karl Popper's falsificationism to your own assumptions.
- Disconfirmation technique: find a single counter-example to a nobody ever does X belief — one example falsifies the universal claim.
- Common startup limiting beliefs: about market size, what customers will pay, what requires funding, what needs a large team — all testable.
- Related to cognitive reframing in psychology and to premortems (identifying assumptions before they become failures).
