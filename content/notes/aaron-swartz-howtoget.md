## Summary

Aaron Swartz wrote howtoget as a compact guide to the mechanics of creating opportunities — essentially a distillation of how he, as a teenager, managed to work alongside people like Lawrence Lessig and Tim Berners-Lee before finishing high school. The underlying principle is that most gatekeeping is softer than it looks, and that the main thing separating people from the opportunities they want is the failure to ask.

The specific advice: identify who you need to reach, send a direct and specific email (not a generic I'd love to pick your brain note but something that shows you've thought about what you want and why they can help), offer something in return even if small, and follow up. Swartz was dismissive of credentials as a prerequisite — he thought most people waited for permission they didn't need and credentials they didn't have to earn before going after the thing they wanted.

This was characteristic of how he operated: he co-wrote the RSS 1.0 spec at 13, worked on Creative Commons while in high school, co-founded Reddit (briefly) as a college student, and was a contributor to the development of Markdown. The "howtoget" guide is him articulating the approach that made all of that possible.

## Key points

- Most gatekeepers respond to direct, specific, well-reasoned asks — generic networking requests get ignored, specific requests get through
- Offer something concrete: what you can contribute, even if modest, is more compelling than what you want to learn
- Stop waiting for credentials or permission; most people are accessible if you approach them correctly
- Follow up: one non-response is not a no
- The opportunity cost of not asking is much higher than the occasional rejection
