## Summary

[Art Diario](/notes/art-diario/) puts a new piece of beautiful, classical art on your desktop wallpaper each day — a simple utility for people who want aesthetic variety in their computing environment without curating it themselves. The selection leans toward classic painting and photography from public collections, rotating automatically.

The concept fits into a small category of apps that bring museum collections into daily digital life: Google Arts & Culture (high-res museum digitization, also used for wallpapers), Museum of Modern Art's digital collections, Wikimedia Commons art bots on social media, and desktop wallpaper apps from platforms like Unsplash that allow filtering by art/painting. The appeal is ambient exposure — you don't have to go to a gallery to encounter interesting visual art; it appears behind your work windows.

There's a subtle argument here about how visual taste develops through exposure rather than study. Surrounding yourself with high-quality art — even incidentally, as desktop backgrounds — gradually builds a reference library of visual styles, compositions, and color use. Apps like [Art Diario](/notes/art-diario/) and Muzei (the equivalent for Android) operationalize this as a low-friction habit. The curatorial question (which art is shown, from which periods and traditions) matters more than it might seem — a well-curated selection introduces genuine visual diversity; a poorly curated one just shows the same Monet water lilies repeatedly.

## Key points

- Daily rotating desktop wallpaper from classical and fine art collections — automatic, no curation effort required.
- Ambient art exposure as a practice: visual taste develops through repeated encounter even without active study.
- Fits the pattern of apps surfacing public museum collections in daily digital life: Google Arts & Culture, Muzei.
- Curatorial quality is the key differentiator — breadth across periods, styles, and traditions vs. recycling famous works.
- Simple utility; the value is consistency over time rather than any single image.
