## Summary

UC Berkeley's I290 "Analyzing Big Data with Twitter" was an early academic course on data science and big data engineering that posted all lecture videos publicly. The course was offered through the School of Information (i-School) in partnership with Twitter, which provided access to their data firehose for student projects. The curriculum covered Hadoop MapReduce, natural language processing for social text, streaming analytics, social network analysis, and machine learning at scale.

The public release of the lecture series in December 2012 was notable: high-quality university courses on these topics were rare and expensive (this was shortly before Coursera and edX began scaling MOOC content heavily). For practitioners trying to understand big data technologies outside of formal degree programs, finding a well-structured Berkeley graduate course available freely online was significant.

Twitter data was an unusually rich training ground: a mix of NLP challenges (short text, informal language, real-time stream), social network analysis (follower graphs, retweet cascades), and genuine big data scale (hundreds of millions of tweets per day). The course used real Twitter data rather than toy datasets, which was uncommon for academic data science courses at the time.

## Key points

- UC Berkeley i-School: information school at Berkeley; the academic home for applied data science and information management research
- Twitter firehose: full stream of all public tweets; provides real-time big data at scale — the course used this for student projects
- Curriculum: Hadoop, MapReduce, NLP for short text, social network analysis, stream processing — the core 2012 big data curriculum
- Historical context: pre-MOOC-scale era; publicly posted university lectures were rare and valuable for self-directed learners
- The 2012 data science field was consolidating around this specific stack (Hadoop + Python + ML); the course captured it at the right moment
