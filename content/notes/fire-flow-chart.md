## Summary

The FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) flowchart is a community-maintained decision tree from r/financialindependence that answers the perennial question: "What should I do with my money?" The v4.2 version is the result of years of community iteration, distilling the consensus order of operations for building wealth toward financial independence.

The flowchart structure prioritizes options by expected return and tax advantage: employer 401k match (free money), high-interest debt payoff (guaranteed return), HSA contributions (triple tax advantage), maxing Roth IRA or Traditional IRA, maxing 401k, then taxable brokerage accounts. Emergency fund maintenance and debt decisions are woven throughout. The philosophy underneath is index fund investing — Vanguard-style total market index funds rather than stock picking or active management.

FIRE as a movement has several variants: LeanFIRE (extreme frugality to retire early on minimal spending), FatFIRE (retire with high spending capacity), BaristaFIRE (partial retirement with part-time work), and CoastFIRE (save aggressively early, then coast to retirement age). The flowchart is agnostic about which variant you're targeting — it optimizes for accumulation efficiency regardless of target.

The 327 upvotes and 70 comments reflect genuine engagement; r/financialindependence treats this as core reference material, not just a popular post.

## Key points

- Order of operations: employer match → high-interest debt → HSA → IRA → 401k → taxable brokerage
- Philosophy: tax-advantaged accounts first, index fund investing throughout, emergency fund as foundation
- FIRE number = 25x annual spending (based on 4% safe withdrawal rate from Trinity Study)
- Variants: LeanFIRE, FatFIRE, BaristaFIRE, CoastFIRE — different spending targets, same accumulation logic
- Community-maintained on Reddit; v4.2 reflects years of iteration and debate
