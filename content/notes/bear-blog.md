## Summary

[Bear Blog](/notes/bear-blog/) is a free, minimal blogging platform by Herman Martinus built around a single conviction: publishing should be simple, fast, and free of surveillance. No JavaScript, no trackers, no ads, no elaborate CMS. You write Markdown, posts publish to a plain HTML page that loads instantly. The discover feed at bearblog.dev/discover/ surfaces posts from across the platform, functioning as a reader for the [Bear Blog](/notes/bear-blog/) community.

The platform connects to the IndieWeb movement and the broader personal blog renaissance that accelerated in the early 2020s — a backlash against algorithmic social media, platform lock-in, and the enshittification of hosted blogging (Medium paywalling content, Substack adding noise, Ghost requiring self-hosting knowledge). Bear Blog occupies the "I just want to write and be read" position: no customization overhead, no growth hacking, no monetization pressure.

The technical choices are deliberate: no client-side JavaScript means no tracking, faster load times, and accessibility without JavaScript enabled. The writing interface is textarea-and-markdown — closer to writing in a text editor than a WYSIWYG tool. For writers who want to build a habit of publishing without infrastructure overhead, this is genuinely lower friction than the alternatives. The discover feed creates a small community layer without algorithmic recommendation.

## Key points

- Free blogging with zero JavaScript, zero tracking, zero ads — fast by design, not by optimization.
- Markdown input → static HTML output; the simplest possible path from writing to published.
- Discover feed at bearblog.dev/discover/ — community reader for Bear Blog posts, chronologically sorted.
- IndieWeb positioning: personal URL, your content, no platform middleman extracting value.
- Contrast with: Substack (email list monetization), Medium (paywalls + algorithm), Ghost (self-hosted complexity).
- By Herman Martinus — also built other small, considered tools with the same philosophy.
