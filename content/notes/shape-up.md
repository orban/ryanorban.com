## Summary

[Shape Up](/notes/shape-up/) is Basecamp's internal product development methodology, written by Ryan Singer and published as a free book. It's an explicit rejection of the Scrum/Agile ceremony culture that dominates most software teams — no daily standups, no sprint planning rituals, no backlog grooming. Instead, it describes how Basecamp actually ships software.

The core structure: six-week cycles with two-week cool-down periods between them. Work in a cycle is shaped before it's assigned — a shaping process that produces a concrete pitch (a written document) specifying what problem to solve, approximate solution, constraints, and risks. Crucially, shaping happens before scheduling, not during. The team executing a cycle has full authority over how to implement the shaped work.

The key insight that separates [Shape Up](/notes/shape-up/) from most methodologies: fixed time, variable scope. A cycle is six weeks. If you can't fit the full idea in six weeks, you cut scope, not time. This prevents the endless deadline-slipping that happens when scope is fixed but time is flexible. It also changes how you think about specifications — a good pitch describes the appetite (how much time this problem deserves), not just the solution.

## Key points

- Six-week cycles, not two-week sprints — long enough to build something meaningful.
- Shaping precedes scheduling: work must be defined concretely before it enters a cycle.
- Fixed time, variable scope: the cycle ends on time, scope is cut if needed.
- Appetite framing: how much time is this problem worth, before you design a solution?
- No backlog — ideas not shaped and scheduled in a cycle are just lost; only current bets matter.
- Available free online; significant influence on startup product culture in 2021–2023.
