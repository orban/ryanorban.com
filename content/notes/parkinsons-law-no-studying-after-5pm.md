## Summary

Parkinson's Law states that work expands to fill the time available for its completion. Sean Gransee's application: impose a hard 5pm cutoff on studying and academic work, and suddenly the available time compresses — forcing focus and prioritization that open-ended days don't require.

The mechanism is real. Without a deadline, the mind treats work as infinitely deferrable. Knowing you *must* stop at 5pm creates a genuine constraint that functions like a deadline. You plan what needs to get done, you start earlier, and you don't allow low-value busywork to crowd out actual thinking. The evening becomes protected time for rest, social life, and recovery — which themselves enable better work the next day.

This connects to the broader literature on timeboxing, deep work (which requires protected recovery periods), and the well-documented benefits of constraints on creative output. Cal Newport would later articulate the shutdown ritual in *Deep Work* as a formalized version of this — a deliberate cognitive boundary between work and rest. Gransee's college-focused version is a more accessible, concrete form of the same idea.

## Key points

- Parkinson's Law: "Work expands to fill the time available for its completion" — C. Northcote Parkinson, 1955
- Imposing a hard 5pm stop forces earlier starts and better prioritization during the day
- The protected evening enables genuine recovery — which improves next-day cognitive performance
- Connects to Cal Newport's "shutdown ritual" in *Deep Work* — formalizing the work/rest boundary
- Applies beyond studying: any knowledge work benefits from hard daily endpoint rather than open-ended hours
