## Summary

UC Berkeley's iSchool course I296A on Data Science Algorithms was one of the early university-level data science courses, predating the explosion of data science bootcamps and MOOCs. The School of Information was a natural home for early data science education: it sat at the intersection of computer science, statistics, and information management without being rigidly tied to any one discipline.

The course existed at a moment when data science didn't yet have a standard curriculum. Instructors were drawing from statistics, machine learning, database systems, and domain-specific methods to build something coherent. Berkeley's broader AMPLab research was producing foundational big data tools like Apache Spark and Mesos during the same period, creating a tight feedback loop between academic research and practitioner curriculum.

By the time Zipfian Academy was founded in 2012 in San Francisco, Berkeley had already been developing data science as an academic discipline for several years. Ryan saved this in late 2013 while attending Zipfian — likely studying what the academic counterpart looked like versus the intensive bootcamp experience.

## Key points

- UC Berkeley iSchool was one of the first academic programs treating data science as a distinct discipline rather than a subfield of CS or statistics.
- Spring 2012 — predating most bootcamps and the mainstream data scientist job title becoming common.
- Berkeley was also producing core infrastructure (Spark, Mesos) through AMPLab during the same period — research and curriculum reinforced each other.
- Saved by Ryan during the Zipfian Academy period — comparison point between intensive bootcamp and university approaches.
