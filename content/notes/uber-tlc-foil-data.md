## Summary

In 2015, FiveThirtyEight filed a FOIL (Freedom of Information Law) request with the NYC Taxi and Limousine Commission and received six months of Uber pickup location data. They published it on GitHub, making it the largest open dataset of ride-share trip data available at the time. The release was significant: it turned regulatory filings into public data science infrastructure.

The dataset covers April–September 2014, containing every Uber pickup in NYC: datetime, latitude/longitude, and vehicle base affiliation. The geographic visualization — plotting all 4.5 million pickups as points on a map — showed Uber's penetration into areas underserved by yellow taxis, particularly in the outer boroughs and outer Manhattan. Eric Fischer produced the most widely-circulated map visualization from the data.

This came at a politically charged moment: NYC Mayor Bill de Blasio was attempting to cap Uber's growth, and FiveThirtyEight's analysis directly informed public debate about whether Uber was expanding access or just adding congestion. The data showed Uber heavily concentrated in areas with poor yellow cab service — an argument against the cap.

## Key points

- 4.5 million Uber NYC pickups, April–September 2014 — latitude/longitude, datetime, base.
- Obtained via FOIL request from the NYC TLC — a precedent for treating ride-share regulatory data as public.
- Geographic analysis showed Uber concentration in outer boroughs underserved by yellow cabs.
- Directly informed the NYC ride-share cap debate in 2015.
- Classic data journalism methodology: public records request + spatial visualization + policy impact.
- Dataset still widely used for urban mobility and data visualization tutorials.
