## Summary

In late 2012, EMC and VMware were in the process of spinning out assets into a new company that would become Pivotal. The GigaOm article covers the re-emergence of this spinoff, which combined several properties: Cloud Foundry (the open-source PaaS platform), Pivotal Labs (the agile software consultancy formerly known as Pivotal), the Spring Framework (the dominant Java enterprise framework), and GemFire (an in-memory data grid).

The strategy was to create a new enterprise software company positioned for the cloud-native era — not tied to VMware's hypervisor business or EMC's storage business. Paul Maritz (former Microsoft executive and VMware CEO) was brought in to lead it. The thesis: enterprise development practices were changing fundamentally, and a platform company built around Cloud Foundry, agile practices, and modern Java tooling could capture the middleware layer as enterprises modernized.

Pivotal went public in 2018 at a $9.2B valuation, having grown Cloud Foundry into a major enterprise PaaS with government and Fortune 500 adoption. It was acquired by VMware in 2019 for $2.7B — a lower price than its IPO valuation, reflecting competitive pressure from Kubernetes and managed cloud services that had undercut the proprietary PaaS model Cloud Foundry represented.

## Key points

- Pivotal (2012 spinout): combined Cloud Foundry PaaS, Pivotal Labs consulting, Spring Framework, GemFire
- Strategic bet: enterprises would need a modern development platform as they moved to the cloud
- Cloud Foundry competed with Heroku, Google App Engine, and later Kubernetes-based platforms
- IPO'd at $9.2B in 2018; acquired by VMware at $2.7B in 2019 — Kubernetes undercut its PaaS model
- Paul Maritz (ex-Microsoft, VMware CEO) led — deep enterprise executive credentials matched to the market
