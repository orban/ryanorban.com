## Summary

Manning's Build a Career in Data Science by Emily Robinson and Jacqueline Nolis is unusual in the data science book landscape: it focuses on the career and professional side rather than the technical side. There are dozens of books teaching ML algorithms; this one teaches how to get a data science job, what to expect when you start, and how to grow in the role.

The book covers: building a portfolio (what projects signal the right skills for which type of role), the job search process (data science job titles are inconsistent — data scientist at one company means analyst work at another), the interview process (the diversity of data science interviews: SQL tests, Python coding, statistics questions, ML design, take-home projects), navigating the first job (stakeholder management, framing projects to show business value, avoiding the analyst trap), and career growth (moving from individual contributor to tech lead or management).

The co-authors bring complementary perspectives: Robinson (from the tidyverse R community and Stitch Fix) has a statistics background; Nolis (from a consulting background) has industry breadth. The combination produces advice that applies across the range of data science roles rather than optimizing for one type.

## Key points

- Career-focused, not technically-focused — rare in the data science book landscape.
- Job search: navigating inconsistent job titles, building a portfolio that matches role type, interview process diversity.
- First job: stakeholder management, framing technical work in business terms, avoiding the analyst trap.
- Career growth: IC → tech lead or management paths in data science organizations.
- By Emily Robinson (Stitch Fix, tidyverse community) and Jacqueline Nolis (consulting background).
- Complements technical books: Best Data Science Books, 160 DS interview questions, [Hiring Without Whiteboards](/notes/hiring-without-whiteboards/) in vault.
