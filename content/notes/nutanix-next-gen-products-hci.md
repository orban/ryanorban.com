## Summary

Nutanix's December 2012 product announcement extended its hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) lineup, reinforcing its positioning in the software-defined datacenter market. Saved via a retweet from Nutanix's official account, this press release reflects the company's active marketing push during a period when HCI was still a nascent category competing against entrenched SAN and NAS architectures from EMC, NetApp, and others.

The software-defined datacenter framing was deliberate — VMware had popularized the term earlier in 2012, and Nutanix was positioning its HCI appliances as the natural hardware substrate for that vision. The key claim: traditional three-tier infrastructure (separate servers, network switches, storage arrays) could be replaced by x86 nodes running Nutanix Acropolis software that handled storage, compute, and virtualization management in a unified stack.

By December 2012, Nutanix had shipped the NX-3000 series and was developing the next-generation NX-6000 (all-flash). The company was scaling its sales force and competing directly for VMware-centric enterprise accounts. Founder Dheeraj Pandey and CTO Mohit Aron (who later founded Cohesity) had built a product that genuinely simplified data center operations — fewer vendors, fewer cables, faster provisioning.

## Key points

- Nutanix December 2012: extending the hyperconverged infrastructure lineup as a pre-IPO growth startup
- "Software-defined datacenter" positioning aligned with VMware's architectural vision for the modern data center
- HCI value prop: replace three-tier (server + network + storage) with unified x86 appliances running converged software
- Competitive context: attacking EMC, NetApp, HP storage, and IBM storage simultaneously
- Nutanix IPO'd at $9B in 2016; the HCI market it created is now standard enterprise infrastructure
