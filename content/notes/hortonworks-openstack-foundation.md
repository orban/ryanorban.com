## Summary

Hortonworks joined the OpenStack Foundation in January 2013, signaling the beginning of a more formal convergence between the Hadoop/big data world and the open-source cloud infrastructure world. Hortonworks was founded in 2011 by former Yahoo engineers who had built much of the original Hadoop codebase; the company commercialized Apache Hadoop and competed with Cloudera for enterprise big data customers.

The join was strategic: Hadoop clusters in 2013 were typically deployed on bare metal because virtualization overhead was considered too expensive for I/O-intensive workloads. But as OpenStack matured and hypervisor overhead shrank, running Hadoop on virtualized infrastructure became increasingly viable. "Virtualized Hadoop is the future" (from the tweet that prompted this bookmark) captured the trajectory: enterprises wanted to consolidate workloads onto a common cloud platform, and Hortonworks joining OpenStack was them positioning for that world.

The longer-term outcome was more complicated: Hadoop as a paradigm faced disruption from Spark, cloud-native data platforms (BigQuery, Databricks), and the serverless data warehousing pattern. Hortonworks merged with Cloudera in 2019. But in January 2013, the Hadoop + OpenStack combination represented one credible vision for the enterprise data infrastructure stack.

## Key points

- Hortonworks founded by former Yahoo Hadoop engineers; primary commercial Hadoop distributor alongside Cloudera
- Bare-metal Hadoop was the 2013 standard — virtualization overhead was considered too costly for MapReduce I/O
- The trajectory toward OpenStack-managed Hadoop anticipated the later shift to cloud-managed data platforms
- OpenStack Foundation memberships were a signal of enterprise commitment; Hortonworks joining put big data in the OpenStack ecosystem
- Hortonworks + Cloudera merged in 2019; the combined company is now Cloudera under private equity ownership
