## Summary

Moritz Stefaner — a prominent data visualization designer — created a series of city maps using Foursquare and Yelp check-in and review data to visualize urban activity patterns. Rather than showing physical geography, his maps encoded when and where people were eating, drinking, working out, and socializing — essentially making visible the social rhythms of city life that conventional maps miss.

Foursquare data in 2013 was particularly rich for this purpose: the app collected dense, geo-tagged behavioral data across categories (coffee shops, gyms, bars, offices), timestamped and voluntarily shared. Mapping density of check-ins by category and time of day revealed distinct neighborhood identities — the morning coffee cluster in a financial district, late-night bar crawl routes in entertainment zones, weekend brunch patterns in residential areas.

The Wired Design framing emphasized the visualizations as both informative and aesthetic — Stefaner's work was notable for its visual quality, not just its analytical content. The annotation noted Ryan wanted a process piece on this — the technical methodology behind the visualization. This kind of urban data work was early urban informatics, predating the smart cities discourse and the more systematic study of human mobility from mobile data.

## Key points

- Foursquare as urban data source: voluntary geo-tagged check-ins with category metadata — a behavioral dataset that existed because people used the app, not because it was collected for research.
- Temporal patterns in city data: time-of-day and day-of-week reveal neighborhood identity better than static density maps.
- Moritz Stefaner's approach: data visualization as craft — choosing color, density, and projection to surface the patterns without overwhelming the viewer.
- Urban informatics: the emerging field of using digital trace data to understand city behavior — this work was early in that tradition.
- Yelp reviews vs. Foursquare check-ins: complementary signals — Yelp captures sentiment and category; Foursquare captures foot traffic patterns.
- Design observation: maps like this influenced the genre of city data portraits that proliferated on data visualization blogs through 2015-2018.
