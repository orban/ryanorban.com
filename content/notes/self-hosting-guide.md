## Summary

The [Self-Hosting Guide](/notes/self-hosting-guide/) by Mike Royal is a comprehensive GitHub reference for running services on your own hardware rather than relying on cloud providers. It covers the breadth of the homelab and self-hosting ecosystem: NAS setup, media servers (Plex, Jellyfin), home automation (Home Assistant), password managers (Bitwarden/Vaultwarden), VPN solutions (WireGuard, Tailscale), ad blocking (Pi-hole), and dozens of other categories.

The guide is comprehensive rather than opinionated — it lists what's available in each category more than recommending a specific stack. This makes it a useful orientation resource for someone new to self-hosting who wants to understand the option space, and a reference for people already in the space who want to discover tools they haven't tried.

The motivations for self-hosting are a mix of privacy (data stays on your hardware), cost (one-time hardware vs. ongoing subscription), control (you own the stack), and learning (running servers is educational). The community around these tools tends to overlap with homelab enthusiasts, privacy advocates, and developers who enjoy the infrastructure work as much as the end result. TrueNAS, Proxmox, Unraid, and Docker are the common substrate technologies that make running many services practical on consumer hardware.

## Key points

- Comprehensive reference for self-hosted alternatives to cloud services — NAS, media, automation, security.
- Covers Plex/Jellyfin, Home Assistant, Vaultwarden, Pi-hole, Tailscale, and many others.
- Orientation resource: lists what exists in each category rather than making opinionated recommendations.
- Self-hosting motivations: privacy, cost, control, and learning.
- Common substrate: Docker, Proxmox, Unraid, TrueNAS make running many services practical.
