## Summary

Saved in August 2020 via a Twitter link, this is part of the wave of institutional Bitcoin investment theses that emerged in 2019-2020 as hedge funds, family offices, and institutional investors began seriously evaluating Bitcoin as a portfolio asset. The original destination URL is a Twitter short link (the resolved target is unavailable), but the genre is well-characterized.

The typical institutional Bitcoin thesis from this era made several arguments: (1) **Portfolio diversification** — Bitcoin had historically low correlation with equities and bonds, making it useful as a diversifier even at small allocations (1-5%). (2) **Asymmetric risk/return** — if Bitcoin succeeds at even a fraction of its digital gold or reserve currency potential, a small allocation could be extremely valuable; the downside is capped at the allocation size. (3) **Store of value thesis** — Bitcoin's fixed supply (21M coins), halving cycle mechanics, and growing institutional adoption positioned it as a hedge against monetary expansion/inflation. (4) **Option on the monetary future** — Paul Tudor Jones, Fidelity Digital Assets, and others framed Bitcoin as an option on the possibility that digital assets become systemic.

By August 2020, MicroStrategy had just announced its first corporate Bitcoin purchase (August 11, 2020), and the institutional narrative was gaining momentum heading into the 2020-2021 bull cycle.

## Key points

- August 2020 context: institutional Bitcoin interest was just becoming mainstream (MicroStrategy, Tudor Jones).
- Standard institutional thesis arguments: diversification (low correlation), asymmetric upside, store of value, supply scarcity.
- Halving cycle: Bitcoin's programmed supply reduction every ~4 years reduces new supply — historically correlated with price increases.
- Fidelity Digital Assets, Grayscale, Galaxy Digital were the primary institutional-grade Bitcoin research publishers in 2020.
