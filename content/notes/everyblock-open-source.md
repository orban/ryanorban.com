## Summary

EveryBlock was Adrian Holovaty's landmark experiment in hyperlocal civic journalism — a platform that aggregated neighborhood-level data (crime incidents, restaurant health inspections, building permits, local news) and organized it by address and block. Founded in 2008 with Knight Foundation funding, it was acquired by MSNBC in 2009 and shut down in 2013 when MSNBC discontinued it.

The content field captures the moment of shutdown: "@ajturner: hoping that someone like @codeforamerica picks up the @everyblock code & deploys for cities" — the open-source release of the code was an opportunity for Code for America or civic tech practitioners to continue the work under community ownership.

EveryBlock's significance: it demonstrated that structured civic data could be made meaningful at the neighborhood level. Rather than city-wide crime statistics, it showed what happened on your block. Rather than aggregate restaurant inspection scores, it showed the specific violations at the restaurant on your corner. The data journalism approach — scraping public records, normalizing them, and presenting them geographically — was influential on subsequent civic tech projects.

## Key points

- Adrian Holovaty created EveryBlock after building ChicagoCrime.org (2005), which first demonstrated Chicago crime data on Google Maps — a prototype of civic data journalism
- Knight Foundation funding was crucial to EveryBlock's early development — a model that became standard for civic tech projects in the 2010s
- The MSNBC acquisition (2009) followed the pattern of media companies buying civic tech experiments, then shutting them when they didn't scale commercially
- Code for America (founded 2009) was the natural home for this kind of work — a nonprofit deploying technologists into government to build civic tools
- The open-source release of EveryBlock's code reflected a principle: civic data infrastructure should be a public good, not proprietary
