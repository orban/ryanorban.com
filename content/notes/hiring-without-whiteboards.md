## Summary

"[Hiring Without Whiteboards](/notes/hiring-without-whiteboards/)" is a community-maintained list (originally a GitHub repository, also mirrored as an Airtable) of companies that don't use algorithmic whiteboard interviews as their primary hiring filter. Instead, these companies use practical interviews: take-home projects, pair programming on real codebases, system design discussions, or portfolio/work reviews.

The list exists because the standard software engineering interview process — solving algorithm puzzles on a whiteboard under time pressure — is widely criticized as a poor proxy for actual job performance. The skills tested (memorizing dynamic programming patterns, performing graph traversal quickly) rarely match what engineers do day-to-day. The result is a hiring process that filters more on interview preparation than on engineering ability, which disadvantages candidates who can't afford months of LeetCode practice.

Companies on the list typically describe what they do instead: paid take-home projects (with the applicant's time compensated), collaborative pair programming sessions on their actual codebase, technical discussion rounds where candidates explain past projects in depth, or systems design conversations without algorithmic tricks. These approaches are generally considered better signals for senior roles where communication and system thinking matter more than algorithm recall.

The list is a useful starting point for engineers who want to target companies with more thoughtful hiring processes — both for finding good matches and for signaling companies worth working for (hiring process often reflects engineering culture).

## Key points

- Community list of companies using practical interviews instead of algorithmic puzzles.
- Practical alternatives: paid take-home projects, pair programming on real code, portfolio reviews, systems design.
- Criticism of whiteboard interviews: tests preparation not ability; disadvantages candidates without free time for LeetCode.
- Useful for job searching: companies that avoid whiteboard interviews tend to have more collaborative engineering cultures.
