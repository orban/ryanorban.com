## Summary

Whistle launched in 2013 as an activity tracker for dogs — a small device that clips to a collar and monitors activity, rest, and calorie burn, syncing data to a smartphone app. The pitch was explicitly framed around the quantified self movement applied to pets: just as humans were tracking their own health with Fitbit and Jawbone UP, dog owners could now monitor their pets' activity patterns and health indicators.

The device used accelerometer data (the same approach as human wearables) to distinguish activity from rest, count steps equivalent, and build baseline health profiles. The app showed daily activity trends and could flag anomalies — potentially an early indicator of illness or injury, since changes in activity levels are diagnostically meaningful.

Whistle was part of a wave of pet tech and IoT hardware startups in 2013 that extended the quantified self concept beyond humans. Tagg GPS tracker, PetPace health monitor, and Whistle all launched within a few years of each other. Whistle was later acquired by Mars Petcare in 2016 for ~$117M, validating the category.

## Key points

- Accelerometer-based activity tracking for dogs: steps, rest, calorie estimate, sleep quality
- Companion app builds activity baseline and flags anomalies — clinically useful for detecting early illness
- Same sensor approach as Fitbit but adapted for non-human movement patterns
- Pet tech category: IoT applied to animals, a niche that grew significantly through the 2010s
- Mars Petcare acquisition (2016) for ~$117M validated the quantified pets market
- The context tweet: "better mobile app than Fitbit for humans" — a real 2013 complaint about human fitness trackers
