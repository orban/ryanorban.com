## Summary

Greg Egan — one of the hardest of hard SF authors, known for novels where the physics actually works — wrote a four-part educational series called Foundations originally published in *Eidolon* magazine. The series covers the four pillars of twentieth-century physics most influential to science fiction: Special Relativity, General Relativity (via a bridge article From Special to General), Black Holes, and Quantum Mechanics.

The stated target is general readers with no more than high school algebra and geometry. Egan's approach is to derive concrete, mathematical predictions from each theory's core concepts rather than narrating the history or philosophical debates — the focus is on *how the theory works* and *what it predicts*, not who discovered it and why scientists argued about it. This makes the series unusually rigorous for accessible physics writing: you end up understanding why time dilation follows from the postulates of special relativity, not just that it does.

For SF authors and readers, understanding these theories at this level matters because it determines what's physically possible in a story and what's hand-waving. Egan's novels (*Diaspora*, *Schild's Ladder*, *Permutation City*) are notable for actually getting the physics right — the Foundations series is partly the pedagogical companion to that body of work, explaining the reasoning that underlies his fictional extrapolations.

## Key points

- Four articles: Special Relativity, Special → General, Black Holes, Quantum Mechanics.
- Accessible with high school math — algebra and geometry only.
- Derives concrete predictions from core axioms — not history or philosophy, but mechanics.
- Written by Greg Egan — hard SF author whose novels depend on correct physics.
- Originally in *Eidolon* magazine; useful as a rigorous primer for SF authors and readers.
