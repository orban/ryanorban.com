## Summary

Jiaxin Huang, Shixiang Gu, Le Hou, Yuexin Wu, Xuezhi Wang, Hongkun Yu, and Jiawei Han (arXiv 2210.11610, 2022) show a simple but important result: a language model can improve its own reasoning ability using only its own outputs as training signal.

The method has three steps. First, the model generates multiple chain-of-thought reasoning paths for each training problem using few-shot prompting. Second, it uses self-consistency — majority voting over the generated answers — to identify high-confidence outputs. Third, it finetunes on those high-confidence (question, chain-of-thought, answer) triples. No ground-truth labels or human feedback required.

This is a form of self-training: generate pseudo-labels, keep the ones the model agrees with most, finetune. The twist is that chain-of-thought lets the model produce richer supervision signal (an explicit reasoning trace, not just an answer) and self-consistency provides a principled filter for quality.

The results on reasoning benchmarks were positive: consistent gains on GSM8K (math word problems), DROP (discrete reasoning over text), OpenBookQA, and ANLI. The gains are meaningful even though the method requires no additional labeled data after an initial few-shot seed.

The paper connects to a cluster of related ideas: STaR (Self-Taught Reasoner, Zeiler & Goodman 2022) uses a similar loop but with a rationalization trick for wrong answers. ReST and ReST^EM later extended the idea. The underlying intuition — that a model can learn from its own correct outputs — is now central to post-training pipelines at scale, where synthetic data generated by the model being trained is often a major component.

The key limitation acknowledged in the paper is that self-improvement is bounded by the model's existing capabilities. Self-consistency as a filter can only select from what the model can generate; it won't introduce fundamentally new reasoning strategies. Extrapolating beyond the training distribution remains hard.

## Key points

- **Self-improvement loop**: generate CoT → filter with self-consistency majority vote → finetune on high-confidence triples → repeat
- No human labels needed for the improvement phase — only the few-shot seed examples
- Consistent gains on reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, DROP, OpenBookQA, ANLI) without external supervision
- Closely related to STaR: both use self-generated rationales as training signal; STaR uses rationalization (showing correct answer + asking for rationale) for wrong answers
- Self-consistency majority vote acts as a filter, not a generator of new reasoning strategies — capability ceiling is bounded by the base model
- Precursor to synthetic data pipelines in modern RLHF/post-training setups
